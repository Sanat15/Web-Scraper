An abortion physician writes an account of his forty-some years' experience, from his early years as a resident in Philadelphia handling botched wire-hanger abortions in the emergency room to the present. Arguing that nobody is actually "pro-abortion," he writes of the struggle to come to terms with the societal necessity for the procedure from a practical, feminist perspective, using voluminous examples from friends, colleagues, and patients' lives.
