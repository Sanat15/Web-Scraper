Titanic. 70 years after its maiden voyage, the name of the superb ship still captures the imagination. Jewel of the White Star Line, the 882 1/2 foot long liner was sunk by an iceberg during the cold hours of the morning of April 15, 1912; its magnetism, however, lived on, pulling others to it. 

Many have been intrigued by the lost liner; novels and movies have fascinated audiences for years. The Titanic survived in the realm of fantasy, an untouchable phantom. It was not until 1981 that the possibility of bringing the ghost ship back to life became a reality. 

In that year Texas A&M’s research vessel Gyre left Woods Hole, Massachusetts. Its guiding light: Jack Grimm, oil tycoon, geologist and gambler, who personally financed expeditions to find the Loch Ness Monster, Bigfoot and Noah’s Ark. Searching for the Titanic, though, became more than a media magnet; it was a search into history itself. 

Reaching the area where the Titanic sank, required the most up to date equipment and a team of scientists to monitor it. Dr. Fred Speiss, of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, headed that special group of men and women; Mike Harris, award winning filmmaker, accompanied them, to record the voyage of the Gyre. William Hoffman and Jack Grimm rounded out this unique crew. 

What these adventurers found - interspersed with chronicles of the story of the Titanic before, during and after its loss - is logged in this exceptional book. Illustrated with pictures taken in 1912 and 1981, complete with charts drawn then and now, and astounding evidence of just where the Titanic is, makes Beyond Reach the most fascinating search in modern history.
