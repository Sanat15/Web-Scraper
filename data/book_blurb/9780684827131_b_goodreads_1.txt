This moving novel resonates with particular meaning in post-Million Man March America as it explores, from an African-American perspective, two enduring questions: How do people become a family? And how does a man become a father?Brothers Quincy Crawford and Elliott Davis have been estranged for years, ever since Quincy rejected and Elliott accepted their stepfather. As Somebody's Child opens, these two, now both in their mid-twenties, confront the issue of parental responsibility in their own lives.

Quincy, a high school teacher, finds himself caught among a pregnant girlfriend he does not love, an old friend who suddenly reappears with a painful secret, and a little boy he believes to be his son. Elliott, his promising basketball career ended by injury, seeks reluctantly to reconcile with the longtime girlfriend who has borne a daughter by another man.

Set in New York City and in Boston in 1976, at the height of that city's school-busing wars, this searing and timely novel is an impressive follow-up to the author's first novel, Crossover. Published in 1992, Publishers Weekly hailed it as a "dynamic debut", declaring, "With scathing realism and taut, visceral prose, Williams delineates the rites of passage to black manhood.
