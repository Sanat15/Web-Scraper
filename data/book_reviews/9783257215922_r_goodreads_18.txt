Rating: 3/5

A mid 1930s screwball mystery that has a bit of everything: a possible suicide, the theft of a corpse, a couple of murders, a midnight disinterment and a touch of gangland rough stuff. It starts with private investigator Bill Crane being asked to confirm the identity of a suicide decedent but his task becomes complicated when the corpse disappears. Crane is joined by a couple of partners from his Agency and the hunt is on. But this is no police procedural. The team goes through most of the usual steps in solving a case like this but often in a drunk and fun loving, semi-raucous condition. The screwball element is in full force. There’s a lot of local color, with scenes set in different types of hotels, restaurants, dance clubs, bars and one eventful penthouse party. Everything in this story is mid 1930s Chicago - from the police, gangsters and taxis drivers to the slang, wisecracks, the music and dance moves. 
It’s very well written but I found the pace slow, at times meandering and a bit confusing. Clues were dropped along the way (amid the many shenanigans of the team of investigators) and there was a nice wrap up to the mystery in the last chapters. All said, I found it an interesting story - very much of its time - in which the screwball elements often overshadowed the mystery and investigative elements.
