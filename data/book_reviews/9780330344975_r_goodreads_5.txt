Rating: 4/5

I suppose I’m a cheap date when it comes to stories set in Cornwall. I don’t need much, really, just a few waves rolling in, the call of a lonely curlew on a cliff, a hot Cornish man (Ross Poldark) in the sand, nearby. Whatever.

This series is beguiling. Book 3, JEREMY POLDARK, was no less so. 

I was positively captivated by the appearance of Caroline Penvenen (one of my absolute favorite characters in the BBC series, as well). No offense to redheads, but even though Caroline is described as one in the book, I met her first as a blonde and a blonde she will remain. I loved her, dearly, and her dog, Horace, is as welcome to the story as Ross’s knee-length breeches.

To the ten or so people who will read this review, and to the 3 of those who have actually read this book, I would like to ask: What do you think was meant by the following exchange between siblings Francis and Verity Poldark:

He frowned at [Verity] as if trying to see her with the unprejudiced eyes of a stranger. His shirt was torn at the neck and his coat stained with mud. Only she knew how passionately he had resented her marriage. Since they grew up his love for her had been selfish, possessive—a little more than brotherly. 

Nothing like this was ever hinted at, in the BBC series, but I felt, after reading this, that Francis might have had some FLOWERS IN THE ATTIC sort of feelings for his sister. Did anyone else get this vibe? I hope it’s just my warped mind reading too much into his being “a little more than brotherly.” 

I like brothers to be brotherly, and I was just wondering what others made of that passage.

Moving on: I love this series. It provides a grand opportunity for some decadent escapism. I continue to marvel at the way Winston Graham depicts multiple marriages, full of humor, misunderstandings, joy, fighting, laughter, infidelities, competition and grace.

It’s all here. I can’t wait to go back.
