Rating: 2/5

Contains some nuggets of wisdom on parenting, but also espouses enough odd views so that I would not be able to recommend this book. 

Odd views:

1. Covenant theology. Though not "odd" per se, he does allow his particular viewpoint to shape the entire book, limiting the value for those who may not share his perspective. 

2. He states that God's primary vehicle to build a people for himself is the family, not the church (p. 27). 

3. He supports strange standards -- no card playing or dancing (p. 54). 

4. He believes that parents are responsible, not just for the behavior of their children, but their salvation as well (p. 115). 

5. Sabbatarianism (pp. 168-173). 

At the same time, he has some wonderful advice for parents. He challenges us that God wants to use our children for the advancement of his kingdom, that he has entrusted them to us for a time, and that we must train them for his glory. 

 - "We may count most confidently on the Father, who has entrusted the child to us, to give the grace to train" (p. 58). 
 - "It is only in a life of pure and whole-hearted devotion, in which selfishness and worldliness are crucified, and our lives lived for God, that we can really train children for heaven!" (p. 90). 
 - "It is only the heart that gains the heart" (p. 105). 
 - "I think with shame that much which I reprove in them, is only the reflection of what they have seen in me" (p. 114). 
 - My favorite quote: "Your child is God's schoolmaster to bring you ever nearer to Christ" (p. 125). 
 - "God looks upon him as His; he has only been lent and entrusted to me to train. The child is indeed not mine but the Lord's" (p. 157).
