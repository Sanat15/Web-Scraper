CATCH YO' DEATH

I was right around twenty,
up to my elbows in some sink,
washing dishes at a big
downtown hotel.

Someone tapped me on the shoulder,
said VIP now checking in,
put on your bellboy hat
and head outside.

I didn't put on my bellboy hat
because I didn't have one,
just the ratty wet apron
I always wore.

Stepping out front
into those sub-zero winds,
I saw a tour bus so sleek,
thing looked like it could fly.

The only thing sleeker
than the tour bus itself was
the mink coat on the woman
stepping off it.

And I'll never forget her words
to my soaked white skinny ass,
there upon the frozen sidewalk
of my youth:

"Boy,
you'll catch yo' death out here."

Instead I caught a cart and
hauled her six or seven bags
into the lift up to her room
on the fifth floor.

Slipping me a crisp Jackson,
ostensibly to buy a coat,
how could she know I'd likely
spend it all on booze.
