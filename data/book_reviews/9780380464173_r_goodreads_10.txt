Rating: 4/5

I have loved the Lord Peter Wimsey novels and stories since I was a teenager, so reading this book was very fun. I've paged through it in the distant past, but this was the first time I read the whole thing.

It reminds me a bit of Sayers' essay "The Dates in the Red-Headed League", because like that, it operates from the conceit that the fictional characters are real historical figures. Particularly fun (if a little strained) are the connections to some of the minor events of history that live on in public consciousness (at least for those of my generation and earlier) in turns of phrase or memorable myths. It was also fun to watch the way the author, who was the Fitzalan Pursuivant of Arms Extraordinary (I had to look up what that post is, but I inferred it is a position that requires considerable expertise in heraldry and genealogy), grapples with conflicting evidence. 

The fact that there is a body of conflicting evidence that includes various publications as well as private letters and remarks of Sayers brought home to me the popularity of Lord Peter during Sayers' lifetime. I knew the character was popular--and he certainly has been much loved among quite a few of my acquaintance--but I'm not sure I realized quite how much before reading this.

I write in Nov. 2025, during the struggle in America over whether we will remain a democracy or descend into a fascist dictatorship. I have to say this makes me think a bit differently about Peter and his privilege than I used to do.

Definitely a book for a devoted Lord Peter or Dorothy L Sayers fan, but not likely of any interest to someone who isn't.
