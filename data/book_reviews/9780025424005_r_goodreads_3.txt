Rating: 4/5

A really interesting take on the barnstorming days and early days of commercial aviation written by pilot turned author, the late Ernest K. Gann. 
The vivid description of the author navigating a plane load of passengers to their destination in bad weather by relying on the imprecise tones of radio beams in his headset is amazing. It’s remarkable pilots, let alone passengers, had the nerve to fly anywhere. 
I think this book is out of print. I found my copy in a used bookstore. 
If you’re an aviation fan, it’s a must read but it’s not as good as Gann’s Fate is the Hunter, also an account of early commercial aviation. 
This book contains some of the author’s reprinted stories from flying magazines plus some additional, new, content so it reads like an anthology instead of a novel. 
But, even for the non aviation fan there are illuminating stories and anecdotes.
