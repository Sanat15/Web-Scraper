Rating: 1/5

TW: 18+
Foolish me thought all classics would be clean and wonderful. I was proved wrong for the first time on this matter. This novel was the literary equivalent of being promised a romantic countryside stroll and instead stumbling into a very explicit nature documentary.

If you’ve ever wondered what happens when a novelist decides to rebel against the British stiff upper lip by writing a story where everyone’s lower half gets far more attention—this is it. The infamous tale of forbidden love between an aristocrat’s wife and her rugged gamekeeper is less about emotional depth and more about anatomical exploration. JEEZ

Sure, there are themes of class divide, post-war trauma and the search for authentic connection. But let’s be honest: those are just the polite appetizers before the author serves up a full-course meal of earthy euphemisms and woodland rendezvous. The prose often feels like it’s trying to seduce the reader—and not in a charming Mr. Darcy way, but more like a sweaty Mellors muttering poetic innuendo in a shed.

Scandalous!!!
