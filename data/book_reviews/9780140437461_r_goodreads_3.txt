i was the woman lying on that bed, stretched out helplessly in her soiled white nightgown, her breath caught somewhere between life and fear.  

bartram-haugh was no manor, it was fuseli's merciless canvas. and fear was the nightmare that emerged from the darkness and settled upon my chest. 

 le fanu had placed me at the very heart of that painting, quietly closing the door behind me to see whether i would still be able to breathe.  

fear didn't arrive as an enemy of flesh and blood.  it drifted in like a shapeless nightmare and laid its full weight across my chest.  it was so unbearably heavy that with every breath i drew, my lungs filled not with air but with the damp, mould and rot of bartram-haugh. 

 i wanted to scream. to tear my throat apart with a cry, to rip that weight from my body. but my voice had already been taken from me.  

my lips moved, words formed inside my mind, yet nothing emerged strong enough to pierce the deaf darkness of the room. it was as though the house had slowly begun thinking in my place. 

 pinned to that bed somewhere between sleep and waking, obedience and madness, i could feel that blind horse's head reaching out from behind the curtain, its sightless gaze fixed directly upon my soul.  

i could feel that the terror pressing down on me was not silas alone. every time i saw him, something inside me recoiled.  my father's voice still lingered behind me, the promises i had made to him echoing endlessly in my ears.  i wanted to run, yet loyalty had fastened invisible chains around my feet. 

i could open doors, walk the corridors, stand at the windows and look outside.  yet none of it meant that i was free.  i could speak, but no one truly heard me. 

at times i felt less like a living person than a ghost wandering through the ruins of her own life. 

 the most maddening part wasn't being certain of anything. i could no longer tell whether my fears were justified.  i searched every glance, every silence, every unfinished sentence for some hidden threat, only to feel ashamed of myself afterward. 

 was silas truly a monster, or merely an old, broken man the world had chosen to misunderstand?  

the more i remembered the unwavering trust my father had placed in him, the more i began to doubt my own instincts. there are few things more terrifying than losing faith in your own mind. 

i feared silas, yet one night i realised that fear had another face.  he had not been the one who brought me into that house. the man who had placed me in his hands was the man who loved me.  my father's ghost haunted the corridors of bartram-haugh as relentlessly as silas's shadow. 

sometimes i found myself confusing their footsteps.  one seemed to call me deeper into the darkness, the other urged me to turn back.  yet both voices already belonged to the past and i walked on alone between them. 

i didn't close my eyes until morning.  the nightmare still rested upon my chest, the blind horse's head waited behind the curtains and the house breathed quietly around me.  yet something within me refused to die. 

 fear taught me to doubt my own mind. it taught me to fear people. it taught me the language of shadows.  yet it couldn't tear away that final spark. because no matter how close the darkness came, i knew i was still looking back at it. 

 and the woman lying on that bed was not the one who closed her eyes in the end.
