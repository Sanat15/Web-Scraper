Rating: 4/5

This book was described on a blog I like as "the perfect read-aloud." I'm not sure it quite lived up to that high expectation set for it, but I did find it to be a well-written young adult book (from an era before the horrible category of YA fiction was a thing). 

Its page-turning power increased toward the end, and I enjoyed the subtle commentary throughout on the evil of Communism vs. the goods of the free world. I don't know how these themes would read to a contemporary young person, but it could be a very good accompaniment to learning about the history of the Iron Curtain and the Soviet era. 

All in all, it is the kind of book I would be happy to see my children land upon in their reading, even though I might not embrace it as a must-read. For the right child (particularly boys) at the right time, it could be the sort of inspiring story that might just be life changing.
