Rating: 3/5

4-star content in a 3-star format (and a bit aged)… the authors delve both too deep and not deep enough, providing a wide ranging and simultaneously narrow (in certain areas) argument for the use of history as a tool in decision making, particularly by those in government. 

It very clearly is taken from lectures, which are probably much more entertaining in person than they are to read. Were it 50% shorter or less discursive, it’d be awesome - instead, you find yourself wrapped up in the feeling of intellectual self-admiration that academics are wont to exhibit.

If you get it from your library, skim the areas that are most of interest and then hit the Appendices.
