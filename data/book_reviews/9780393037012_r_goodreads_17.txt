Rating: 3/5

Louie L’Amour for the Napoleonic War crowd.

And I like Louie L’Amour, he’s a better writer than he generally gets credit for (probably mostly from people who have never read his work) and he tells a good story.

Patrick O’Brian is a better writer than he may get credit for and he tells a good story.

We follow an odd couple of sorts, a rough and tumble British Lieutenant who gets his first command and a first time ever ship’s doctor who battle the enemies, are tough guys on their own boat and who have a swashbuckling good time doing it all.

It was enjoyable to dust off my nautical terms and O’Brian has created a very atmospheric piece set around 1798 in the Mediterranean and the author was good enough to mix in some timely sub-plots to keep the pages turning. 

This is also the first of the successful and entertaining Aubrey &amp; Maturin books that would go on for more than thirty years and would see O’Brian publishing twenty-one books. From what looks like, the series itself is a bildungsroman about Aubrey’s career and this was fun, and I can see myself reading more from this series and from this writer.
