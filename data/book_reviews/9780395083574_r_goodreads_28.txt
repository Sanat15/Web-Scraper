Rating: 3/5

A rather dull and dry work of anthropology, Ruth Benedict's Patterns of Culture throws around unscientific terms like "Dionysian" and "treacherous" to describe cultures from around the world in what is ostensibly a serious scientific enquiry.
Focusing on the three case studies of the Pueblo and Northwest Coast Indians of North America and the Dobu of the Pacific, Benedict makes broad generalizations of these cultures, describing them as "Apollonian", "Dionysian" and "treacherous" respectively in an attempt to capture their essence.
Unfortunately her analysis seems unconvincing, apart from rightly pointing out that certain "human types" are considered aberrant in some cultures while in others they would fit right in.
The work is at its best when it merely describes the various customs of the people studied but flounders when it comes to analysis.
Overall I found the book rather boring or maybe I just realized that I don't like anthropology all that much.
