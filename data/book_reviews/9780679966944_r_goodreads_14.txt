Rating: 5/5

Junie B. Jones occupies a very special place in my heart---even as an adult. 

As a growing reader, I was drawn to Junie B.'s humor, chaos, and authenticity. One of the most enchanting things about Barbara Park's best-known series is that it represents little girls without the sugar, spice, and everything nice (which is often peppered into a lot of other children's media). Park makes little girls---weird little girls---feel seen in a way that isn't demeaning or forced. As a (once) weird little girl, that means a whole lot. 

Reading this material intended for children, written by an adult, makes me appreciate the ways in which authors must go outside of themselves to create meaningful stories. I enjoyed Junie B. as a six-year-old because she was me, and I felt like she was telling her story in a way only *she* could. Park's authorial voice slips through in the adult dialogue---imperceptible to a child, but recognizable (and hilarious) to an adult. 

In a world of rude adults and meanies named Jim, Junie B. tells young and returning readers alike to make the best of things. Even yucky blucky fruitcake. 

I love you, Barbara Park!
