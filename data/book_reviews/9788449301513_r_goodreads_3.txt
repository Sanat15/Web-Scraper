Rating: 5/5

Incredible. Ibn Sina was the first philosophical fiction writer. Some of these full blown fiction allegorical symbolic narratives read like watching Antonioni’s Red Desert, or a Michael Haneke film.

I wasn’t familiar with your game Ibn Sina. I think he wasn’t a heretic just truly ahead of his time. Islam proper and a balanced creedal understanding for the masses hadn’t been put to paper yet from the experiences of the mystics. They knew, what the balance between reason and mysticism was, between theory and practice. Between God’s immanence and his transcendence. They saw it through action. But because a canonised system of being an X Aqeedah Y Fiqh Z Sufi didn’t exist — instead you find these guys like Ibn Sina, Ghazali and Suhrawardi competing and constructing a hackneyed model of what a balance between those three looked like without having the tools or an understanding and initiated audience. The results actually were therefore far more interesting, and loyal to the avant-garde, too sublime to really articulate essence of Islam — an abstract force. I think we’re the victims today, for us its so outlined, explained and systemetised that in a sense we lose some of that essence of its true mystery. That hadith really is pertinent I think here: “Islam came as something strange and it will return as something strange”.

So inspiring to read this stuff. I thought Ibn Sina was gonna be all dry non-fiction theory I had no clue he had a pen like this. Masha’Allah.
