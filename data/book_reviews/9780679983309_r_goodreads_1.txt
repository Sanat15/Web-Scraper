Rating: 2/5

Like so many dogs in film and literature before him, Smasher's poor qualities -- unattractive and physically awkward to the point of property destruction -- make his owners question whether they should keep him . . . until one good deed wipes the slate clean.

On the negative side, when the farmer and his wife are considering getting rid of Smasher, they talk about going full Kristi Noem on his furry butt, right from the first sentence:

"Kindest thing I could do for you," said Farmer Buzzard, "would be to knock you on the head."

Additionally, Ken and Martha Buzzard spend a lot of the book lying to and deceiving each other, so they are perhaps not the best role models.

If your kids don't fixate on the euthanasia thing -- and it does come up again and again even harder -- they will probably enjoy the dog's antics and inner thoughts.
