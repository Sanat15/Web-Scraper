Rating: 3/5

I’ve read almost two-dozen Elmore Leonard novels over the years, and in thinking about them, I can easily merge them into generalizations about his style and his characters. I would say that his plots are crisp and unpredictable. Turns of events are commonplace, and they work with his stories because he knows that humans are mercurial and impulsive. When he creates believable characters it allows his stories to be pushed the boundaries of reasonable behavior.

Mr. Paradise falls short of all the above, and it has a damp blanket feel to it. His human characters slide into stereotypes rather than maintain their unique individuality. This is made worse by the presence of too many characters. Each one gets a reduced share of Leonard's writing, which results in each of them receiving only a basic level of personality. The plot is also an issue in that it's not very imaginative. Mr. Paradise is about crime in Detroit, and that’s about it. The crimes themselves are typical to the city and are similar to those reported almost every day. 

But still, this is Elmore Leonard. While this is certainly not his best work, it brings to mind the age-old saying: there's no such thing as bad pizza.
