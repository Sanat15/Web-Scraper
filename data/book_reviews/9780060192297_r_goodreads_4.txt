Rating: 2/5

It might be unusual to start reading a series by picking up its fourth story, but this was my introduction to the Rei Shimura books and I liked this mystery novel for a lot of things – but not necessarily the mystery. 

In this novel, protagonist Rei Shimura is a writer for a Tokyo newspaper called Gaijin Times, for which she's working on an article about Japan's famous comic books, called manga. As she begins her research, she stumbles upon the death of one of her subject's artists, which leads her to get involved with the Japanese gangster world. 

Rei Shimura is in the special position of being half-Japanese and half-American, making her an outsider within a culture she's familiar with by nature. And that's ultimately what's fun about this book – while the mystery was at no point truly engaging, it's the little things you find out about Japanese life that are fascinating. There are little insights, such as Rei having to pay more money at a waxing salon due to her being hairier than a Japanese woman would be or people changing the way they talk to her upon noticing her accent. 

Thematically, the novel focusses on introducing Westerners to the world of manga and cosplay. I grew up reading quite a few, so I'm fairly familiar with the world of Japanese graphic novels, but I felt like the book did a good job at weaving a cultural lesson into the actual plot. In an unpatronising way, you'll learn the differences in manga genres, the importance they play in people's lives and how characters and makers are idolised. A quick and informative, easy and entertaining read.
