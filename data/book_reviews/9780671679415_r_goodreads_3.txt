Rating: 3/5

There's not too many people I love more than the Marx Brothers but this book could cure anyone of such an affliction. If you've read Lardner, Thurber or Benchley you've read these sentiments handled with greater literary skill. Sure it has its moments but it's hard to feel sorry for Groucho and the decisions he made that led him to this point, and if you know how his life ended then you'll really be scratching your head trying to figure out why it let it happen time and time again. If you don't know how it ended and you're more interested in the mirth than the man - leave it that way - it's sad. The best comedians are informed by powerful pathos and the biographies of Chaplin, Arbuckle and Groucho reinforce this. Chico loved women and cards, Harpo loved everything and everything loved him, Groucho loved a laugh at any expense and it's no surprise to learn that he was rejected socially by literary types, family members, lovers and socialites alike. Banjo players and cynical comedians can wear out their welcomes with similar speed and lack of sustain; plucky and stimulating when the rhythm is right, shrill and grating otherwise. 

It was the brothers combined that made it work on stage and in the movies. It was the rubes and the straight-men that made You Bet Your Life work so well. On his own and without the charm of his brothers to temper his feral cynicism - the tickles of critical humor turn to jabs of bitterness really fast. 

Sadly upon conclusion of this read, and I've just about read all there is to read about my fellow school mates, (Lyons Township High, LaGrange, IL) I have reached the conclusion that the folks of the Algonquin Round Table were right - Harpo was the one you want most at your party. It's Harpo's birthday tomorrow - he is my hero and I hold this day sacred.
