I mostly enjoyed this second volume in the Thieves' World anthology, with plenty of depressing stories of crime, abuse, revenge, and survival, all with a healthy dose of dark humor. I will go on the record as not really liking the introduction of Tempus in one of the stories, an immortal divine avatar who heals from any injury almost instantly. That kind of ruins the gritty street vibe that drew me to the series in the first place. I did some research, and apparently he becomes a huge part of this shared anthology moving forward, but I hope he doesn't take over.

I want to hear read about scoundrels outsmarting evil wizards by the skin of their teeth, or mercenaries' dark plans against the unpopular city governor, not about an invincible god-man strutting around shrugging off anything thrown at him. Otherwise the story becomes about the god-man standing out from the smallfolk, as opposed ABOUT the smallfolk planning and plotting and surviving in a dark, gritty city. Sanctuary started out as a city where EVERYONE is vulnerable, from the beggars and thieves to wizards and even the governor. Thanks to Janet Morris' story, Sanctuary no longer feels like a city where everyone is vulnerable. Now one character seems to be playing by very different rules than everyone else.
