Rating: 5/5

A beautiful overview of the history of Ragusa/Dubrovnik, which for most of its history was a semi-independent (or even fully independent) city-state, though often dominated by greater powers - the Byzantines, the Venetians, the Hungarians, and the Ottomans (and, of course, also interacting with the Serbians, Croats, and Bosnians). The book includes several inserts of pictures showing the art and architecture of Dubrovnik. Most of the book covers the history chronologically, while several chapters towards the end cover society and politics, religious life, cultural life, and architecture. A fascinating history of a city in a rich multi-cultural part of the world and highly recommended for anyone wanting to better understand the plurality of political structures in European history.
