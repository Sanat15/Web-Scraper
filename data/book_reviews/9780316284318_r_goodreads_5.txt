Rating: 4/5

The title is a little bit of a misnomer frankly and while Anais Nin was certainly promiscuous and famous for her posthumous erotic books, long association with Henry Miller and his second wife June, and many other luminaries, this biography doesn't really focus on the nitty gritty erotic details of her affairs and 2 marriages but is rather an exhaustive and at times exhausting tale of a life that was created to both expose and hide the trauma of her childhood. Fitch Riley's thesis is that childhood sexual abuse formed the artist and writer who still remains a fascinating enigma all these years later.

A biography that held my attention to the end, a huge amount of research and work must have gone into the writing of this biography and very well done considering the shifting amorphous sands of the exterior and interior life of the writer/dreamer Anais Nin.
