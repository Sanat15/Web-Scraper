Rating: 3/5

There's only one thing worse than having no clients in Nero Wolfe's world and that is having too many, especially when they are all concerned with the death of Thomas Yeager, executive vice president of Continental Plastics Products.

Archie Goodwin, Wolfe's assistant, meets the man on that day when Wolfe has no clients, and hires him to find out if and who is following Yeager. Only things go very wrong — Yeager is found dead. And Archie quickly finds out that he and Wolfe are dancing on the edge of a very sharp knife. There's a secret love nest that apparently has been visited by a number of women; that that room is in a house in which resides an innocent family; the man who identified himself as Yeager was an imposter; and with Yeager's death, there are a number of people, including the dead man's wife, all eager to hire Wolfe to not find the killer but to protect Continental Plastics.

That is but the premise of a twisted and convoluted mystery that for a while seems to even confound the famous sleuth. And the reader is along for the wild ride in this story first published in 1960.
