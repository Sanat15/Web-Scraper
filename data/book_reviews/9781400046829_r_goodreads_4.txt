Rating: 4/5

I love Jimmy Breslin. I know he's a cantankerous old bastard, and his output hasn't always been 100%. This book, however, should be used as Exhibit A in any argument for his continued relevance.

Here, Breslin recognizes new wrinkles in the eternal hyprocrisy of the city and the world. If you live in a neighborhood with a heavy Mexican and Central American population, particularly a large day-laborer section, you owe it to yourself and your neighbors to read the story of Eduardo Gutierrez and his travails from Puebla to Brooklyn.
