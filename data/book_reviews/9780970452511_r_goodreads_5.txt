Rating: 4/5

finished this one this morning. as the book description says and then some. reminded of that quote from pynchon...mason &amp; dixon if i recall correctly, and this is a paraphrase, some believe that science can do anything; others are afraid that it will. 

certainly, there is much more information, information that is unavailable to the public, national security and so forth and so on and scoobie doobie doo doo. i've lived long enough that the cynic in me says, yeah, typical, not surprised. much of the content is told in a journalistic and dry style, just the facts ma'am. much of the content describes individual cases, sirhan sirhan, mark david chapman, patty hearst, candy something, one or three others.

the fabled manchurian candidate and ross details work toward that end here, makes a convincing case. too, there's much here about the use of lsd by the scientific community, how that was all the rage, before it fell out of favor for new toys and gadgets. 

but, yeah...science. we kill how many millions a year in the abortion clinic and we call that good. so many billions walking the planet...there are some bad apples here and there. or everywhere. the many described here, all well-known, well-to-do, yet willing to look the other way, for whatever their reasons...motivation isn't investigated as much, although "in the interests of national security" covers much of it. 

i doubt anything is going to change. the overall tone of this is reflected and echoed in another book with the same message, the title escapes me, something about bioethics today. Culture of Death: The Assault on Medical Ethics in America we never get off the playground. happy campers, one and all.
