Rating: 5/5

I read this book shortly after it appeared, having the benefit of a mother who was an aficionado of every good book review she read in The New York Times or Kirkus Reviews, to both of which she subscribed. 

With that advantage she also had been given free reign by town hall to order whatever book had stellar ratings and would consequently enjoy a big circulation in the library she ran for them.

Furthermore, if she loved a book she would tell me about it, and I'd borrow it.

And that way I could read great extracurricular books in my teen years at no cost...

But my marks naturally suffered as a result!

That year, when I turned thirteen, I abhorred George Smiley's Circus and its bitter sang froid with regards to the slightest touch of humanism. 

I was all teen emotion at the time, but later would naturally see the Circus' hidden humanity once le Carre's own humanism mellowed with age.

Appreciation for workplace excellence will do that to you.

You let down your guard with sheer admiration!

So it was with President Kennedy in the previous year, as his finger hovered over the red nuclear button, like Peter Sellers in Dr Strangelove.

The only great guts and glory I could see in such machismo at the time was the vision of what we'd all turn into AFTER it was pushed!

Now, of course, much older, I only see Kennedy's sheer stamina and bravery -

Just as I now know that for Alex Leamas, inhuman political expediency was a calculated risk back then, given the grim times.

However, now with Coronavirus, careful and humane medical treatment in many areas is sadly lacking due to sheer volume of cases. 

Are we now, perhaps, finding ourselves once again in the crisis-averse deep waters of starkly expedient measures?
