Rating: 4/5

Willam J. Mann mainly focuses on Hollywood and its makers in his non-fiction books and it shows. He's extremely focused and an expert on what he writes. Behind the Screen is a vast and detailed account on film making from the (mainly) gay mens side before, during and after the moral clauses were placed on the industry starting from the 1930's. Because the subject is so vast and Mann sometimes focuses on pin point accuracy on certain people, the slightly off kilter structure (going always forward in time but still jumping back and forth in some cases) made me as a reader sometimes very confused.

Of course someone jumping to the Hollywood history without any knowledge of why and how it was established is going to have a hard time with this book anyway: the "backstory" of Hollywood is only slightly being mentioned here. But if one manages to trawl through this it's going to be wild ride into happy and sad moments, success's and tragedies and lots of open ended thoughts as well: "What if....?" Mann's books are a must read for those interested in cinema history.
