Rating: 5/5

Absolutely brilliant work of history writing, about one of the lesser studied fronts of The Great War, the Gallipoli campaign. There is so much nuance to the western front, but it also is very repetitive, scholarship mirrors the events in that regard. But the Gallipoli campaign is a totally different beast, involving collaboration between the Army and Navy, the coming of age of the ANZAC forces, and a totally different set of circumstances to the usual battlefields of WW1. Morehead does such a good job mixing in primary sources and perspectives from both the rank and file and the military/civilian leadership. It’s a bit dated being 70 years old, but you wouldn’t really know it. I look forward to reading more from this author.
