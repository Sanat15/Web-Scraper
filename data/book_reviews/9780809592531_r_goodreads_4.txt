Rating: 3/5

Gilbert Keith Chesterton (1874-1936) was an English writer and polemicist who ventured into different literary genres, including poems, books (nearly 80!), Theater, etc. Today, we will focus on the character of the priest detective he created in 1910, Father Brown.
More or less, Chesterton's hero may be in the lineage of Sherlock Holmes, who predates him. With a highly developed sense of observation, reflection takes precedence over action, reasoning wins over immediate logic, and Father Brown dismantles criminal scenarios with a mastery that leaves you speechless. Only slight criticism: each investigation being only about thirty pages long, the resolution of the puzzles is swift. I would have preferred that Chesterton / Brown had more time to get us to the goal. On the other hand, unlike Conan Doyle, there is a lot more humor in the turn of phrase, and there I find a very slight parallel with Wodehouse, the unforgettable creator of Jeeves.
This book, The Innocence of Father Brown, compiles twelve stories of the ecclesiastic among the fifty in which he was the hero, and was collected in five collections between 1911 and 1935. I advise you not to read them in a row, because they do not extend over very long pages and are quite quick to read, as I have written above. The writer's "tips" and Father Brown's reasoning can become dull or repetitive, so it would be a mess, even though it is about an excellent book.
