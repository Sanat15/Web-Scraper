Rating: 2/5

"Wet Work" is an early book of Christopher Buckley's, and it shows. It is also very hard to find, and so I am assuming it is out of print. The novel is entertaining, at times, but it is not as gripping as some other Buckley books I have read. It is also about 30 pages too long. Frankly, it is a story I could have walked away from and felt no remorse.
"Wet Work" is essentially a revenge fest in the vein of Shakespeare's early play "Titus Andronicus", and like that early work of the Bard it suffers from weak characterization with a protagonist who is a mash up of Charles Bronson, and every revenge film you have ever seen. There is really no depth to the story, which hinders it because you are supposed to believe that the protagonist is taking his revenge on the drug cartels due to his overwhelming love for his granddaughter. I was not too bothered by this, but if you are expecting more than a surface level text you will be disappointed by this book.
To its credit the novel moves quickly (mostly) and is a very easy read. Buckley does shift point of view often and it sometimes results in temporary confusion because the transitions in the text are choppy and don't flow.
Yet, "Wet Work" is a nice summer read. However, it does lack the wit and comedy of Buckley's more recent output. This text is not a humorous satire and seems out of character when compared with what later became Christopher Buckley's trademark style. That huge difference can be jarring for regular readers of his novels. Be warned.
