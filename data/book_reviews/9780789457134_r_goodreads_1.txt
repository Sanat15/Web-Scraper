Rating: 4/5

This was one of my children's books when they were going to school. Now that they are all adults they left it behind.

This is actually a good book. As a kid, I loved books like these because not only did they teach me to read but they got me curious about the subject at hand. This book, as the title makes it obvious, is about spies. But it touches on the different types of spies. And how the spies had to adapt throughout history. It describes the differences with spies as we knew them during the cold war and going back further, spies of the revolutionary war. One of the neat things that kids will love about this book is that it talks about real gadgets that spies used to get the job done. 

The book introduced real spies like Ethel and Julius Rosenberg who spied on the United States for Russia. Also Kim Philby who turned out to be a double agent. And what book like this would be complete for kids without mentioning the most famous fictitious spy of all, 007, Bond, James Bond.

What I liked about this book as I had written previously is that no specific detail was given on any one subject but, you could then look up the different topics that interested you and research it further. For example, on further examination you would find out that James Bond was not real but that the author of the books he was based on, one Mr. Ian Flemming, was truly a spy for the British Government. You could do more research on the spy gadget called the Enigma and what role it played in World War II.

So, this is indeed a good book and I would recommend it for kids. Interesting stuff!
