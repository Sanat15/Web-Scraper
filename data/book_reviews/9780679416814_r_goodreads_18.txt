Rating: 3/5

This is probably the book with the most brilliant setting I've ever read: the narrative is written in response to the an open call from Reminisce magazine on 'memories of the Ford administration'; hence the title: the person doing the writing, however, is a got-to-be-a-little-crazy history professor who could never quite bring himself to finish his book on James Buchanan and who was busy wrecking his and various other married lives during Gerald Ford's run as president. And so what you end up with is a narrative that is half set in the period immediately before the Civil War, half set in the early 1970s, and sprinkled throughout by erudite, dazed and beleaguered philosophical/ psychoanalytical attempts at understanding how it all went wrong. Fantastic. Astonishing. 

The reason it doesn't get five stars from me is that Updike's books always make me think through the definition of pornography? And the moral implications of something that, while probably not pornography perhaps in the pure sense, pushes the boundaries of artistic decorum pretty close to the breaking point. And Updike is so, ah, precise!
We have on record people feeling that they simply could not sleep with Whitman's newly published book in their home. In a similarly prudish move, I'll be donating this one to my local library now.
