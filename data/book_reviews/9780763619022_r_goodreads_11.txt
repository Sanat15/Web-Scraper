Rating: 2/5

What a beautiful looking book - hefty, solid, gorgeous. The illustrations are scratchy and detailed and wonderfully atmospheric, they remind me of Antony Maitland’s work with Leon Garfield. I was also excited by the children’s novel in verse aspect and I always love a tale of curses and buccaneers. 

Unfortunately, I found the words to let the rest of the package down somewhat. I found the verse clumsy, with herky jerky metres that never quite settled into a pattern and a rhyme scheme that went for a rhyme whenever it could but was happy to let it slide when it couldn’t.

I was also disappointed with the story. A man cursed with eternal life till he fulfils certain obligations is a wonderful notion for a story but I didn’t find that the weight of time was all that well portrayed, nor did I get firm sense of Bruno. As for the swashbuckling, there was only really a ship chase, there were no wind in the sails or cannons ablaze. Most of the story was foiling a rather obvious plot about poisoning sherry. I was also confused with what universe this was taking place in, as ‘the English Queen’ and ‘the Spanish King’ has almost no relation to Elizabeth or Phillip. How did Morgan become the King’s heir? He had children. 

The verse didn’t help the tension either, everything being too sing-song to really kick off properly. It would have been better if the verse sped up for the exciting times, slowed down for the descriptions or did anything other than trundle along like a barrow with a misshapen wheel.

I did enjoy the scene where the ghosts of the Scandinavian fisherman had their meal in the pub, that was suitably unnerving. I also liked how Morgan became an octopus for the last part, being a strange sight under an English sea.

Ultimately, I can see why the book could not find a publisher but I can also see why it did so well after it was self-published, it looks so good but It didn’t read well. For me at least.
