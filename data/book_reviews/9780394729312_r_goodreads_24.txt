Rating: 4/5

Norwood Pratt is released from the Marines after his father passes away because his fat and ugly sister Vernell is incapable of caring for herself. Now working as a mechanic in a run-down gas station in East Texas, Norwood's daily struggles living with Vernell take some humorously dour turns before she finally gets a job at a diner. 

Once Vernell settles a bit and makes a serious life change (quick aside: Norwood's interactions with and opinion's of Vernell's fella are just great), Norwood heads off to New York City because some grifting big shot employs him to deliver a car there, which he accepts because there's also an old friend there who owes him $70.

"Norwood" (1966) has a fun tone to it, centered on the poverty-riddled Southern folks he interacts with on his journey which is a lot of fun. I'd say Norwood's character is equal parts Holden Caulfield, Huck Finn, and Mattie Ross, and the folks we meet along the way are almost as interesting.

Verdict: A classic misadventure tale set around a 1950's road trip from Texarkana to New York City and back. Short and funny enough that I think it would appeal to a fan of any literary genre.
