Rating: 4/5

This book has taught me that I’d rather read (a) a brilliant author whom I disagree with than (b) someone less-than-brilliant whose views align with my own.

Jan Morris – a Welsh former solider and, incidentally, one of Britain’s earliest openly trans celebrities – certainly won’t be my primary source for any historical facts. Her account of empire, while sometimes critical, is ultimately apologetic. And the tone with which she sometimes treats non-British, and typically non-white, peoples made me queasy at times. (This latter point is somewhat complicated by Morris’ tendency to convey contemporaneous attitudes through her descriptions, so it can be difficult to assess what tone is Victorian and what Morris’ is own.)

For those able to stomach such limitations, what Morris’ book delivers is a stunning portrait of a zeitgeist – a chronicle of empire, as she says in the introduction, as seen by a centurion stationed out on the frontier. A fraught book, for sure, but one I’m grateful to have read.
