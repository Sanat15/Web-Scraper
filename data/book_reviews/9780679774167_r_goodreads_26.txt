Rating: 2/5

Tony is an outstanding defense attorney who was unjustly accused of murdering his girlfriend in his home town of Lake City when he was in high school. No charges were ever brought. Tony goes to Harvard and moves to San Francisco. Almost 30 years later his friend Sam, still living in Lake City, is accused of murdering Marcie, a 16-year old student at a school where Sam is vice principle. 

Tony returns to Lake City to be his friend’s lawyer. He doesn’t know if Sam is guilty. Just because Sam had sex with the student, sodomized her and got her pregnant doesn’t mean he’s a murderer, Tony reasons. So, Tony is willing to do anything to defend Sam, ignoring his crimes against the student. He casts unjustified doubt on the veracity of another educator, to defend his client. He threatens a potential witness, to defend his client. For Tony, anything goes as long as there’s a chance Sam is innocent of committing murder. 

So in many ways, Tony is a despicable human being, despite his exceptional legal skills. The author masks this fact in a morass of unconvincing relationships and gobbledegook explanations of human interactions. 

In addition to the unbelievable characters permeating this book, it is overly long and repetitious. It was necessary to skip a lot to get through it. 

The ending does tie everything together though. It turns out that Sam murdered Tony’s girlfriend, and Marcie. Confronted, he commits suicide.
