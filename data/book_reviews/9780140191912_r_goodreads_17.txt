Rating: 4/5

I didn't think much of the book at first. However, I find myself constantly applying the concepts explained. "Oh, this is just what Koestler wrote about", etc. 

The main ideas are two: creativity is for the most part an act of bisociation (finding a common point between different "matrices", or realms of thought). Secondly, bisociation can be comical, scientific or artistic depending on the emotional state of the viewer. 

The examples given are amazing; Koestler was clearly a very well-read and brilliant man. My personal favorite is how one can go from mocking Don Quixote's endeavours against windmills to relating to them as a tragic figure. The bisociation of the common, harsh world against the backdrop of romantic delusions can be either a joke or a gut-wrenching tragedy. 

I don't give it 5 stars simply because the writing can become a little wordy and repetitive at times.
