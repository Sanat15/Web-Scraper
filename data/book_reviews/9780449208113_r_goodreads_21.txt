Rating: 5/5

I love how the trauma of Jemimah is palpable without ever having been described in details. She's a great character - as are all of Madeleine Brent's leading ladies - essentially good, strong, self-reliant but flawed all the same. I also love that for so long from Jemimah's standpoint it is not a love story at all. It is her story, how she endures and grows. And then in the end there's love. The fact that it's not with who I thought was suprising and made me think long after I had finished the book, but I actually love it. It's much more realistic and thus more romantic. Because what good is obvious romance if it can't last? Caspar is a hero, because of how he keeps his moral center regardless of what others in his life do. If Jemimah hadn't yet fallen in love with Henry, Caspar not asked to marry her so shortly after his wife died and the book gone on to build more of a relationship where Jemimah gets to know Caspar without his Kassim identity - then it would have worked. But as it is, Caspar was in love with the idea of who Jemimah was rather than knowing her. Reflecting on the times Jemimah spent with Henry on the roads I realized that they had good communication and just knew who the other was as a person before falling in love, and that's how it's really supposed to be.
