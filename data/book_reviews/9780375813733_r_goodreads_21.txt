Rating: 2/5

I read this as a read aloud with my 4-year-old. He enjoyed the book, and for a quick Christmas time read with a historical setting it was ok, but as an adult I was not impressed. I read some of the Magic Tree House books as a kid, and a few of the Merlin Missions too, but I read them when I was between 8-11 (so in the target audience range for the books). I liked the books as a kid, but as an adult I thought the plot moved way too quickly, the characters were too flat, and everything was too easy/convenient for Jack and Annie. For example, near the beginning of the book, Jack and Annie are given 3 riddles to solve, but they never actually solve the riddles themselves. Instead, they are either taken directly to the places they need to go (example: taken to the iron gate by the white stag) or literally handed the things things they needed to find (example: the three missing knights hand the kids the objects they need to find to compete the quest). These kids are supposed to be smart and tough enough to go on a quest that knights have failed, and yet things are done for them or challenges are defeated too easily (example: they defeat 4 dragons with flaming sticks after drinking the water of memory and imagination, because apparently all he kids needed to do was imagine that the dragons would be defeated to actually beat them). Annie even says at one point (after the gifts they are searching for from the second riddle are placed into their hands and they’re about to look for the water of memory and imagination) that “‘This quest is really easy’” (chap. 10, pg. 76), and I could not agree more.
