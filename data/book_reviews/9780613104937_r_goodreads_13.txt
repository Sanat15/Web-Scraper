Rating: 5/5

This older book is a variation on the story of Baba Yaga--a witch figure in Russian and Slovakian folklore . 
It is gentled (a little bit!) for young readers by Tony Johnston's retelling and Tomie dePaola's delightful illustrations. The story is set in the Southwest region of the US. Manuela, a young shepherdess, loses her sheep. While searching for them, Manuela stumbles upon Alice Nizzy Nazzy's house--which stands on road-runner legs and is surrounded by a fence of prickly pear cactus. Alice turns out to be the kind of witch who like to eat children, and promptly puts the girl in a cooking pot. But the quick thinking child outwits Alice Nizzy Nazzy, and all ends well.
ELA: folk tales, compare with Baba Yaga, Hansel and Gretel stories.
Social Studies: discover what aspects of the story show the Southwestern setting.
Good story for Halloween season. Plenty of visual humor. And what fun to say the witch's name!
Reading level: M
