Rating: 2/5

Quite basic. The chapters are so short that it almost makes no sense to have them. The book is one continuous stories, so it's not like in the other Pippi books, where there is a need to separate different events and adventures.
The events follow each other so fast that there is barely any reason to care for what is happening, it reads more as an author writing down plot they want to realize for a novel, rather than a book with all the necessary details itself.
There is a "moment", when Pippi is separated from Tommy and Annika, and they go searching for her, though at first it's more about them finding shelter, and when Annika goes: "We'll never see Pippi again! Never!", the very next line is "But they did. Hurray they did!" and proceeds to have a rather emotionless reunion.

For some reason Annika is also quite obsessed about cleanliness, and wants to clean the stranger's ears in whose house they are staying for the night (sus on it's own), and then the pig... and then tries to soap Tommy, when they end up in a lake...
And, of course, the big divide of Tommy treating this as an adventure and being very "boyish", while Annika is the scared one, and fussing about things. It seems pointless to write a very girl-power character, when every other character then proceeds to neatly fall into gender-roles and never challenge the status quo. Perhaps girls will read this and *maybe* (a very big maybe) will see that as girls they can be strong and fun and run amok, but any boys picking this up would see themselves as Tommy, and therefore will see themselves as emotionless, endlessly-laughing boys who make fun of their sisters.
