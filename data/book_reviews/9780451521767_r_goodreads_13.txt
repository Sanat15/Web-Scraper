Rating: 5/5

I first read this classic by Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn in 1985 during my first year of Literature at the Coast Guard Academy. It simply tells one day in the life of a "zek" --- a political prisoner in Stalin's gulags. In so telling the story of one day in a life of a years long sentence in one of the worst places on Earth, it shows the crudity and cruelty of a communist system completely at odds with the high ideals it professes to the world. For a zek, a good day is simply one avoiding a beating or being placed in a punishment cell --- or getting an extra morsel of food --- or finding some way to stay a couple degrees warmer in the ice cold hell of a Soviet concentration camp.

Most of their work --- done without proper tools or resources --- is practically senseless, and relative to the effort expended, wasteful. For example, having prisoners dig post holes in ice hardened permafrost that's as hard as concrete without sufficient tools to do so. The theft and corruption in the network of camps is rife throughout the gulags, further adding to its inmates' misery. 

It's a snapshot, captured in one day in the life of one of its own, of a system not only tyrannical and oppressive, but also inefficient, dishonest, and stupid. 

Also, included in this addition are other writings about the experiences of others suffering in the communist system.

Definitely, a great work. I recommend it for anyone who enjoys Russian literature.
