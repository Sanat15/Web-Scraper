Rating: 5/5

This is Gerald Durrell's account of starting his zoo, the adventures and issues in pioneering the sort of zoo he considered essential, changes in how the field looks at animals in terms of survival, educational, informative, and scientific, rather than light amusement of children. There is nothing wrong with amusing children. However, it needs to become oriented towards advancing knowledge of rapidly disappearing wildlife as the priority. Many animal species are disappearing at a terrifying rate, and we owe it to these creatures to try to save them. The ultimate aim is to eventually be able to release the descendants of these first wildlife back into protected areas that they came from. The Jersey Zoo has an excellent set of files that I would assume is now computerized full of information. If you read this book for entertainment value, you might find the listing of the various categories of files boring. I found it fascinating. Obviously, this book is out of date in terms of the field of conservation, being published in 1976, 50 years ago. But it is still of historical and literary interest. I am hoping to plan a trip to the zoo, which has undergone several name changes now, in the next year, so I found this fascinating!
