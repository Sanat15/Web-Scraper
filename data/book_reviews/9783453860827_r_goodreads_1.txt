Rating: 4/5

There are too many books in my house. Why not get rid of some that I will in all probability never read? Like reference books that are dated? One candidate was this one. A book that gives a short chapter on the history of the Oscar but then basically just lists the Oscars that have been awarded until 1984. Information in other words that is easily to be found on the Internet.

And yet. After browsing for some minutes I found myself reading the whole thing cover to cover. And I was endlessly fascinated. For every year the best picture is presented with all other categories that it won and was nominated for. But then all other films are listed. Which means that you want to know which song won you need to go all the films. At first I found this annoying but then I loved it, because it meant that I needed to read more or less everything. 

The first amazing thing I noticed was that all winning songs of the thirties I knew. And this was only the case (for a couple of years) in the seventies again. The fun thing, of course, is to see which of the all time great films did not get the Oscar for best film, the most glaring omission being Citizen Kane. But Rocky instead of Taxi Driver is also pretty cruel. But by and large the Oscars for best picture were at least tolerable. I could name an alternative for nearly every year, but I can nearly always accept the choice made. Maybe with the exception of Oliver in 1968. That was the year of 2001, Shoes of the Fisherman, Rosemary’s Baby, Thomas Crown Affair, The Odd Couple, Charly, Bullit, The Producers, Planet of the Apes. All of these were not even nominated for best picture. But Lion in the Winter was. Each and every of these films was better than Oliver. And, I know you have been waiting for this, better than One Battle After Another (which was good, only not great). 

But I resist the temptation to talk about the quality of movies in the present.

Will I throw away the book? Certainly not.
