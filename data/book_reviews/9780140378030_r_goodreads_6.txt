In a household of high flying adults and precocious children, Hero is the silent one - she's selectively mute, watching while her parents and siblings go about their dramatic lives, made even more dramatic when her adult sister Ginevra returns home after years away. Quietly Hero slips away to the house down the street where Miss Credence pays her to take care of the garden - but there are secrets and silences in this place too. 

This is such an odd book. I found it absorbing, I was so interested in the way Mahy draws the Rapper family with the mother who's famous due to her books on raising children, her stay at home husband, and their four very different children. But then there's this whole other storyline with Hero and Miss Credence, which complements the main storyline thematically in its ideas about children, family, silence, etc - but take such a very sharp veer into gothic melodrama and horror.
