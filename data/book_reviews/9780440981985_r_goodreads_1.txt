Rating: 2/5

I had not heard of Englishwoman Vivien Alcock. She drove an ambulance in WWII after taking commercial art classes. Apparently her husband Leon Garfield, was a famous author to whom she suggested book ideas. Eventually, Vivien’s wish to be published in her own right, overrode her shyness. Lo &amp; behold, she was award-winning and adapted for television in paranormal mystery and fantasy. Débuting in 1980, her first novel, “The Haunting Of Cassie Palmer”, sounds like my cup of java and I will look for it eagerly. In 1981, came “The Stonewalkers”. It is blended so bizarrely I did not know if Vivien had trouble choosing a genre, or if the trained artiste viewed her novel as a canvas for the whole spectrum of her colour palette.

The opening is suspenseful, magical, and has heart. Due to playing with foreign oddments from basement storage during a storm, a lawn statue moves. There was a compassionate feeling about Poppy consoling the animated statue, inviting the heavy ornament inside the house, out of the rain. Poppy was a liar, who needed to show her Mom such a surreal metamorphosis, for her to believe it. The statue bolted in a hasty misunderstanding of her surroundings, thereupon declining the story’s tone and contents. They grew unpleasant and the rash shift to an unkind mission, by a statue who had been offered friendship, had no justifiable origin. The circumstances become dystopian. I pushed to finish this novel and gave it two stars.

There are morals on friendship, kindness, and giving people chances to shine. Neither Poppy, her Mom, or school friend are likeable throughout this 142-page novel. A nice ending shows her busy, housekeeper Mom, understanding that she needs to praise and hug her child more. Criticisms are not the way to build confidence and hope.
