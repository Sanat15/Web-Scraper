Rating: 4/5

This relatively unknown, self-published book is a hidden gem that part of me wants to shout about to all the world, and part of me wants to keep hidden all for myself. The issue with self-published books is that if a book is good often it will find a publisher somewhere willing to publish it. Self-published books have a reputation for being rather poor. In the case of The Voyage of the Arctic Tern, I don't think it was turned away because it was not good, however, but on the grounds that it was most likely not good for the intended audience of young children. In the midst of lovers of Diary of a Wimpy Kid with it's humorous cartoons and middle-schooler's diary prose there's not much room for an eerily charcoal illustrated adventure story written entirely in heroic couplets. I think all this book needed was a publisher daring enough and a marketing department brilliant enough to turn this into the next big thing in today's juve fiction genres, but instead I get to enjoy it almost alone.

This book is an excellent work of a lost genre. It is an epic in every way. Spanning many different era's with a wicked villain and a monstrous beast and hidden treasure and a ghost with unfinished business. Some people may claim that the verse is pedantic, but for the story it looks to tell it provides a rhythmic backdrop that almost adds to the hypnosis of stormy nights and desperate revenge plots. The illustrations combine perfectly with the prose and the setting is appropriate even as the story progresses in time.

This book is not particularly realistic, but if it was I think it would steal some of the charm from what is truly an old-fashioned adventure epic.
