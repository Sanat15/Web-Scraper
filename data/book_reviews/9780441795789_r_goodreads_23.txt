Rating: 4/5

This follow up to Thieves' World is more of the same, equally as compelling and complicated and, at times, awkward.

The first story by Phillip José Farmer is the longest and least congruent. It's kind of jarring. Because you read book 1 and it flows along at a brisk pace and then you hit this wall of the Farmer story and it slows, almost tediously, and takes you out of the city for a different adventure. It's a good read. The main characters are really interesting to me. A woman of the city whose husband is a drunken lout and who has a kid and an old mother to care for... a woman with few prospects for a good future. And the other character is a weird priest who rubs button on his body. Very interesting story.

I liked the rest of the stories very much and they are right in the pocket of the first book's tone. Quick, dark, gritty, sometimes darkly funny, with a lot of overt discussion about gray morality. I liked it.
