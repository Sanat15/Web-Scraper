Rating: 4/5

I was hooked from the beginning, though I felt the ending was a little rushed considering everything the plot had been building toward. Still, the closure Jessica received felt right, and the ending gave the protagonist exactly what she needed.

At its core, this is a story about ambitious women and the complicated ways they love. It explores how living by logic for too long can turn the heart into an empty vessel. Jessica, a documentary film producer, has built her identity around her work, and her personal life reflects the same drive and determination that define her career.

The novel also examines the impact of television—not only on the people who consume it, but on those who create it. It looks at people fighting for a cause, those harmed by the system, and the emotional cost of being caught between the two.

One of my favorite aspects of the book is its portrayal of women's love. It argues that a woman's love does not have to exist within the boundaries set by a man, and that leaving is sometimes a choice made not out of necessity, but because staying no longer serves a purpose.

More than anything, the story reminded me that losing yourself is never truly permanent. If there is still a calling inside you, there is still a part of you worth rebuilding. The novel explores whether Jessica's loss of her profession means losing herself entirely—or finally finding her way back to who she is.

A short read at around 250 pages, but one that offers thoughtful reflections on ambition, identity, love, and self-discovery.
