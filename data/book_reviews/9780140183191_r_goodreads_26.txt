Rating: 3/5

"'He must know by now, I should think, that I can give as good as I get!'" (Gigi, 40).

I love Colette. Everyone knows this. She is witty and charming, and her writing is lush, comic, and evocative. But she isn't always right, and she isn't always good.

I think the translation of the first story by Roger Senhouse (Gigi) simply felt lackluster, or perhaps the short story itself was too dialogue-heavy? A teenage girl being pursued by a seasoned cad just gave me the ick. Although I guess Gigi's family grooming her to become a courtesan is also not ideal. Colette's description of the "heron-like legs of a girl of fifteen" being folded under the dress was excellent though. Succinctly encapsulates a gangly, restless age. The tale's conclusion: "I've been thinking I would rather be miserable with you than without you" (57). Ah, yes. So romantic. Just what we all want to hear from our spouse-to-be.

The male protagonist in the second story (The Cat) has an adorable cat (yes, they are troublingly intertwined, but that's another conversation), property in Paris, and a fashionable young wife with a high libido, yet he is still profoundly unhappy. Boring. His steak is too juicy and lobster too buttery, etc.
