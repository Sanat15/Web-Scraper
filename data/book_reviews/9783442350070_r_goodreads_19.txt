Rating: 3/5

I feel like it's a sacrilege to give an Eva Ibbotson book just three stars, but there you go.

Sweet and somewhat discombobulated romance set before WWII. As usual quirky characters without being too twee, but the H and h just did not connect enough for me, literally and emotionally. I enjoyed the characterization of Heini, the spoiled and talented pianist, that Ruth serves as his devoted acolyte. And the tragedy of even part Jews being rejected by their country and sometimes even families is touched on in the most gentle way possible, but poignant all the same.

If you are going to start with one of Ibbotson's romances, I suggest A Countess Below Stairs aka The Secret Countess, A Company of Swans, or Madensky Square. Her stories are chock full of interesting characters, her heroines and even heroes are usually intelligent, and lots of smart humor.

Whatever you do, DON'T listen to the audio. I think the producer/director must hate her or her readers as once again, I could only understand about half of what the Austrian characters said.
