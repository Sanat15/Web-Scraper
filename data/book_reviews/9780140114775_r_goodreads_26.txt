Rating: 5/5

There's not as much pain and agony in death as is in the wait and anticipation of it. It's the moment before the death that's painful, not death itself. 

The first two pages and the last two pages are like the two ends of the string that knot and close the loop.

This novel drills through your strongest emotional barricades on the account of Anticipation of Death and Tragedy. It has TRULY captured what the people living through riots might feel, the uncertainty, the fragility of life, the anticipation of death! 

I can't stress more on the word 'Anticipation'. Not that death or tragedy doesn't actually occur in this novel, but it's the possibility of it happening at any moment that keeps your throat choked and heart sunken.

If I were to paint a colour to this book, I would give it a dull orange with a red in its middle and dirty brown on the periphery.
