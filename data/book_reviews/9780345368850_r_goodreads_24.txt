Rating: 5/5

Similar to book 1 this one slowly builds, laying on discoveries, information, characters and a richly realised and depicted life of the places and people encountered, leading to a building intensity that is gripping.

This upended my expectations of what it would actually be about in several ways and it is so much better than I imagined. I will have a hard time not jumping tight into part 3.

The ever growing and expanding mystery is as captivating as the growing world being revealed.

I expected this book to have a short part Bel and Rowan travelling through the Outskirts to find the fallen Guidestar, and then further adventures and reveals to come. In the end, getting to the Guidestar was the least important part of the story. 

The experience of the Outskirts and learning about the people, culture and history is what this story it about, and where more secrets and questions are revealed. The delicate and tantalising threads of new mutual alliances I think will be essential and something the Wizards, or Slado, will do everything to try and destroy.

But so much of the delight of this book is the time spent understanding and piecing together the culture and history of the Outskirts and the people. Their history and memory. I loved one part where the use of knowledge was so beautifully expressed. That you don't know what information you don't need to make a decision, until you have more knowledge than you need in the moment.

As each question is answered, more questions appear and this mystery, starting way back at the beginning with just curiosity on some strange blue gems, is expanding on ever growing scale of intent and mystery.
