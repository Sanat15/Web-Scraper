Rating: 2/5

What a strange little book. You think at first it's going to be one of those mean-teacher-from-hell stories where it turns out the narrator (Arnold) and his classmates do something to make the mean teacher realize the error of his ways. But very quickly, you understand that the narrator is a jerk, because the supposedly mean teacher, while very strict, is liked by everyone except the narrator. Arnold is impossible to like.

But then it's chapter two, and you see that Arnold is the grownup in his family. His mother, who seems to be mentally ill in a harmless way, depends on Arnold to keep things as close to normal at home as possible. Arnold is doing his best to keep authorities away from the trailer they share. Arnold has neither siblings nor a father. Suddenly, his misbehavior at schools is easier to understand.

The novel continues this way for some time: one chapter at school with Arnold the jerk and one chapter at the trailer with Arnold the good son. Then a third character is introduced and the entire thing swivels 'round in a completely different direction. This third character is an entirely different kind of mentally ill, and now Arnold's energy is spent holding normalcy up in two relationships.

It's an ambitious little novel, a short read of only 103 pages, and while it is ultimately unsatisfying, it's still worth the read. I'd recommend it to middle elementary-schoolers, especially those mature enough to think seriously about mental illness and all its many incarnations. The author handles a difficult subject deftly, 'though I wonder if the resolution could have been more tidily handled, especially considering the intended audience.
