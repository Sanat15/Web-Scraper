Rating: 4/5

This is the final volume of the four-book Federation of the Hub collections organized by Eric Flint &amp; Guy Gordon, with this one featuring the remaining Federation stories not involving Telzey or Trigger. We've got 10 stories, originally published between 1955 and 1969, and one of those is actually a short novel (The Demon Breed). A couple are interesting for being from the perspective of non-Federation protagonists ("The Other Likeness" and "Attitudes"), and aside from "Grandpa," none involve members of any of the departments of the Federation's Overgovernment. As much as I like Telzey and Trigger, I do like seeing stories from the rest of the Hub.

My favorite stories were the two featuring Nile Etland ("Trouble Tide" and the aforementioned Demon Breed), who's a biochemist/troubleshooter for a pharmaceutical company on her homeworld. Aside from some obvious conveniences, The Demon Breed was genuinely fun and interesting. "Grandpa" and "Balanced Ecology" were great for some fun alien biology/ecology issues, and "The Other Likeness" was a fun bit of apparent espionage. The others were interesting, but didn't always bring something new or interesting to the setting (I've also realized just how much Schmitz likes to write "cat and mouse" chase stories). I also thought "A Nice Day for Screaming" did not live up to its fantastic title in any way (Keth Deboll was fine as a protagonist, but the abrupt twist ending made me roll my eyes more than appreciate it. Also, not really any worthwhile screaming).

That said, Schmitz does a great job for the time period of having female main characters--competent and not usually sexualized, such as Nile Etland. You're probably only reading this to be a Schmitz completionist, but if you come across this book, you should at least read The Demon Breed.
