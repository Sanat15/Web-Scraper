Rating: 4/5

Oh what a wonderful, encouraging, enlightening, and powerful book!
Not only was it beautifully and carefully written, but if held such a fullness of joy in it as well. We follow a few different characters, but mainly Brother Andrew, a monk in 1000 AD, during the time that Roman Catholicism ruled the known world, and how Andrew realized for himself as he studied the Scriptures, that the Catholics were wrong and terribly corrupt.
We take for granted the truths we know, and the freedoms we have, but this book reminded me of our safety and our privilege in today’s world, not only to have our own Scriptures, but to have the freedom to study them out for ourselves and believe something other than what a bishop might be forcing at us (and burning us at the stake if we refuse to go along with them). 
It was such an encouragement throughout the book, and especially in the last paragraph, as we watched Christians be martyred for their faith, but to have so much peace and joy!
One thing that I found was that the writer would just drop major plot twists in the middle of the chapters without warning! One second the cathedral is coming along great, and literally the next paragraph, the whole thing collapses and everyone dies, and then she just moves on with the chapter! It made it completely unpredictable and exciting!
