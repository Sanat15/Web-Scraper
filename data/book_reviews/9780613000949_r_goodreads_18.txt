This book always made me uncomfortable as a child, and in rereading it I wanted to find out why. So, honestly, a baffling reason to be here to begin with.

The conclusion I’ve come to is that this is a pretty cisgender book. You’ve got the class split up into girls who like boys and boys who like girls, and the way they join the club and speak to each other and embarrass each other all reflect relatively uncritically portrayed issues in society. It’s not that the book is unrealistically sexist—and in fact I just wouldn’t go as far as to say it’s sexist to begin with—but that it was hard to face down a book where a girl’s greatest asset is her long hair and the most embarrassing thing is to have a love note sent to a boy.

With that said—I reread it! I enjoyed it, as odd as it is! And I’ll likely read it again, or at least I want to have it around, so I’m keeping it on my shelf. Would recommend if you’re a Sachar completionist, but I don’t think there’s much other reason to read this vs. all the other books in the world.
