Rating: 2/5

(2.5*)

There were some nice moments here, and some wonderful writing, and some interesting issues raised, especially of woman’s identity and autonomy; but I’ve not read a novel before that could not make up its mind. 

There are murders, there’s may-hem / comedy, and science fiction (a little bit of the cloning process is discussed by its inventor, the impact of this is also kind of shown, though some of it didn’t make sense to me).

Where the novel worked was when it was scrutinising romantic relationships, the role of mothers, the dynamics between mothers and daughters and gender roles. 

Where it didn’t work was how it kept flitting between different points of view that were at odds with each other, it also flipped from being omniscient to restricted omniscient. I got the bigger picture, but in the end I wasn’t sure who or what I was rooting for.
