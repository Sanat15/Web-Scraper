Rating: 3/5

Master of Falconurst, is, I think, the final instalment in the Falconhurst, starting just before the American Civil War, and concluding a short while afterwards. Seismic changes happen at Falconhurst during this time, and many of the major previous characters don’t survive to the end.
Like Mandingo and Drum, Master of Falconhurst takes a very candid, and brutal view of slavery in the deep south. It doesn’t pull any punches, and the morality of the times are skewed to say the least. 
Also like Mandingo and Drum, many of the deaths, some of them seriously horrific, usually occur after a sexual encounter deemed highly immoral for the standards of the southern states at that time.
This is book was a good read, and there were quite a few plot twists along the way. There was also some continuity from the earlier novels, although Master of Falconhurst can be easily enjoyed as a standalone. The ending was extremely savage, and if you’ve read the earlier books, pretty much telegraphed in advance.
While of course, this is a work of fiction, this book tells the story of huge moral ambiguity, and all I can say is that I’m glad I wasn’t living in those times.
If you’re reading the kindle version, which I was, there are some glaring and very lazy formatting issues. I think that this was transferred from print to ebook electronically with no human oversights, and a few tweaks via the Mark 1 Eyeball might have made the reader experience more polished.
