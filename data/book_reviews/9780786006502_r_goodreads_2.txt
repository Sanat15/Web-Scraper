Rating: 3/5

An entertaining story of a rake, told sympathetically by his niece. He was a popular man yet somewhat a misfit for a rather garish flamboyance during a time New York and Long Island society was becoming modern, but not yet as crass as it was later to become. The author, who was not a professional, writes eloquently and evocatively. A minor criticism might be its pandering to the American desire constantly to equate a prosperous upper middle class with European aristocracy; two generations of wealth drawn from profitable commercial and financial speculation do not nobility make and that manner of bragging goes against the principles of the republic.
