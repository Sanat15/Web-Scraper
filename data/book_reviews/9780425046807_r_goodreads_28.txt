Rating: 4/5

In this sulfurous and scandalous novel, Vladimir Nabokov succeeds in "lulling" the reader's ethics to bring him to consider the point of view of a pedophile's story. He skillfully plays with words to make himself an accomplice and tolerate the sordid fantasies of his main character.
It goes beyond the sole question of pedophilia and incest, tilting received ideas with intelligence and finesse to deliver a heartbreaking story full of contradictions.
Bold, shocking, and haunting, despite our disgusting aversion, Nabokov delivers us the diary of a pedophile by playing down the role of the victim and embellishing the most disastrous baseness of this intolerable inclination.
The enormous provocative load can confuse more than one. It is disturbing, asking the reader to set aside their prejudices and suspend their comfortable certainties about good and evil for a moment.
We acknowledge the facets of the author's sophisticated, facetious, poetic, and erotic genius, who employs first- and third-person narration to confound the reader and occasionally allows them to take a step back from the abject narrator.
Solar and lonely, this reading shines with a dark glow long after the last page turns.
