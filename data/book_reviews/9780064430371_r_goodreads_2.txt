Rating: 4/5

Somewhere we have the first Amelia Bedelia which I wanted to pass on to my granddaughter, but so far, I haven't been able to find it. For her birthday last year, I gave her books from the new set, by Peggy Parish's nephew, Herman Parish. She LOVED them and my daughter said please to get more. I don't know how Rose will take to the original Amelia Bedelia but we're going to try this one for her upcoming birthday. (I still hope to find the copy of the one her mommy and aunt read when they were little!)

This is the second and it's in the same vein with literal Amelia mixing up all the directions which a nervous and harried Mrs. Rogers gives her, such as stripping the sheets, checking her husband's shirts, paring the vegetables, separating the eggs and scattering the roses. Our little Rose should love that one especially! All of this is to get ready for the visit of a particular aunt, but of course, once she tastes the famous pie, she, like everyone is won over.

A great way for young ones to learn homonyms, which is what I grew up calling them not making a distinction between homographs—words that mean different things but have the same spelling (regardless of pronunciation)—or homophones—words that mean different things but have the same pronunciation (regardless of spelling). In Amelia's case, she mixes up both. 

You still can't beat her pie, P-I-E, not, P-I!
