Rating: 3/5

3.5 Stars

Killing Gifts was an enjoyable addition to the series, though it didn’t quite reach the level of some of the earlier books for me.

One thing I appreciated was the change in setting and cast. It was refreshing not to spend as much time dealing with Wilhelm and some of the recurring characters from the first five books. I also really enjoyed that the story takes place in a real Shaker village. Being able to look up the actual buildings and locations added an extra layer of interest and atmosphere to the reading experience.

On the downside, I continue to struggle with Ginnie as a character. Her behavior often felt whiny and immature, and at times she came across more like a teenager than an adult. She remains one of the weaker aspects of the series for me.

I should also mention that there was a fairly large gap between when I started and finished this book, which may have affected my overall enjoyment and connection to the story.

Even with those reservations, I still found the mystery engaging and would absolutely recommend both this book and the series as a whole to fans of cozy mysteries and Shaker-themed historical settings. While not my favorite installment, it was still a worthwhile read, and I’ll continue on with the series.
