Rating: 4/5

Technically, this is a re-read, but I recall no details from the first time, just an impression that I liked it more than her time as a nurse, though not as much as being a cook. Got a copy as an impulse purchase a few months ago, decided to bring it along on a recent trip.

Had it been strictly about her time at the newspaper, I would've likely found it difficult to maintain interest. Main issue was that I didn't get much of an idea of her colleagues who were just sort of ... there, cardboard minor journalists. She makes it clear they're not even that in some cases, lots of regurgitation of bare facts that many could do just as well. It's the office politics, and some of the stories if being "on assignment" (mostly court cases) that held my interest most.

However, partway through the book she moves to a boardinghouse, with all the drama that ensues. Her landlady proves rather a dragon (think: the Red Queen in Wonderland), but the other residents seem almost invented, unlike at work, these folks are quite differentiated. The mysterious basement lodger comes into his own at a wild party, a married couple of acrobats are good for sorting out priblems, a tragic story which I won't spoil, finally the ending where her work and home lives connect, forcing a conflicting choice. 

Overall, it was a good decision for a re-read. Obvious some of the stories are wild exaggerations of events, if not fiction. Didn't matter to me, but others might need to suspend a bit of disbelief.
