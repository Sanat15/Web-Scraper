Rating: 4/5

Let's be realistic about this book. It's not an amazing read. In fact, anything this book might have told you thirty, forty, or fifty years ago can be easily researched on the web in a few minutes. Not only that, but you'll get much more accurate and useful information from a deep dive into a subject on the web, especially the dark web. Much like George Hayduke's dirty tricks and revenge oriented books, this book stands more as symbol if not a practical guide.

I believe I got this in 1990, when I was fourteen. It was outdated even then, but that simple sleek black, white, and red cover drew my eyeballs to it immediately. I'd met some older skaters and punks around Tacoma, and this book always seemed to come up in conversation. Having a copy on your bookshelf was a badge of honor indicating that you belonged the counterculture. It was a big banner notifying friendlies and foes of where you were placing yourself in society. 

Anyway, I gave it four stars, not because it's a four star read, but because I owe it something. Much like punk and hardcore music, this book pointed me toward a way of looking and thinking about the world and the people in it. Much like punk and hardcore music, it only gave me a primer, the basic building blocks of a different way of thinking, not a comprehensive ideology. Much like punk and hardcore, it encouraged me to seek out other sources so that I could expand my mind. Much like punk and hardcore, it encouraged me to think for myself and disagree loudly when I saw injustice. So give it up for The Anarchist Handbook, not a great book, definitely no recipes with actual food, but a pretty good societal provocateur and battle cry for wayward youth.
