Rating: 3/5

I found The Ones You Do a bit hard to pin down. On the surface, it feels like a series of loosely connected stories rather than one clear throughline, which made it difficult for me to follow the main gist of the novel. Woodrell’s writing, however, is undoubtedly strong—his language has real grit and rhythm, even when I didn’t always understand the dialogue. At times, the conversations seemed to be about things outside my own frame of reference, which left me feeling slightly on the outside of the world he created.
As for the characters, Renee Shade stood out as a basically positive presence, but I also felt he came across a little weak, still living in the shadow of his father, John X (who also appeared in the earlier books of this trilogy). Certain moments of tension worked really well—the scene where Lunch takes John and Mary Smith in the car, for instance, was gripping. But in other places, the story seemed to meander, dwelling on details that didn’t feel like they pushed the narrative forward.
Overall, it reads as a sort-of crime novel, but with a tendency to wander into other genres or tones. For me, that mix sometimes diluted the impact of the story, even though Woodrell’s prose and sense of atmosphere remain impressive. I’d call this more of a crime novel with leanings into other genres. Interesting in places, but uneven—hence my three stars.
