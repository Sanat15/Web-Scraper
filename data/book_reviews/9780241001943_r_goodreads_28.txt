Rating: 4/5

The Garlic Ballads is a captivatingly violent Novel. It practically captures the sufferings of humble Gao Yang and Gao Mao during the Cultural Revolution. The novel is basically horrifying event after horrifying event, interspersed with images of garlic to the theme of the title. Aspects of Mo Yan's hallucinatory realism are on display as sometimes it is hard to follow what exactly is happening due to how Yan sequences events in his writing. There tends to also be a lack of clarity in the prose, which I couldn't make out as intentional or not.

Nonetheless the things that happened to Gao Yang and Gao Mao and their families really stuck with me. I could see myself re-reading this book once in a while here or there, as I felt Yan's portrayals of violence and tragedy were unique, and I got a lot of experiencing his style of writing.
