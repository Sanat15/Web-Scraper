Rating: 4/5

I read this to my aloud to my kids, we all enjoyed it. It's a continuation of Ralph Moody's memoir, mixing a look into life in American of yesteryear (this time in a farm in Maine) with stories that illustrate character. In this book he is a hard worker, but faces a new challenge when he moves in with his grandfather. His grandfather seems like a truly frustrating and mean man - proud, harsh and hypocritical. (A sidenote here, Ralph had several cousins who were writers, and one of them wrote a book about how his grandmother divorced this grandfather. Not surprising based on how he is portrayed here)

To me the book was a great example of Ralph learning to honor a very difficult man. He learned to understand his grandfather's actions. And although it excuses none of his mistreatment of others, it seems like his grandfather had a noble goal at heart of finishing the work that his father had left him, and take dominion by rebuilding and expanding the family farm. The book starts slow, I think a bit of editing would have been in order, but it picks up in the second half and gets quite touching by the end. 

There's a few parts I skipped where it was getting a bit romantic, and had a bit of profanity.
