Rating: 5/5

Audible sale (#8 of 40) 13 hours 26 min. Narrated by David Baker (A)

Having been totally ignorant of the Soviet campaign during WW II, I am listening to this fascinating history a second time to have a better comprehension of what happened.

A second listening was even better than the first. I can't find any reviews to read and compare, but this book is so well-researched and well-written that I will probably listen to it again in the future. Hitler left two million men (German, Hungarian, Italian, and Romenian) to "fight to the last bullet." Thousands did; others succumbed to starvation, frigid temperatures, lack of water, typhus, or worse if they surrendered. The Russian pow camps were as bad as the Nazi concentration camps. Of the estimated 91,000 who surrendered only about 5,000 survived. In the final chapter, the author followed up with information about what happened to many of those named in the book after the war.

Again, I found maps of the campaign (August, 1942--January 31, 1943) with the help of Google and photos of Stalingrad after the battle and also 20 years later. The city of Stalingrad was almost leveled. It is now called Volgagrad. 
The author has also written about the last days of Japan in WWII, and I probably read that history, too, in the future. Note: The World War Two Chronicles contains both books.
