Rating: 4/5

George Higgins' book, written in the aftermath of the Red Sox' epic 1986 World Series collapse, is largely a memoir of his years of baseball fandom. But really, it's much more a reflection on living and dying, as mediated by baseball (and Thornton Wilder). While I didn't always follow his structure, Higgins' stories of his family and his love of the game endear the various generations of Higginses, and reading the end of this book was a surprisingly tender moment for me. This was more enjoyable than I anticipated, and should be a must-read for Red Sox fans.
