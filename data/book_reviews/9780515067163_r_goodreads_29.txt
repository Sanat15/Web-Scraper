Rating: 2/5

The first novel of the Golden Age of Crime Fiction that has really annoyed me. I realise that I come for a different age and a different background than the readers March thought she was writing for.

The Lampreys are minor members of the aristocracy who live beyond their means and think that the world owes them a living. They constantly have financial issues and can’t pay their bills but that’s ok because it is only tradespeople that suffer. Some of these plebs have the audacity to want to be paid and have the bad manners to send bailiffs to collect what is owed. These men are referred to as “bums” by those too posh to get a job, earn money and pay. They expect their rich uncle to pay them £2,000 and bail them out again. He is boring and mean because he doesn’t join in their silly games and fantasies and just give them all his money.

He is murdered after refusing them the handout they expect. 

Of course Marsh expects us all to be on the side of the spongers and the solution to the murder follows class lines. Of course, posh people don’t do that sort of thing.
