Rating: 2/5

Alias Grace's most obtrusive weakness is chronic long-windedness, which is sure to thoroughly annoy readers with an impatient streak. Atwood had a tendency to spend exorbitant amounts of time on trivial subject matter. This book is very long, unnecessarily long.

This story is a fictional account of a true case of murder in the 1800's. I appreciate ambiguity on fiction, but what is the point of reconstructing a crime, examining it, if you do not give any opinion as to what actually took place? Frustrating. 

The book segways into a disappointing monotony which leaves the reader bored and no longer interested as to the fate of the protagonist. 

I read such glowing reviews of her books from others that I always want to read one more because I feel I am missing something, or because it has been a long time since I've read one and I feel that I have forgotten. But ---- I read one more and remember that even thought her writing is excellent, her novels leave me in a cold and lonely place when I am finished – a place to which I do not wish to return. I think that after four Atwood novels, I am giving it up.
