Rating: 5/5

It appears that I never reviewed this in 2014, which was when I read and rated it here, though it likely wasn't the first time I read it. I did acknowledge it--Batman: The Dark Knight Returns (1986) as one of the best comics volumes ever, which seems to me still to be true twelve years later, written and drawn by comics icon Frank Miller. Widely recognized as one of the top five Batman books ever, with Batman: Year One also in the top five, also written by Miller. These two comics volumes were important in shaping Christopher Nolan's classic film, Dark Knight (with Christian Bale and Heath Ledger) though Alan Moore's The Killing Joke and Jeff Loeb's The Long Halloween are usually credited as more influential.

The heart of this begins with crime particularly out of control in Gotham, with a group called The Mutants leading the path of destruction, so in spite of Batman's having been retired for ten years, and being 55, he comes pretty violently into action, not killing people, exactly, but doing a lot of damage to criminals, decried by pop psychologists and media dopes as deranged acts. During this time Harvey Dent (Two Face) gets released from Arkham Asylum on "good" behavior, so of course he goes nuts killing people, too. Then the Evilest Psychotic Killer in the DC universe, The Joker, comes out of his catatonic state to do even more damage. These guys and the Mutants do kill a lot of people. 

This volume stands smack dab in the middle of the most dark and dystopian period for Batman (c0ntrast this with, say, the campy Batman and Robin pop art tv show in the sixties), but Jim Gordon, a girl Robin, Selina Kyle (no longer Catwoman) and Superman lend a hand in Fighting Evil in Gotham, and does the Bat ever need it, almost killed by the Joker in one of those epic showdowns. Worth a look again, Bat vets!
