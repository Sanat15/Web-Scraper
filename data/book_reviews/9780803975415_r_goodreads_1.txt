Rating: 5/5

This is a wonderful monograph full of memoirs written as personal ethnographies that come from different academic backgrounds. What they all have in common is that the authors can be at a certain point in their lives considered to be conscious and active agents who aimed to progress the institutionalisation of gender studies within their fields (from psychology and literary science, to development studies and political science).
The monograph is definitely not what I expected. But because of the way the authors choose to write about their experiences and perspectives in a very self-reflective manner, these are much more than regular texts of ethnographic research. As one of the authors of the monograph states: “Every gender studies scholar has got their own repertoire of “atrocity stories””. I believe this was the focus of the monograph – to collect these individual stories and accounts of resistance and agency, and to put them side by side to compare them and deconstruct them. And eventually, help other researchers in their struggles. 
I am at peace as I am to unapologetically copy this book and distribute it among my colleagues.
