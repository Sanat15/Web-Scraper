Rating: 1/5

Thomas Merton is not a great philosopher - he is a great wordsmith.

I forced myself to finish this book, hoping with every turn of the page (each with a sigh of relief that I was one page closer to the end) that I would eventually find the nuggets of wisdom that make his books so popular. Instead, what I found was 264 pages of nonsensical and often completely incoherent writing that turns the simplest idea into 4 pages of head-scratching confusion.

Merton is a Christian and his writings are centered around a relationship between him and his spirituality. But, if you grew up going to Sunday school and paid attention even a little, you have already learned all of the concepts he has written about. Philosopher? If saying we should love others is philosophy, then we are all philosophers. What a Sunday school teacher can talk about in 30 minutes would likely take Merton 5 hours, and it would be painful to hear.

Wordsmith? Absolutely- his writing is pretty. Philosopher? Nothing new here.
