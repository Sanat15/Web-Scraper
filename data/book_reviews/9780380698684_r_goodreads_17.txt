Rating: 3/5

Armino Fabbio is a tour guide for Sunshine Tours. As the novel opens, the bus has arrived in Rome where the 50 tourists will visit some of the sights and then head to Naples. On the night of the first day in Rome, Fabbio goes out by himself, and sees a woman, obviously drunk, lying on the steps of a church. He thinks he recognizes her as Marta, the woman who was a servant in the household when he was a boy.

This beginning forms the foundation for what is to come. We soon learn that Armino believes he is the sole living member of his family. His father and brother did not survive the war and his mother died of cancer a few years later. Still, something pulls him back to Ruffano, the city of his boyhood. It is there that we begin to see a parallel between the evil sadistic Duke Claudio of 500 years earlier and current events. 

I am not afraid of reading dark novels, but I admit to being disgusted with pure evil. My daily reading periods got a bit shortened because I could take only so much at a time. I was rewarded for not simply setting it aside as the ending is perfect. Both writing style and plotting is what has me returning to du Maurier, though there is certainly enough characterization to hold it together. 

This is came late in du Maurier's career and I have read that her later novels were not of the same high quality as her earlier novels. While I do believe she lost something in this, it was still worth my time. There are also several story collections which I'm anxious to try and so she will stay on my lists. I think I would be exaggerating to find a 4th star for this title, but only a little - it is very close and I admit to being tempted.
