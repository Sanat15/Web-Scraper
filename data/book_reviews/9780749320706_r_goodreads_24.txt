Rating: 4/5

Pooh and the Philosophers is a playful and surprisingly useful book. On the surface, it is a comic attempt to show that all paths in Western philosophy lead to Winnie-the-Pooh. And it works better than it probably should.

Some of the links between Pooh and the philosophers are stretched at best. But the better parts are clever and oddly wise.

Yet what surprised me most was its value from a midlife point of view. When viewed in this way Pooh’s simplicity starts to look less like childishness and more like a kind of mature wisdom. He listens. He tests things. He accepts uncertainty. He acts when action is needed. He does not make life more complicated than it needs to be.

The book is especially good on the idea that wisdom can come from ordinary life. Honey, friends, failure, fear, useful pots, and small acts of courage can all carry meaning. 

So I genuinely found more here than I expected. It made me think about humility, experience, courage, and the slow work of finding meaning.

A strange book, but nevertheless a valuable one.

4 stars.
