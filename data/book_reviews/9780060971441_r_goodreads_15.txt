Rating: 4/5

I think I may have been in an odd headspace for the second half of Renata Adler’s Pitch Dark as my partner and I received the saddening news that our 14 year old cat is terminally ill with an untreatable cancer. She is comfortable for the time being, but, time being what it is, that will be assuredly too short.

So my apologies to Adler. What I did digest here was evocative and beautiful, quick and witty, dark and murky. An effusively written work. The sequence with the car running out of gas in Ireland and the subsequent escape from the country under pretenses is comic because of how procedural it is in expressing modern complications and how they compound. The dissolution of the narrator’s relationship was too dimly painted for me to fully invest, but the moments between them are beautifully sad.
