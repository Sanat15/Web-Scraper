Rating: 5/5

'If, reader, I had more ample space to write, I should sing at least in part the sweetness of the drink that never would have sated me, but, since all the sheets made ready for this second canticle are full, the curb of art lets me proceed no farther. From those most holy waters I came away remade, as are new plants renewed with new-sprung leaves, pure and prepared to rise up to the stars.'

And so, Dante concludes his sojourn in Purgatory. It was a journey worth taking, but I must return soon ... to the book. Please not to the real place. I knew before and am all the more convinced of the importance of praying for the dead. It gave added emphasis to my reading of Day by Day for the Holy Souls in Purgatory: 365 Reflections. I could go on to Paradise but I am not sure I want to just yet. 🤔

So long as Dante and Virgil were moving up the levels of the mountain and encountering the various vices and virtues, I was doing okay. But when Beatrice showed up and Virgil disappeared, I became rather lost. She was very critical of Dante, and I'm not quite sure what he had done, except that she referred to the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, so presumably Dante partook of some forbidden fruit, but I do not know when, how or what it was and I could even be wrong about that. So, I suppose, I need to go back and reread that before going on. (sigh)

According to my notes, I read this 6 years ago, but I don't remember finishing it...

2019: Interesting interpretation of Purgatory. It was certainly a relief after the Inferno. I want to read it again before writing a review. On to Paradiso.
