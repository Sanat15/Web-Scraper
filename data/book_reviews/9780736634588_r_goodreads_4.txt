Rating: 4/5

The division of this occasionally unwieldy twelve-volume comedy of manners into four seasons invites the reader to take a more reflective and rounded approach to Powell’s masterpiece. Rereading them in this three-book omnibus is a joy and I thoroughly recommend seeking out this edition.

Here are my original reviews of the first three titles:

‘A Question of Upbringing’
Self-deprecating, slightly self-conscious and rather wonderful, sums up the first of this twelve-volume extravaganza replete with an ever-growing cast of boors, charmers and eccentrics moving throughout
its pages. Widmerpool, Templer, Stringham and narrator Jenkins inspire much entertainment and some laugh-out-loud moments in this social history of manners of the 1920s.

‘A Buyer’s Market’ 
A subtle and compelling exploration of class manners from a narrator whose movements remain so ambiguous that they occasionally trip up the unwary reader.

‘The Acceptance World’
Volume 3 of Powell’s addictive saga is starting to display qualities of the stellar British documentary series ‘Seven-Up.’ We have become familiar with the characters; are drawn to some more than others, and look forward to the next instalment with anticipation. Fortunately, in the case of ‘A Dance to the Music of Time,’ there is no seven-year wait between courses.
