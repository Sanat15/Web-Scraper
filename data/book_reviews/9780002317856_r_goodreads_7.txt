Rating: 4/5

New Zealander Gwenda Reed, who thinks she has never been to England, buys an old house there with her husband. But when she moves in, she gets this strange feeling that she’s been there before. Memories slowly rise to the surface like ghosts of a distant past. She thinks she’s going mad, so she asks Miss Marple for help.

What an interesting concept. Not a typical cozy mystery, but in a good way. It’s rather slow-paced. But it’s gripping, and there are some good red herrings to keep you guessing. The investigation of a murder from so long ago with such an unreliable witness provides Miss Marple with plenty of challenges to overcome. It reminds me a bit of two Poirot books with a similar concept. Though I have to say, all these stories still manage to feel unique. Because they all tackle this concept in a different way.

Agatha Christie wrote this story during the Second World War. Should anything have happened to her during the war, this story made sure that her husband would have been taken care of. She did the same thing for her daughter with Poirot’s final book. Though she apparently may have revised this story after the war, which I didn’t know. And it got published posthumously.

It doesn’t feel like the end of the Miss Marple series, but this feels fitting for Miss Marple as a character. Unlike Poirot, who did get his big epic swan song, Miss Marple has never been a character who demanded to be in the spotlights. So it feels nice and fitting to not have her overshadow the other characters or the plot in this book.
