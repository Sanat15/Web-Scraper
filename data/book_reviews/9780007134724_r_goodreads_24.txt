Rating: 1/5

It seems I still haven't learnt my lesson when I said I will no longer read music history books that aren't memoirs, because so far, every one I have read has been a waste of my time and inevitably puts me into a reading slump. This included. 

The thing is, this started off pretty decent for the first 100 pages but QUICKLY got tedious. I thought the author's writing style was somewhat entertaining due to his quips that were borderline edgy at times, but once you get used to it, you see just how little Harris has to say about this era. I don't think it helps that this moment of music didn't even really have a solid core subculture to it and was mainly about the top few bands who managed to score mainstream success... and here's the thing, just like the author, they don't actually seem to be interesting outside of their diabolical British banter, so I suppose there isn't much to write about in the first place. 

We learn next to nothing about the music itself, beyond one-liner opinions from Harris, which is lazy when you're writing about this subject. I couldn't care less about the drugs these people were downing left, right, and center, not to mention having to hear about it countless times over. Sure if it influences their creative process in some way, but this went beyond that. It seemed to be used as filler because Harris felt there was little else to talk about.

Harris either needed to cut this down by half or even more of its word count (a lot is fluff anyway) or speak about the music and people in DEPTH beyond their questionable lifestyles.
