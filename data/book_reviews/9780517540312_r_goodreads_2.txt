Rating: 4/5

While it says it is a Biography of the family, it is more a biography of the parents. Little is said about the girls. There is mention of the log books that Bronson did about the oldest 2 and how important a read they are for childhood development and mentions here and there when it is a comment Louisa put in one of her writings about someone. But generally, the rest of the family is pretty much a footnote. This does not mean the book does not have value just that you , inspite of the title, should not expect a full biography of the entire family.

It is a great overview of many things that happened to the family because of the parents. It follows both of the parents through all their follies and hard work. Bronson could not have been an easy man to live with in many ways but his devotion to his family and children is evident. Even if he could not manage to have any concept of finances to feed his family he did love them very much and they knew it. This book does make it clear the the true force in the family was Abby May. She was the driving force that often kept the family fed. She was also devoted to her husband and children and was the one that made the various houses a home for the girls. Her ideas are from a more frenetic mind than Bronson's but many of the ideas she had on many things is just as valid. It was her temperament that got in the way of implementing them as she did not have the greatest people skills. Truthfully, both parents had some issues there. 

Well worth finding if you want to have a firm background on how Louisa was brought up. The foundation of families in her works are shown in the snippets in here about her childhood family life. They were all close in spite of the frequent house moves and tribulations their parents caused.
