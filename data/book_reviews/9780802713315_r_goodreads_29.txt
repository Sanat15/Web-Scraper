✩ 5.0 stars ✩
In telling the story of one of the most famous problems in mathematics, Simon Singh reveals the beauty of number theory, hidden in the simplicity of the problem that Andrew Wiles fell in love with all those years ago. He maps out a tree of mathematics with Fermat’s Last Theorem as the trunk, the central piece, paying tribute to the countless mathematicians that paved the way for Wiles’ proof. And of course, he describes the sheer tenacity of the mathematical genius as he grapples with the problem for 7 years. I loved the detail with which Singh explored the history of mathematics — he maps out intricate branches of time, delving into the history of Pythagoras, Euler, Turing, Taniyama and Shimura, and so many others, as he attributes to each their own contributions towards Fermat’s Last Theorem.

This is a must-read for anyone interested in maths, but perfectly accessible for laymen of any sort.
