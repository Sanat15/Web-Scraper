Rating: 2/5

This one was okay, but not great. On the plus side, it's an older book, so it's more tame than a few I've stumbled onto more recently. Any time the male protagonist visited his mistress, there was a passionate kiss and then a scene cut. But therein lay my main issue: why on earth do we have books wherein the laudable gentlemen are making frequent visits to their mistresses while trying to woo the lady of their dreams? Of course, the mistress always seems appreciative (He's such a noble guy, I'm glad he finally found a good woman to marry!) and the wife-to-be shrugs it off as long as he ends it when they get married. Perhaps this is authentic to the time period, but it kills me nonetheless. 

On the other hand, we have Diana, who is basically the ideal woman. She's smart and beautiful and likeable, and somehow she manages to have a lovely townhouse with adequate staffing despite being so deep in debt that her country property is falling apart and can only be repaired piecemeal. She's really too good to be true.

Despite my Austen-inspired love of all things Regency-era, this one didn't strike a chord with me.
