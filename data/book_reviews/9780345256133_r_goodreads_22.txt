Rating: 3/5

Surprisingly the book seems period-friendly. All protagonists are firmly grounded in the historical setting, so are their personalities and opinions. The importance of social obligations, paying heed to what's proper. It's refreshing. 

Alathea is also an enjoyable heroine. Intelligent, perceptive, and witty. I thought the author took a cue or two from Austen's Pride and Prejudice. She may be bluestocking, but the community and family do their job to remind her of the feminine obligations and societal expectations.

On the con's side, although the hero and the heroine's interactions make me smile, there is little to none build-up to romance. I don't believe in Beau's change of heart. There is no particular switch from his initial plan to add another conquest under his belt to a marriage proposal. This is a very Heyeresque approach, but Chetwynd Ley is no Heyer to pull it off.
