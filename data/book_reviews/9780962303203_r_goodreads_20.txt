Rating: 3/5

I discovered this book as a fourteen-year-old, through word of mouth, at my local public library. I was pleased, first and foremost, that the book was available at my library and second because I possessed a student ID card, which meant I could check it out. Checking it out proved to be tricky because they did not allow you to remove the book from the library, evidently too many copies had not been returned. Instead, you were required to trade your ID for the book and remain inside the library in plain view (no doubt the ID cards were photocopied by the library attendants for future reference). During that era there were no copy machines available to library users, so copious notes were made during a four-hour period, all of which related to drugs. I was fascinated by the concept of drugs and, having been tasked by my physical science teacher with doing a report on hallucinogens, I was eager to discover the real information. I was disappointed to find that most of the drug-related content was either too complex (talking chemistry here), or too silly for me (at the time...fast-forward one year and I was scraping the insides of banana peels, cooking it in the oven, and smoking it). End of the day, as an adult I cannot, in good conscience, give it much more than a three. Yay for the freedom of information; boo for violence.
