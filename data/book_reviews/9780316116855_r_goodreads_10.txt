Rating: 3/5

In the early 1980s I was addicted to Carl Sagan's PBS series Cosmos, and when it ended, I was desperate for something to take its place. James Burke's Connections seemed like it would fit the bill, but somehow it didn't grab me. I watched a couple of episodes, and there are bits I still remember, but more than once I drifted off to sleep and lost the thread. That was that, but then I saw Burke's companion book in a used book store, and thought it looked worth another try. It sat on my shelves until a few days ago. "It's time!" thought I (or something to that effect). Sadly, I found reading the book to be a similar experience to watching the show. I don't know why. I appreciate Burke's approach to history, which emphasizes chance, the interconnectedness of ideas, and the work of the many, rather than the single Great Man. I also learned a few things that I hope will stay with me, as did the revelations of the stirrup and the long bow, from my viewing of over forty years ago. But somehow Burke's presentation leaves me underwhelmed. Too many disparate parts, perhaps, too many threads strung between thumbtacks that might just as well have been strung another way. Sometimes I felt way ahead of him, sometimes I didn't know where I was or where I was going. I think I understand why so many love his work, but I often found the book to be a slog.
