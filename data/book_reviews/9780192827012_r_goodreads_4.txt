Rating: 5/5

History was written by Emile Zola, who recounted it working in a coal mine in France and describes a bloody strike that marks the beginning of the labor movement. It begins with Étienne, who arrives at the mine looking for a job and knows Bonnemort (GoodDeath), so-called because he survived mine accidents countless times. Étienne is very close to the Good Death family, where practically everyone works in the mine except the younger children and the mother. Like most families in the area, she needs to care for them, as the more people work, the more money they have to buy bread, which is better, because wages are miserable. The working conditions at the mine are inhumane and pose a daily challenge, requiring workers to venture to its depths. Étienne begins to incite the workers to strike, so they decide to establish a reserve fund to prevent going hungry during the strike. It turns out that the money collected soon runs out, and with the bosses threatening to lay off everyone and bring in other workers, some strikers begin to want to return to work so as not to starve, creating even more tension.
