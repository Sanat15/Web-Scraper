Rating: 1/5

I literally got a headache reading this book.
The premise was interesting at first: the hero likes the heroine, marries her, and takes her home. But from there, everything goes downhill. The heroine absolutely hates him and wants nothing to do with him. Her constant hostility and unwillingness became exhausting and started to grate on me. Just when she finally begins to like the hero, he does something that pushes them even further apart.
The unnecessary drama is on another level of stupidity.
First, the hero’s former mistress gets jealous and creates insecurity for the heroine. Then the hero’s friend causes jealousy for the hero. On top of that, the heroine becomes pregnant and loses the baby due to the foolish behavior of both leads.
To “fix” the relationship, the hero takes the heroine to France—but of course, more drama follows. A Comte becomes attracted to the heroine and actively pursues her. The hero is insanely jealous, yet somehow agrees to stay at this man’s castle. This makes absolutely no sense. Cue more pointless drama, ending in both men literally fighting over the heroine.
By the time they leave France, the relationship is worse than ever. The hero is angry at the heroine just when she has truly started caring for him. They avoid each other for the rest of the voyage back to America.
Then comes the shipwreck. From here on, I mostly skimmed. The heroine ends up with evil people and loses her memory. The hero ends up on an island with another woman who constantly tries to seduce him. While he never actually cheats and remains emotionally loyal to the heroine—even believing she might be dead—the whole “island girl” plot was unbearable. I hate other-woman drama, and this was excessive.
You think the book ends there? Nope. The hero’s former mistress and heroine’s former fiance shows up again to stir up more nonsense. By then, I was completely done.
This book was a total waste of my time. I wish I had known beforehand that it was packed with so much OW drama. Absolutely horrible.
