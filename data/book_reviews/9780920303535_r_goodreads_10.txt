Rating: 5/5

Both methodical and frank, the first time I read this through I was somehow still jolted when the less public body parts appeared. It felt like a surprise. But the second time through, I appreciated how very logically everything is arranged -- the body parts begin with hair and moved from top to bottom, pausing with toes and ending with outsides (skin) and insides. Of course, right in the middle are chests and breasts and genitals and bums, sandwiched between the more comfortable hands and fingers and legs and knees.

For toddlers and preschoolers learning about bodies -- both their own and others' -- the text of this books offers an opportunity to name them all. For grownups learning how to talk about bodies, this book is a fabulous resource. But the illustrations are where this edition really shines. I'm sure *everything* is not represented here, but there is A LOT of humanity seen on these pages. Clearly identifiable are amputees, people (of all ages) with hearing aids, pregnant bodies, the elderly, wheelchairs for different ages, burn marks, hijabs, all kinds of natural hair and dyed color combinations, makeup on various gender presentations, eyeglasses, scars, piercings, breastfeeding, tattoos, stretchmarks, fat rolls, vitiligo, and curves. It's a glorious representation.
