Rating: 3/5

Four plays about romantic misadventures - they're all very dated, both in terms of style, subject, and structure, and they're rarely as entertaining as Wodehouse's literature. For me, "Good Morning, Bill" is the gem of this collection - it's genuinely funny and sweet, Wodehouse's voice and comedy feels effortless, and it's as tight as a three act comedy from the 20s is likely to get. "The Play's the Thing" isn't terrible, although it's one solid idea that takes a long time to pay off (but it still gets the occasional staging, so it might work with a good cast and judicious cutting). The Jeeves and the Psmith plays, though, are just monsters - enormous casts, multiple sets and three long, long acts and worse, they're light comedies that just aren't all that funny (there's a real straining for jokes in Wodehouse's usually sparkling dialogue - the Jeeves script even italicizes some words so the actors know where the punchlines are).
