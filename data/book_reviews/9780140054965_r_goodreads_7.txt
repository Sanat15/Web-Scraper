Rating: 3/5

Grabbed this, as well as V. S. Naipaul's In a Free State, to read while traveling not knowing how similar their settings (as well as their 1971 publication date) would be: newly independent African states in the 1960s beset by internal rebellions witnessed by outsider whites, in this case an American insurance agent building (for a while) a business in Malawi, and two British ex-colonials in a Uganda-like country in Naipaul’s title piece. While Naipaul's two Brits observe a downtrodden country further losing its moorings as they drive across it, Theroux’s American encounters a foreign-funded guerrilla movement while embedding himself with the local society of expat drinkers and a Malawian woman. Beyond their similar premise, the stories of course diverge in their specifics but also along American and British lines and so provide some interesting contrasts in the respective cultural and historical experiences and perspectives.
