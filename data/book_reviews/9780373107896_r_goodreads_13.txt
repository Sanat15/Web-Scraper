Rating: 4/5

No Gentle Persuasion is more complex than most Harlequin Presents, which is one reason I like Kay Thorpe so much. Our heroine is torn most of the novel and from what we can see, our hero is also having a difficult time choosing his direction. What he wanted for years earlier now seems less desirable and now he's falling in love with his wife.

I disagree with the reviewers who believe he's cheating; I think he is tempted (as he admits) but is not doing so. He comments once that she very much takes things at face value and that things very seldom are as simple as they appear. 

It's not obvious why she agrees to marry him the morning after the ungentle persuasion. He implies he wants her to love him and that he's falling in love himself but he doesn't say it. Later when she learns of the Italian Grandpa problem it's easy to believe he was manipulating and using her.
Good characters, setting, plot.
