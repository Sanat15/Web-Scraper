Rating: 2/5

Three aunts live on an island to protect it. But they know they can’t keep doing this forever. They need successors who can keep up the good work after they’re gone. So they decide to kidnap three children.

This is a book that’s very tough to rate. There are some things I enjoyed. Like, the three quirky aunts who care about the island are memorable. What’s interesting about them kidnapping the children is that they had the best intentions when they took the kids. And both the good and the bad are shown, this encourages readers to think for themselves about what’s right and what’s wrong. The island is full of fantasy elements like mermaids, selkies and krakens. It’s a story that teaches us the value of kindness, hard work and teamwork. And the core theme of protecting the environment is done well.

But for everything I like in this book, there’s also something I don’t like. Like the three aunts drug the children, kidnap them and work them like slaves in the beginning. I’m sorry, but this just makes them a bit unlikeable in my opinion. There are some dark, mature and sometimes outdated undertones that don’t always mix well with the rather light and humorous approach of this book in my opinion. It takes a while for the story to really get going. The prose, while good, can be a bit distracting at times. And even though Lambert might be seen as a terrible kid who refuses to do his chores, it doesn’t really feel fair to see him as a bad kid because he refuses to be worked like a slave by his kidnappers.

I’m sorry. I really wanted to like this book more. Though I’m clearly an outlier, as a lot of people seem to like this book. So don’t let my review stop you from reading it should you be interested as there are definitely some things to enjoy in here.
