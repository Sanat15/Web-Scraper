Rating: 5/5

I originally picked up The Pop-Up Book of Phobias on something of a whim several years ago, being familiar with (but not actually having perused) the same production team's presumably grown-up The Pop-up Book of Sex. Adult pop-up books generally fall into two categories, as far as I can tell: Full-on art books, with ridiculously detailed and complex paper-crafting, such as Blue 2: A Pop-Up for Children of All Ages (a book my ex-wife and I originally bought for our child, until we realized that Blue 2 was very much not a pop-up book for kids); and pop-up books that simply deal with "adult" subject matters, such as the aforementioned works by Melcher Media, at the hands of Gary Greenberg, illustrator Balvis Rubess, and paper artist Matthew Reinhart (or some permutation of the above).

Generally speaking, The Pop-Up Book of Phobias is a truly fine example of one of these grown-up pop-up books. Reinhart's pop-ups (especially for dentophobia [with its pulley-operated twisting dentist's drill], acrophobia [with its perspective-challenging, impossibly tall building that almost literally jumps out of the pages], and claustrophobia [which doesn't even allow the reader to open the pages fully]) are marvelously complex and detailed, Rubess' illustrations are suitably (albeit gently) disturbing where they need to be, and Greenberg's writing (and, presumably, research) is simple yet reasonably detailed, with a light touch to boot. Simply put, The Pop-Up Book of Phobias is indeed a work of art in the world of pop-up books, and it deserves to be actively enjoyed by anybody and everybody.
