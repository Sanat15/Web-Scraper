Rating: 3/5

Note: this is not the edition I read, however Goodreads took away the option of being able to post different editions so we are stuck with whatever is on here. My edition is the much better Zebra paperback that has 430 pages

It’s 1990, only a year or so removed from the historic Loma Preitta Earthquake that rattled San Francisco and Oakland and showed the rest of the world why Californians aren’t messing around when we talk about being prepared for earthquakes. Arnston’s The Big One taps into that fear and asks the question, what would happen if we could actually predict these natural disasters? Would everyone be on board or would it be something kept secret? 

The book is far from perfect but it’s still a well written novel that showcases a tremendous amount of research and dedication. The detailed world-building and exploration of complex topics reflect a commendable effort, especially for an author with no formal writing training who began his career later in life. Arnston’s ability to tackle such an ambitious project is inspiring and speaks to his natural storytelling instincts.

That said, the novel is not without its flaws. At times, the pacing drags, making the story feel overlong. Additionally, some characters make decisions that seem implausibly rushed, detracting from the realism of their arcs and the otherwise immersive narrative. The premise of a 9.5 scale earthquake isn’t really truly fulfilled either, but, this is a Zebra afterall, and, even though it’s not one from their famous horror line, misleading potential readers isn’t exactly anything new. 

Despite these shortcomings The Big One remains a decent read, particularly for those who appreciate meticulous detail and can forgive its uneven pacing. Arnston’s late start as an author, transitioning from a auto parts manufacturer to more than adequate author, adds a layer of admiration to his achievement, making this an imperfect yet respectable entry into the literary world.
