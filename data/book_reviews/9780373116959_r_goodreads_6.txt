Rating: 2/5

Overall, a meh read from Michelle Reid, and one that reads like an earlier book from her (1994--her 8th book I believe). It had a weird, dated feel to it, as if she was emulating historicals--the H and h are "to the manor/manner bred," their families are local gentry more or less, and there is even a family feud that mostly plays out on their insular social scene. The H is around 10 years older than the h, and as in many MR books, that age difference is a large part of their problems: the h is wild and immature at 18 when they first get together, and a severe trial to our older and more urbane H. There is a blowout and their engagement is broken off, and she goes to stay with her mother in Boston for four years, becoming a typical (in books, anyway) cool, reserved high-society deb, burying her impetuous and wild side under the armor of convention.

It's clear that they are still crazy about one another when she returns to England, but there are misunderstandings and jealousies to be resolved, and it was all very tiresome and only skim-worthy until the reconciliation at the end. I was bored by the h's histrionics (shown largely in flashback), not really compelled by either the H or h, and thought this one fell flat. I think that MR was still finding her way in Harley land, trying out different approaches until she found the formula that worked for her (and largely for me, based on some of the later books by her I've read). So a middling foray by an author I am growing to like more and more, and will consider this one a training exercise that eventually leads to a mastery of the genre.
