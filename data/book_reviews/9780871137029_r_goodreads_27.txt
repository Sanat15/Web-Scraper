Rating: 4/5

A brutal and harrowing recounting of a brutal and harrowing moment of a brutal and harrowing time of (way more modern than I think most people appreciate) Irish history. A moment that needn't have happened if the colonizers of England had shown an ounce of humanity or the soldiers of the IRA had acted less violently at points. A story of no real winners, as even if the POWs of Long Kesh did eventually get political status in all but name, it did cost the lives of not just the ten men but the dozens others who died in tit-for-tat violence from the IRA and the UVF. 

My few criticisms of the book is it's quite long, a bit of a slog at points as often the same things are revisted repeatedly from alternate viewpoints, and I could do without the ficitionalized account of a prisoner at the end of every chapter. While it was well written and honest, I do felt it took away from the reality of Bik trying to hold the hunger strike together with his bare hands while in prison.
