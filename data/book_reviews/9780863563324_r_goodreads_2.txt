Rating: 4/5

A solid work of what can be termed “little power history,” describing the fate of the Ragusa city-state from its semi-mythic foundations to its fall at the hands of Revolutionary France, through to the siege of 1991-1992. Dense and at times a little dry, it remains more or less readable. Dubrovnik is the ultimate little power, buffeted between a variety of imperial, covetous neighbors but uniquely able to tack between them with a lot of careful diplomacy and no shortage of bribery, until it ran into Napoleonic France, which never met a beautiful, venerable institution it didn’t want to “save.” I wish Harris had focused a little more on some of the overseas entrepôts, one of the distinguishing features of Ragusa commercial life, and the work has little to say about the period from 1815 through to the present day, but it is still a worthwhile contribution to the corpus of Adriatic and Mediterranean history.
