Rating: 4/5

This brand new copy was published in 1978 and my brother must have won it several years later. I didn't know this marvellous story, that I easily give four stars. French wouldn't impact on our memory or meaning as strongly as English. It is fun to be brushing up on it a lot. Since we attended French immersion, this is: “Donald Et La Boîte Magique”. He must have done his French reading once and just savoured winning this prize. Mom saved it in crisp condition. His name is on a cupcake taped inside and on a blue ribbon, indicating an athletic competition. He will remember this.

I'm unversed in Donald Duck lore, to know where his nephews' parents are. They appear to live with him, if their mail arrives chez-lui. As the synopsis and opening pages say, Donald only gets flyers and bills and the boys concur about only receiving school business. I come from the realm of penpals and say: write letters to receive them! These ducks echo many, for whom mail brings no magic, nothing personal to generate excitement. A new friend treated me to a box of goodies just two weeks ago, not even for an occasion. The elation really is magical. A mobile antiques dealer happens to have the fanciest mailbox any of us has ever seen. They derive excitement from how this beautiful receptacle looks at least, compared to the usual tin rectangle.

For Huey, Dewey, and Louis with bizarre, presumably equivalent French names, the castle mailbox yields massive prizes: circus tickets, camping equipment, and a snorkelling week-end! Suspending disbelief does not cover someone mistakenly selling Granny's mailbox and relatives not recognizing it. I prefer stories about good fortune that isn't temporary but the thought is lovely, that they keep receiving mail from their Granny.
