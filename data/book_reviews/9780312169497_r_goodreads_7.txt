Rating: 4/5

This was both interesting and a slight disappointment. 

I first encountered Gardner when I picked up one of his Mathematical collections as a teenager in the 1970’s. Further collections of his articles written for Scientific American followed as I went on to study Mathematics at university. I had his annotated Alice, revised over the years. Other than that, I hadn’t read him for around 40 years until I came across this collection and thought that I would renew my acquaintance. 

Of course, I expected that I would be reading a selection of the best of those Mathematical essays with their puzzles and sense of fun. What I hadn’t known was that Gardner’s writing roamed far wider than Lewis Carroll and maths. That was the disappointment. However, I found much of his writing on economics and philosophy interesting. Some is dated now: the economists and Reaganomics are a long time ago and he assumes a knowledge of current events that we no longer have. Similarly, I didn’t always follow the detailed philosophical semantics of US philosophers that I had never heard of. But there is always a next essay which is more accessible.

Glad I read it but I shall probably just return to my old mathematical ones.
