Rating: 4/5

Another excellent entry in this series. While I must admit that the cast of this book went a long way toward making me dislike them - a distinct difference from the previous books - the courtroom antics and witticisms flew just as thick and landed just as true as all the previous five books. In particular, the mercenary attitude that almost every character exhibits in this book made me revise my opinion in a negative direction of almost all of them. They snipe at each other, backstab each other, and generally make asses of themselves in ways not seen as frequently in the previous books. Perhaps this is intended to be an indictment of the characters who tend to make up the bar, in the author's eyes at least.
Despite this, it's clear to me that John Mortimer has proven himself to be a genius in not only tone and content but also in his ability to make his main character fit into a groove that feels comfortable rather than worn. While this proclaims to be Rumpole's Last Case, I welcome the next few entries in this series and will happily join our main character as he heaves himself "up on his hindlegs" and proclaims for his defendants all the more.
