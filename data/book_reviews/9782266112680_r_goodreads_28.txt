Rating: 4/5

“It was terribly hot that summer Mr. Robertson left town, and for a long while the river seemed dead. Just a dead brown snake of a thing lying flat through the center of town, dirty yellow foam collecting at its edge.”

Isabelle is a fairly quiet single parent, working as a secretary in a factory in a relatively small town in New York. Amy is her daughter, a young girl-woman teetering on the verge of becoming a woman. Amy and Isabelle have a relatively quiet home life, although occasionally tense with unvoiced resentments. Isabelle seems to have no one in her life aside from Amy and her boss, who lives larger in Isabelle’s fantasy life with him. Of course, Isabelle could never bring herself to act on such fantasies, she barely even acknowledges as such, limiting herself mostly to thoughts of the better wife she could be, would be. 

Amy has one friend, Stacy, a girl who lives life more dangerously than Amy has ever dreamed of. Amy doesn’t dream about any boys, unlike Stacy who has a boyfriend, one she’s even, you know, done that with. Amy only dreams about one guy, the new substitute teacher, who seems to see her as no one else does, makes her feel things no one else ever has. 

Not much happens in this small town that goes unnoticed for long, it’s a safe, if sleepy town. Some things just can’t stay hidden, at least not for long. However, as is typically with Elizabeth Strout novels, this is less about what happens than it is about the people. The strength of Amy &amp; Isabelle is in Elizabeth Strout’s ability to create characters so real that you become invested in them, and not just Amy and Isabelle, but the people in their world.
