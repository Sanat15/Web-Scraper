Rating: 5/5

This 1996 legal thriller was adapted into a feature film in 2003 starring John Cusack, Rachel Weisz, Gene Hackman, and Dustin Hoffman. When I decided to revisit the novel in order to write a review, I had nearly forgotten how much I also enjoyed the film. But as I settled back into the book, the actors effortlessly slipped into the roles on the page, and all the excitement and tension came rushing back.

It also reminded me exactly why this novel captivated me the first time.

A ruthless tobacco conglomerate is being sued by the widow of a man who died from lung cancer. Convinced they can only win by controlling the outcome, the corporation hires a shady jury consultant who will stop at nothing to secure the verdict they want.

But what happens when one juror arrives with a past that even the consultants can't uncover? Who is this mysterious juror, and why was it so important to be seated on this jury?

In this fast-paced, thought-provoking page-turner, the real drama extends far beyond the courtroom. The battle isn't confined to the attorneys' arguments—it unfolds in the tense, high-stakes cat-and-mouse game taking place both inside and outside the jury room. As the manipulation intensifies, readers are left wondering who is truly pulling the strings.

Knowing the tobacco conglomerate will do anything to influence the verdict, are they behind the jury machinations? Will the widow prevail? Will the jury award compensatory or punitive damages? Or will the tobacco industry once again evade responsibility?

No spoilers from me.

The twists keep coming, and just when readers think they have figured everything out, Grisham changes the game. Both the novel and its film adaptation are masterclasses in suspense, building relentless tension all the way to the ending.

For me, this is John Grisham at his very best.
