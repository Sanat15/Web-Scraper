Rating: 5/5

All the Myriad Ways is a wonderfully wild ride. It ardently refuses to stick to one genre, bouncing erratically between sci-fi, fantasy, and even the sociological consequences of teleportation. But one thing remains constant: Niven is - to my mind - the undisputed master of grabbing a readers interest. The opening line of every story or essay evokes either awe at the brilliance wasted on a savantly-silly premise, a small chuckle, or steinbeckian noiresque intrigue.

Acute genre awareness and subversion serve magnificently to showcase a theme park of Niven’s most eccentric, cynical, and delightfully bizarre ideas.
Environmentalist allegory wrapped in a sword-and-sorcery cloak; a detailed analysis of Superman's love-life; and coining a law of temporal mechanics: conceiving of anything this book is missing must require certifiable insanity.
