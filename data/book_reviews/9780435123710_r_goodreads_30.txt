Rating: 3/5

"Sorrow has its life like people. Sorrow is born and lives and dies. And when it's dead and gone, someone's left behind to remember it. Exactly like people."

In an attempt to finish the books I have at home before justifying a new purchase, the first book I picked up upon my return turns out to be a children's book... This is a story about a young boy who seems to be different from everyone embarking on a mission to escape them and towards freedom. The book is filled with many enjoyable little nuggets of wisdom and it portrays David's journey of discovering the world and humanity outside the confines of a concentration camp in an interesting (albeit unrealistic) way. What really irked me, however, was the last 30 or so pages which felt rushed, rendering the ending severely underdeveloped. Perhaps I would have enjoyed this when I was at the age of the target audience for which this book is intended... though most probably not.
