Rating: 4/5

Elias Chacour is a Palestinian, Arab, Israeli Christian priest. This autobiography covers key highlights in his youth, and time in priestly service in a small village. He covers various interactions he has had with Israeli bureaucracy and the police, bureaucracy and corruption in the church, and his various methods of non-violent peacekeeping. 

I hate to say this, but I did feel that he came across as a humblebrag in the way he handled difficult situations, it is kind of suspicious to me that he never really shares an anecdote where he comes across as the bad guy. Of course, several years later we learn that he was accused of sexually assaulting one of his employees, who was later fired. I don't know if there was merit to her accusation or not, though I do tend to believe women who have more to lose in this power dynamic. 

Still, I think people are complicated and a bad person (if he is one) can still have a right philosophy and approach to life. In the book and in interviews, he is consistent about his 2 beliefs: God does not kill, and God is love. I appreciated his non-violent approach to resisting occupation and feel that I can empathize more than I did before.
