Rating: 4/5

Ended up really enjoying this. David Halberstam is the best (think summer of ‘49 and October of ‘64 are better) but thought the last 150 pages were great. It was kinda a slog in the middle (the minutia was a lot) but again, ended strong. Ramsay is a very interesting figure and I actually thought the book humanized Walton in a way where I might actually be less of a fan of him now then I was going in, as he seems like a bit of a headache as a teammate/person. I still think he’s cool as hell, and what he did in ‘86 and ‘77 are incredible, but I previously thought of him as a God, and this told his story from a different lens. Enjoyed Billy Ray and Marvin Barnes, as well as the excepts about the late 70s Celtics, and the descriptions about how fragile the NBA was both in terms of the existence of the league and maintaining a good team.

Overall, great stuff, but 462 pages was a bit much, think maybe some ‘79 trailblazers details could have been streamlined, the team won 38 games after all.
