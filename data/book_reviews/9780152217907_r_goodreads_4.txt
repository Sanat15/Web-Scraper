Rating: 5/5

This was a book I read in fifth grade--it was just on a shelf in the classroom, something available for geeks like me to read after we were done with our work or whatever.

I found a copy of it recently and read it again, and it definitely stands the test of time. It's the story of a young boy's experience behind the Iron Curtain and his attempt to flee to freedom. Engaging, suspenseful, and believable.

This is an antidote to the sleepy and forgetful attitude that the West has toward the nightmare that was the reign of the USSR over Eastern Europe. It is a reminder not to take liberty for granted, and to realize how hard it can be to regain it once lost.

I can think of a lot of books on school reading lists that I would be glad to bump in favor of this one.
