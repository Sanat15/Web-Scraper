Rating: 4/5

Wordy, but enjoyable tale of orphans and reliance on wit

A nifty, but discursive story of the triumph of good over evil, Nicholas Nickleby is Dickens third novel. It is also a mystery and keeps the reader engaged throughout, even though there are additions that could well have been left out and not hurt the main story one bit, such as Nicholas's plunge into acting. Also, Dickens always puts such long speeches into the mouths of his characters that sometimes the meaning is lost. His characters are splendid creations and so lifelike, notwithstanding the creative names often given them which let you know if they're to be good guys or bad 'uns. For example, the brothers Cheeryble are of course benevolent and good-natured while Mr. Squeers is the nasty and violent head of Dotheboys Hall. "Do the boys" get hit and neglected? Yes and often.
