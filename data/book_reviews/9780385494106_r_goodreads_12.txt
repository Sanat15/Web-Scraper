Rating: 5/5

Truth is a human right. It is an obligation owed to us by our religious structures. Garry Wills never fails to blow me away with his writing. In Papal Sin (2000), Wills argues effectively that folks falling away from the (Catholic) faith both in the laity and priesthood is rooted in the several Popes and the curia's lack of historical honesty in regards to a number of positions: it's whitewashing of various Catholics' role in the holocaust, opposition to ALL forms of contraception, and a celibate male exclusive priesthood. 

In particular I appreciated this text as a survey of historical theology in Catholicism from the papacy of Pius IX through John Paul II. The various Piuses (Piusi?) of the 19th and 20th century play a strong role in the theological dishonesty that Wills points out as the modern papal sin - in contrast to the more materials sins of the church in the past a la Popes such as Julius II. (Side note: If a future Pope takes the name Pius, its not a good sign) Wills' Catholic heroes and role models he puts forward to provide an antidote to this problem are John XXIII, Augustine, Lord Acton, John Henry Newman, and - most conspicuously to me - Rene Girard.
