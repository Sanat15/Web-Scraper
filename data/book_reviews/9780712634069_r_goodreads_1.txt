Rating: 5/5

Holy shit, this author is wise.

The wisdom contained in this book is by far one of the more superior and insightful available in my library.

There are of course other books that go deep into the archetypal realm, but none have so aptly divulged the archetypal patterns of a woman's psyche and the path to her individuation in relation to men.

I recommend this book to all my loved ones and friends who are similar of mind and heart, for this book packs a punch in universal truths.

You could call book Jungian red-pill, laser sharp in precision, but wholehearted without making women good or bad. 

I loved that the generalisations were so on point (read: 'I love archetypal patterns and this book has them'). The generalisations Harding makes are never so stale and outdated as to be un-useful, which astounds me, given that the introduction was signed by Carl Jung himself in 1932. Take a moment to think about how much our society has changed. How much we think about women has changed. How different our lives are nowadays. On a galactic scale, not so much, obviously, but I'm certain you can imagine or remember some differences.

We don't just stop millions of years of evolution in its tracks by becoming modern and our collective unconscious allegedly contains all the history and images from our ancestors, which provide the archetypal material via our unconscious. 

It appears to be a hype among post-modernists and in Woke-ism to deny gender binary as being true or real and call gender a spectrum, but my sense is that there may be a psychological reason for that going topsy-turvy in our day and age. After all, I believe we remain hard-wired for being human - the way we've done it for ages: It is up to us to become more conscious of our unconscious material to become more whole individuals. 

I wish everyone to make use of the time we're given and use life's phases for the improvement of our planet and the wellbeing of our future children.
