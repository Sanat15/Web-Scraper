Rating: 5/5

This is a collection of personal interviewers with speculative fiction authors that writer and editor Charles Platt did initially in the late 1970s than revised (and added a few more) in the mid 1980s. Unlike other interviewers (say, in The Paris Review, or, to use examples from current SF publications, Locus or Clarkesworld), Platt has a slightly more combative style or, at the very least, a more confrontational one. He has opinions and he’s not hiding them from you the reader or from his subjects. And that makes these interview/profiles all the better for it. You get a real sense of the person he’s talking to, from the manic Keith Laumer, the wry Theodore Sturgeon, the practical Frank Herbert, and the thoughtful Thomas M. Disch. If I must lodge any complaint against this book it has to be in his omission of female writers, and even the one he chose, Alice Sheldon, is here in the guise of her nom de plume, James Tiptree, Jr. Platt rectified this later in a second volume, but it’s an unfortunate oversight in this volume.

I spent a lot of time reading these and thinking about them, capturing what I saw as highlights or insights for each author. This full commentary is over 5,000 words, so I didn't post it here, but is available on my Patreon and by request. 

I highly recommend this book if you're interested at all in these authors.
