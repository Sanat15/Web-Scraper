Rating: 5/5

Podkayne of Mars was serialized in Frederik Pohl's If magazine from November of 1962 through March of 1963. (If had bi-monthly publication.) It was published in hardback by Putnam's in 1963, and in mass market paperback by Avon in '64, and has had many editions over the years, some with inappropriate covers for a book narrated by a fifteen-year-old girl. It's generally regarded as one of Heinlein's juvenile books, perhaps the last of them, though there's some dispute about that, too. It's the story of a girl and her younger brother who accompany their uncle on a voyage from Mars via Venus to Earth where he is to attend an important political conference. They're taken hostage by terrorists. There's a lot of discussion about politics and space and science and gender roles and Heinlein's ideas about life, the universe, and everything. So far so good. (Spoiler ahead...) The book ends with Poddy saving a Venusian's life, though she is badly injured, when the terrorists detonate a bomb. Except Heinlein's original ending ended with Poddy dying in the attack. The publisher's made him change it. Baen published an edition in 1993 that had both endings and asked the readers to choose which one they liked better. It was like when DC comics asked readers to vote on whether or not to kill Robin, except less tasteful. I've never read the version with the restored ending... maybe I will someday, but probably not. I prefer to live in a universe where Poddy survived and continued to make things better for everyone. Doyle wasn't allowed to kill Holmes, after all. Heinlein created a -lot- of great characters, but Podkayne was especially special. I could go on, but will stop with just the observation that the Goodreads aggregate rating is pretty much in the middle for this one, people either loving the book or hating it, making it one of the most debated and discussed of the RAH canon.
