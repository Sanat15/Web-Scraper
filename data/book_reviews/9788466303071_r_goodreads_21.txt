Rating: 4/5

I'd give it a 3.5, but I can't, so I rounded to 4. 

This book was extremely well written and well researched. But, it was also emotionally difficult to get through. I never once took a pause while reading this and felt good. Funnily enough, it was also an addictive read, so I couldn't stop reading and feeling bad. 
A note on the style: It read like a history book or a creative newspaper report. If it was a painting, it would have been a paint-by-number. In other words, it was emotionally moving just by the nature of the events, not by the way in which the story was crafted. 

I already knew a good bit about the story, since I read "Chinese Cinderella," a shorter version of Mah's autobiography, which is intended for a younger audience. This book contained much more relevant historical context and information about Mah's adult life, including her relationships and ultimately her family. 

While one might consider this the ultimate underdog success story (because despite her family's cruelty, Mah ultimately found success in both her career and personal life), it strikes me more as an emotionally bitter and enduring defeat of a woman that follows her from childhood into adulthood. The perversion of filial piety that took place in her family is incredible, and the fact that this is a true story makes it that much more of a horrifying read.
