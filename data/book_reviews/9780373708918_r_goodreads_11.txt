Rating: 5/5

Mallory and Drew grew up together as next door neighbors and had been best friends all their lives. When they find themselves crossing the line one weekend, they part, agreeing that it can't happen again, their friendship is too important. When Mallory finds out she is pregnant, she is ecstatic. At 36, she had almost given up on a family. Raised by her aunt after her parents' deaths and watching Drew grow up next door with his single mother, Angie, she feels she can do it. Drew has always been a restless one, a journalist who will travel anywhere to get the story, looking for the front-page byline, never to be tied down. When he cancels his Thanksgiving trip home to the small town of Port Carling, Mallory realizes its time to call and let him know. Before that happens, his mother dies unexpectedly, and the news is put on hold. Drew is overwhelmed, uncertain, and guilty, dealing with his mother's death and the fall-out: her death, the house, and the family paper. Then the shock of finding his best friend and rock with his baby throws him for a loop. He already had a hard time forgetting their weekend together, and now dealing with a baby and his feelings. 
Mallory and Drew have a lot to wade through. Drew has so much hitting him all at once and has so many things to decide and make peace with. The one constant in his life has changed, and there is no going back. Both he and Mallory have to decide if friends can be more and be happy together for love and not just for doing the right thing. 
I enjoyed this book and being a part of the struggle these two faced. C.J. Carmichael has, again, written characters that tug your emotions and make you not want to put the book down.
