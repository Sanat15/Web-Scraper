Rating: 4/5

Another great collection of Wolfe short stories, with the usual mix of good and great supported by Archie's witty comebacks. In Kill Now - Pay Later we get an interesting take on the usual formula of dead bodies. A man is killed, another one supposedly kills himself out of guilt, and the police are satisfied that the latter did in the former, but not Wolfe. My favorite part of the story was Wolfe insisting that the second man's daughter reside with him for protection until the murderer is caught. We've seen this before in Wolfe stories, but not where he's the one to suggest it and insist.

Having seen Murder is Corny on the A&amp;E TV series I knew what to expect, and the show adhered so well to the book that there were no real surprises from reading it. 

Blood Will Tell was my least favorite, partly because Archie's pique didn't seem to have any solid basis and I often felt like I was concentrating too much on trying to figure that out. I did still find a lot to like in the wording and imagery, though. Even without knowing who Archie means, you can still get a good sense of a particular character when he says, "Fougere grinned. I had decided the first time he grinned that I would never grin again." It would have been easy to describe the grin, but instead Stout gives us this menacing glimpse and lets us determine what it means. I do recommend this book, but then so far there isn't any Nero Wolfe book I wouldn't recommend. Read them all.
