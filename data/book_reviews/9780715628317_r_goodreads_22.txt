Rating: 4/5

Another fabulous book from a very talented writer. The Master Georgie of the title is George Hardy, a medical doctor and surgeon, and an amateur photographer. The medium of photography is the framework the novel is hung upon – six photographic plates, each represent a chapter, the early ones taken in Liverpool in the 1840’s and the latter in the 1850’s in the Crimea where Georgie is serving as a battlefield surgeon.
The story is told from the perspective of three characters: Myrtle, a foundling brought up as Georgie’s adoptive sister; Dr Potter, married to Georgie’s sister and an amateur geologist and Pompey Jones, a street urchin turned photographer’s assistant. The three follow Georgie from Liverpool to the Crimea. 
Georgie is a complex character, attracted to both men and women but also a dedicated and determined surgeon. Myrtle, Potter and Jones each try to get close to Georgie as the story moves from Liverpool to the Crimea. 
This is a complex and multi-layered novel that defies simple summary. It does not shy away from the horrors and futility of war; not does it follow a simple or clear path to victory or defeat – mainly it shows the messiness and disorder of war, where soldiers seem to die as easily from disease in camp as they do on the battlefield. 
My description doesn’t really do justice to what was a critically acclaimed book when published in 1998 and is still worth reading now.
