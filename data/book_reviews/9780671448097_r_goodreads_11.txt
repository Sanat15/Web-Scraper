Rating: 4/5

What a strange little book.

Puffball tells the story of Richard and Liffey, a young couple who moves to a countryside cottage to escape the hustle and bustle of London, which immediately causes strain on their relationship. They (or, Liffey) want what all young couples want: a baby. In comes Mabs, their new rough-and-tumble countryside neighbour who also dabbles in witchcraft when she has the wherewithal to take her nose out of the village's business. 

The story that follows is written in a unique, jumpy, erratic manner. It flits around, following strings of thought that change on a dime and perspectives of characters changing three or four times a page. Apart from Liffey, Richard, Mabs, and her husband Tucker, there are a number of smaller side characters who introduce temptation or stoke discontent within the unhappy couple: Bella and Ray, the older sophisticated married couple in the city who thrive on infidelity, Helen and Mory, Liffey's friends subletting her London apartment with nefarious intent, Ms. Martin, Richard's dumpy naive assistant who might be offer more than meets the eye, and so on and so forth.

Between all the characters and the hijinx they find themselves in, the story is broken up by what reads like snippets from my university Developmental Biology textbook, as we learn what happens inside a woman's body once sperm fertilizes an egg. It's very detailed!

All of this, the relationships, the affairs, parenting and pregnancy and magic spells and, of course, puffballs, are found under the shadow of Glastonbury Tor, the iconic hill in the English countryside that adds a sense of myth and folk horror to the tale. Weldon mixes the mystical with dark humour, genuine horror and some truly shocking "mean girl" behaviour to create this one-of-a-kind hidden gem. This is my first read by Fay Weldon but I'm looking forward to reading more.
