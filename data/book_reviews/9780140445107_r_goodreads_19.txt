Rating: 3/5

At times, there is some good knowledge to be found here, especially when Aristotle waxes poetic about friendship, virtue, and character. At other times, I was reminded of how foundational this work is; each argument makes sense because that is just how we do things in our society. But much of the time, this felt like I was reading the dictionary. Words are introduced, definitions are given, and then pages of bullet point examples from antiquity are given. Maybe that’s cool for a specific reader, but as someone reading this casually for fun, this felt like running straight into a brick wall.

 The Oxford edition, as always, is fantastic. The lengthy introduction by Harvey Yunis is clear, direct, and easy to follow. The end notes are extensive, detailing the many classical allusions. If I ever come back to this, for whatever reason, I’d probably end up spending more time in the introduction and then just reviewing a few key passages.
