Rating: 4/5

The author is quoted as saying this novel scared people because most don't understand it. I’m one of them, but that's not all bad. 

I didn’t completely understand all the complex and interconnecting themes - love, black rage, white guilt, infidelity, envy, homosexuality, comparisons of sexual and racial difference, regret, racism in both its obvious and hidden forms, I could go on - but I understood enough to see this as a powerhouse novel. I can equally see how it would have caused waves in the early 1960s.

It’s heavy stuff and Baldwin takes so much time and text to lead us, sometimes not gently, into the lives, minds and troubles of his characters, often a little too far that a reader, like me, can feel overwhelmed: 
‘Then he turned back into the room, pale with assessments, with guilt deliciously beginning to gnaw at the rope with which he had tied it, sharpening its teeth for him. And yet, at the same time, he felt radiantly, wonderfully spent.’

I’ve read ‘Another Country’ was published at a pivotal time in America’s racial discourse, but I was also interested to read that it helped raise the ugly head of racism here in Australia, where the importing of it was initially banned. Our Censorship Board in the early 1960s not only questioned its ‘indecency’ but also how the novel’s racist messaging could create comparisons to race relations occurring in Australia at that time. To avoid damaging Australia’s reputation (presumedly that meant our emerging multi-culturalism?), a partial ban succeeded so that the book be available to the ‘serious-minded student or reader’. Ah-ha… 

4.5 stars.
