Rating: 4/5

A fascinating book, published in 1977 - well researched and extremely well written. Deighton covers the summer of 1940, the famous Battle of Britain. 
The book is in separate sections, covering the strategy, people, weapons, tactics in a thorough manner. Also the development of the aircraft, organization of the air forces, and the advance of radar and other technologies. Here Deighton does a good job of providing the background to the Battle also comparing the aircraft involved and the personalities of those involved at the top level
No room for romanticism, he covers factually how the Battle of Britain was won and lost and exposes the blunders and incompetence that led to victory.
One downside in the paperback version, the detailed maps were too small to get a sense of the operational scale.
Not quite as unbiased as Deighton wound want the reader to believe, he clearly wasn't a fan of Churchill, Leigh-Mallory or Keith Park. 
What did shine through was the unfathomable bravery of the pilots and crew on both sides, many of the RAF pilots with very limited flying experience, rushed into service. Many astonishing first hand accounts, from both sides, are included in the book.
I remember watching the 1969 film and am now struck, after reading Deighton's research, on how accurate it actually was.
