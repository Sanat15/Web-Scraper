Rating: 4/5

There is a lot of truth in this book: truth about husbands and wives, mothers and fathers, country folk and townspeople, the West Country and London. The inner strength a new mother finds is also central to the plot, although one wonders at the end why Liffey, the central, compliant character, would ever want to see any of the other characters again. Her compliance becomes adaptablity, her indifference (or is it openness?) rewards her with everything she thought she wanted. The question the book leaves us with is: could she have changed others if she had tried? Or must we learn to accept their terrible actions and therein find our own happiness?

“No one has a baby alone. Every pregnant woman carries with her the aspirations, the ambitions and the fears of others - friends, relatives, and passers-by - and good and ill wishes of such intensity as might put the sun right out.”
