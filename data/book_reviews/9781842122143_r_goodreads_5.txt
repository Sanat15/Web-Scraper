Rating: 3/5

Smith, an American news correspondent based in Berlin, describes the conditions in Germany during Hitler’s rise to power and the first two years of WWII. The civilian government was in chaos. Production of everything dropped, as food, clothing (as in “voluntary” collection of warm clothes) and all supplies went to the Russian front. Rumors were rife, propaganda and lies everywhere, and morale declined into deep depression. Most ordinary Germans did not want war, remembering too vividly the previous one. News was highly censored, even for foreign correspondents, some of whom disappeared without a trace. Smith left Germany in Dec, 1941, after being secretly warned it was in his best interest.
	Several chapters toward the end were particularly interesting as Smith described aspects of society, Nazi takeover of education, and the military (including the old, rigid army vs the young, opportunistic SS). My only quibble with the book was that Smith rarely provided dates (e.g. “that April”) so it was difficult to match events he described with the little I remember from history class. A useful book for anyone researching this particular aspect of the war.
