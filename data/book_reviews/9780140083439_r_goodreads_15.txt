Rating: 3/5

I am always up for a good Holmes crossover... real or fictional are both fun. Houdini seemed like it should be a great choice. There was something about the book that just fell flat for me. Perhaps the simplicity of the mystery. I'm not usually all that good at beating the characters in the book to the answer, but I had this one less than 1/2 way though. 

I love the author started with the 'I found these pages that Dr. Watson wrote' and went all in portraying the Great Detective as real, but he was trying way to hard to prove he knew his Holmes lore. His Watson fit in with the idea of it being written when he was very old, but that made it a bit ponderous as far as descriptions go.

The biggest thing though, was the plane chase. I don't think the planes of 1910 were nearly that good, and it just didn't fit with Holmes. 

As far as Holmes himself, he was written, to me, alot like the Benedict Cumberbach version, which was a bit odd considering it was written in the 80s. He was too cordial with Lestrade, but setting the book when Holmes and Watson are old men, it makes sense they would be a bit less adversarial (at least for a while).

Overall, not the worst Holmes pastiche I've read, but definitely not the best.
