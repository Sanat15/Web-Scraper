This collection is the 1977 follow up to the earlier books (and exhibitions) The Family of Man and The Family of Woman, curated by Edward Steichen. Actually I'm not sure if Steichen was involved in The Family of Woman or if there was an exhibition associated with that collection; this volume is dedicated to him.

The photographs are arranged in themes ranging from stage of life (infancy, etc) to universal exeriences (joy, suffering etc). There are beautiful quotes that touch on each theme, mostly from poets/writers like Yeats, Blake, Auden, Roethke. The only women I recall seeing here are Dickinson and Jean Rhys. Oh, Gertrude Stein. There are Native American expressions throughout, and Bible excerpts, but I'm not sure I saw any other ethnicities or cultures represented-- this is jarring to the 2022 reader. The photos are far more inclusive, from countries and cultures all over the world. There are more USA photos than any other country, but the representation is more balanced. I noticed that while almost all countries are identified only by nation, there were a few entitled Harlem. Not sure what to make of that. Photographers represented include Mary Ellen Mark, Emmet Gowin, W. Eugene Smith and a host of names I do not recognize-- some so perceptive and/or beautiful that I will look them up to see more work. Few photographers have more than one entry, maybe two. One other note is that Mason says this book was made in conjunction with the 1977 Unicef Year of the Child. It is sobering to think how far the world has to go to make life sweet for most children. 

I've written and said this more times than anyone cares to hear, but there is something so immersive about spending time with a book of images-- photos, paintings-- it can't compare to the laptop screen.
