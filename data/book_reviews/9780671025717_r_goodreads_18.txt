My review from my prior GR account: 

“It was my conviction,” wrote Jeffrey Masson, “that what Freud had uncovered in 1896—that, in many instances, children are the victims of sexual violence and abuse within their own families—became such a liability that he literally had to banish it from his consciousness.” Thus the expediency of the Oedipal Theory, which postulated that the sexual abuse often recounted by Freud’s patients was merely an unconscious fantasy of the child, a sexual longing for the opposite sex parent that typified a stage of psychological development. In other words, the abuse was not real; it never happened. “It was a comforting view for society, for Freud’s interpretation—that the sexual violence that so affected the lives of his women [often Jewish] patients was nothing but a fantasy—posed no threat to the existing social order.”

Masson was a trained psychoanalyst and a Freud archivist, and much of his work evidences this fact: he covers the correspondence of Freud and his compatriots, notably Wilhelm Fliess and Sandor Ferenczi (both Jews). Fliess was a close mentor to Freud, so close that Ferenczi would later allege that the two had a homosexual relationship. The two often discussed the role adverse sexual experiences played in the etiology of neuroses, with Fliess inclining Freud to abandon his seduction theory in favor of the Oedipal Complex, i.e., that the patients’ confessions of sexual abuse were hysterical fantasies not to be trusted. Fliess’s disfavorable view of the seduction theory was, Masson suggests, likely related to the fact that he had sexually abused his own son, Robert Fliess. Thus, we see how the sociology of the scientists influenced their science—their Jewish science.
