Rating: 4/5

Though I had a pretty good idea the general direction this was going to go and the high spots of the plot, there was enough nuance and dread that I very much enjoyed this. It got that power is at the heart of a lot of horror. 

The book tells the tale of a town that is too perfect that seems to good to be true and people realize that, yet they go along with the corporate overlords to get along. Plus the adults' kids are getting their 98 pound weakling kids turned into strapping lads who become stars of the football field in a matter of weeks. 

What's not to like about that?

Turns out, if you've got half a brain, quite a bit. And if you don't have half a brain, an affliction from which a lot of the townsfolk of Silverdale suffer. 

I liked this because it dealt with corporate power and the horrors it can inflict upon us, dismissal of those who question authority, the real psychological power of gaslighting, all with a bit of science-based body horror. Though some of the body horror elements are pretty fantastical, the dismissal of women's concerns, the greed for sports excellence so many fathers have and place vicariously upon their sons, all that is very credible. 

Cuz that happens. 

A lot. 

Like every damn day. 

The book wasn't perfect and while there were parts of the end that pleasantly surprised me, there wasn't a whole lot of suspense as to figuring out what Point A was, how Point B followed, and what Point C was more or less going to look like.

But still. Very enjoyable. Not every book needs a blind-side twist.
