Rating: 2/5

2.5 stars. This is a pretty straightforward mystery, as it says on the tin: Roger has been asked to take a young woman who is to be married to a cousin to said cousin's home. There is already some wariness, because the cousin did not show up, as promised, to escort his bride himself. Matters are only made worse when they arrive at the Gildersleeve residence and discover that elder brother Peter has been missing for 3 days. Soon after Roger arrives and agrees to try to find Peter, younger brother Mark also disappears. 

Roger follows a long and circuitious route to try to figure out what Peter and Mark were looking for as a bead on discovering where they may be now. The village is already whispering that they might be practitioners in the Dark Arts, or have been swept away by the Devil, which is only making things worse for their poor widowed mother and this 16-year-old headstrong girl.

Roger himself is a bit of an idiot, and given that this is told from his first person POV, it was a bit grating to be along for the ride when he's spilling his guts to the wrong person and is completely, blissfully unaware of it. I don't have much patience with narrators who are completely oblivious, and the fact that his long and winding road takes him on basically a wild goose chase for the Holy Grail was just too much for my suspension of disbelief.

This is book #7 in the series but this one stands alone very nicely, so far as I can tell. I'd be willing to try another in this series before writing it off completely, but will likely not do that anytime soon. Once again I thank the good graces to have access to a wonderful library system!
