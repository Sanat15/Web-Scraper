Rating: 4/5

"No, the man of the house and the woman of the house have to be equal with one another. If not, their relation becomes a form of exploitation."

This was nothing like I was expecting, firstly, there are no descriptions, it's all dialogue, but that didn't bug me, because you learn everything you need to know anyway.

Secondly, you get to experience different movies as the characters tell them to each other which is pretty fun. I'll have to watch the ones I haven't seen at some point this year. 

Though, quite a big chunk of the book is movies being described word for word so if you don't want to be spoiled, you could probably skim through them and read the rest of the story properly.

There's also a bunch of footnotes, which I have to admit I mostly skipped. They were basically about the medical reasons why someone might be gay, but were very outdated and I just didn't want to put myself through that. But you are welcome to, if you want.

Now, I really enjoyed the subtext in this novel, and wanted to know more about Valentin's comrades, so I wish the ending turned out differently. It was romantic, in a way, when you think about it. But also really sad. 

I don't know if I'd ever reread this, but it was good, I'm looking forward to the movie now.
