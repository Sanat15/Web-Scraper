Rating: 3/5

I finished “Barbarians to Bureaucrats” by Lawrence M. Miller (1989). This was recommended by Patrick Bet David in an interview (I think with Dave Rubin). I couldn’t find it at the library so I ordered a cheap used copy off of Amazon, thinking that PBD’s recommendation might cause the book to get snapped up en masse and the price to then climb if I didn’t act. When it arrived, I made the mistake of literally judging a book by its cover: the cover design looks like something that a 90’s CEO would read and that wouldn’t appeal to anyone else. I was pleasantly surprised, then, to see that it was actually a pretty good read.

The book kind of reminded me of “The Fourth Turning” in that it proposes that businesses have different phases of ‘life’ and that different types of people/archetypes are suited for running the business at those different phases. The book is also a warning that different phases of business tend to breed certain types of managers that might not be good for the business’s future. I don’t run a business or have any management experience, but I still found it interesting to explore different personality types. The book also has suggestions for how to work for each personality type and how to let them work for you. 

I think it’s a testament to the writing ability of the author that I could stay focused on a topic that isn’t especially relevant or important to me. The use of anecdotes and stories about the rise and fall of various companies was enjoyable to me, even for their own sake, so attaching those stories to lessons about how to best manage people and businesses was a bonus.
