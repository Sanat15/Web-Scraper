Rating: 5/5

There are essays and thinkpieces aplenty for Ramona Quimby, as there should be, but where are my 10,000 words of meta about Leigh Botts?? I love him. This one and its sequel, Strider, are the most YA of the Cleary books I've read and they're really good. As usual, Cleary nails the minutia of a kid's hopes and worries. 

Some observations from grown-up me:
- I always felt this one was more modern than the Ramona books, despite mentions of typewriters and the kid writing this author via snail mail instead of Googling info about him for the book report. (To be fair, Google didn't exist when I read it either.) The backstory about his mom and how her parents didn't support her getting an ~education so she basically married to get out... definitely dates this, though.
- Boyd Henshaw is both a saint for putting up with this annoying kid giving him shit via letters and kind of a thoughtless dick. Leigh TOLD you he was poor!!! He doesn't even own the books by his favorite author, he has to check them repeatedly out of the library! Why didn't Mr. Henshaw send him a bunch of signed copies of his books or a notebook for the diary or something! It would have cost him almost nothing!!
- But also the way Leigh randomly ambushes his favorite author with letters in between breaks of writing to the pretend-author/diary is very funny to think about from Mr. Henshaw's end. Like this guy is living his life, going about his business and just randomly keeps getting these proto-Facebook updates from this random child he humored once or twice. X years later, did Mr. Henshaw ever think, oh, yeah, what happened to that weird kid who kept writing me all those letters??
- Speaking of the adults, the deadbeat dad was even more of a lousy deadbeat to me this time around. Holy shit - the phone call where Leigh overhears him taking some other kid to a pizza parlor was crushing. And the mom? Working jobs and going to school and still making sure she's there for her kid? Having standards and deciding to GTFO out of a marriage she was unhappy with? She's fantastic. I hope Bonnie finds a dude (or a lady) who appreciates her, she's the true MVP of these books. All of that was very deftly done; Leigh's writing from his limited perspective but you get plenty of story around the edges about his parents anyway.
- Leigh realizing at the end of the book that he can't rely on his father to keep his promises was heartbreaking. He's ELEVEN. 
- I can't believe multiple children were having their lunches stolen and none of the staff did anything about it. The janitor guy knew it was going down and all he did was go 🤷‍♂️ shit happens. NO IT DOESN'T, MR. FRIDLEY, WHAT IN THE WORLD.
- (Was HE the lunch thief?!?!?! 2021 is where I have headcanons about children's books published in 1983 I guess.)
- I am not sure how I feel about the "writing advice" at the end! Writing horrible derivative crap is just what baby authors do, it's how you eventually muddle your way to your own voice and ideas. I don't think it was great to discourage Leigh (and those other kids) from writing fiction, although I understand the point trying to be made there. On the other hand he's never going to go through his room 10 years later and discover poorly written Mary Sue fanfic for a book he really liked when he was eight, NOT THAT I WOULD KNOW WHAT THAT'S LIKE . . . . .

Audiobook: Read by Pedro Pascal and, assuming it's that Pedro Pascal, is 100% why I checked this out of the library, not going to lie. He's good but also he's narrating as a sixth-grader here, so like, DON'T JUDGE ME. Since this book is 100% in the form of letters or the narrator's diary the direction to not go wild with voices was a good one. Pascal managed to capture the often petulant, young kid tone the earlier letters/entries take. Very solid. One minus is that I'm pretty sure this was originally stitched together as multiple CDs and so there was a weird musical break in the middle, presumably through the CD change, with jaunty elevator type music, but... it kept GOING through the start of a chapter, and that chapter was Leigh being really upset about something. The jaunty happy music blaring over the top of the narration was distracting and ill-timed, plus it was hard to hear the narration because the music was so loud. 👎
