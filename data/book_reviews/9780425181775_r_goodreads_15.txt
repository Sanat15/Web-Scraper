Rating: 4/5

This is a very well written story of a newly married couple, Emily and Georges, both of whom make unwise choices after their rather sudden nuptials (those nuptials being a rather unwise choice to begin with). The ramifications of their individual mistakes are disparate. Only Georges seems likely to reverse what he’s done. While it’s possible to correct neglect, it’s impossible to undo infidelity. 

In his neglect Georges is very much like Middlemarch’s Casaubon, leaving his new wife repeatedly in pursuit of his great calling. Unlike Eliot’s Dorothea, Emily doesn’t put up with neglect for quite as long. She initially complains when he eschews her company in favor of his mother’s. But Emily quickly casts off her interest in being George’s wife and seeks fulfillment elsewhere.

Throughout the novel Emily’s chronic irresponsibility is rampant, impulsive, and selfish. I found myself surprised whenever she behaved in an outward-facing manner. Miller writes her with respect and understanding, so the reader senses what drives her. That doesn’t make her any easier to like. For a woman supposedly driven to grasp her purpose in life, she seems simply to have turned her back on every consequence of her decisions in order to do very little with her life. 

But this book reads like a classic. One is left with questions about how the future will unfold for the people Emily has touched. I found the story compelling
