Who Rides with Wyatt, Will Henry (western, historical fiction)
Jeff Book Review #304

1955's "Who Rides With Wyatt" is written as an interview with an old-timer who is relating the story of Tombstone to us, centered on Johnny Ringo's and Wyatt Earp's mentor/understudy relationship (which I don't think actually occurred) and then historical events up to the gunfight at the OK Corral and its repercussions, with folks like Doc Holliday, Curly Bill, and the Earp brothers. 

The author Will Henry, who also used a few other pseudonyms, wrote a whole lot of westerns and won five Spur awards (I believe that's second-most all-time, next to Kelton's seven) and this is the first of his books I've attempted. His writing style takes some getting used to; for me it comes across at times a bit blocky and just doesn't flow well from one word to the next. It takes more effort to translate words into a story - I'm not sure how else to say it.

"Who Rides with Wyatt" is also perhaps a victim of its timing. It was written in 1955 and a lot of its Tombstone facts are not very accurate but some are, which put it in that no-man's land between being a historical tale or an alternate-history tale, so believability makes it hard to accept at times. I also need to note that "Warlock," the alternate-history version of the Tombstone tale written only three years later in 1958 by Oakley Hall, one of the best westerns ever written, is just such a better read. I couldn't help but compare them as I read it.

Verdict: Not a good novel in my opinion. Hard to read, too much logistical legal wrangling, exposition, odd phrasing, action sequences mostly free of any tension or stakes; almost like it is caught between trying to be a textbook oral history or a thrilling POV narrative and failing to do either. Will Henry has more well-regarded content out there and I'm guessing this one just isn't up to his usual standard.

Jeff's Rating: 2 / 5 (Okay)
movie rating if made into a movie: PG-13
