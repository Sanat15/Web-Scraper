Rating: 4/5

Let's get the two things out of the way that didn't work for me. One, an earl's 2nd son is the honourable, not lord anything. Pretty simple to research this I would think, or to pick up at the copy-editing stage. Secondly, some of the dialogue and plotting owed a little too much to Georgette Heyer's Faro's Daughter. But other than that, a delightful book. Lady Diana is almost too good to be true, intellectual, independent, charming, with the gift of bringing out the best in everyone she meets. 

But it's the hero especially who is kind and extremely bright, two characteristics that only are only seen by his closest male friends from public school and college. The two protags brings out the best in each other. 

Fair warning, there's an adorable caustic parrot and a darling puppy (le chien just comes on the scene at the end) so if you don't like pets, be warned. I suppose if you don't like mistresses having a place and a voice, you might not like this book either but ballerina Suzette de Charenton is almost a caricature of a passionate yet dispassionate Frenchwoman and there's never a feeling of betrayal by anyone. 

I just liked it.
