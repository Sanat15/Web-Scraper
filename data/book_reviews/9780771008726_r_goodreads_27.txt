What an incredibly interesting, well-written and comprehensive guide to Canadian Literature! It's the best possible introduction to the subject; Atwood knows what she's talking about, and using her dark humor, wit and vast knowledge of Canadian literature she wrote this very useful guide.

In Survival, Atwood argues that the central theme of Canadian literature is "survival" - the struggle to endure harsh environments, both natural and social, which shapes a distinct national identity. She presents the four "victim positions": 1. Denial of being a victim, 2. Acceptance of victimhood as a matter of fate, 3. Repudiation of the victim role, and 4. Creative Non-Victimhood. Atwood highlights the recurring figure of the “victim-heroine,” who navigates adversity and may emerge resilient or remain trapped in vulnerability. 

By connecting literature to Canada’s geography and history, Atwood distinguishes Canadian writing from other traditions, establishing a cultural conscience and offering a “geography of the mind” that maps the nation’s literary and psychological landscape. Though some aspects of Survival are now dated, it remains a fundamental work in understanding Canadian literature and identity.

Would highly recommend for those interested in broadening their knowledge of Canadian literature, culture and history.
