Rating: 5/5

Katherine Kurtz continues to amaze me in her writing. She not only tells exciting political and religious stories, but she has fantastic twists and surprises that keep me on the edge of my seat.

It's particularly impressive that Kurtz doesn't rely on action in her books. In fact, depsite being this major book featuring three armies potentially clashing, Kurtz doesn't include battle sequences. Instead, most of the scenes in this book are characters in a room talking, and it's absolutely delightful to read.

The characters of Morgan, Duncan, and Kelson continue to be most excellent in their characterizations. Morgan in particular gets a potential love-interest in this book that adds a new dynamic to his character. We also get to see the POVs of some other characters here, including Sean Lord Derry, Arilan, and Bran Coris. 

The moral dilemma of church vs government over the issue of magic is now in its third book. You would think that theme would be played out by now. However, Kurtz continues to make it fresh and interesting and ask tough questions. She throws in a lot of themes about prejudice here that add a lot ot the story. 

There is a sequence in the middle where Kelson tries to convert someone to his side, and that scene alone was just spectacularly done. Kurtz really mastered a reluctant character to a convert there. There's also a particular scene at the end, featuring a duel between 8 characters that was so shocking and well done I'm sure I had my mouth open as I read it. 

Overall, this book is absolutely spectacular. Kurtz has cemented herself among the best Fantasy writers. I'd venture to say this series is now somewhere in my top 3-5 fantasy series of all time. If the rest of the Deryni series is this good, it could seriously rival my top spot. 

But for this specific book, I think it's the best of the trilogy and gets a pure 10 out of 10! Well done Kurtz!
