Rating: 5/5

I confess to being less than objective when it comes to this book: it was perhaps the first SF book I fell in love with (I read it when I was about 9), and it influences me to this day.
Imagine Star Trek...but in a hospital. And with actual ALIENS, not human actors with a bit of putty on their faces, but really different ALIENS, beings utterly unlike us. Sounds fun, but how do you run a complex institution when many of the races breathe completely different air? Or when some are as tiny as a pencil where others are larger than the hospital itself? Or (and this is one that comes up in the book) when the doctor resembles a vicious predator that preys on the patient's species? 
It's much more than just dry questions of management...this has a pretty epic story. Lioren, who is furry and has a bunch of eyes, is a brilliant doctor who just wants to do the right thing. He makes a mistake in a moment of desperation...and the consequences are catastrophic. Not quite able to bring an end to himself, Lioren is demoted, and struggles to find purpose, and to atone for his sins. Yes, that's right, it's also a pretty empowering tale of overcoming depression and finding friendship in the oddest of places (some of the chums Lioren meets in the cafeteria include an empathic insect and a beaver-like creature who is incapable of lying). It may not be the most celebrated of SF nor have the most astounding of ideas (and I see most rate it pretty low even in the Sector General series to which it belongs) but I love it, and recommend it heartily.
