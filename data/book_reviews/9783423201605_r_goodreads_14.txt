Rating: 1/5

John Tyerman William's started his journey with Pooh and the Philosophers asking one simple question: if I take a wonderful work of art for children and partner it with overly intellectual language, can I make my reader feel smart enough to believe I've added something to this world through my writing? Sadly, through 212 pages, editors, publishers, and the public eye, he never was able to accept that the answer to that question is no. 

It all felt like an episode of Film Theory if they were really aggressively trying to gate keep anyone who hadn't had the privilege of higher ed. 

Not to mention, possibly the most relevant connection that the Pooh books have to modern day western philosophy is absurdity, specifically in relation to Camus' thoughts; which is only dedicated two pages of the book. I feel robbed.

As quoted by Williams' himself on page 93, "If we take in our hand any volume; of divinity, or school of metaphysics, for instance, let us ask, does it contain any abstract reasoning concerning quantity or number? no. Does it contain any experimental reasoning concerning matter of fact and existence? no. COMMIT IT THEN TO THE FLAMES: for it can contain nothing but sophistry and illusion." - David Hume
