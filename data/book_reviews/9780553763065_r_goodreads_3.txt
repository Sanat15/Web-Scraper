Rating: 2/5

I never thought I would ever give a Rex Stout Nero Wolfe book a rating below four stars (usually five) but this is the only one. 

It is not the plot, which is interesting and complex, and takes place at the WV iconic hotel/spa, The Greenbrier but using a different identifying name. (Having been to the resort several times, I give Stout credit for describing it perfectly). Wolfe is an honored guest at a gathering of a famous group of world famous chefs. Of course, a murder takes place which only Wolfe can solve.

My big problem with this book is the rampant racist slurs that occur every other page. The waiters and maids at the resort are African-American and are described as rather slow-witted although they are good employees. To top it off, Polish and Italians also are targets. I kept trying to remember that this book was written in 1938 and many books of that time contained racial aspersions.. But need they be repeated time after time? It ruined the book for me and although I would recommend the other books in this enduring series, I would not recommend this one. I was very disappointed.
