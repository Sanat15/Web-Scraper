Rating: 3/5

50 Short Science Fiction Tales collects stories below 3000 words in length, most published in the early 1950s, with a few published in the 1940s. None of the stories is a "classic." It has a representative sample of big names from that era: Heinlein, Clarke, van Vogt, Sturgeon, Boucher, Brown, Kornbluth, etc. A few of the stories are one-offs by authors otherwise unknown or not really connected to SF. With the short short story, one is likely to get two kinds: fables and jokes. This collection has about an even split of both. In essence, each is a "punchline" story, so there is no significant development of the usual aspects of fiction. Some stories are clearly outdated. For my favorite in the collection I am selecting C.M. Kornbluth's "The Altar at Midnight." My least favorite would be Will Stanton's "Barney." These are relative. There are no outright stinkers in the collection. It is all quite enjoyable and entertaining, but not more than that.
