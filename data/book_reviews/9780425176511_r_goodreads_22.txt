Rating: 4/5

This is another brilliantly written murder mystery story by Agatha Christie. The plot is well structured, and the mind-blowing twist at the end quite shocks the readers. I'm truly impressed by the craftsmanship of Agatha Christie in this particular story. She has a great ability to lead the plot without giving any hint or clue as to the guilty party until the very end. In this case, it was quite a shocking revelation. 

Poirot works alone in this case without Mr. Hastings, but a new acquaintance partly helps him with the investigation. This is one of the mysteries where the genius mind of Poirot is greatly tested and his "little grey cells" challenged. But Poirot comes out being the winner of the day, apprehending the culprit and dazing the readers with his astonishing discovery. At the same time, Poirot shows some consideration for the criminal from being exposed and humiliated. The great detective is both brilliant and humane.

Christie's choice of the narrator is fascinating. I don't know what made her choose so, but it's quite an innovative move. It worked wonderfully at the end when the truth was revealed, which truly shocked me. I was like, "What on earth just happened?" :)

This was one intriguing and interesting read. I enjoyed every minute of it. I missed Hastings, but there was a substitute to keep enough humour. :) There is no doubt in my mind that The Murder of Roger Ackroyd is one of the best murder mysteries in the Poirot series.
