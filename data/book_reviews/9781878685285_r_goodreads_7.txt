Rating: 3/5

It's... a story.

Given that the titular "character"—the friendly snowflake—lives and "dies" entirely within a few seconds at the start of the book, it's sort of a stretch to make this into more of a story than that. Yet here we are: Jenny has an idea in her head that, of a handful of snowflakes that appears in the field, *one* specifically finds her nose and melts on it. She thinks not only must that snowflake have been a friendly one, it was a *male* snowflake named Harry.

The rest of the book deals with faith-based philosophical musings on, "Could a snowflake be friendly?" (Her brother Dennis so vehemently denies it as to come across as specifically antagonistic toward Jenny, which is jarring given the otherwise wholesome tone of the story.) There are some good ideas presented, like how water evaporating and becoming clouds to become rain or snow (or hail?) and melting and evaporating again is like reincarnation, but it just so slightly doesn't sit well with me that I end up feeling mixed about the whole thing. 

It's a little odd to note in the introduction that the author mentions not knowing how to talk to children, and yet his son illustrated the book. That sort of gives me pause, that any parent wouldn't know how to talk to children, barring extreme circumstances (involuntarily kept away while the child was both talkative and a child, which seems unlikely in this case). So that may have unfairly hindered my opinion of the book, though the story itself doesn't help much.

It's not a BAD story, it just reeks of one of those that has an idea but doesn't know how to execute it. Probably a good story for Christians, but also just secular enough for non-Christians to enjoy, too.
