Rating: 5/5

The question is, what exactly is it about death that instills such fear within us? Is it the idea of everything ending forever? Or is it the unsettling notion that we don't truly know what lies beyond? Perhaps it's the fear of ceasing to exist entirely and being forgotten in a world that can be so cruel and indifferent. This novel raises a multitude of questions, challenging the reader to reflect deeply.

Because what is death without the experience of life? And conversely, what is life without the shadow of death? These two states are intertwined; they are inextricably connected and, in essence, are two sides of the same coin. To have truly lived, one must confront and understand death; to have died, one must have experienced life fully. The narrative also probes the concept of time itself, how it inexorably passes so swiftly that we are often left contemplating our inevitable end, the final curtain closing on the stage of our existence.

This is the core of what this book explores: Death, Life, and Time. All the characters are engaged in struggles against one of these elements (or sometimes all three), and their stories reveal just how interconnected these aspects are.

There is Clarissa, my beloved Clarissa, who embodies a zest for life that is contagious. Yet, she also harbors a secret longing for death. But does she truly wish to die? Or is it simply the allure of the unknown (the mystery) that seduces her? Meanwhile, she finds life itself incredibly compelling and beautiful. She is undeniably a fascinating character; immersing myself in her perspective was always captivating.

Then there is Septimus, who is consumed by thoughts of death and plagued by fears of it. He yearns to hold onto life, yet death seems to have a firm grip on him and refuses to let go. His suffering is intense, ongoing, and relentless. Despite his pain, he wishes only to continue living, if only death would release him from its hold.

Lastly, there is Peter, who is engaged in a relentless battle with time. He is rooted in the past, scarcely able to find fulfillment in the present, where disappointment often prevails. He refuses to admit his feelings openly (preferring to judge others harshly), but the story gradually reveals how deeply he longs for the past and how much he believes he deserves better. Honestly, I found myself unable to like him and cared little for his point of view.

In conclusion, this was an outstanding piece of literature. I am delighted to have read a book by Virginia Woolf at last and to have gained insight into her remarkable works. Her ability to explore such profound themes with nuance and depth is truly impressive.
