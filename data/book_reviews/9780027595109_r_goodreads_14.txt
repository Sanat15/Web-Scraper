Rating: 4/5

I knew not what to expect. In fact, I was worried that my sentimental side, especially when it came to the animal world, wouldn't hold up. Nevertheless, it was precisely because I was forewarned that I made sure to keep my over-emotions in check when approaching this novel—even though I read it mainly with my brow furrowed in worry about the dogs' cruel fate.
And yet! What I feared would be an ordeal turned into an incredible pleasure of reading and discovering a world I knew practically nothing about. Jack London doesn't wallow in sentimentality; he tells a story. He describes it with the restraint and intelligence of a true storyteller, alternately placing his narrative on the plane of human psychology. That of animal psychology, emphasizing the reasons for each, their aspirations for life are inevitably thwarted by their survival instinct.
This magnificent novel is classified as "children's literature."... It is up to us, adults of today, to continue the transmission by making works of such quality available to today's youth.
