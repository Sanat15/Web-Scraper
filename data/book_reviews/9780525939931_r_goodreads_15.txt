Rating: 3/5

I have read a couple of others in this series, but they were later ones. The premise in those is that Bernie Rhodenbarr has a used bookstore as a front for his actual occupation which is that of being a burglar. In this, apparently Bernie hadn't yet caught on to needing a front. I'm guessing he decided at some point that he needed to convince the cops that he had given up crime. 

Yet here we were with Bernie's same old problem. He was out breaking in and engaging in his true occupation when a dead body turns up. In fact, the murder happened while Bernie was hiding in the closet. Naturally he doesn't report the murder and makes a stealthy escape. 

Despite the crime aspect, these are fun - in fact, told in the first person with more than a trace of humor. I wouldn't ever want to have a steady diet of Bernie's escapades, but I'm glad to still have some to break up my more serious reading. Bernie Rhodenbarr is a solid 3-stars and I'm glad he's out there. However, for crime with a bit of humor, I actually prefer to turn to Rex Stout's Nero Wolfe, where the characterizations and interactions of Nero and Archie is why it even exists.
