Rating: 4/5

She Stoops to Conquer is a 1773 comedic play written at the end of the career of Anglo-Irish writer Oliver Goldsmith (1728–1774). The play takes place over a night and a day in Squire Hardcastle's country house, also inhabited by his wife, his daughter, a scamp of a young son Tony Lumpkin and Miss Neville, his niece. The Hardcastle’s hope to arrange two marriages: their son to Miss Neville when Tony comes of age and, the more imminent one, their daughter with the son of Hardcastle’s old friend, Sir Charles Marlow. 

Young Marlow along with his friend Hastings, who is also Miss Neville’s friend, is on his way to the Hardcastle house to meet the bride his father has chosen for him when, losing his way, he stops at a tavern where he is tricked into thinking the Hardcastle house is an inn. That begins a whole series of misunderstandings, deceptions and confusions that complicate the prospective matchups and the attitudes of the characters to one another. 

It all resulted in some very clever dialogue and scenes. The characters often portray their true thoughts in fairly witty asides aimed at the audience rather than other characters. Surprisingly, these worked well and enhanced the clever dialogue and well-paced activity. 

This was a pleasant surprise, especially after reading Oliver Goldsmith’s more tepid storytelling performance in the novel The Vicar of Wakefield. I rate this as 4 stars.
