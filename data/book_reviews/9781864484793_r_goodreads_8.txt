Rating: 3/5

an ought-to-read for students of Southeast Asian studies. this book gives a survey history of Southeast Asia, which is a daunting task since this region--to borrowed from Benedict Anderson's remark--is extremely fragmented like Western Europe. i don't find the writing style compelling at all, but it's fine if you want to grab a general understanding of the region. though you may benefit from Osborne's useful bibliography and suggested readings, i am irked by his serious dearth of in-text citations/footnotes. it made the impression that the writer pulled the information out of his memory instead of being in a conversation with previous authors and the readers. also, this dearth made it extremely hard to double check the presented data.

will return to this book and write an expanded review later.
