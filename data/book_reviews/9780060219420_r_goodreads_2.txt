Rating: 3/5

I picked up this book because it was mentioned in Jean Craighead George's autobiography, Journey Inward, and I wanted to learn more about Rocky Mountain goats.

3 stars for the goats - I did indeed learn a lot about these high mountain creature who are not actually goats! (they are more closely related to antelope)

But otherwise, there are some strange things going on in this book. First, a marriage of a 15-year-old and 17-year-old that is sanctioned by the boy's aunt even though the two families are in a blood feud? Creepy. Then there is the wise Indian trope in the character of Ignatius... except George seemed to use this character to advance the plot through idea that the spirit of the dead teen, Will, is in the billy goat, Old Gore. Weird and offensive. And most problematically, Marcus is a dolt of a teen outdoorsman who assumes his new wife will do all the cooking, cleaning, and homemaking, even though he left for a summer's work in the wilderness as a single dude. Melissa seems to exist only to help Marcus learn that the law of compensation isn't always true. 

Having read the autobiography, it's clear that George was working out her own frustrations at being treated as the supportive and serving wife of male naturalists rather than having her own career as a scientist. I would rather have read a book about a female scientist who pursued her passion. Where is the book about Melissa the brilliant botanist? 

I liked the anti-hunting theme, though. I just don't get hunting as sport. Hunting for food, yes, but sport? Not my jam.
