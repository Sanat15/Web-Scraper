Rating: 5/5

I've read a handful of Len Deighton books, and I wouldn't count myself as a big fan as such, but for me Spy Story stands head and shoulders above his other work. I'd count it in my top ten favourite novels.
The Studies Centre backdrop is a large part of the appeal. It's fascinating in its own right, and it lends a thick extra layer of authenticity. I also found it a lot easier to identify with the protagonist than in other Deighton works. He's outside the intelligence services (at this stage in his life), he doesn't really have any privileged knowledge, and he's as in the dark as the reader. Like an iceberg, the intelligence community machinations largely take place out of sight. No 'WOOC(P)' and the like.
The book has been rebadged as 'secret file number, #6', but I don't think that's helpful. It's best to approach Spy Story as a standalone work. Certainly, I wouldn't look at it as a 'Harry Palmer' novel. The Spy Story protagonist doesn't feel remotely like the character from IPCRESS et al, at least to me. He can handle himself, but he's basically a bit of an early seventies geek, who writes low level computer code and has a "Masters and all those other qualifications: maths and economics; potent mixture!" 
I could be wrong, but that doesn't sound like the earlier 'spy with no name' to me. And definitely not the Michael Caine version!
