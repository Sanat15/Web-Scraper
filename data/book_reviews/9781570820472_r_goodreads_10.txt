Rating: 3/5

Woodcarver Geppetto creates a puppet named Pinocchio, desiring him to become a real boy; the Blue Fairy animates him, assuring that he will become real if he demonstrates bravery, honesty, and selflessness. Jiminy Cricket is designated as his conscience, yet Pinocchio is easily misled by the cunning Honest John and Gideon, who entice him to skip school for Stromboli's puppet show, where he is imprisoned. After escaping, Pinocchio ventures to Pleasure Island, a place where disobedient boys are transformed into donkeys, imparting a grim lesson about consequences. Meanwhile, Geppetto, in search of Pinocchio, is swallowed by the enormous whale Monstro. In a display of courage, Pinocchio enters Monstro, discovers Geppetto, and guides them to safety, albeit at the cost of his own life. The Blue Fairy resurrects Pinocchio and grants him the status of a real boy due to his selfless love and bravery. The essence of the story conveys a clear moral dichotomy, illustrating how poor choices result in punishment, while virtuous actions yield rewards. Pinocchio's adventure reflects a child's journey toward maturity, emphasizing personal accountability and the importance of diligence. The relationship between Pinocchio and Geppetto propels the narrative forward, culminating in Pinocchio's ultimate act of selfless love, thereby satisfying the fairy's requirement. Characters such as Stromboli, Honest John, and the temptations of Pleasure Island symbolize worldly distractions, standing in contrast to Jiminy Cricket's wise counsel. Disney adapted the more somber and cynical elements of Collodi's original tale, transforming it into a universally relatable narrative about overcoming personal shortcomings.
