Rating: 4/5

Ancient Egypt has always fascinated me, and I absolutely loved River God by Wilbur Smith. It is an epic historical adventure filled with political intrigue, battles, romance, and unforgettable characters.

Taita, the narrator of the story, is a fascinating and unusual protagonist. He is certainly not a conventional hero: he is a slave and a eunuch, yet he possesses extraordinary intelligence, creativity, courage, and loyalty. He is also proud and occasionally vain, which only makes him more human. I particularly enjoy flawed characters, and Taita’s complexity makes him one of the greatest strengths of the novel.

I cannot judge the historical accuracy of the book, as I am not an expert on Ancient Egypt, but I read it as a work of fiction rather than a history lesson. What matters most here is Wilbur Smith’s ability to create a vivid and immersive world and to make the reader feel transported to another time.

The story itself is a grand adventure. After the ruthless invasion of Egypt by the Hyksos, the Egyptian court is forced into exile, traveling along the Nile while trying to survive and preserve their hopes of one day reclaiming their homeland. Years pass as they build a new life away from home, leading eventually to battles, political struggles, impossible loves, and the rise of a new generation.

At over 600 pages, River God is a true page-turner. Wilbur Smith’s storytelling makes the journey along the Nile feel like an unforgettable voyage, full of danger, discovery, and emotion. It is an incredibly enjoyable “road-trip” through Ancient Egypt, carried by a remarkable narrator and a captivating story.

Highly recommended.
