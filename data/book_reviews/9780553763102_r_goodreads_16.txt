Rating: 4/5

The Nero Wolfe series nearly always has some observations on developments in contemporary society. This one from 1961 has two, though neither is completely new. The first is that Wolfe uses an expensive ($1200!) hidden recording setup in his office to record the perp’s confession and deliver the tape to the police. The second has been mentioned in passing in at least half the previous 34 books, but now it takes center stage. It is the confiscatory Federal tax rate of 91%, which now becomes the motive for a kidnapping and double murder. The perp wants to deduct the casualty loss from the $500,000 ransom on their taxes, where it has an after-tax value of $455,000. That makes it “The Final Deduction”. 3.5 stars, rounded up.
