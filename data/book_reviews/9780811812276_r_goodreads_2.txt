Rating: 4/5

I've been reading about this book ever since I discovered Leavitt, back in October 1998. Eventually, I got hold of it!
A very enjoyable reading on Italy and Italians.
Sometimes a slight glimpse of stereotypes kick in, however it sheds a light on Italy in the early Nineties, with some very interesting excursus from past authors on Italy.
The chaotic Italian politics unfortunately is still there...
Few notes:
- “sagra”, p.35. In Tuscany, the sagra was mostly sponsored by the Communist Party, but elsewhere it was not always sponsored by the (now defunct) Italian Communist Party. There were, and still are, a variety of “sagre” all around Italy, mostly linked to local traditions (either historical or enogastronomic). 
- “pan d’oro”, p.70. Whilst this is the meaning, the way Italians spell this Xmas cake is “pandoro”, created by Mr. Melegatti in Verona at the end of 19th century.
- sometimes some spelling is mixed with Spanish, e.g. the TV program, “Perdonami” p.133, is actually written in its Spanish form, “Perdoname”.
