Rating: 3/5

“The Irredeemable Miss Renfield” is the third book, I believe, in Regina Scott’s Zebra books regency series. Overall, I’d divide this book into two sections - 1: a sort of long but humorous build up section that explores our hero and heroines character and flaws as Cleo and Les have a “pretend” romance and try to be as shocking to society as possible so Cleo can trick her family into granting her more freedom and 2: a quick but brilliant flurry of conflict and plots within plots leading to the happy end. While, as usual, Scott’s writing was brilliant and most of her characters and plots were lovely, I’ll admit to struggling to read this one a little… *i digress* it was just that the main male villain rather reminded me of the majority of most contemporary men (other than
some extreme wicked stuff towards the end) and in many stories would get the roll of being the hero; honestly I’ve seen far to many would be prospects for myself lost to that sort of riff raff, so it was as upsetting to read about here…though luckily here heroism and true romance win, so probably I was just born in the wrong century I guess. *end digression …mostly* and as is often the case with regency work, a lot of the plot consists of, to be blunt, the foolishness of the hero and heroine…there are many times when one wants to have a serious word with the characters …but yeah, the plot was taxing for those reasons, despite the humor. However, in the second section, the epic wrap up, I think it really came together. And as usual, I love how interconnected these books are-excited to read the next one now that I know who the heroine will be and the starting context. Upon mulling over a proper rating, I’d probably give it around 3.5 cursing parrots out of 5 and round down to 3 due to my personal tastes on this one, though definitely recommend it for fans of Scott and Regency in general.
