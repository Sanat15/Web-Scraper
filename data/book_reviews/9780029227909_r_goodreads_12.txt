#Read for class!

An exploration of how foreign policy makers/presidents have used history to make good and bad decisions. The authors propose the Known-Unclear-Presumed to Likeness-Difference test for people to compare analogies and see when historical events do and don't line up. I like this framework and think it would be helpful in my studies. However, I don't think this book was super engaging, and the authors lack humility in talking about their framework, which bugs me from a political science perspective. There is not always a simple solution for every issue, and the authors acknowledge this point, but it should be taken with some caution. Regardless, current policy makers today clearly don't use this advice, so I'm walking away a little more depressed than anything else. Imagine if people in power fought against their biases in favor of an analytical approach based in history and fact.
