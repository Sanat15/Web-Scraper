Rating: 4/5

Jeannie Main is the feisty twelve year old daughter in a family of Scottish fisher folk, out of a coastal town by the name Nairn. It is 1745 and Bonnie Prince Charles has decided to seek his father’s patrimony, with the aim of taking the crown from King George of England. The Highlanders are in revolt, Nairn is occupied by first one combatant force, then the other.
 
High adventure, danger, daring-do all take place within this very real historical context. Add to this the second sight of the gypsies, the magic of the Kelpie, prophesy . . . and the reader is taken on a wild ride. Jeannie Main is up to the task of finding and riding the Kelpie—she’s a delightful character, full of spunk and resourcefulness. Besides the historical events, the kind of work expected of children in 18th century Scotland is part of the story. And there is the exploration of the power of belief . . . all in all, a wonderful book for readers of young adult fiction, whatever the reader’s age.

It is obvious to any reader of history who happens to enjoy a bit of magic that author Frances Hendry not only loves her homeland, but also loves to tell its history as well as its legends and mythic creatures. She is engaging, and should entice any young reader with the historical detail, fast pace, and . . . magic. I like the way she used Scottish slang throughout the tale, with a fine glossary placed at the front of the book.

As Hendry writes in her postscript: “I have tried to be true to the spirit of the times, without going too deeply into the horrors, filth and casual brutality which we may nowadays find hard to imagine. I hope you like reading about Jeannie Main as much as I did writing about her.” Indeed, I did—my enjoyment equaled five stars!
