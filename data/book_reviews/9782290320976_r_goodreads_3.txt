Rating: 3/5

Although this volume again vividly evokes belle epoque Paris, it’s ultimately a means to set Adele up for WWI, which breaks out in the final frames. So there’s plenty of space for the author to digress. For example, he riffs on an 1895 accident at Montparnasse, in which a train ploughed through the station and crashed 10 metres down onto the street below. A press photo captured the locomotive balanced vertically against the façade of the station. This is portrayed in the book (almost 20 years later) but Adele’s role could hardly be more passive, having been lured by an anonymous phone call to wait outside the station. Likewise she’s sent free tickets for the Titanic, just so that we can see its sailing and demise. The vestigial plot involves the demon-worshipping cult previously seen in volume 2, although this time with the violence being more graphic. (As mentioned before, the series is not suitable for very young kids.) Adele has a diabolical statue slipped into her flat, which makes her sleepwalk to the final showdown, where the deus ex machina is a quartet of characters from one of the author’s non-Adele books.
