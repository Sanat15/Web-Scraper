Rating: 5/5

I read this very speedily as part of my research on the Sanctuary Network, a network declared in late 1995 by Christian organisations, to which some 12,000 Australians signed up, agreeing to hide East Timorese asylum seekers should the Australian government decide to begin deporting them back to East Timor once their asylum claims have been rejected. In this book, Andrew, a teenage budding palaeontologist in year 9 is drawn into helping his sister Anna hide a young East Timorese asylum seeker, Rosa, in their rambling home in inner-west Sydney. Their parents are reluctant, because although they believe in 'real issues' like human rights in Bougainville, East Timor and Rwanda, they really don't want to put up with guests, who, like fish, 'stink after three days'. There is also the issue of protecting their jobs and incomes, two things that could be jeopardised if they were to break the law. 

It is 20 years since this book was written, seems unbelievable. I really enjoyed this book, because the dialogue is quite well-written, there is snappy action, as well as all the tensions a good YA novel should have, in terms of all the things going on in a young person's life including confusion about school life, place within the family, love relationships, values and norms of behaviour. The book seeks to educate YA readers about what happened in East Timor and encourages readers to seek more information by contacting East Timor support groups.
