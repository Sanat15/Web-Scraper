Rating: 4/5

In my first reading, I struggled with the first few chapters until the crime was committed and Poirot's inquiries began. I read the series in order, and in Murder on the Orient Express is where I was presented with a different mystery - a locked room mystery. Also, the setting of the mystery rendered the standard police procedures impossible. All these held me at bay initially, not allowing me to form a full connection with the story. That is why I struggled and was slightly disappointed in my first reading. The second reading, however, altered my opinion significantly. I was no longer disappointed in the different structure that Christie adopted. Rather, I felt it appropriate to Poirot's "order and method".

Murder on the Orient Express is one of the ingenious murder mysteries by Agatha Christie, in my opinion. I can vouch for it after reading it twice. The plot is complex, and Christie takes readers through an arduous guessing game. I did my own guessing, but I tell you, I couldn't even come close to the truth. Hercule Poirot shines at his best; his "little grey cells" work their magic here. His expertise, long experience, and brilliant analytical mind help him untangle the complex knot and solve this complicated murder mystery. 

But it was the ending that completely knocked me out. I never saw that coming. And even after knowing the story, I was still in awe when Poirot neatly summed up his case. At first reading, I was surprised by Poirot's action. But this time, I was appreciative. Poirot proved that, within the brilliant detective, there lives a humane man who sees that justice is not always found in rules and regulations. It may not be the right view. But it felt right. Well done, Poirot! I love your humane heart. A bouquet and a respectful bow from a lawyer, who daily witnesses justice drowned in rules and regulations.
