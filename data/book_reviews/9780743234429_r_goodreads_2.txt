3.5/5

It's hard to give this an honest review as I was picking it up and putting it down over two months. 

I think Winton captured the eternal boredom of living rurally as a kid. I found myself drifting out of focus with the plot but the writing always drew me back. 

At it's core I think this is a story about a young boy, Ort, realising his parents are not perfect and that not all adults are moral. He doesn't have the language to explain this directly and the audience needs to do the heavy lifting of working out his feelings. It's also a story about choosing hope or survival. I think that hope for these characters, especially for Orts's mum, is necessary to keep going for those around them.

The character of Henry Warburton is an underlying threat throughout the second part of the story. I didn't read too closely but I find it staggering some people consider this novel to be beating it's audience over the head with christianity. Nothing about the religious characters are presented sympathetically.
