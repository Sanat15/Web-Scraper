Rating: 5/5

Author Deborah Woodworth’s Shaker mysteries are usually set at the fictional North Homage Shaker village in Northern Kentucky, but this fifth in the series is set in the real-life Hancock Shaker Village, located in the Berkshires. Sister Rose Callahan, the North Homage eldress, has solved more than her share of murders, so the leadership invite her to come and help them with the murder of a hired girl. 

Woodward won the Barry Award for Best Paperback Original for this novel, and readers will quickly realize why. Sister Rose brings her friend and onetime protégée Gennie Malone, and between the two of them, they ferret a lot about the latest folks to join the Hancock Shakers. The victim, beautiful, rebellious goodtime girl Julia Masters, was strangled in village’s summerhouse. Why would Julia, who worked in the Shakers’ store, be in the summerhouse dressed in a pink satin evening gown and dancing slippers? Actually, it being February, why was she in the summerhouse at all? And would hate Julia enough to kill her? A co-worker gives a clue: “Julia liked her men rich and important and goodlooking. If she couldn’t have all three, she’d settle for one while she kept on look.” Readers will have so much fun finding out!

Unlike the real Shaker villages in Kentucky, the last of which closed in 1922, the Hancock village still had two dozen members in the late 1930s; it survived until 1960. Like the Shaker Village at Pleasant Hill, located near Lexington, Ky., and the South Union Shaker Village, located near Bowling Green, Ky., Hancock Shaker Village, located near Pittsfield, Massachusetts, is open for tourists.
