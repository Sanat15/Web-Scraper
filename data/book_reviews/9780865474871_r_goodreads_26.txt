3.5 stars

Such an interesting book!

L’Engle makes the case that artists (even secular ones) are co-creators with God. A true artist will pay attention to the imaginative and the miraculous (what some might call their “muse”). In this way their work often says more than they ever intended it to say. “When the artist is truly the servant of the work, the work is better than the artist.” (p. 17)
She talks about excellent art and terrible art, and how the label “Christian” does not necessarily mean that it’s good.

One of my favorite ideas was that, “Stories, no matter how simple, can be vehicles of truth. Stories are able to help us to become more whole, to become Named. And Naming is one of the impulses behind all [true] art; to give a name to the cosmos we see despite all the chaos. (This was my favorite theme from the third book in the Wrinkle in Time trilogy.) 

You do not have to agree with L’Engle on all theological points to benefit from the rich ideas in this book.
