Rating: 4/5

This is a remarkable little book – literally only 11 by 8.5 centimetres, and short, a mere 71 pages yet it is a masterpiece of distillation, full of specific detail and avoiding generalisation. 

Containing all the major events of Frank Sinatra’s life, the author Esme Hawes captures the controversies, the bad associations, the volatile romances with Ava Gardner and Mia Farrow, among others, the man's pettiness and narcissism as well as his talent and magnetism. 

It’s all here, Dolly the dominating mother, the early marriage to first wife Nancy, the constant womanising thereafter, the bobby-soxers, his dubious contribution during World War Two. Starting with Bugsy Siegel, the association with gangsters, which ultimately soured his relationship with the Kennedys (Sinatra eventually turned Republican, raising funds for Ronald Reagan). Having read James Spada's excellent Peter Lawford: The Man Who Kept the Secrets I understand the influence Sinatra wielded, no better illustrated by the Nevada Gaming Control Commission renewing his gaming licence despite the evidence.

However, there is no denying Sinatra's staggering success in concert, on record and on the screen, as anyone who has watched Von Ryan’s Express or listened to In the Wee Small Hours of the Morning can see.
