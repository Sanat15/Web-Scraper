Rating: 1/5

Mercifully, this is the final entry in to the Forever Hero series. In addition to all the writing problems the first two books, this one was also very boring. It was like, "Devil Kid Does Spring Cleaning" or "Devil Kid Does His Taxes one Last Time Then Retires". In addition to this, the main character's actions and motivations in the latter half of the book are opaque and kinda monstrous. If you've read the first two books and want to know what happens, probably best to just try and find a detailed plot summary of this one, unless you are trapped on a desert island with just this book for entertainment.

Quick plot recap below:

Basically, Gershwin wraps up all his business by going to some random planets to overthrow various governments, kill random people and fund some bioscience projects. He relocates Leer to some retirement chateau somewhere and bails on her.

Eventually (I think he's maybe 600 years old at this point), Earth is suitable for habitation again, though it's not exactly a paradise yet, it seems like. He goes there to retire, and a bunch of legends about him have developed. He establishes himself and then — and this is the weird part — starts being a hermit who sometimes has sex with women who are having trouble conceiving (mostly women who want a baby but their abusive husbands are sterile or something)? In the two sex scenes Modesitt describes, he has consensual sex with the women, but then they get him mad for some reason and he straight up rapes them. This happens twice. WTF?

Eventually he has spawned a whole bunch of kids who live a long time (not as long as him), and are high intelligence. He lives in a stasis bubble and comes out once a year to have sex with some random woman and get her pregnant. They study his offspring and are trying to set up a breeding program, but apparently his genetics are kinda recessive or some weird non-mendelian thing. The whole ending is kinda weird.

1.5 of 5 stars
