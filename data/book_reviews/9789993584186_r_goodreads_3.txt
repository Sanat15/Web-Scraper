Rating: 4/5

Revolt in 2100 is a collection of three stories set in Heinlein's Future History series. All three were originally published in the pulp Astounding SF magazine in 1939 and 1940, before the U.S. entered World War Two. It's about an uprising against a theocracy that has taken over the country, and a couple of looks at the results afterwards. It's not as well-written as The Moon is a Harsh Mistress, which is similar in theme, but it was a very influential and ground-breaking work in its time, and involves a lot of the foundations (nope, that was Asimov) involves a lot of the cornerstones of his Future History sequence, including Andrew Jackson "Slipstick" Libby and Nehemiah Scudder. The stories are "If This Goes On-" (which became a something of a tag-line in the genre for stories of that type), Coventry, and Misfit. "If This Goes On-" won the Retro-Hugo for best novella of that year, and Coventry finished in third place. Heinlein also finished in second, with Magic, Inc., and also finished in first and second place in the novelette category with The Roads Must Roll and Blowups Happen. He finished second in the short story category with Requiem and altogether appeared on the ballot nine times with different stories from just that one year, which is absolutely phenomenal when you think of it and shows how much he dominated the field at that time. Revolt in 2100 is an important and thought-provoking collection, and though the social assumptions and technology may have changed, the message is just as important now as it was then.
