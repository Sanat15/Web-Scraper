Rating: 3/5

It’s definitely on the slower side. 

Although I was intrigued by the title and by yhe brief story about a slave girl in Buchi Emecheta’s other book “Joys of Motherhood”, it took me a while to get into this one. Not because of boring or bad writing but I was just afraid of how a small girl slave would be treated. This book didn’t do that though.. yes Emecheta did mention a couple of times that the slaves were mistreated and dehumanised however that sentiment was never really felt. On the contrary, I felt they were actually treated quite well and treated how a “house girl “ of today in Africa would be treated, however this could also indicate that they are poorly treated.. whichever way you want to look at it, I felt like the title is more conveyed in the “possession” and lack of freedom of Ojebeta rather than in the treatment of her.

So I am sort of disappointed but also, I took away something else from this book.
