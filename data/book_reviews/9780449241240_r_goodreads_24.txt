Rating: 4/5

I enjoyed this read. It was referenced in James Michener's "Alaska", which I had just finished. I thought it would be a good add-on, and it was, as it mirrored many of the themes as Michener's. This novel highlighted a narrower window of time, of course, and spun a narrative pitting the two conflicting views of Alaska prior to and leading up to statehood, which was a current event in the 1950's when this novel was written. It captured the view of the times when many Alaskans wanted statehood so they could manage themselves and control their resources, yet were met with resistance by the Outside (Seattle, San Francisco, New York, Washongton, D.C.), who were represented by big business. Business interests prevailed in that conversation, since may outsiders had gotten rich off of Alaska and wanted to keep it that way. 

I liked Ferber's writing, too - I had to google a lot of words that were in vogue at that time!
