Rating: 4/5

You can almost see Le Fanu coming to a realisation about the treatment of women in this: that their dependence upon, and necessary supplication to, men places them in danger and unfairly curtails them. Sometimes, by reflecting on something in a genuine way, a writer comes, or almost comes, to a realisation that's ahead of its time. You can almost feel Le Fanu recalibrating and deciding that actually, it's just that the two men in this book are bad, it's not an inherent problems with an unjust system. 

This is a gripping, entertaining, and sometimes overblown story of Maud Ruthyn, an extremely wealthy heiress, whose father makes a terrible mistake when, upon his death, he places her in their hands of her unscrupulous and greedy Uncle Silas. Maud faces Gothic misfortune after misfortune, in a novel that is engaging and likeable. I was interested to read in the forward that Le Fanu had set his previous works in Ireland, but was contractually obliged to set later works in England, as his publisher believed an Irish setting wouldn't sell. But Le Fanu had never spent much time in England, and in this b0ok Derbyshire seems to be a thin veneer placed over settings that feel distinctly Irish.
