Rating: 1/5

This is the last book in the Paget Family Saga and tells a complete story. It could stand alone. I haven't read the second book, but I doubt there is much need for that to enjoy this one. There is nothing from the first book except the family name.

Some have shelved this a Romance, but that is an exaggeration. There are so few pages spent on the romance. This is a story about Ellen and any other characters, including her opposite, are really secondary.

What's worse than a boring book? How about a gloomy boring romance that isn't a romance. There are really no redeeming qualities to this book. I didn't find much at all funny. Admittedly it gets a bit active and even confusing in the last 5 or 10%, but that doesn't save it. Gloomy is the best description. There are some mildly interesting perils here and there, but they don't rise to any level of excitement. For much of the book, the most dangerous thing Ellen faces is potential scandal, and not the romantic ruination kind you usually expect in this kind of book.

It is sad to read because many of the children in the book are abused, not physically but psychologically through abusive language and neglect. That goes for Ellen as well.

Ellen is the main character, but she is not much of a heroine. He main qualities are perseverance, kindness and she keeps her head. But she is often timid and a little whiny. Not that she doesn't have things to complain about, but it isn't pretty in a heroine or hero.

The climax is disgusting as regards how one group of people treats another thinking they are justified to do so.

The ending is way too abrupt. This is one story that could use an epilogue or the equivalent.

Mature themes: there is no sex. There is murder/suicide  including a 4 year old child  and finding the bodies is described. I mentioned verbal abuse and neglect.

Why did I finish this book? I should have put it down after about the third chapter.
