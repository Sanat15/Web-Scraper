Rating: 2/5

Jack, Marquess Pemerton, has got the secret poors. Lady Mary Haviland has got the secret riches. Eventually Jack finds out about Mary's cash and decides to marry her. They make out a lot and catch feelings before their secrets eventually destroy their happiness because this is a romance novel. 

1. When Mary discovers why Jack wanted to marry her, she flees his estate and calls off the wedding. Jack is so devastated that he goes to London and sleeps with, what, 40+ different women? (The text states he's in London for 6 weeks, sleeping with a different lady every night: I hereby thank my elementary math teachers for teaching me the ability to determine just how gross Jack is! THANK YOU MATHEMATICS!)

This all happens in like the second to last chapter of the book, right? Before he rides off to Scotland to apologize for being a fortune hunter. I mean. I don't. 

Bad, bad hero man. So bad. 

2. Mary's whole thing is that she grew up in an abusive household and her one attempt to escape left her ruined and convinced that men would only like her for her money. So Jack's betrayal was ultimately HUGE but it's kind of played off as being equal to Mary's abandonment of Jack??

I find this to be bullshit??

3. I was into this for the first 50% or so, watching Jack and Mary forge a friendship and then their partnership once they're engaged: I liked watching them figure each other out even while knowing the other shoe was going to drop and ruin everything. But the revelations about Mary's past and Jack's rage were just a bridge too far, and it took a fun romp into ridiculous territory really fast. 

4. Also, the over-reliance on describing Mary as "small" or "tiny" or "diminutive" got EXHAUSTING. I GET IT SHE IS PETITE. GOOD FOR HER!
