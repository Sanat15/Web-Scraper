Rating: 1/5

How can you possibly mess up a dog book? J.R. Ackerley found a way!

This book is boring, cringy, and could have been funny but wasn’t. If animal cruelty bothers you, do not read this book.

On Audible, it has the lowest rating that I have ever seen.

Before we dive in, a bit about how I arrived at this book:

I grew up in a small village with two acres and a fenced yard. We had a cocker spaniel who was my shadow. When he wanted to be let out, he would go to the back door and shake this string that had a bell. When I moved to the city, although I love, love, love dogs, I just didn’t feel right about keeping a dog in a small apartment all day while I worked long hours. This is why I keep cats or rather I keep one cat at a time. Melville was with me for 10 years before succumbing to a medical issue. After three years of mourning, I had the joy of adopting Bill, my Russian Blue cat. He is an incredibly sweet animal and my snuggle buddy. He can frequently be found in my Instagram stories as he sits on my lap while reading.

My Dog Tulip is focused on an Alsatian or German Shepherd living in a small London flat (British speak for apartment). The book focuses on the dog using the restroom, and the irresponsible dog owner trying to find “dates” for his dog and the graphic details of the subsequent dates.

Ackerley is astonished when people are unhappy with his dog messing all over the place and in their houses and sidewalks. He should have heeded Bob Barker’s words of wisdom on “The Price is Right.”

This book is uncomfortable, and it devolved from there. The ending of the book was predictable but horrifying. I am going to hug Bill a little tighter tonight.
