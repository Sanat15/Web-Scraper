Rating: 5/5

🌊 The whole series of some 20 volumes is a marvel. They read like novels and not at all like genre pieces, which in some ways they are (nautical fiction) and many others they are not, for they are complex, detailed, extensive and highly original. While at the same time being rife with adventure and romance and danger. Sails snap on the tall square riggers, the decks lean underfoot, whitecaps crack into the hull, salt air stings the nostrils. 

⚓️ Jack will, in due course over the spread of the series, face the French, Spanish and Americans in war and in friendship. He will rise in rank. His friendship with the ship’s surgeon, Stephen, an Irishman, will grow. There will be tragedies. Triumphs. Losses and griefs. Great joy and high spirits. Love. The series is about the very whole of life.

🌊 I first began reading these books, beginning with this one, at the same time as I was learning to sail on the Pacific Ocean. What exhilarating books to turn to at day’s end, in harbor or at anchor - windburned, saltwater lashed, sun driven, fully alive. What astonishing memories these books bring back and they are written beautifully, beautifully. 

A hundred stars.

[A movie was done on this book with Russell Crowe and Paul Bettany. 2003. Great film.]
