i spend the night at the desk inside the high-ceilinged hall of casterbridge town hall. 

spread before me are neither grain merchants' promissory notes nor ledgers of wheat. these are the pages of this town's twenty-year echo report. 

every line records the same experiment anew.  i trace how a single sentence, once spoken, travelled silently across the years before finding its way back and how, by the time it returned, it had already altered the lives it touched.  

it all began at the weydon-priors fair, with a single sentence that fell from the coarse, drunken mouth of a man inside a tent: 

   "i'm selling my wife!" 
 

in the echo report, this stands as the first entry.  the words that slipped beyond the canvas walls that night didn't disappear the moment they struck the stones of casterbridge.  the town didn't forget them; it simply kept them until the time came to send them back. 

now i walk through the streets of casterbridge, studying the report's second entry. 

 as michael henchard sits in the mayor's chair, he believes silence has finally taken his side. the heavy fur draped across his shoulders and the gold signet ring upon his finger only strengthen that belief.  

yet no entry is ever erased from the echo report.  casterbridge returns every word entrusted to it, complete and undiminished, when its time arrives.  

i turn the next page of the report. 

 susan's return is the first measurement showing that the return time of the original entry has finally elapsed.  

with donald farfrae's arrival, the measurements begin to change.  two different voices move through the same town: one carries the weight of the past, while the other has never known such a burden.  the report suggests that sometimes an echo doesn't amplify the original voice, but the answer given to it. 

when lucetta's letters are revealed, the measurements rise once more.  it is no longer only henchard's past that speaks; the town itself begins repeating the same sentence through different mouths.  

in the empty margin of the page, i add a single note:  echoes never return alone. 

yesterday evening, i saw henchard standing by the lock gates beside the river, utterly alone. he was covering his ears. 

 but the return had already begun. the only thing he could silence was his own hands, never the answer that had come back to him.  

i read the final line of the report and close the file. the measurements may have come to an end. 

 yet i know now that every sentence begins its own return the moment it is spoken.
