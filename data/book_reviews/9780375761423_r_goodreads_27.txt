Rating: 5/5

FINALLY! It only took me, what, 9 months? It's pretty much impossible to spoil these books (unless you don't know where or what Alaska is, and then me ruining it for you is the least of your problems). Each chapter might as well be its own book with later chapters getting quite a lot longer. 

This is one of Michener's great epics where the land itself is both the setting and the main character. 

I got stuck on the part about the salmon. It was like the part of Chesapeake about the geese. I also wasn't a huge fan of ships or department stores or the men from Seattle who ran things. 

What I liked: the Russians, the natives, whales, seals, and caribou... gold rush towns, sour dough pancakes, bush pilot, taking down the corrupt judge, the old lady turned lobbyist, cannaries, fish traps, shamen, totem poles, the Chinese supervisor who stayed, dog sleds, and so much more. 

Each chapter covers a different era, which is usually remote enough from what came before to have separate characters. Sometimes, a character spans more than one section like the case of Missy who starts off in the gold boom making pancakes in the 1890s and who eventually fights (to the death) for statehood in the 1950s. Other characters are the children of previous favorites, like the daughter of a resourceful man who first shows up to keep Chinese labor in line at the cannery.

Side note: After reading this, I'm convinced Puerto Rico deserves statehood.
