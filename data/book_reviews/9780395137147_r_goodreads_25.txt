Rating: 4/5

Which heroes? 

Hercules, Peseus, Theseus, Orpheus, Meleager, and Jason &amp; the Argonauts. Of course, the book does not provide all the details for any of these--just a few stories for each hero. 

I bought this book a decade ago when I took my children to the National Gallery of Art. However, I just now read it because I teach a first-year seminar on Classical Athens. My students depict people from 5th C. BCE, and each of them is also assigned a patron so that they can learn about mythology along with learning about politics, geography, and warfare. Theseus and Perseus are among the patrons, so I decided to finally read this book. Also, I had never heard of Meleager, so I wanted to learn about this unsung (to me) hero. 

It was a good, clear retelling of a few hero tales with a reduction of some of the more salacious details. However, I was very unsettled by the authors using a MIX of Greek and Roman names for the gods. Why Zeus and Neptune instead of Zeus and Poseidon (since the title of the book mentions these are Greek and not Roman heroes)? We also meet Plato, Minerva, Diana, and other Roman-named divinities. For this reason alone, I would suggest parents and teachers select a different retelling of these tales for young readers.
