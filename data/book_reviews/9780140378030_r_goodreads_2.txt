Rating: 5/5

I read Mahy's book when I was quite young. I think it was published in 1995 so it must have been sometime after that. 
Since then, I have re-read it many times and I still love the sentiment of rich and content solitude which eminates from Hero's perspective, her voice.
She doesn't speak (by choice) but she is a very observant little girl. This makes her a somewhat disregarded secret keeper. Her narration is crowned by occasional references to fairytales and classic childrens' stories. She separates her life - and the chapters - into what's real and what's true to her. It's the true life which ends up being more real than she could've anticipated.

I was never really sure what to think about the ending. I'm still not sure. Up to a certain point the darkness is subtle enough with the occasional 'pang' of evil, always underlying Hero's slightly innocent point of view. At the end, a drastic reality check awaits her which is of definite disturbing nature and partially tears down her childish worldview (unfortunately).
 
Ergo, I would say that The Other Side of Silence falls mainly into the coming of age, loss of innocence category. I found the story to be superbly written, every word is in its right place. Mahy has done very well.
