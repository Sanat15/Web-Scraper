Rating: 4/5

Seems like every time some website generates a list of recommended books for me, Victor LaValle is on it somewhere. I have a real admiration for people who obliterate genre boundaries, something he seems to be admired for. I recently read his extremely entertaining, Lovecraftian short story "Up From Slavery," and wanted more. The Ecstatic is his first novel, written in 2002.

The narrator here is an outsider, even in his black New York neighborhood, an innocent of sorts but also wickedly insightful with an outsider's perspective. The style here reminds me of Charles Portis, one of my literary idols. The story involves beauty pageants, dogs, conmen of various sorts, family relationships, horror films and fiction, and the fragile nature of humankind in a world none of us really understand. It's very funny and, like Portis' work, powerful in its humor. I'm looking forward to reading the rest of his novels which apparently veer further into weirdness. This one made an excellent starting place.
