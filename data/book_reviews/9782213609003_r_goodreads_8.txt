Rating: 4/5

This has become one of my favorite subgenres: the highly literary thriller with menacing undertones. Other books in this subgenre are The Magus by John Fowles and The Dark Labyrinth by Lawrence Durrell.

It's impossible to read about the upper class gangs in Super-Cannes "hunting" in the immigrant housing estates of southern France (even setting up "hunting lodges") without thinking of the new dystopian sport: ICE chasing brown-skinned people through American cities, sometimes leading to their deaths.

Ballard really lays the similes on thickly here, and I wallowed in them. The references are smart - a head of security is a "corpulent beach Beria." One of my favorite passages, not just for the writing but because I'm a puritan with an averted gaze myself: "Parked behind the Jaguar on the sloping forecourt was Wilder Penrose's sports saloon, a low-slung Japanese confection with huge wing mirrors, grotesque spoiler and air intakes large enough for a ramjet. To my puritan eye the car was an anthology of marketing tricks, and I refused even to identify its manufacturer."
