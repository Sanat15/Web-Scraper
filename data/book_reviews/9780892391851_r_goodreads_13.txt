Rating: 5/5

A lovely book that really ought to be better known. This book is ultimately more about people than about horses, as it follows conversations the narrator has with friends and family members. The narrator asks each person (real people known to Richard Van Camp): "What's the most beautiful thing you know about horses?" The friends' and family members' answer range from amusing to poetic. By demonstrating that people may view the same animal in many diverse ways, the book invites readers to think about the wide range of perspectives different people may have. And it does this without being at all didactic! 

I use this book in a college-level course on children's literature, but I have also read it to my own children. In my opinion, it has both kid-appeal and parent-appeal. I could easily see classroom activities built around the book, as a teacher could ask students what THEY think is the most beautiful thing about horses. 

Picture books by Indigenous authors and illustrators are unfortunately still too rare. This one ought to get more attention!
