Rating: 4/5

Lady Lucy is miserable in her aristocratic marriage. While her husband drinks, gambles and consorts with loose women, Lucy is lonely and confused. She is unaware that her parents "bought" her a titled husband, but now he has gambled away all of the money he received.

When Lucy meets the Duke of Habard, she is alarmed by the feelings he arouses. The Duke feels sorry for Lucy and tries to befriend her, but before she knows it, she finds she is falling in love with him.

The title is a little misleading because, of course, Lady Lucy does not have a lover. Her husband, in addition to his other vices, gets involved with opium, and from that point his life is on a downhill skid.

I love Marion Chesney's books and intend to read them all. I am not yet deeply involved in the Hamish Macbeth and Agatha Raisin stories that were written as M. C. Beaton, but I expect to get to them also.
