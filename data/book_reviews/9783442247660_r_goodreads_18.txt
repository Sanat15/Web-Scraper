Rating: 4/5

⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️

Wedge’s Gamble by Michael Stackpole is a book I enjoyed more than the first X-Wing book, Rogue Squadron. One thing I really liked about this story was that we took a break from the starfighter action. This book follows the Rogues as they attempt to liberate Coruscant, and I enjoyed seeing the team involved in a larger operation beyond just flying missions.

One of my favorite parts of the book was the subplot involving a sickness that targets alien life. This was a unique weapon compared to anything I have read so far in the Expanded Universe. I liked the idea that even if the Rogues successfully take Coruscant, they will still have another problem to deal with: using resources to help those who have been affected by the disease.

My biggest complaint is that space action has always been my least favorite part of Star Wars and sci-fi books. Characters constantly seem to run into each other, and solutions to major problems can sometimes feel too convenient. That being said, the story kept me engaged from cover to cover.

My favorite character in this book was Tycho Celchu. He has been suspected for a while of being an Imperial sleeper agent, and later in the book he appears to be framed for a terrible crime. I personally don’t believe he did it, and I enjoyed the mystery surrounding his character.

The epilogue was probably the highlight of the book for me. It left me wanting to continue the series and see what happens next with the Rogues.

Overall, Wedge’s Gamble is a strong improvement over Rogue Squadron. If you are reading through the Star Wars Expanded Universe, this is a book worth picking up. I’m looking forward to seeing where the X-Wing series goes next.
