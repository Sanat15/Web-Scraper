Rating: 3/5

Not quite sure how to really rate this book. I liked a lot of things and other things were super frustrating. The basic plot is that Belle grew up poor and she and her mom were not treated well by the family. She inherits a good fortune but becomes aloof due to her past experiences. During her debut (around 8 years before), the Colford tries to flirt with her and, when she wants nothing to do with him, he forces a kiss on her. She reacts with outrage, as she should, and he dubs her The Golden Icicle. They don't have much contact with each other until she brings a cousin (I believe) to London for her debut, and the cousin becomes enamored of Colfort's cousin.

Things I liked:
•	Colford saying he wasn’t interested in girls who were almost young enough to be his daughter when asked if he’d be interested on Julia, the debutante (he was 35 and she was around 17-18, I believe).
•	The H and h were hot headed and said things they didn’t mean in the spur of the moment, but they actually talked afterwards and apologized.
•	The OW wasn’t really an OW. She was a nice person who took care of her family, and the H actually respected her – he just didn’t love her.
•	The mature and sensible heroine, except for the two outbursts, which were plot devices that could have been handled better.
•	The heroine does not feel a secret yearning when the H forces a kiss on her. She’s outraged.
•	The H was an a-hole in the past but admitted to it and apologized for his behavior.
•	The fact that the H and h had conversations and spent time with each other. You could see how they came to care and respect one another.

Things I didn’t like:
•	The heroines outbursts, especially the one at the end, which made no sense as a set down and only seemed a very poor excuse for the author to create another conflict.
•	The H and h from the previous book. I know that readers enjoy having characters come back, but they seemed to get in the way of the protagonists interactions rather than adding to it (it doesn’t help that I found Phillipa annoying and Alex rude to Belle behind her back – even if he was trying to get a rise out of Colford).
•	The ungrateful family members. They weren’t bad people, but they seemed to think nothing of Belle’s assistance to them.
•	The lack of background on Belle and a frank discussion with Colford. Apparently, her background was exposed in book one, and we only get mentions here and there in this book. The characters also kept saying that Colford would understand Belle better if he knew her background, so I kept waiting for a frank conversation that never happened.
•	The other characters “pushing” the protagonists towards each other. I know a lot of people enjoy these plots, but I find it more compelling when the protagonists accomplish this on their own. To be fair, they did seem to be getting there, and it was more of a push for things to happen faster.
