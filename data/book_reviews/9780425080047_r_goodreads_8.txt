Rating: 3/5

Soooooo now I can officially say that I've FINALLY met the iconic detective duo everyone - and their cat - seems to know about🙂‍↔️✨

*Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson, it's nice to finally make your acquaintance*

*shakes hands*

I went into this moooostly curious - after all, we're talking about one of the most influential detective series ✨ever written✨, so I felt like it was about time I actually picked this up-

And honestly?

*clears throat*

I had quite a good time🙂‍↔️

One of the things I immediately appreciated was the dialogue; I absolutely LOVE when conversations feel witty, intelligent, and carefully thought out - which was precisely what was delivered

The exchanges between Holmes and Watson were easily one of my favorite aspects of the book: there's something incredibly entertaining about watching Holmes casually connect dots that nobody else even realized existed while Watson (and honestly bestie, me as well lmaoo) is left standing there wondering how on earth he connected all those dots in 0.3 milliseconds-

The book contains two separate cases: 1) A Study in Scarlet and 2) The Sign of the Four. If you ask me, both mysteries were well executed and engaging in their own way; the investigations unfold at a satisfying pace, gradually revealing clues and motives without making the solutions feel completely obvious from the start (if nothing)✨

Our duo has quite the dynamic/energy going for them - entertaining from the very beginning, aaaand it's quite easy to see why they became such beloved literary characters; Holmes' brilliant/eccentric personality contrasts wonderfully with Watson's grounded perspective, creating a balance that makes the story work🤧

That being said, I wasn't *sadly* completely blown away - while I appreciated the mysteries and the characters, there were moments where the pacing slowed down for me, for example when the narrative itself shifted away from the investigation-

*screams into a pillow*

BUT-

OVERALL THO, I'm very glad I had ✨finally✨ picked this up; it's always fun to see where so many detective tropes originated - especially when the source material still holds up surprisingly well more than a century later🥹

*can't wait to get my hands on the next book; holding my hopes high that the 'enjoyment' bar will rise as I continue reading these heh*

*fingers crossed*
