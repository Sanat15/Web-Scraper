Rating: 4/5

This was another novel on the list of movie adaptations that I really enjoyed and wanted to see how the original source material compared.
Admittedly it’s been so long since I’ve seen the 2006 film but remember it enough to know that the premise are the same but each has a completely different feel.

The main crux is the human population can no longer reproduce, with the last child born 25 years previous and how it impacts on society.

With the story set during 2021 it couldn’t have been more timely, I definitely feel that there’s a disconnect with Baby Boomers and Millennials today - so a novel that explores themes of an ageing population without a new up and coming group just highlighted how the young and old complement and need each other.

From a maternal instinct of those doting on pets and the need for sex having been diminished to the elderly becoming more of a hindrance to a declining work force, James vividly creates a dysfunctional landscape perfectly.

The book is certainly a lot slower paced which actually aided the story that James is trying to tell. 
The gradual decent into chaos as people lose focuses as inevitable but distant end is more clearly felt.

It’s one of them great occurrences where both the book and movie can be enjoyed separately.
