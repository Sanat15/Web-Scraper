Rating: 5/5

"The Black Snowman" is about a little boy, Jacob, who hates being black and does not understand the significance his heritage. Jacob and his little brother, Peewee, go out back, where the snow is full of dirt and soot, to build a snowman, a black snowman. In search of something to warm his shoulders, a kente is found; a scarf type shawl that is colorful and full of the memories of African ancestors. Placed upon his shoulders, the snowman becomes magical! 
I thought this book was excellent, in all the dynamics of writing. The words were easy to read, the illustrations were bold and colorful, they set the mood and dramatized the history when needed. The story gives insight into the African Heritage and what it means to be "black". It also depicts the meaning of family and how important that is. A line from the book that I found to be very interesting is during a conversation with the snowman. The snowman says, " What is more important in a book -- the white pages, the black words, or the message the book holds?" This question to me is a question anyone could ask themselves. Can you answer this with true sincerity? In a classroom setting, I would use this book during Black History Month! It would allow students to get onto a personal level with other cultures and to understand the trials that they too can face. It also shows the potential one can have in themselves if they just believe. It has some wonderful basis for discussions in the classroom as well. The book can be geared for any age group, it is a picture storybook; I believe only ages 8 and up would grasp the meaning of the story and the significance of the story line.
