Rating: 5/5

This book is one I would call “a timeless classic”, because re-reading it never gets old!
Carl and Helen Doss were a young couple in the late 1930’s. Carl had a calling to become a minister, and Helen deeply desired to become a mother. Adoption was the only way this would happen for the two, and after several years of waiting for approval from agencies, Baby Donald became their first!
Over the next ten years, the Doss family expanded exponentially, with the adoption of eleven more children. All except one were of mixed-race, considered in the 40’s and 50’s to be “unadoptable, same as any child with a defect”, according to the social workers. Luckily for these youngsters, Carl and Helen heartily disagreed with that way of thinking…
I first read this book as a fifth-grader, and was immediately intrigued! Although first published in 1954, the lively voices and antics of the children are just as charming to read about again in 2026, kids being what they are, then and now. 
There is a chapter about the family being featured in a Life magazine story in 1951. In a streak of incredible luck, I was able to obtain a copy of that magazine, with all of the wonderful pictures of the Doss family! I’ve looked at it so much over the years that I can identify the kids without the captions!
So, on this wintry weekend with snow and ice outside, I enjoyed another “visit” with this family, consisting of Donny, Laura, Susan, Teddy, Timmy, Rita, Diane, Elaine, Alex, Richard, Dorothy, and Gregory, and again fell in love with this sweet book…sigh.
