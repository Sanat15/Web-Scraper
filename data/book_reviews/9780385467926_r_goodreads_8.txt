Rating: 5/5

Origins Reconsidered: In Search of What Makes Us Human is a fantastic book on human origins by one of the leading palaeoanthropologists of the last century. The book is intended for a largely non-academic audience and employs a quite personal angle, including descriptions of the search for and excavation of ancient hominin remains (primarily around Lake Turkana in Kenya) and exceedingly honest comments about scientific rivalries and disagreements over interpretations. Each section of the book focuses on a particular "search" within human evolutionary studies - the search for fossils and evidence, the search for the earliest human ancestors, the search for the origins of our genus (Homo), the search for the origins of our species (Homo sapiens), and the search for the origins of modern human cognition. As a palaeoanthropologist myself, this is the kind of book that I need to read every once in a while. More academic treatises are important and can often provide new insights more immediately useful to me in my work, but it's books like these that really remind me why I chose to study human evolution in the first place and how privileged it is to be a part of the work to understand ourselves, past, present and future. Highly, highly recommended.
