Rating: 4/5

Before I read this book, I expected a typical Nicholas Sparks romance, but it ended up feeling much heavier and more reflective than that. It follows Theresa, a divorced woman who finds a love letter washed up on the beach and becomes drawn to the man who wrote it. From there it turns into a story about grief, second chances, and how people hold onto love, even when life keeps moving forward.

What I liked most about this story was that is didn't try to make love feel perfect. It felt messy, emotional, and very real at times. The author has a way of writing relationships that make me think about my own life, and the people I have loved or lost, and this book really did that for me. Some parts made me stop reading for a minute, just to sit reflectively with what I had just read. The book has many messages of its own, throughout the story, and I really enjoyed their symbolic gestures.

The biggest symbol in the book is obviously the bottle and the letters inside it. They're not just there to move the plot along, they represent connection across distance and time. The letters feel deeply personal and vulnerable, almost like pieces of someone's heart being sent out into the world without knowing who will find them. That's what makes the story feel so emotional from the start. Theresa doesn't just become interested in the man who wrote them; she connects to the honesty in them, and I think that's something a lot of people secretly want, to feel understood that deeply.

The ocean is symbolic too. It constantly feels tied to memory, loss, and uncertainty. Things drift away, things return unexpectedly, and people can't fully control where life takes them. There's this underlying feeling throughout the book that love is powerful but also fragile, and timing matters just as much as feelings do.

What I think Sparks does well here is that the symbolism never feels forced or overly literary. It's simple enough to connect with emotionally, which is probably why I enjoyed reading it so much. Message in a Bottle is mainly a romance/drama, but I'd also describe it as an emotional fiction, because it spends just as much time exploring grief, loneliness, healing, and timing as it does the romance itself. It's definitely a love story, but not in the cheesy or overly idealised sense. It feels more like a story about what love leaves behind, how it changes people, how they carry it, and how hard it can be to open yourself up again after loss.
