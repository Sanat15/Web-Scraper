Rating: 3/5

The "Daughters of England" moves into the 18th century and the story of Clarissa Field, the illegitimate daughter of Carlotta, the doomed heroine of "The Song of the Siren." A visit to her long-lost father's Northern England estate when she is a teenager will shadow the rest of her life, both for her encounter with two vastly different men and with her late father's Jacobite cause. For nearly 95% of the novel, Carr continues the plot she has set in motion; then, abruptly, she winds the existing action up and introduces two new plotlines (one expected; one out of left field) to see the novel to its next chapter. Given the amount of decades Carr gallops across in the novel, many of the surviving characters from the four previous novels are summarily dealt with. "Will You Love Me in September" is not a bad novel, but after a long leisurely ride through the 17th Century, this novel's pace is a bit jarring. Nevertheless, Carr continues to do what she does so well: blending history, a bit of romance and a huge dollop of suspense into a satisfying mixture.
