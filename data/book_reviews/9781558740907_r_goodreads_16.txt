Rating: 5/5

Criminally underrated. This book is way ahead of it's time. When you see society today, these are all very common problems in relationships.

Opposed to other self-help books I've read, this book is a swiss army knife:
- Role of fear, shame, and guilt
- Emotional processing
- Boundaries and self-worth 
- Codependency
- The subconscious' role in our behavior
- Neuroplasty of the brain - Take repeating behaviors we've seen in the past. Repetition compulsion.
- Endless examples of functional and dysfunctional environments
- Endless archetypes of abuse
- Examples of dysfunctional coping mechanisms (e.g. Listening to music instead of reflecting)
- Examples of transference of duty, unfilled needs, 
- A theoretical chart regarding how trauma stems into self-doubt, codependency - A bit incomplete but solid for anyone starting. Human helplessness is not just from lack of self worth, but also helplessness, pain and discomfort, and death.
- Body language
- Endless questions for reflection - This is needed to heal!
- Examples of dysfunctional codependencies
- Identity as a requirement to give love without harming ourselves
- Conflict advice
- Boundaries in bed
- Warning of transactional mentality
- Teenagers and children
- Passing of loved ones

Other Handy Items
- Adults going major philosophical changes every 10-ish years

I highly recommend for anyone that has lived under an abusive household and is recovering from trauma.

Only Flaws?
- 80% Non-Verbal communication isn't entirely correct but holds truth
- This book needs a tiny bit of updating, given the theoretical codependency chart could use more variables.
- Teaching casual sex friends - This is very psychologically dangerous. The human body releases chemicals associated with attachment. Doing this creates dependency, habits, high risk of expectations given most people are psychologically unintegrated, STDs and pregnancy risks. There is no guarantee the other party is exclusive towards you.

I don't get how others complain this book is "too traditional" or "dated." This is clinical psychology regarding universal human needs, not a book of instant gratification, cultural political correctness, or a feel-good thriller.
