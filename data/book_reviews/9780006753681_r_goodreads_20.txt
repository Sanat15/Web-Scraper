Rating: 4/5

How do you rate a childhood favorite?

Along with A Wrinkle in Time, the Chronicles of Narnia defined what a good children's book was for me. Because, while I loved Ramona Quimby, there was always something better about a little magic in the world.
Besides, her family was a little too normal. I grew up in a cult-y home, so reading about her family, or the problems that the characters in a Judy Blume book had, was more like a peek into another world that didn't quite make sense to me.
It was easier to wrap my head around what was happening in Narnia.

I don't think all the themes translate well into a modern-day children's fantasy, simply because there's too much of a cultural gap at this point. Aslan telling Susan she was never to fight in the war, for example. But there are some incredibly nuanced character developments that are still striking to this day.
Edward, the somewhat cruel older brother to Lucy, and peevish younger brother to Peter &amp; Susan, really hit that middle child angst dead center. You can see his desperation to impress Peter and not look foolish to Susan spiral out of control when he realizes that Lucy was right about another world inside the wardrobe. If he had just come out of that closet and backed her story up, how different his story would have been. 
It's a good lesson we (hopefully) learn in childhood - why it's important to admit when you're wrong, and how quickly small white lies can spiral into horrible life-changing ones.

I loved these books when I was a kid. And I'm not sure I want to revisit the entire series with my jaded adult eyes, but regardless of what I think of C.S. Lewis' religious themes now, I'll always have a soft spot for this children's fantasy.

Recommended for that classic nostalgia.
