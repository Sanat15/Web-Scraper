Rating: 3/5

Slan is a 'golden age' classic, written in 1940. I put this into the same category as H.G. Wells or E.E. 'Doc' Smith's work. Due to its age, you cannot expect much in the way of the science involved holding up to well over time, although van Vogt gave it a good go with atomic energy for something preceding the Manhattan Project. This is basically a quest/adventure tale, with our lead Jommy Cross, a Slan (mutated human) trying to find others of his kind in a world very unfriendly to mutants. Over the course of the novel, we learn there are three types of humanity: 'real' Slan, who can mind read and have golden tendrils in their hair and more advanced internal organs, tendrilless Slan who can pass as normal, and finally, base human stock. The outer planets are colonized by tendrilless Slan while Earth is ruled by a base human dictatorship. Jommy sees his parents killed at a young age and is forced to go underground until he reaches an age to really begin his quest. This was a fun read, and I can imagine our great grandparents were enthralled!
