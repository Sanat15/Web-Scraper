Rating: 4/5

“mary” (3 stars):
- i recognize that this is an extremely anachronistic critique for a proto-feminist but there is a lot of “not like other girls” sentiment in mary and i find it grating. 
- over-reliance on the term “sensibility”, especially since wollstonecraft acknowledges that it can never fully be explained. i actually really hate the concept of sensibility specifically as it pertains to class but i can’t get into that rn
- i grew weary of her constant dissection of the faculties, a task better left to locke
- also: anne and henry’s chronic illnesses were flimsy plot devices. flesh out their experience! explore the sanatorium!
- wollstonecraft should have described mary’s adventures with henry’s mother, their grief/guilt had so much potential
- charles could have been such an interesting vehicle for woll’s arguments against marriage. he’s not evil but he’s still not good for mary bc she didn’t choose him. there’s so much nuance to be explored, but we never actually meet him!!!!! smh

“the wrongs of woman” (4.5 stars):
- better than “mary” in every way
- finally some thought put into the portrayal of the lower classes!! jemima’s story is touching, although it’s eerie how quickly she fades into the background again once her chapter ends
- maria declaring herself divorced was so so good
- darnford potentially relapsing into rakehood is very bleak and funny
