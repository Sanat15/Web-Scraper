Rating: 3/5

Maybe closer to 2.5 stars for enjoyment, but rounded up for exceeded expectations. 

The Beau and the Bluestocking reminded me a lot of Arabella. You have the young innocent from the country who catches the eye of bored flirt and proceeds to win him over with her artlessness and moral integrity. 

Unfortunately, unlike Arabella, the heroine is every bit as Mary Sue as she sounds. She's so noble and educated and pure that I got rather tired of her. I did like the Hannah More connection. That was clever and tied into the Bluestockings reference quite nicely. But as far as characters go, Alethea is not one of my new favorites.

Nor, I fear, is our beau, Sir James Devenish. I think he had a lot of potential. And to give this book credit, it relies almost exclusively on showing rather telling his growing infatuation with Alethea. I just needed...a little more to go on. Maybe if we got a little less of the side characters (particularly her cousin) and a little more of those two I would have bought the romance more. 

Or maybe not. With Alethea always sitting around prim and proper, I'm not sure more scenes of her seriously conversing about Shakespeare or quoting Latin would have helped. 

I'm glad I gave this one a try. Goodreads has been recommending it to me for years! It is clean and entertaining with a satisfying payout. Just...it made me want to pick up a Georgette Heyer novel instead.
