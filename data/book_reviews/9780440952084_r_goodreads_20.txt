Rating: 5/5

Madeleine L’Engle’s The Time Quartet, made up of A Wrinkle in Time, A Wind in the Door, A Swiftly Tilting Planet, and Many Waters, is a brilliant and unforgettable blend of science fiction, fantasy, faith, philosophy, and family drama. These books are imaginative in the truest sense: they do not simply create strange worlds and extraordinary journeys, but use those journeys to explore courage, love, identity, responsibility, and the battle between good and evil.

The series begins with A Wrinkle in Time, a classic story that remains powerful because of Meg Murry. Meg is not a perfect heroine, and that is what makes her so memorable. She is impatient, angry, insecure, loving, and fiercely loyal. Her flaws are not erased; instead, they become part of her strength. Through Meg, L’Engle shows that bravery is not about being fearless or flawless, but about choosing love even when fear is overwhelming.

The following books expand the scope of the story beautifully. A Wind in the Door moves from the cosmic to the microscopic, showing that even the smallest life matters. A Swiftly Tilting Planet explores time, history, and the consequences of human choices, while Many Waters takes the series in a more mythic direction through Sandy and Dennys. Each book feels distinct, yet they are connected by L’Engle’s belief that the universe is mysterious, meaningful, and shaped by moral choices.

What makes the quartet exceptional is its refusal to talk down to readers. The ideas are big, strange, and sometimes challenging, but that is part of the magic. L’Engle trusts readers to think deeply and feel deeply. Her writing is poetic, emotional, and full of wonder.

The Time Quartet is a rich, thoughtful, and inspiring series that rewards rereading. It is adventurous, intelligent, heartfelt, and timeless. For its imagination, emotional power, and lasting impact, it deserves a full 5 out of 5 stars.
