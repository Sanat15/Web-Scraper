Rating: 5/5

Fans of Dr. Dorothy Sayers’ hero, Lord Peter Wimsey, have long been intrigued by his brilliant criminal deductions and have always wondered what were his familial roots. Wonder no more! The late Charles Wilfrid Scott-Giles, Fitzalan Pursuivant of Arms Extraordinaire, has provided us with a very detailed history of the Wimsey family, or Guimsey as it may have been known by its bearers at the time of their assault on Hastings in 1066.

The book begins by a lengthy chapter describing how Scott-Giles was able to compile this family history, including correspondence with and notes from Dr. Dorothy Sayers and the discovery of a 24-foot long genealogy scroll; the greater part of the family origin was damaged beyond repair, but readable beginning about the time of the Norman Conquest of England. From there, the author describes the way the two types of Wimseys were an integral part of English history, living and fighting near His Majesty every step of the way up to the marriage of our intrepid hero to Harriet Vane, and includes a description of the Vane escutcheon next to that of His Lordship’s.

It would be useful to the reader who is rusty on English history to brush up a bit in order to fully appreciate the true contribution of the Wimsey family to the land they helped to conquer and learned to love with all their hearts. Otherwise, just enjoy the book and learn how Lord Peter’s family created the traditions of loyalty and service – in some of the members - so evident in Sayers’ descriptions of His Lordship’s many adventures.

It is certainly time for an enterprising historian/genealogist to trace the origins of the Holmes family. I am certain it will provide many clues to the genius of Sherlock!

PS – a synopsis of the Wimsey family history may be found in Wikipedia under the title “Duke of Denver.”
