Rating: 5/5

This book shook me.
The epilogue broke me.
Calling Funny Boy brilliant and poignant doesn't come close. This book goes deeper than language can reach. It's powerful and leaves a quiet heartbreak that stays with you.

Told through the voice of a child, a naive, sensitive boy, the narrative matures with him. You can't pinpoint the exact moment his voice shifts, but you feel it. You sense the quiet growth, the layers of experience settling in, shaping how he sees the world.

The story begins with a young Arjie realising that he is somehow different from those around him. As he begins to explore that inner difference, the world outside him is shifting too, sometimes slowly, sometimes violently. There are personal changes within his family, but also broader, seismic shifts in his community and country. Through the lives and experiences of those around him, Arjie absorbs the harsh truths of the world. He sees how injustice operates, in small, quiet cruelties and in overwhelming systemic ways that crush individuals and entire communities.

Selvadurai brings together themes of queerness, identity, and belonging with the brutal reality of political conflict and terrorism in Sri Lanka, showing how the personal and the political are never truly separate. Arjie's journey of self-discovery runs alongside rising ethnic tensions, and the threat of violence becomes another force shaping who he is and who he's allowed to be.

We grow with Arjie. We hear his thoughts evolve. We feel the weight of each new awareness, each personal and political devastation, through his eyes.

Selvadurai maintains perfection from the very first page to the last.
There aren't enough words to capture the emotional force of Funny Boy. It's brilliant, it's devastating, it's unforgettable, and a new favourite.
