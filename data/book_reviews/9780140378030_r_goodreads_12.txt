Rating: 3/5

This is a very quirky, fun read even though there are some quite dark topics: child abuse, unwanted children, egomania, etc. I believe it's young adult fiction. This leads me to a realization I've had a few times. We flippantly give young adults dark art to consume in school: Poe, Lord of the Flies, 1984, Romeo and Juliet, etc. Why? Let me use this work to reflect on that. 

All the youth in this story are surviving amidst a hurried, dysfunctional adult environment. Sure, we all grow up in such an environment to an extent, but works like this one take it one step further. They add serious and dangerous elements such as the main character choosing not to speak. That is a very serious decision, yet the main character seems to suffer little to no consequence. Likewise, her sister gets pregnant and almost kills herself driving in demolition derbies with similar little to no consequence. There are even more examples. 

Sure, such topics coupled with escapism fit young adulthood very well, but is the art a mirror of young adulthood or vice versa? Why does it become mature and not for young adults when the consequences are real and final? Is that because older adults have experienced the consequences of their actions?

This is a fun read, yet it did disturb me society seems to continue to push such art on young adults. The shallow ending with life appearing to move on flippantly in spite of horrendous, criminal and psychopathic character actions bothers me. I think it should bother us that we consistently steer young adults to such works.
