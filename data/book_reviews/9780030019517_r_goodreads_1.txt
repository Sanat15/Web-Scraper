Rating: 4/5

This book is not a memoir of someone’s sublime pubic topiary. Now you’ve recovered from that disappointment, let me re-ignite your sagging interest by explaining this novel takes place in the 1600s (stay with me) and explores the misadventures of a Brazilian plantation owner’s son (come back!), who embroils himself in various deadly escapades, among them traipsing through the scrub with a delusional non-General, incorrectly entering hostile territory to find himself the victim of sustained buggery for yonks and yonks, becoming the only white resident of a plantation run by a black tyrant, and struggling to control the splashing of his seed across many lavishly bosomed young slaves and non-slaves. As a rip-roaring account of the period, Ghose serves up pages of pulsing narrative excitement, while unflinchingly exploring the systemic rape, torture, and murder of the black and Indian slaves roped in by the Portuguese settlers as they attempted to carve a coherent nation known as Brazil. The trilogy of novels is known as The Incredible Brazilian, this one is technically The Native, the others are The Beautiful Empire and A Different World, out of print the lot of ‘em.
