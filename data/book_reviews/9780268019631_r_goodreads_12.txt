Rating: 4/5

I’m happy that I was able to devote the attention to this book that it warrants. I was assigned it with my friends in undergrad, but was not disciplined enough to sit with it and read it.

Chacour is a beautiful example of a creative rebel, seeking nonviolent avenues to end his own experience of oppression, occupation, and division. I’m so inspired by his action in the face of terrifying obstacles. 

I am also incredibly impressed with the honesty that he has about himself. He does not minimize his accomplishments in the way that I would be tempted to. In the same way, he does not hold back his frustrations and negative thoughts about people or circumstances he is in. 

He writes in a way that is entirely void of shame. And it reminds me that my own experience as a human with my various accomplishments and misgivings is not a story that I should share lightly. Rather, I should share it boldly, because the bold proclamation of my life connects others to me. It allows others to find affirmation of their experience. 

But that requires me to trust that I am indeed like other humans. I must believe that I am relatable to others, that they can connect to my experience and, in some way, see a reflection of their own.

Thanks for reading my review that is directly related to the book that I read: We Belong to the Land.
