Rating: 4/5

Another enjoyable trip down memory lane! Although, much like the first time I read this book (a frighteningly long time ago...), I found myself initially struggling to connect with some of the new characters introduced in it.

I very much enjoyed seeing some more growth from Mary-Lou, particularly in the latter chapters. Who would've thought back in the first book that this timid little mouse of a girl would end up braving a life-threatening experience for one of her classmates?

Darrell, as always, has me wondering why she's the protagonist when she's so often overshone by the others. Aside from her aggressive late-night confrontation with Ellen, it's difficult to recall her doing anything actually noteworthy.

There truly is an overabundance of societal stereotyping in Blyton's works, and this one is no exception. Darrell's easy-to-provoke temper, Mary-Lou's emotionally-delicate constitution and Ellen's struggle with academic burnout are all written in such a way that the reader is meant to believe that these are personal failings of the girls themselves, and that they must simply 'buck up' or 'better apply themselves'. It's a testament to the time period, of course: the classic British 'stiff upper lip', and all that. Definitely something to keep as a critical mental note if you're someone who's going to read this series for the first time!
