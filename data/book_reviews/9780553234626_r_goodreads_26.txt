Rating: 4/5

Almost all of Farley Mowat's writings are non-fiction, but this book contains short stories that took place in the Canadian North, where much of his non-fiction writing took place, too. Some stories seem to be ones shared with Farley Mowat by indigenous people, while others clearly grew out of his own mind. The first and last stories feel like they are non-fiction, as he brings out the key elements of snow in the first one and a court case against two "Eskimo" (the term he usually uses in the book for the indigenous residents of this area) men for the killing of a Native woman who had gone mad because of actions by the Canadian government from the 1930s to 1960s. He brings out the harsh life the people in this area had to endure due to both the physical environment, hunger, and actions of the Federal government. Another powerful collection of writings by Farley Mowat.
