Rating: 5/5

I haven’t added anything to my “delights” shelf lately. There have probably been candidates; I should really scroll through my recent reads to identify any. But this one so clearly belongs there that it forced the issue.

Maupin wrote this book at the end of the 70s, and his cultural references made me grin on every other page. The ubiquitous stuffed mushrooms, quiches, macrame and the potential for “fern poisoning”. And of course, free love before the price of HIV was attached.

The characters are the heart of the book. These are people who would be versions of themselves in any time or place, but are their truest selves in San Francisco in the 70s. The house at 28 Barbary Lane is the ideal location to showcase the inimitable combination of personalities, sexes and sexualities of Mary Ann, Mona, Michael, and Brian. Along with the peripheral characters who enter and leave their lives, it’s a delightful stew lightly seasoned by landlady Mrs. Madrigal.

I’m sure that if I were so inclined I could find quibbles - but I’m not so inclined. Full marks for this tale that borders on “heartwarming”, but obviously doesn’t cross over that line because everyone knows I’m allergic to that genre. :-)
