Rating: 4/5

Suburb New York, in the 1950s and 60s: that's the setting for this story about a man and his wife, Frank en April Wheeler. They are struggling with a very early kind of midlife crisis (they're just over 30). Frank is the main character, April is less elaborated. 
Most reviews on this book see it as a criticism on the empty life in the American suburbs. And it certainly is. But to me the strength of the story lies in the wry description of the human condition. The focus is on the existential loneliness and the self delusion of men, and on the delicate relation between the Wheelers, ending in a rather dramatic way. In a way it's related to Albee's Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf?, but Yates takes it to a much more tragic level. 
Also the writing style is special: it seems very conventional, but in reality it is very sophisticated. This book really grabbed me by the throat.
