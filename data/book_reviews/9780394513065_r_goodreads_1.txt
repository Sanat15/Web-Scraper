Rating: 3/5

This book ultimately reads like a textbook in a History 101 course. The author, Lena Hornes' daughter Gail, indicates early on that she found a bunch of family memorabilia in a trunk. In the end the book reads as if she took all that memorabilia and wrote about it interspersing paragraphs on what was happening in the world with what was happening with different members of her family. Interesting in a clinical kind of way, I would have liked more depth, not an expose (who was sleeping with who etc.) but more reflection. She touches on the Hollywood blacklisting, WWII, civil rights, all of which is basically skimmed. In the end, I find myself wanting to read another biography of Lena Horne in hopes of learning more.
