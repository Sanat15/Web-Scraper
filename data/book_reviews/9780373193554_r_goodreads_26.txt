Rating: 4/5

I am currently reading Callaghan’s Bride and was surprised to learn about the Hart brothers' backgrounds in politics and the army. When I read A Long, Tall Texan Summer (with the Christmas cowboy prequel), the brothers seemed unpolished, but they are definitely charming and rugged here! However, I still struggle with the typical Diana Palmer trope where grown men act mean to much younger women. Callaghan "Cag" Hart acts like a complete nutcase early on—throwing a huge fit and almost assaulting Tess's boundaries over a simple birthday cake.Despite his anger, Cag clearly cares for Tess Brady. He bought her a beautiful 14-karat gold necklace for her birthday, even though he continuously tells her she can leave the ranch. Their 16-year age gap makes him push her away out of fear she will leave him for a younger man. The dynamic reflects how much romance standards have shifted by 2026. The plot takes a dark turn when Tess is attacked by a sleazy doctor introduced by her late mother, revealing her past trauma and why she is guarded around men. Cag's response should have been pure comfort rather than seduction.The ending is classic romance chaos. After her mother passes away, Tess thinks she is inheriting a million dollars, but bad investments leave her broke. Meanwhile, the Hart brothers trick a drunk Cag into her bed, trapping him into marriage. Cag initially storms off thinking he was trapped, but after Tess leaves for a month, he tracks her down through her attorney. It is a great addition to the Long, Tall Texans series, even if the forced marriage plot was totally wild!
