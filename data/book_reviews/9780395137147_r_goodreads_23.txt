Rating: 3/5

Wiseman, Anne M. Adventures of the Greek Heroes (1961). Heroes, motif- death. Story-How Perseus Slayed the Gorgon. In my mind, I envisioned this story as the sequel to Varitan’s Medusa story in his “Illustrated Greek Myths” story. The setting is Ancient Greece, where we find the hero Perseus at the shore of a freezing sea, conversing with the Three Gray Sisters. These figures are seen in different myths, with my latest recollection of them being the witches in Shakespeare’s Macbeth. After inquiring about the gorgon with them, he is sent to a city of lonely maidens who keep asking Perseus to stay and play with them (whoa, convenient). However, Perseus refuses and says that he must slay the gorgon. The maidens give him the hat of darkness so that he can be protected and not seen, thus not being able to be turned to stone. Once at the gorgon’s island, Perseus slays the Gorgon, in one line, which is a bit anticlimactic. He then takes the gorgon’s head with him as a trophy. The story of Perseus reminds me of other Greek heroes such as Odysseus and Achilles, being strong mortal men that overcome evil beasts or wars and become famous because of it. Also, like those other heroes, I found Perseus to be one-dimensional and a bit arrogant, which are traits that I would not really like children audiences to emulate. However, the oral tradition of these stories persist, and should, as much of these heroes are re-tooled and retold for younger generations. While I did not see much substance in this story, I did enjoy being able to analyze just how far these myths transcend and are alluded to in other works. Target audience: 4-9.
