Rating: 3/5

This is a really sad story that must have been difficult to write and to publish back in 1980. The heroine was the victim of a heinous sexual assault when she was a teen and she has carried that trauma all her life, even moving to another part of the country, keeping herself distant from her family, and avoiding men at all costs. The hero comes bulldozing into her life, first to kick her out of her shop’s lease because he is taking over her building for his own vast corporation. Then he takes over her personal space and her heart, in a whirlwind courtship that results in a disastrous marriage. When the heroine finally “confesses” her trauma to him, he is devastated and agrees to her suggestion of divorce. A thunderstorm and some stray kittens reunites them. 

The author wrote with a lot of sensitivity about the subject of rape, its long term emotional impact, and society’s disgusting and hypocritical attitude of victim-blaming and slut-shaming. The heroine’s rigid, compulsive habit of keeping her hair coiled in a perfect updo, panicking at a few strands escaping, because she doesn’t like to look “untidy” was a heartbreaking demonstration of her fragile state of mind and her obsession with keeping control and order in her life, long after it was so traumatizingly derailed. The heroine’s mother decrying in a moment of despair that her daughter would never be able to have a white wedding and the heroine, seven years later, choosing bronze as the color of her dress because she accepted subconsciously that she should be ashamed and was unworthy to wear a white dress. The fear and revulsion of running into a former acquaintance who talks down and vulgarly to her because she is soiled goods and therefore a target for sexual innuendo. There were so many things the author filled her novel with that allowed the reader to really understand her female protagonist and to root for her. It was really well done in the context of the era it was written in.
