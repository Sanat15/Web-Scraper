Rating: 3/5

A collection of essays which elicited a variety of reactions, including: “funny”, “haha what an ass”, “still relevant today”, “very dated”, “what are you talking about?”, and “man, he hates Methodists.”

Despite his cutting and frequent asides directed against Methodists, Baptists, Presbyterians, fundamentalists, Celts, William Jennings Bryan, the Kiwanis and Rotary clubs, journalists, literature professors, bureaucrats, democrats, Babbitts, chauppaqua lecturers, chiropractors, osteopaths, prohibition agents, prohibitionists, bootleggers, rural people, liberals, patriots, the South, the Mid-West, T. Roosevelt, farmers, moralizers and demagogues, his writing is not bitter or sour. Must be the fat man effect.

In the political realm, the collection offers insight to the changes happening in the US from the perspective of a man who might be called “tory”, conservative, libertarian, anti(world)war, germanophile, Nietzschean, elitist, or just Menckenian.

Regarding this edition, I would have liked there to at least be a date of first publication at each title for the sake of context. NB: All articles are from 1917-1927, and so are only a portion of his career. The book alternates between long and short articles and lends itself to a before-bed read. An interesting work.
