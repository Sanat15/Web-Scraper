Rating: 5/5

A coming-of-age story set in 70's New York, about Esme Singer, who wants to be "different from her parents." She observes her mother's love affairs, her father's get-rich-quick schemes, while trying to define her own identity. 

I picked up this book when I was 12 at a used books store. I really love these sort of coming-of-age stories, so perhaps I'm predisposed to like them, but it was pretty well-written; the peripheral characters are memorable; I love the dialogue and years after, reading this again, I can still relate to Esme's naivete, the process of trying to make herself cultured as if it were homework for her to listen to Tchaikovsky, or like Cartier-Bresson, or have opinions about Chagall, etc. :)

EDIT (April 11, 2014) : I saw that the cover for the book was updated. This is probably the e-book version? I don't really love it. I prefer the cover of the book I own. It's kind of Art Deco and reminds me of a Tamara de Lempicka painting.
