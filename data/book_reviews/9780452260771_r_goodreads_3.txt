Rating: 5/5

The fabulous Edward Gorey illustrations on the front and back covers give this book a distinct appeal. The publisher's description: "A WRY, MACABRE TALE OF SIMPLE LIVING, BRUTAL MURDER, AND A REASONABLY HAPPY COUPLE" make it entirely irresistible. To me, at least.

This is a darling of a book. She's an eccentric British aunt with a few things up her sleeve. Where has this book been hiding, I ask you, why is it only appearing to me now? Do any of you know of this John Bowen person, this writer who cozies you in and then, once he has you, subjects you to the darker (and danker... and stinkier) sides of life? Yes, stinky, even in the charming 1970s world of Cotswolds, England!

I'm rambling. Tony's review of this is better, and it's because of his review, and his mention of Patricia Highsmith, that I immediately sourced my beautiful copy. I see some of the reasons why people mention her name, but my opinion is that Pat Highsmith is inimitable, and is far more serious and brooding than dear Mr. Bowen. So let's leave her to the side for now, and say that this story about two women, partners in love and life (and... death?), is enchanting, revolting, fascinating, entertaining, and ultimately satisfying.

P.S.: There's a scene with a man on a white horse, who shows up in the early morning mist, and who has an excellent sense of smell, and knows just what to say, which is a particularly wonderful bit of writing. You should read it.
