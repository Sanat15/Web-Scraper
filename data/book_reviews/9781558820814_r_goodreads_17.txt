Rating: 3/5

This trilogy of mysteries was honestly pretty lame.

In this one, two professors go missing after one chases the other off the college campus after witnessing him pocket an expensive piece of lab equipment (an innocent student had previously been blamed for the missing equipment). They end up in a near-deadly standoff on a deserted street on the edge of a canyon (because...of course). The professor who pocketed the equipment makes it out fine; the one who was chasing is gravely injured and is basically kidnapped by the crazy old "witch" who lives on the block. She proceeds to convince herself that the man is her long-lost son (who is in prison in real life?).

The missing person investigation ends up being headed up by the professor's wife, because the detectives won't take the case up, thinking he just escaped with a mistress somewhere. The wife's involvement had a nice Nancy Drew feel to it, so that I enjoyed. The mystery itself is basically just a 150 page physical search--there really aren't any tricky clues or misdirections. It turns out that the other professor's young wife had stolen the lab equipment. She ends up killing herself (???) in the end. 

Overall, Charlotte Armstrong seems to be much more fascinated with supernatural-esque characters than mysteries with a logical puzzle at their hearts. This book was pretty dull, with the exception of the wife character, who I liked.
