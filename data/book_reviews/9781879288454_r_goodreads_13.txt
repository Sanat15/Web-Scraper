Rating: 3/5

Julian of Norwich is a English mystic who experienced a series of divine visions in the late 1300's, which she set down in two texts, the first called the Short Text was composed in 1373. The Long Text was completed about twenty years later. These divine revelations are focused on God's desire to share with Julian how much God loves His people and wants them to know that God is very willing and desirous to share His forgiveness with those who would acknowledge their sinful ways as well as their inability to atone for sin outside of Christ's love. 

The Short Text shares her revelations and is divided into 25 short chapters. I found chapter 9 to be the most interesting, which God shares a vision of supreme joy with Julian as well as a vision of suffering and solitude. The Long Text repeats the layout of the "showings" as Julian calls them, but then goes into some of Julian's speculations on theology that she has gleaned from her showings. I am very glad that I got around to this text. It is a very valuable read. The reading did seem to be a bit repetitive at times. I learned a lot about the Medieval mind set from reading this, which I greatly appreciate. In comparison to another text from this time period, that I recently read, The Cloud of Unknowing, I preferred Julian's text.
