Rating: 2/5

"Did you ever cross the South China Sea in a forty-foot boat during the typhoon season?"

Odd way to start a story, as I'd assume for most of us the answer is "um...no?" 

Anyway...I thought this would be more of l'Amour's "tales of the South Pacific" as in the earlier short story collections Night Over the Solomons: Stories and West from Singapore: Stories, both of which I enjoyed, (the latter much more so, although it was the former that introduced me to the "smatchet" is — look it up). But turns out only two of these previously unpublished stories here are set in the region: the longest (and also the best), "The Diamond of Jeru" which is a specifically Borneo-set adventure,* and the shorter (and inferior) title story. As for the rest, I'm afraid they're either forgettable or (in my case) outright skippable; two are boxing stories, two are crime stories, and one is a Western, (and I've never been a fan of l'Amour's old-timey Westerns).

No dates are available for when these were written; apparently these were "found" stories put together by L'Amour's children/estate some dozen years after his death. L'Amour seems to have lived a pretty colorful life prior to getting pigeonholed as "the Western guy" after WWII (boxer, globetrotting merchant seaman, etc.), and it's mildly interesting to ponder what he might have become it he'd continued writing across a wider variety of genres instead of going full cowboy. Ah, well...

* This story also includes one amusingly/depressingly prescient line: "In my travels around the world, I've noticed that identifying yourself as an American never helps; it just makes the locals get violent or want more money."

"Diamond of Jeru" was made into a 2001 TV movie, starring box-office-poison actor Billy Zane, and is available on YouTube. But this is FYI only; I am NOT suggesting anyone actually watch it!
