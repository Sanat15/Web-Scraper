Rating: 2/5

In my journey through Larry Niven's collection "All the Myriad Ways," I found myself drawn primarily to one standout story involving fog and parallel universes. This particular tale offered substantial philosophical depth, inviting genuine contemplation about the nature of reality and choice.
However, the remaining stories, while competently crafted, didn't provide the same level of intellectual engagement I was seeking. They felt more like traditional narratives without the philosophical underpinnings that would have given me more to ponder.
I would have gladly read an expanded novel-length version of the fog story, as its concepts deserved further exploration. While "All the Myriad Ways" is undoubtedly well-written, it highlighted my preference for longer-form fiction that allows ideas to breathe and evolve throughout a complete narrative arc.
