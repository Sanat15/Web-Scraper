Rating: 4/5

I always approach Kay Thorpe's books with mixed feelings. On the one hand, she's a terrific writer, who specializes in flawed, complex characters and situations. But she's doesn't write easy, fluffy romances typically, and her stories are fraught with moral ambiguity, musings on fidelity, almost real-world angst as MCs are drawn together in spite of their reservations and existing relationships. It makes her a compelling, but challenging writer, and I have to be in the mood for her, since at least half the time, her endings are only ambivalently happy. The EA part of HEA is sometimes anyone's guess.

All of this comes into play in The New Owner. On the one hand, so happy I read it--the setting alone is wonderfully done: an isolated rural British community on an island off the coast of Cornwall. But it's not an easy, lightweight read--people get hurt, choices are double-edged, nothing comes easily. But it's the complicated relationship at the center of the book that really engrossed me--intense, emotional, irresistible. Really good stuff that is almost painful to read at times, and only a writer of KT's skill could have pulled this off as a believable HEA.

Thanks to Naksed for turning me onto this one--check out her review for some excellent observations and details.
