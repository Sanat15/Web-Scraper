Rating: 3/5

2.8 "an overwrought(sometimes maudlin) inter-racial, bisexual New York 1950s literary soap opera" stars !!!

 Warm thanx (and apologies) to Spenx and Mirnes who strongly encouraged me to give this a go.

 On topical interest, this would be something my soul and heart craved but my reading experience was only fair with a few moments of sublime appreciation.

 The biggest issues I had was that I did not believe in the motivations and actions of all the players. They were theatrical, over-the-top and in some ways felt very stereotyped despite the variety of themes of queer love, gender and race relations as well as bohemian artistry. I sometimes enjoyed watching them play their roles but more often I was annoyed by pretension and artifice and adolescent grandstanding. I also did not believe in the narrative and much of the dialogue. The prose was clear and strong but often felt that it was more for show rather than deepening experience or connecting to something I cared about.

 Having said that there were three scenes of deep authenticity and beauty that will stay with me for a very long time. These were five star quality and for me elevated the novel to something I am glad I experienced. For those of you that have read the book they are:

1. the initial romance and lovemaking scene between Ida and Vivaldo
2. romantic interlude in France of Eric and Yves
3. Vivaldo's scene of wandering New York and utilizing alcohol and marijuana to escape emotional pain while hanging out with other bohemian types

The majority of the novel was of 2.5 quality but am putting on my 3 star shelf on the strength and authenticity of the abovementioned scenes.

I look forward to trying one more book by this much beloved author.
