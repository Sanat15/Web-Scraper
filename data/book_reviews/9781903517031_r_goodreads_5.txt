Rating: 2/5

I forced myself to finish this book off, just to get it off my TBR, where it has sat for years! Christopher Harris is not a gifted story teller, and if you are looking for fast moving plot, strong characters, action or adventure, then look elsewhere. You will find none of them in this volume.

What you will find is a detailed account of the situation in late 9th century Constantinople. There are sketches of key historical figures - the emperors, Michael III, Basil I, and Leo I. Churchmen like Ignatius of Constantinople and Photius. There are descriptions of all kinds of doctrinal disputes of the time, and the author has clearly researched these at length, and portrays an account that, although clearly fictional, does have some plausibility about it.

The problem is that the author feels compelled to dump all the learning into a dry sequential accounting of events, and his idea of spicing up the story is to describe a series of orgies and debaucheries, none of which seem in any way necessary for the story (such as it is) itself.

I won't be reading any of the author's other books.
