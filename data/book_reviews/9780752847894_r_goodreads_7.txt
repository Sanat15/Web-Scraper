Rating: 4/5

Published as Riptide in the UK and Bluffing Mr Churchill in the USA, the Kindle edition I read came with the American title. 
This is a slightly odd book in that it is number 4 in the series but set in a period before the preceding 3. A further curiosity is that so much of what we have learnt about our hero Troy in the other books is not evident here. He doesn’t play the piano, his wider family and family home are pretty much absent, some of the characters we have come to know are absent. All that said, it is still very good. It is the familiar mix of police work and spooks with a dash of politician thrown in too.
It is set in England in 1941 and concerns the search for a double agent who has escaped German and is thought to be holed up in London. As usual, we have a hefty body count of murder, plus inter-department rivalry (mainly between the police and the spies) and the political and miliary backdrop of World War 2. It is, of course, excellent and I found the description of war time London life to be particularly convincing. Hunting for a spy in the midst of bombed out buildings, displaced people and various émigré groups proves to be a challenge.
Recommended, but read the series in the order they are published.
