Rating: 1/5

Written in 1974, this covers a brief time in the arid life of Jane Clifford, age 32, who teaches Romantic Poetry in the English Department of a college in the midwest. For an unmarried professional woman, she's not focused on her career, on publishing, nor on pursuing tenure (a more realistic goal during the Vietnam Era than it is today). 
Instead Jane is obsessed with Gabriel Weeks, an art historian she met at M.L.A., a married man as bland and inoffensive as herbal tea. During this two-year affair, the lovers have seen each other 13 times and always on his timetable. Remarkably, despite her endless fascination with Gabriel, he barely breathes on the page. Stale bread is more exciting than this character.
Moreover, every chapter is an exercise in inaction and overthinking, even the long section where Jane shops for a dress on 5th Avenue, then leaves it in a cab. 
Three-quarters of the way through this tedious novel, Jane connects with an actor who played a villain in The Fatal Wedding, a man who supposedly "ruined" her Aunt Cleva in 1905. The reader waits for the secret that will be revealed, a rise in energy ... but the episode flat-lines. 

Gale Godwin wrote 14 novels and other books. However, after this unimpressive waddle of words, I doubt I'll look into any of them.
* * N.B.: If you have trouble sleeping, then this yawn of a book might be a remedy.
