Rating: 2/5

Regarded as "A classic of the Chicano urban experience." Reads as if it were a movie script. Interesting portrayal of life in midtown eastern Los Angeles by a young boy of a working-class family of Mexican-Americans. Reads easily and fast paced. Smart-alecky language. Some interesting events, some brutal language, bazaar actions, such as killing a chicken for their weekly meal by holding it up and shooting its neck with a pistol. The last quarter especially weak. Guess the author produced the events and language that he intended, so that it received high marks for what it was. Even for that, I do not rate the story highly.

Like nothing I'd ever read before, however additionally notable because the author's name is a pseudonym. Danny Santiago in 1984 was revealed as being Dan James. During the 1930s and 1940s James had worked in Hollywood and joined the Communist Party. In 1951, three years after he left the Party, he and his wife were "blacklisted" and writing under his own name became impossible and became socially unmoored. They lived in a Mexican American neighborhood in East Los Angeles. Dan regained confidence writing as Danny, being "so much freer than I am myself."
