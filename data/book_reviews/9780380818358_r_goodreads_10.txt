Rating: 2/5

I wanted to like this book, but it seems that the women in the King's Blades books always end up as victims. Also, it seems that each new country is more misogynistic than the last. I'm alright with misogynistic societies in books if the book does something thoughtful with the misogyny, but in this case I don't think there are any interesting things being said. 

The Czarina, Sasha, is one example of how female characters are so often made to be victims, or powerless. Sasha is in a forced marriage (she couldn't refuse the Czar), and loveless (although she turns to Beaumont, a Blade, for comfort). When she does act, it is for the sake of her son, not herself. It is Beaumont that frees her from the marriage by arranging for the death of the Czar, and it is Beaumont that stops the Czar's son from raping her. 

Also, I found it annoying that Beaumont chides Tasha and considers her a spoiled brat. I thought she was coping rather well with what was more or less an abduction, with the threat of rape if she was discovered. She had a minor moment of panicking, then pulled it together. Sadly, though, this only lasts as long as she is in men's clothing. Once she changes back to women's clothing, she fades into the background and really does start acting like a spoiled brat.

Basically, I don't like the treatment of women in these books. If there were minimal female characters I think I would enjoy the book with reservations, but as it is, I was disappointed.
