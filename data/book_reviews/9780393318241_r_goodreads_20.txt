I was drawn to the idea of reading a mystery written in 1985, set among the beautiful “Golden lads and girls” of Oxford. I would have found it appealing when I was younger and if I had read it when it was released and as a follow on after watching, and reading, Brideshead Revisited.

However, I am less receptive now to the antics of overprivileged youth in a location that hosted Cameron and Boris Johnston and their Bullingdon cronies. 

I don’t like the way Fraser characterizes some of the female characters - the way she is demeaning and patronizing towards women who are in subordinate positions to her main character, Jemima.

As for Tiggie, don’t get me started! A tiny, infantile 24-year old, who can wrap men around her little finger and has her own self interest at heart. I think we are supposed to be enchanted by this sprite of a woman, but I found her intensely irritating. Maybe Fraser was going for a recreation of a Nancy Mitford character like Northey, in “Don’t Tell Alfred”, but I don’t think it works at all.
