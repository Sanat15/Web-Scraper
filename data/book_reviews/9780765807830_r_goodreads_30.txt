Rating: 5/5

I've always hated Theseus. And yet I bought and read both books of Mary Renault's Theseus duology. Why? Because she obviously doesn't like him either.

This (short) series is a very innovative take on a mythological retelling, despite being written more than fifty years ago. The supernatural and divine plays a central role, at least from the main character's point of view, but its existence is never actually confirmed. Whatever influence is attributed to the gods, can almost always be explained with more trivial events, and it was thrilling to read how the author converted heavenly force into mortal actions. 

The Bull from the Sea is more action-packed and fast-paced than its precursor novel, since Renault somehow managed to subtly incorporate every detail from the original myth. There were a few twists that left me open-mouthed. One of them in particular, which was only explained in the afterword, so make sure to read that! She obviously researched every source she could lay hands on.

Some reviewers seem to have had the impression that, somehow, the book is sexist. I... really don't understand that. There is an interesting theme of matriarchy (Minoan Crete!) vs patriarchy going on here, but Theseus, while close to being the #1 women-hater in Greek lore, and despite being the story's protagonist, is never depicted in a particularly sympathetic light. Throughout the story, the reader is aware of his every single flaw, mistake and misogynistic action. All of them are written in a strikingly neutral, if not ironic, voice. Non of them seemed to me excused by the author. Which I loved, as I love everything that confirms my dislike of a sworn enemy.

5/5 would bask in Theseus' downfall again
