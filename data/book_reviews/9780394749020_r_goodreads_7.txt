Rating: 4/5

Part II of Justice William O. Doyle's autobiography, the book describes Douglas's time on the Supreme Court. As with part I, Douglas portrays himself without warts. Nevertheless, the book provides interesting insights into his view of the law, gives his opinions on the McCarthy Era and other contemporary events and on his colleagues, and details his relationships with and opinions of presidents from FDR to Nixon. It does not say much about his life outside the court or his world travels. One will need read a biography to discover his warts and his often-controversial life outside the court. There is no indication that he intended to write a part III, and I suspect that with regard to his travels he would tell you to read his other books and that with regard to his personal life he would suggest, in no uncertain terms, that you mind your own business.
