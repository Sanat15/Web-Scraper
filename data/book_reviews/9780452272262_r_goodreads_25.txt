Rating: 4 ill-fated birds out of 5

Fantasy of manners as it should be sans too abrupt ending. Set in 18th century France the almost-period novel about what the peculiar chambermaid saw and experienced plays around with fairytales and magic realism. I recommend for everyone who doesn't enjoy fast-fashion literature and appreciates slow story-building, rich prose (I had to search for the meaning of various words, especially particular pieces of clothing and furniture), many small characters and their small lives and the crawling unrest underneath (caused not only by the call of French revolution but by the much closer workings of the family curse). 

The dry humor is present and much to my liking. The supernatural aspects are scarce at first, the social problems not voiced aloud but hidden in the context which brings gradually more and more unsettling tone.

The book is well-researched, cleverly written and full of references. The sapphic vibes between the narrator and her mistress (I personally pitied and disliked the lady) are somewhat cute though toxic from contemporary pov. I quite enjoy (pseudo)historic novel not focused on the straight romance (this book is not focused on any romance and the narrator seems to be ace although it is not disclosed).

I would love to read more about Pompey and Sorcerer Maid but this story is told by the witness, not the classic heroes. Still the book goes to my favorites shelf with all its folk tale charm and creepiness. 

TW: domestic and sexual violence, racism, murders
