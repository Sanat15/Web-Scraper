Rating: 4/5

This one feels different from the first two — not in tone, but in company.

There is still an ensemble cast, just not the original one. With only Mudge returning as a steady presence (and a brief cameo from Clothahump), the group dynamic shifts quite a bit. I enjoyed the new characters — Roseroar in particular stands out — but I found myself missing the chemistry of the original party. There’s something about those early interactions that gave the first books a certain warmth that’s harder to replicate.

That said, the formula still works. Jon-Tom continues to grow in small, believable ways, and the tone remains comfortably whimsical without drifting into parody. The world is weird, occasionally dangerous, and consistently entertaining.

Talea is referenced often but never really brought into focus, which leaves a bit of emotional space that isn’t fully explored. It doesn’t hurt the story, but it does add to the sense that this installment is more about forward motion than deep character development.

Overall, this is still simple fantasy fun — steady, imaginative, and unapologetically itself. It may not have the same group magic as the earlier books, but it’s an enjoyable continuation of a series that knows exactly what it wants to be.
