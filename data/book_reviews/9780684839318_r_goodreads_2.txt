Rating: 4/5

I picked this book off the shelf on a whim for something new to read and it really was unlike any book I've read, and yet it also managed to be aligned with my general interests in historical homes and figures.

I especially enjoyed the chapters on Presidents Adams (both), Madison, and Lincoln; the look into their homes and life at home was fascinating. I was interested in the chapter on Cherokee Chief Vann's house in Georgia and the Tlingit Clan House in Alaska.

I was haunted by the Rebecca Nurse Homestead in Danvers, formerly Salem village, Massachusetts, and I happened to read it on June 10, the anniversary of the day the first woman, Bridget Bishop, was hung in the Salem Witch Trials.

I was intrigued learning about Julia Morgan and was thrilled William Randolph Hearst, the notorious newspaper publisher, chose a woman to design and build his Hearst Castle in California. I loved the chapter on Eleanor Roosevelt and her Val-Kill Cottage, a former furniture factory, where she spent the last years of her life after the death of the president.

On the whole, a very interesting book to read if you like reading about historical figures and the homes they chose to spend their lives in. My one criticism of the book is that there weren't enough pictures of the homes! The whole purpose of the book is to show off these historical buildings, and there weren't nearly enough pictures to give the reader an idea of what these homes were truly like. There were whole pages without pictures, even two whole pages of text where I thought a picture could have been included. On the other hand, I enjoy looking at pictures of homes to the extent it is unlikely there would ever be enough pictures for me. Hence the 4 stars rather than 5.
