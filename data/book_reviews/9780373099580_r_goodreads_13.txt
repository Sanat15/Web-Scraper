Rating: 3/5

I love the angst of the OW drama. The competition, the torment, and then the triumph of winning out over a (preferably hateful) OW for the H’s love. But you know what I hate? The dead OW. There is no sense of winning anyone over, or being first-choice, when the h is pitted against someone who isn’t alive to put up much of a fight. And, most importantly, we as the readers are never left satisfied that the h is the H’s one true love. This book was tolerable in some of this regard in that the dead OW was the original OW. In the opening scene (really well-written, captivated me from the first) our h is snooping on her husband, the H, who is saying goodbye to the OW, and the h witnesses a passionate kiss between her husband and this OW. Our h tells him to kiss it, they divorce, and H marries the OW, who very soon in the plot then dies of an aneurysm. And the H grieves his lost wife, even though he did always love our h. I really wish the author had kept that OW alive, and then the H could have picked our h over her, and we could have all been left satisfied (and in the case of the OW alive and breathing). But, not meant to be. H enters the h’s life again after OW has passed away, acting as her defense attorney after she is wrongfully accused of murder. The story was well-written, the h is very like-able, and she experiences lots of understandable mental circles – attracted to and still in-love with the H, but not knowing how she can trust him again. Beyond wishing the OW was alive, I had one more gripe with the book - the H does grovel, but I like a deep, painful, grovel and I didn’t quite get that. Still, he does apologize and win the h over all throughout the book, the book was interesting, and both MCs were likeable, even this willy-wally H.
