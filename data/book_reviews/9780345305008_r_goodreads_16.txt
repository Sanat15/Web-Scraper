Rating: 2/5

Consider: a man murders a woman and dumps her body in the middle of the road to look like a hit-and-run victim. Another car sees the corpse, swerves to avoid it, crashes, and catches fire. The body in the road, despite being far from the car crash, is somehow burned enough from it for police to think for a large part of the plot that she was an occupant.

... Am I crazy or is this just a giant lapse in logic? 

Which is a shame because up until the denouement (in which this very confusing assumption by many major characters plays a large part) I was kind of enjoying this book. It's a serviceable mystery, very mechanical, but based on one of those interestingly tangled structures of two seemingly unrelated crimes with a hidden connection between them. It was interesting to follow as both plots thickened but ultimately failed to live up to their intrigue by leading finally to a simplistic solution by an underdeveloped culprit.

Ruth Rendell isn't a bad writer: I just have yet to be really convinced of her talents. She does have a talent for keeping up suspense, and I do kind of like how well she constructs the drab lower-middle class setting -- a place in which vital clues to the mystery are related to how characters can afford such luxuries as refrigerators and washing machines.
