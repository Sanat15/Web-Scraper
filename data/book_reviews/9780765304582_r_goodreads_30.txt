Rating: 3/5

As with the first book, the pace of this one is relatively slow. Whyte seems to like going into languorous detail about things that may interest some readers but not others. If you’re a history aficionado, you would probably eat up the rich detail of what everyone wore to the wedding or what everyone was eating for every meal. For me, some of those details are nice flavor, but not to the level that Whyte does it. I feel that it really bogs down the story. However, I was a bit more prepared for that in this book than I was for the first one, so it was easier to accept. 

Other than that, I do feel that his treatment of the subject is a bit odd in that he doesn’t even get to the forging of the sword until at least the last tenth of the book. Unlike The Skystone, there were fewer related threads of the metal or sword woven into this story and it almost felt tacked on. And the threads we do see are hard to connect to the product until very late in the game.

What he’s tried to do, write a book about the forging of Excalibur (or 2 books really), is admirable, but it’s just not quite there. I’d definitely say that the greater appeal of this story is on the fall of Rome and those who are left behind trying to make a life for themselves. And while may pick up the third book at some point, it will be when I need something with a predictable and familiar feel. 

Oh, and for the love of Pete, Whyte should NOT write romances. When it comes to women, his men are about as one-dimensional as they come…even for the time in which he’s writing. Sheeesh…urine? Really?!
