Rating: 3/5

A rollercoaster of a book, but for probably all the wrong reasons.

So I absolutely hated the main character of the book. Hated, hated, hated. He was a prototypical Arrogant American who decided that the only way to prevent an intergalactic war with another species was by directly attacking them. His reasoning, such as it was, was that the aliens would cower in the face of Earth’s obvious overwhelming might, retreating to their corner of the galaxy so Earthlings could be free to colonize planets as they pleased. The main hinderance to his plan were the humans who believed that peace could only be achieved by diplomacy, thus avoiding the possibility of an intergalactic war. 

Unironically, the MC calls these people “peacemongers” and means this as derisive.

I don’t know anything about Poul Anderson as a person, so I approached all of this as a character study about a specific, maybe even common personality type instead of an author’s manifesto of What Is Right, mainly because the hypocrisy in the messaging was so obvious that it bordered on parody. At no point are we given any reason to believe the aliens are evil, despite what the characters tell us. But they are several scenes that portray the humans as initiating violence for no reason other than their egos. So the first third of the book was painful to get through, to the point where I started to actively want the aliens to win; the second half offered some hope that the protagonist would learn some valuable lesson and recant his jihad, which made me eager to finish; and the final third of the book is one of those “love it or hate it” polarizations.

Personally I hated it. Overall a very well written three star bad take about humanity’s ultimate moral something or other.
