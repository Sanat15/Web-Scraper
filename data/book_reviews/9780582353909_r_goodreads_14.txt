Rating: 4/5

I had heard of this play before reading it, but I had been under the impression that it was only about a man who sells his soul to the devil, to discover that it’s a complex story is a surprise. 

At times, the reading of this play is as intense as its premise but Marlowe breaks it up with comical moments which verge on the slapstick. One scene is with the Pope who feels a spirit is taunting him when it’s Faustus who has been made invisible by Mephistopheles. To watch this scene play out would be funnier than reading it, where I can see Elizabethan audiences of Protestant England laughing loudly at it.

What interests me most about this play is how Marlowe makes that effort to entertain us whilst his chorus delivers a moral message of how wanting knowledge is bad, bad, bad. If we disagree, we just have to look at Faustus and see the price he pays.

But then I look at my bookshelf and the many books I yearn to read, and though I do not have Faustus’s ambition, the message would have to be a lot graver for me to give up my books.
