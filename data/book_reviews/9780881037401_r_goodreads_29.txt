Rating: 5/5

Like most people, I loved A Wrinkle in Time both when I read it as a third-grader and when I re-read it in my 40s. But I had no use for the sequel The Wind in the Door. So how does A Swiftly Tilting Planet rate? As good as A Wrinkle in Time, even for an adult!

Meg Murry, now Meg O’Keefe after marrying Calvin O’Keefe, is pregnant and home for Thanksgiving when her father receives word from the president of the United States that a tinpot Latin American dictator is poised to completely obliterate the planet with nuclear bombs. As in the two previous books in the series, it falls to baby brother Charles Wallace, now 15 and aided by a unicorn from the stars, time travel and telepathic communication with sister Meg, to literally save the world. (If this sounds weird to you, you haven’t read the previous two books.) 

Sure, Madeleine L’Engle has woven a suspenseful fantasy novel that kids and their grownups will enjoy; however, more importantly, she reminds us that small incidents can have unimaginable impacts in the future. Always a good reminder.
