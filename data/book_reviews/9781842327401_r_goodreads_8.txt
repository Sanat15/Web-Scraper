Rating: 4/5

An absurd and nonsensically labyrinthine plot, packed with coincidence more than usually improbable. A wild efflorescence of undisciplined descriptive prose. Basically, Michael Innes was having tons of fun, and didn't let an editor stop him from having said fun. So, while reading the book can be frustrating at times, it is lots of fun to watch an author let himself go and have as much fun as he possibly can. In an Innes book, the appearance of children is often a good sign that he's going to be lighthearted and break a bunch of rules. Bonus points for the physicist and the physicist's teenage son both sounding like Innes himself, and the narration making up psychological reasons for why that is so, when what's really happening is that Innes thought up some good lines improbable for the characterisation and couldn't bring himself to cut them.
