Rating: 4/5

I was all set to give this time travel story, in which Matthew, a modern boy, slips back in the past to bring home Jeremy Visick, a boy who died in Cornwall copper mine a century ago, whose body was never recovered, five stars. It is a vivid depiction of both past and present, and the inexorable pull from the past builds beautifully and movingly towards the horror of the ending. Really really good reading. But then the author betrayed me. After working up the powerful emotional engagement between Matthew and Jeremey's tragic fate, the book throws it all into the trash by having Matthew grinning about how he knows what life was like for kids in the mines. To heck with that. Also Matthew is probably damaged for life by undiagnosed concussions sustained both at school and thanks to his father knocking him down in a fit of rage. Father needs anger management classes. On the plus side Matthew has a great history teacher and I loved this part.
