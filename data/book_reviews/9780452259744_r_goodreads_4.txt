Rating: 3/5

I read this for a class. I think it's a good book, but not a great one. The fact that the author's name is fake so that an old white dude could pretend it was written by a Chicano author does, in fact, take away from the book. I don't blame him for doing it because I know he was a writer working with Chicano youths who had a good idea for a book but knew it would be taken more seriously if it was actually written by someone from the ethinic group he was writing about. What was he supposed to do, throw his idea away? No, I'd say he should have just been honest. He should have used his real name and let people take or leave the book accordingly. At the same time, since he was a man who had been blacklisted from Hollywood during the McCarthy era and written notable works using pseudonyms, I also can understand why he'd be like, "Well, fuck everyone, I'll keep using a pseudonym to my benefit." 

In any case, even if the author had been honest, it would still only be a pretty good but not great book. I liked the parts about how this intelligent young man faced obstacles he could not overcome even if he did everything he could to get the most out of his schooling. Basically, the system was set up against him and he could not get past it. And I'm glad it didn't end happily. I enjoyed the metaphor of this young man trying to use grafitti to force society to notice him. But ultimately, I think this is only a 3 star book, especially considering the author's dishonesty.
