Rating: 3/5

If you enjoy the first chapter, you’ll probably enjoy the book. Narrated in first person and in a tough-guy vernacular that sometimes made me think East Coast rather than Chicago, but never to the point I forgot where I was. I’ve never lived in Chicago but I’ve visited there since I was a little kid. Poked around a bit after I finished the book, and if the author ever lived in Chicago, it’s news to me. He did, however, grow up in New Jersey. 

I am more familiar with Chicago architecture than with Chicago politics but best I can tell he’s presenting an accurate picture for the times. I think now there’s less mob activity and more gang activity, but Chicago politics is still a very “I scratch your back, you scratch mine” sort of thing.

I had problems keeping track of some characters but I am bad with names in general, so this is an ongoing issue with me and complicated mysteries and may not bother most readers. The plot moves along sprightly and I like the main characters.

The Kindle version has bad editing – commas where there shouldn’t be, missing periods, occasionally a line out of place – which is particularly annoying when the author has two characters talking over each other in a complicated commentary.
