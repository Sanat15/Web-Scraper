Looking through my shelves I realized I have this book marked to read, though I actually read it in 2010. I wasn't yet blogging at the time, but this is what I wrote in my Journal Entry on www.bookcrossing.com:

Vicki Lewis Thompson is one of those authors who I love so much that I snatch up everything I can find by her. And this book ultimately lived up to my expectations. But along the way I had a little trouble with the heroine. Ginny is a single mom, raising 13 year old Arnold alone after her husband died five years ago. She is a successful businesswoman, who has completely devoted herself to her business and Arnold. She does not date, and has fostered in Arnold a love of classical music and other such worldly pursuits, dreaming that one day he'll be a famous classical musician. When Arnold joins a summer wrestling team, Ginny is horrified and dismayed. Then Mitch, a teacher and the wrestling coach, comes into their lives, and thank god he did because quite frankly, Ginny was in danger of raising Arnold to be a total mamma/sissy boy. Under Mitch's influence, Arnold begins to flourish, getting a girlfriend and a healthy dose of self-confidence. But Ginny struggles with the new Arnold, feeling left out, forgotten, and betrayed by her young son. She was defintely a heroine I could not relate to in the least. But Mitch, of course, wants to relate to her and is patiently determined to overcome her fears, while hiding a big secret of his own. 

Overall, an enjoyable read, with the author's tradmark humor injected in all the right places.
