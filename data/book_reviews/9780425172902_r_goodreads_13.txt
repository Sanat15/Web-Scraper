This was my favorite technothriller before I even knew what a technothriller was. And it is supposedly based on true top-secret events. I was curious how Tom Clancy got his inspiration for this story. I was curious and did some--admittedly superficial--research. Here’s what happened in real life--probably--maybe. 

Someone who may or may not have been attached to the U.S. defense department and who during a private conversation that may or may not have involved potable liquids told Clancy some very general facts about a 1968 incident in which a fully loaded Soviet missile submarine went missing. The Soviets a mounted frantic search for the sub which led to the U.S. deciding to search for it too, because, as you know, finders are keepers and losers are dirty commie pinkos. According to Clancy, he wasn’t given any more details than that, but these basic facts he heard inspired him to write Red October. 

In recent years, however, after the end of the Cold War, some new information allegedly emerged about the K-129. The Soviet missile submarine indeed left port in 1968 with orders to take up station in the Arctic Ocean, where it would hide from the filthy capitalist imperialist pigs in case Armageddon broke out. The sub was supposed to report by radio to confirm it had reached its designated patrol area, but the designated time and day for reporting came and went. This, of course, caused concern among the Soviets. Had the K-129 been destroyed? Had K-129 gone rogue? A Soviet war ship going rogue was a real possibility in this hellish worker’s paradise. Was it going to fire on the U.S. without orders and start a nuclear war? Was it going to defect to the West? Was its disappearance the beginning of a revolt against the Soviet government? So, the Soviets mounted a huge search, using all their available resources to try to find the sub in the Arctic Ocean. 

The U.S., meanwhile, was just standing around Hawaii with its hand in its pockets, whistling, and acting all innocent. 

🌟🌟🌟🌟🌟 Stars. I won’t continue relating this story because I am tired and going to bed, and because I don’t want to give you any information that might spoil your reading of Red October. I will say, though, that I think Clancy didn’t know any more about the real incident than he admitted to. The "real story," however, is nearly as interesting as Clancy’s fictional version--maybe. Note that according to Wikipedia, the CIA files about the incident are--according to one source--still Top Secret more than 50 years after the event--does that seem suspicious to you? Maybe! According to Google AI, in 2010, the U.S. CIA released some information confirming they had ended up with the K-129. Whatever! I loved Clancy's version whether it really happened or not.
