Rating: 4/5

Dead Man's Ransom is both a familiar and unusual entry in the Brother Cadfael series. We still have the tumult and unrest of civil war, as well as young star-crossed lovers desperate to be with each other, yet separated by circumstances and pledges that prevent them from the happiness they crave. But what makes this book different is that our killer is a sympathetic character; someone who, perhaps ironically, is a good and decent person that you cannot help but root for. As explained by Cadfael himself, the Welsh view murder by degrees, much as our modern criminal justice system does. We also get a hint of Cadfael's pride, being shamed and angered at the friendly joke made about his old age. Perhaps that is the moral of this story--that all of us, young and old, rich and poor, are at the end of the day human, and therefore fallible.
