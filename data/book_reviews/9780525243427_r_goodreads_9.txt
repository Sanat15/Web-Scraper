Rating: 4/5

More fairly a 3.5, but I feel ok giving it a 4. A mostly well-written collection of life snippets from an upper class New England family. Complete with the stereotypes you'd expect; drunk, aloof father, over-burdened mother, with some "poor little rich kids" pathos thrown into the mix. Interesting that only the opening chapter "Hiding" is in first person. I read that "Hiding" was published as a standalone story a few years prior to the novel’s debut. I wonder if the autobiographical nature of the material became a little too prominent, so Minot needed to distance herself for the rest of the book as a 3rd person narrator. Writing about family can be extremely hard, which is also why perhaps I as a reader felt like I was being offered a window, but never really allowed to come in.
