Rating: 4/5

I happened to read about this book in one of the gardening forums, and got it from the library. I enjoyed this book immensely, and I am planning to buy it. Ruth Stout is a very different gardener than anyone you would have known. She questioned the authorities on gardening and did her own experiments. She challenged the written word or the “established” facts of gardening from the “experts”. She is indifferent to convention, not afraid to ignore customs, traditions, and tried her own experiments in the garden – which led to her discovering her unique method of using “hay” as year round mulch.The book is lot more than reading about her method of mulching. Through her folksy way of writing, where she does not hold anything back, you get the feeling that you are with her and she is talking to you. As you read, you will get to know her garden just like your own. You will get to know her family &amp; friends just like your own. Not even once, in the entire book she has given any “advice”, or said this is the “right way” of doing things. She has written about her own experiences and her observations in the garden, such as, this is what she did, this is what worked, or this did not work. She is letting the reader draw the conclusion on her own. Here are some quotes from the book: 
"Gardening is like cooking. Read the recipe and then use your head. A dash of skepticism can do no harm. Go lightly on caution, heavily on adventure and see what comes out. If you make a mistake, what of it? That is one way to learn, and tomorrow is another day".
"Farmers are philosophical. They have learned that it is less wearing to shrug their shoulders than to beat their breasts".
