Rating: 3/5

There were things I didn’t like about this book, but it’s far better than the first book of this series. The writing style is sophisticated, the narrative is tighter, the characters’ motivations are clearer, the forced marriage is an interesting beginning to a romance, and the relationship develops in a more cogent manner. There’s a couple of interesting romantic side stories with the Duke and Duchess’s estrangement and the meeting of Hal and Blanche. There is a real sense of place and this period in history. 

Still, I had problems accepting several things because they just didn’t feel authentic. The supposedly super smart heroine, forced to marry someone she doesn’t know, doesn’t approach the problem directly by questioning her intended about how they can escape the marriage, but instead decides to anger and insult him then claims to be promiscuous, then whines because he’s angry and insulted and treats her like she’s promiscuous. A quotation or two is pleasant; four dozen of them is pretentious. Anxiety about the wedding night might be normal, but hysterical terror seems overly melodramatic. I liked Blanche but the idea that a wife would be best buds with her husband’s former mistress is too far fetched. AND HE HIT HER. I can accept dark elements in a story, especially if those dark elements reflect the historical setting, but at the very least he should have been beyond horrified at himself for hitting her and she should have been incensed. There should have been repercussions and I’m upset that there weren’t. It’s ridiculously irresponsible that there weren’t. 

Based on the improvement from book 1 to book 2, I’ll continue with the next book in the series and hope it’s the story of Hal and Blanche. Blanche has crazy mad knife skills so hopefully Hal has better sense than to backhand her.
