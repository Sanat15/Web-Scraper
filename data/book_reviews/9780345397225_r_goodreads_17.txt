Rating: 3/5

Things I liked:

* Rudy is left on his own for awhile (while Gil and Ingold are off on a mission) and has to learn how to cope with heavy responsibility for the first time in his life. He also has to learn some new magic on his own and spends a lot of time probing the deeper magic mysteries of the Keep. It's an interesting period of discovery for Rudy's character.

* New types of magic.  Non-human magic. Rudy's discovery that the dooic people also use magic was very cool and surprising. 

* Tir and the burden of his memories. A small child coping with the memories of his ancestors. It was well done.

Things I didn't like:

* I felt it could use a little more editing. Sometimes I'd read a few sentences, and then the paragraph seemed to suddenly change topics and I was just confused.

* One of the major battles happened "off screen".

* There was one antagonist character that Rudy had to deal with, but I thought the resolution of that relationship was weak.  I'm talking about the mage-born child, Scala. Rudy has this spoiled, bratty child that he has to teach magic to. He's worried about teaching her anything too dangerous because it's clear that she can't handle the responsibility yet, but on the other hand, they desperately need her to learn magic because not enough people can do it. I was really looking forward to Rudy's struggle to help this child grow into a better person, but then Hambly just killed off Scala instead. It was too quick and easy. 

* Lazy resolution to some problems.  For example, when Rudy used his magic against a villager, and he realized that he had turned that whole village against wizards by harming one of them. Instead of having to spend the time to repair the damaged relationship, the village was immediately wiped out by an ice storm, so there were no consequences for Rudy's behavior.
