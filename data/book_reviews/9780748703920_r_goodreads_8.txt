Rating: 5/5

Oh lordie, this is gripping! I've never read Forsyth before and assumed this would be an airport thriller - but it's so well crafted that it exceeded all my expectations. Having not seen the film, I was pretty pleased to have guessed the date of the assassination attempt as well as spotting the clue to the hiding place of the rifle - though the final scenario is more artistic than I had imagined. 

What really impressed me is the level of detail in the writing: from the tradecraft of the Jackal to the complicated politics of the manhunt, everything feels authentic and plausible. I'd like to think that as a BBC journalist, Forsyth knew enough people to have an insider's knowledge. 

At heart this is a cat-and-mouse plot with France's best detective and an enigmatic, ruthless assassin each faced with his nemesis. We know the history but somehow that doesn't stop the tension ramping up right to the end. 

For all the good stuff, we can't forget we're in the 1960s (the book was published in 1971) from the representation of women who are insistently sexualized and the unpleasant homophobia. 

That aside, I found this an absolute page-turner, all the better for leaving the Jackal unknowable to the end.
