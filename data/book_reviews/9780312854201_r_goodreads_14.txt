Rating: 4/5

"Anyway, did you ever consider that God sent us here so that we could put things right?"
Catherine was quiet for a moment.
"No, I never did," she admitted. "Do you think that's why we always seem to be in the middle of chaos?"
"I don't know," he answered. "But I think that, as long as we are, that's what we should try to do."

Take a Brother Cadfael mystery, blend in Dorothy Sayers' Busman's Honeymoon, add the generational antipathy of a Scooby-Doo cartoon, and the end result could be this novel. The actual mystery here wasn't all that compelling for me but the author knows her history and does a marvelous job for depicting Twelfth Century France - including the excellent portrayals of Abelard and Héloïse. As for the book's two young and newly married protagonists, despite being very well educated for their era - or ours for that matter - they might just remind you of a Medieval Nancy Drew and Joe Hardy foiling a crooked plot of a gang of grumpy middle-aged ne'er-do-wells. But even so there's plenty here to enjoy and I will definitely be continuing on with the series. 

3.5 stars
