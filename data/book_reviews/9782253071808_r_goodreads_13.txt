Rating: 2/5

The bombardment of chaos kept me reading, but eventually became unpalatable.

This novel is the second over-populated Earth novel I have read this year. The book previous was very lean and interesting while this one was very bloated and chaotic. To be fair, the chaos kept me going past my usual crap-cut-off of 100 pages. There are these short chapters with clever snippets of the world and the functionality of the world outside of the story, which felt like I was surfing TV channels (or scrolling through social media for you young'ns). About halfway through the book, this novelty became quite tedious. It was more about wrapping small story beats in a blanket of filler to continue the feel of channel surfing.

There are many moments that add to the very bloated nature of this book. One that really got under my skin was when we first met a character who was an obvious blowhard. He goes on and on in unrelated paragraphs about his thoughts on every matter under the sun. ... Have you ever met a stoner who thinks every thought that comes out of his head is a good one? Yeah, that guy was there. Now, there's something to be said for showing and not telling that someone is a blowhard, but once I had read this particular chapter I went back to count exactly how many pages had wasted my time--46! I get it ... you have made your point. This dude sucks.

There are very interesting thoughts about eugenics woven throughout the narrative, and the way it was written felt experimental--if you are into that sort of thing. I also understand that the author may have been giving the reader a taste of a bloated society through a bloated narrative. However, about two-thirds of the way through ... I was ready to decrease the surplus population myself.
