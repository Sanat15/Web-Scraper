Rating: 3/5

The Winter's Tale is a late romantic play by William Shakespeare. Even though the play is listed under the comedy genre, it blends elements of tragedy and comedy to make it a tragicomedy. Not only does the play defy genre, but it also blurs the line between reality and fantasy. Altogether, The Winter's Tale has received a different treatment compared to Shakespeare's other romantic plays. 

The first three acts of the play generate a sense of tragedy. Leontes, the King of Sicilia, in his unwarranted jealousy, unleashes a chain of events that ends in grief for the King and the Kingdom. The final two acts work towards reconciliation, culminating in a happy ending, thus providing the play with elements of comedy. Thematically, the play is partly intense psychological drama, and partly magic and mystery combined with romance. 

To begin with, I didn't much enjoy the reality-fantasy combination. They weren't in harmony with each other. One didn't complement the other. I didn’t feel that dream-like, ethereal quality that is so often produced by such a combination. Then, the unwarranted jealousy of the King of Sicilia was ridiculous. There was no proper cause to arouse such passionate jealousy in him. The tragic elements built on the absurd premise didn't quite capture me. But the turn of events in Act Three lifted my dampened spirit, and I was able to enjoy the rest. 

Having said that, I honestly think my problem with this play is that I expected something different from what Shakespeare offered. I imagined a wintry atmosphere, but instead, received a wintry tone! If there is anyone to blame for it, I have only to look in the mirror. :)
