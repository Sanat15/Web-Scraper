Rating: 3/5

Humble and hardworking, young Chen Ping did everything his greedy employer Wing Fat asked of him. Up in the mountains to cut wood one day, he drops his axe into a rushing stream, and dismayed at this loss, sits down and begins to weep. Aided by an old man who suddenly appear, Chen Ping regains his axe, after first disclaiming any ownership of the silver and golden axes offered to him. Rewarded for his honesty with a magical axe that does most of his work for him, the young boy shares the story with his employer, who sets out to trick the old man and win the golden axe, only to meet a very different fate...

Having enjoyed a number of other folk and fairy-tales retold and/or illustrated by Demi, I picked up Chen Ping and His Magic Axe with anticipation, and was not disappointed. The story itself is a classic tale about the rewards of honesty, and the punishment of greed, while the artwork was delicately appealing. The style feels somewhat less ornate than other books from this creator that I have encountered, although the decorative motifs are still detailed, and rich colors (particularly red) are used. Perhaps the difference is that there is more white on the page here, than in some later titles (this one was published in 1987), and that the book itself has smaller dimensions. In any case, the result was charming. The story itself felt familiar—I think I have encountered a similar tale before, with a female figure (a fairy? or a goddess?) acting the role of helper—although I can't quite place it. I wish Demi had given her source for the story, so I could track down other tellings, but leaving aside that critique I would recommend this one to young folklore enthusiasts, and to anyone seeking traditional Chinese tales.
