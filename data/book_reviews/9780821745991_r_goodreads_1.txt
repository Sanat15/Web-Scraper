Rating: 4/5

In order to meet the requirements of her deceased father’s will, and inherit a priceless book collection that has been in the family for generations, Jessica Seton must wed by her twenty-third birthday. She’s had many offers of marriage, but her grieving uncle, Jessica’s guardian, has refused one offer after another for Jessica’s hand. With less than a month left before her birthday, Jessica, with the help of John Dryden, a very handsome, very dangerous, privateer, devises a plan which will ensure that she’ll be able to keep the beloved collection, married or not. 

Poetic Justice is a charming, unique tale, and my first taste of Alicia Rasley’s writing. It’s an intriguing story with lots of twists and turns which kept things moving fairly quickly, with a storyline I’ve not read before. While I enjoyed the entire cast of characters, especially John and Jessica, their romance was slow to build and was very tame, sexually speaking. I don’t feel that the lack of romance or physical intimacy detracted from the story at all because, as I mentioned, there’s enough going on to keep things exciting. That said, readers who prefer their historical romances to be heavy on the romance and steam should keep this in mind.

This book was provided by and reviewed for RomCon 2012.
