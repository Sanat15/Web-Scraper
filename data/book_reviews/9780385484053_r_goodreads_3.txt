Rating: 3/5

“I always feel that God is in that fantasy dust - in every atom of it as it billows in great golden waves, settling in sunshine dunes - on and on and on - and even farther into the pictures the eye creates and beyond into imagination. The desert defies the finite. It is grounded in the center of the universe and kisses the skies. You see it, you don’t see it.”
- The sand sifter, Shahrukh Husain.

.
My expectation is high especially when it was sold as women as men in folklore on the book cover. Not only it took a long time to get into the stories but almost none of the stories in this book impressed me. Is it because i am
not familiar with the featured folklores or the way the author rewrote those stories, i just couldnt pinpoint where the fault lies. I still decided to give it a go and finish the book but from 12 stories that ive read, i barely remember one or two from it, the rest just simply forgettable. As much as the concept of the book is interesting, the way she penned it doesnt seems work. Is it thought provoking? Not necessarily. One stories retold a story of how the protagonist wanted a penis more than anything and it didnt sit well with me. The other story downplayed infidelity and made me just wanted to tore the pages away. However, since this is a folklore, maybe these stories should been taken with a grain of salt. Overall, a decently witty and disturbing collection of stories.
