Rating: 5/5

First time reading Emily since high school, and I just didn’t get it back then. I get it now and this book is such a mix of humor and tragedy/misunderstanding/intensity. LMM pulls it all together with such a light touch. The characters shine. I don’t feel as strongly about Emily’s world as I do about Anne’s but Anne’s is so much deeper in me. We’ll see how rereading the next two books puts Emily into my mental world. I do have vague memories of some things in the next two books but I have a feeling they’ll still feel as new as this one. Growing old is for the birds so they say but the depth of one’s understanding when rereading as an older person is a gift. For example, there were several subtle references to A Midsummer Night’s Dream that I get now because of my growth as a reader. Very gratifying. And actually Emily has a similar story of growth in her writing. I love the scenes of her burning her childish early writing efforts. She knows she’s growing and learning and expanding. Reading about her development as a writer and person is very satisfying. 

Reading this trilogy for MontgomerMay with a group of lovely, insightful folks is already a highlight of the month. 

P.S. I love Cousin Jimmy. It just now occurred to me that he’s in the position of one of Shakespeare’s fools. He is nearly always right in his judgments even though he’s simple whereas Aunt Elizabeth is very often wrong. I looove this turning on its head of wisdom and foolishness.
