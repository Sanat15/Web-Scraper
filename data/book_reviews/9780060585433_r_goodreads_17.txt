Rating: 2/5

I felt a bit weird about this one. The book covers the history of the affairs of kings and the lives of their mistresses and wives, but seems to lack the critical and careful language of historical texts. Rather, it is openly biased and at times felt questionable in its understanding of the greater context in which these affairs took place. The author uses scathing language to talk about some of the mistresses, calling them ‘nasty,’ ‘ugly,’ ‘selfish,’ and saying about Wallis Warfield Simpson that her face resembled ‘the metal part of a garden shovel and her body the wooden handle.’ She is one of the few women discussed in the book who we have pictures of, and I found this description to be both inaccurate and inappropriate. Therefore, I had a hard time believing the author’s description of other women in the book and I got the impression that the ‘facts’ put forth in the book were to be taken with a grain of salt.
