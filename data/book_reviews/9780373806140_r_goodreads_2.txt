Rating: 3/5

The Italian's Touch is a nice story. I can't get more enthusiastic than that. It was only Carol Marinelli's 5th book &amp; is quite well written, apart from the brief sex scene.

ED nurse Fleur Hadley is returning to work after her police officer husband Rory died in the same ED after a car smash two &amp; a half years ago. She's since become over-protective of their 7, soon to turn 8 year old, son Alex. (Why are there so many only children in novels? I know hardly any in real life. Alex &amp; his 2 best friends are all only children, as was Rory, &amp; there's no mention of Fleur having any family other than her elderly parents-in-law.) And on the subject of cliches, MMC, ED doctor Mario, has blue eyes, as do almost all romance heroes, an unlikely combination with his black hair &amp; olive skin. Roman Mario is on a 12 month working holiday in Melbourne, visiting his sister &amp; her family &amp; also taking a breather from his matchmaking parents.

Fleur is hesitant about starting a relationship, especially as Mario is, of course, drop-dead gorgeous whilst she considers herself to be average looking, &amp; he only has another 6 months in Australia. She's also something of a worrier. The story is pleasant enough, no real dramas until the last 3 chapters. I did think that what her colleagues did to bring Fleur &amp; Mario back together was rather unlikely.
