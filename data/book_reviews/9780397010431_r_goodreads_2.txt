Rating: 3/5

This book is a great read for any Bloomsbury fan, but it definitely has its flaws.

Positives:
- focuses on the menfolk of Bloomsbury, which is a somewhat unusual perspective
- goes back to Bloomsbury's "roots" in Cambridge
- compelling (if not entirely believable) characterization of the principle figures
- weaves the social web beautifully

Negatives:
- DATED
- ... which leads Edel to a strange over-reliance on pop psychoanalysis (i.e. fixation on the mother explaining Strachey's personality) 
- ... and to make some extremely simplistic conclusions about relationships in Bloomsbury based on his perception of the gender binary (how "men act" vs. how "women act")
- also ends somewhat abruptly! I'd love a few more chapters, even with Edel's weirdness.

Still a great read, but I wouldn't rely on it for a complete picture of Bloomsbury.
