Rating: 3/5

Curiosity got the better of me with this one. While reading another book about a woman who owns a second-hand bookstore, this title was mentioned as a book the character recommended. So, as a longtime fan of Agatha Christie—especially her iconic characters Hercule Poirot and Miss Marple—I decided to request it from my local library.

Going in with very little background, I quickly discovered this was not a traditional Christie mystery. First published in 1944, the novel is set in Ancient Egypt around 2000 B.C., making it her only historical mystery. While that alone makes it an intriguing departure, it’s also far outside my usual reading preferences.

The story centers on an Egyptian family thrown into turmoil by the father’s controversial household decisions—particularly when he brings a much younger woman into the home to oversee his sons and manage domestic affairs. When she is later found dead at the base of a cliff, the central question emerges: did she fall, or was she pushed—and by whom?

Christie complicates matters further as more deaths follow, layering the mystery with suspicion and tension. With no forensic science to rely on, the investigation leans heavily on witness accounts and character psychology, giving the story more of a psychological mystery feel than a traditional detective-driven one.

To her credit, Christie weaves elements of ancient Egyptian culture, customs, and spiritual beliefs into the narrative with care. However, the addition of a romantic subplot detracts from the core mystery, diluting the tension rather than enhancing it.

Ultimately, while the premise is certainly unusual and full of potential, the execution falls short. The resolution feels anticlimactic, leaving the story less impactful than expected. For a Christie novel, this one is more notable for its setting than for its mystery.

Still, even if disappointed with this one, I still enjoy continuing to read and review Madame Christie.
