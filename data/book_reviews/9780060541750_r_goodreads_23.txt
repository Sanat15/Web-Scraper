Rating: 5/5

Dylan Moore is a talented musician and an even more talented composer. A head injury gives him tinnitus, and he cannot hear the music above the ringing. With a career in ruins, a muse saves him, and he'll do anything to gain her favor.... including look for her for years only to find her years later playing in the orchestra at a ball. Dylan offers Grace the position of governess to his daughter, and is utterly transparent that he wants her in his bed and will bide his time until she realizes the same. but Grace was married to an artist before, and cannot bear to be another man's muse. 

After a disappointing read with Guilty Pleasures, I really loved His Every Kiss. I don't always like a governess trope, I rarely like the mistress trope, and yet somehow Laura Lee Guhrke makes this work really well for me. I think because Dylan is utterly transparent about his desire to get Grace into his bed, it has the opposite power effect a governess book has. There's nothing predatory about it, because while she's desperate for money, he lets her control the pace at which she'll accept his pursuit. Dylan's daughter was an unexpected pleasure for me. She arrives at Dylan's house, unbeknownst to him, and suddenly he has to learn how to parent an 11-year-old. Grace has a firm hand, and watching his daughter try to push her own boundaries was a lot of fun. She's a precious musical genius, so she perhaps seems older than 11, but Grace firmly treats her age appropriately.
