Rating: 4/5

This was an interesting, and very literary, take on Faust, with a bit of metaphorical vampirism thrown in. There is nothing explicit here, neither violence nor sex, but there is quite a bit of implied sex. 
The narrator's friend, Edward, becomes a major literary figure after the death of a major author. That is the premise of this subtle chiller, which the author uses to skewer the literati and question the trade-offs of fame. His conclusion seems to be that artists need to be true to themselves, but for everyone else, that way spells doom.
Still, despite that rather sobering opinion, this is a fantastic read, quick and easy yet containing quite a bit to mull over. Anyone who digs more literate and intelligent horror would do well to pick this up.
