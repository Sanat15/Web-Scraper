Rating: 2/5

One of the most boring books on my shelf. It is based results from surveys done by a data-mining (for profit) company. It appears they already got paid by the marketing companies that wanted the data, but I guess it is still not enough, so they published this book to try to make the most of the work they've done. 

There is no ground-breaking insights to speak of. The data is interpreted from a heavily Canada perspective. Most social sciences are already not as robust as natural sciences, this 'surveying of public attitude' is probably on the lower end of the social sciences. Although the large data set were processed with scientific methods (mostly statistical), the observations drawn from it at the end were anything but objective.
