Rating: 5/5

The first book I bought for my kids was a collection of Hans Christian Andersen's fairy tales, carefully selected for them because honestly, not all fairy tales are morally good.

To be honest, I didn't enjoy his stories much when I was a kid. Cunning and deceitful tricksters often seemed to be rewarded more than honest people. The little girl who sell matches died a tragic death in the cold street, and the naked foolish king was ridiculed but suffered no real consequences while our protagonists in these tales experienced disasters, struggles, undeserved good fortune and misfortune, bittersweet love, and loyalty that was either rewarded or unrewarded. This is not fair.

It was only later in life that I realized perfection was always an imperfect idea and imperfection is the reality of life. It's these imperfections that make life rich and fulfilling.

As I grew up, I instinctively disliked those who were always showing off. I despised those who gained glory by trampling on the efforts of others. Such people had only hard and withered souls. Only those who bravely face life's hardships are worthy of respect.
