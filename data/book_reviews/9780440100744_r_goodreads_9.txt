Rating: 3/5

This memoir is a window into Margaret Craven and her era. Very simple, very elemental. Not anywhere into the action or plot of her masterpiece, I Heard the Owl Call My Name. Which is one of the best fiction books (top 10 for sure) I've read in my lifetime. 

This shows the nature of her simplicity and contained spirit. It reads choppy at the beginning and when past the half got much better. The Northwest wilderness (USA) and her Stanford locations were her base worlds- and it seemed it always remained so. It also might be a decent window into jobs women almost always had then. Much more secretarial than for the last 30 years. Perhaps even if the title of your job was Journalist. 

She seems very distant at points in this- and not at all exposing of her depth beyond that of belief. Almost insolating her identity, keeping it close. Not a sharer of guts (effusive emotions) as so exposed in memoir in today's market. She was a good person. But reading this was not a large door into seeing her, just more pictorials of her times and place. Only my own opinion.
