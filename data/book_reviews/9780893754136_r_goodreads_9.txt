Rating: 5/5

Vast narrative pleasure. The adventure is set some time after the failed Jacobite Rising of 1745–46, which was the final and most famous attempt to restore the exiled Stuart dynasty to the British throne. I do not know how closely the narrative hews to history, but used as fictional propellent, it works quite wonderfully.

Our narrator, David Balfour, all of eighteen, must see his old uncle to claim his inheritance. This miserly uncle, however, has him kidnapped and sent to the Carolinas to be enslaved. But the ship wrecks west of Scotland, he makes it to shore, and must travel through the Highlands full of clan rivalries to return and claim his legacy.

All the characters jump vividly from the page, even those around for only a page or two. RLS is a deft hand with dialect so the reader doesn’t drown in arcane spellings. Occasionally a footnote is used to translate a Scots word, but this is unobtrusive. I can see why Jorge Luis Borges was wild about him.
