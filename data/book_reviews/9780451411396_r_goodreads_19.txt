Rating: 1/5

The first three books in this series are mildly entertaining "cozy mysteries". Melrose Plant can be a bit of an annoying snob but Richard Jury is likable and the setting and stories are fairly clever. I can't say the same of 'The Dirty Duck'. 

Richard Jury is his usual self and the little boy was entertaining but everyone else in this book range from unlikable to horrid. There's an angry, ugly tone to this book. Every character, from the victims to the suspects, are presented as trashy, vulgar, despicable people. A number of people seem to be giddy that there's been a "sex crime", even though the police never refer to the murders as such, and no one other than Jury's boss seems to care that someone is gutting tourists.

When not focusing on the serial killings or the little boy's story, the book features chapter after boring chapter on theories about Christopher Marlowe. I'll admit, I began to skim the discussions on Marlowe and Shakespeare just so I could finish the book and see who the killer was. And what an eye-rolling resolution that was!
