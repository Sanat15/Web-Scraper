Rating: 5/5

Renowned Canadian author Margaret Laurence gives us a masterclass on creating an utterly unlikeable but nonetheless captivating point-of-view character. I'd never heard of Laurence until recently, but based on this I'm going to be reading more of her work. 

Though this novel was published in the early 1960's, it feels like it could have been written today in a lot of ways. As an American Midwesterner, I recognize a lot of the traits of the main character, Hagar Shipley, as over her long life she created a prison for herself with sheer stubbornness, pride, strength of will, and closed-mindedness.

A very impressive novel and a classic of Canadian literature.
