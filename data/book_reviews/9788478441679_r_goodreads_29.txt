Rating: 4/5

R.I.P. Cees Nooteboom, who died last month. He used to complain that he was more popular abroad than in his native Netherlands and indeed I had only read one other book by him at school. That book, Rituals, was instrumental for me, because the protagonist at some point drinks his first whisky and observes it tastes like “smoke and nuts”. Ever since reading that, I wanted to taste whisky myself and, years later, became a fan of the most smokey-tasting whiskies.
That aside, it was the author’s death that inspired me to read a second book, which is something of a literary travelogue, composed of the author’s many trips through Spain in the 1980s. Given that Spain tends to develop slowly, most of what he wrote is still valid and I easily managed to create an itinerary for a future vacation based on the first half of the book.
The second half is less directional, though, and sees the author visiting yet another church in yet another tiny village where time stood still. This is all paired with historical and literary ruminations that are sometimes good, sometimes not so. Parts were tough going, simply because of an overload of similar descriptions. 
Kudos to the author for making me a whisky-drinker on the one hand and inspiring me for my next vacation on the other hand. I will read another novel of his sometime.
