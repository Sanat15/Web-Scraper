Rating: 2/5

In 1870 while on an exploratory expedition in what is now Yellowstone National Park (formal park designation did not occur until 1872) Truman Everts became separated from the other members of his party. Despite his frantic attempts to relocate the others and their attempts to find him, he became hopelessly lost in a little-known wilderness. After 37 days of wandering, he was finally discovered and rescued about 50 miles from where he first became lost.

Although this is a very interesting story because of the hardships Mr. Everts endured in such a spectacular wilderness wonderland (perhaps my favorite place in the whole world), reading this story in the embellished and contrived language of the author and his editors (who were exploiting his story to sell newspapers) was quite annoying. And to top it off, while I appreciate the Mr. Everts' difficulties, I could not keep from almost laughing at what a bumbling fool he had to have been -- to get separated in the first place, then to head off in the wrong direction (repeatedly) trying to relocate them, then to lose his horse, then to ignore the pre-designated plan of what to do should one become separated, then to accidentally start a forest fire which injured him, then to almost lose a shoe and his magnifying lens (which he needed for starting fires), and throughout all of 37 days to manage a net of only 50 miles!

It was a short read -- a total of about 75 pages with some interesting notes to illuminate the reader as to the geography and history of the account -- so it was worth reading. But I'm glad it was short.
