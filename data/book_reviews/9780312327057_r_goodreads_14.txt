Rating: 5/5

The book was very cinematic (I could imagine the leads being played by David Duchovny and Ron Perlman) , reading like an 90s action flick with its frequent smoking scenes and dialogue. 
The beginning was a bit drawn out and some parts were repetitive but overall I enjoyed the book. The authors take on jinn is refreshing, portraying them more accurately to tradition than the west’s interpretation of them. 
Instead of wish granting genies portrayed in western culture he writes of beastly humanoids like the ones Muslim children (such as myself) were taught to fear. 
I also loved the scientific aspect he used to explain the creatures. There was clearly a lot of research put into this book. It was unclear to me whether Sidina was meant to be the prophet Mohammed’s nephew (Ali) and while I do not, some people may find that offensive. The transition from brogan to sidina I feel was too abrupt, being given no transition period to adjust. I understand this was most probably intended to act as an element of surprise but for me it cheapened the character that I’d grown attached to. 
I felt that brogan wouldn’t so easily abandon his children, even if it was to reunite with his wife. 
Overall, the book kept me reading until the early hours of the morning more than once and I appreciate the effort put into it and it was definitely an awesome book.
