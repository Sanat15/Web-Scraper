Rating: 4/5

What a story. The author did a great job capturing the remarkable journeys of Shackleton (especially the last one on the Endurance), weaving together excerpts from crew journals and his deep knowledge of Antarctic exploration. (I also enjoyed the near-continual roasting of the ignorance of Shackleton and other people from the UK, as they arrogantly dismissed the power of skiing in polar exploration and took forever to realize dogs are important, too -- even bringing horses on one trip instead!) 
It's truly astounding what Shackleton did to survive and lead his men to safety, and a heck of a tale.
There are weird things about the book, too -- the author's attitude toward Shackleton's extramarital affairs is bizarre and jarring, and the author attributes a lot to nationality (much of what Shackleton did was, apparently, primarily because he was Irish). 
I didn't love that, but the book was still a great accomplishment and I'm very glad to know more about the incredible details of Shackleton's epic adventure.
