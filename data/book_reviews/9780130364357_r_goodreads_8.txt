Rating: 5/5

I came to this book by way of recommendation from a for-me credible and noted theological scholar. This is to thus acknowledge a telling characteristic of the lens associated with my observations: I am well-read particularly in matters of religion, theology, and human behavior. I am less well-read wrt history. Reading this work is one attempt of many efforts to expand.

Observations:
• I appreciate the attention to source attribution including explicit discussions regarding potential source bias and efforts to suggest views alternative to those asserted by that perhaps-biased source. The discussion regarding Flavius Josephus is illustrative.
• 2,000 years in 325 pages is obviously a task sure to invite critique: too little written about A, too much about B, and inevitable bias. I’m sufficiently under-informed to offer any such specific criticisms. 
• Rather I have observed and applaud clear indicators of attention to providing thorough coverage (not comprehensive) and responsible inference and interpretations. 
• Content is often corroborated by other regarded resources with which I’m familiar; and delightfully contrary to some in well-articulated and reasoned argument.
• One might suppose that history is slower reading, less entertaining, and / or less compelling than well-crafted fiction. I certainly have. This work is slower reading (than fiction) but IMO it is not less entertaining nor less compelling. Surely that is a reflection of authorial expertise and effort, as well as editing and publishing this third edition.
• As a student of scripture I have been and remain more enlightened by the context this work provides to The Bible and other scripture I have studied and yet study. 

Fabulous for these reasons and more.
