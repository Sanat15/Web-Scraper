Rating: 2/5

So while spending a lot of time home during the 2020 lost year and cooking at home a lot, we decided it was time to review what cookbooks were on the shelves. It was time to see if there was something we wanted to make out of these books, or if they were unjustly taking up space and energy from better books. If we couldn’t see ourselves using the books, then we determined whether or not we would want to pass these along to someone who might. 

While there’s a lot to appreciate here with whole grains and natural foods, eschewing wheat, milk, MSG, and salt is not endearing. Because of this, it feels like a fad diet cookbook.

Also, it contains this gem:

“Early Chinese records dated before 2000 B.C. mention soybeans, and some historians credit this humble plant with being the reason for China’s survival as a nation. Whether or not this is true, it was just about their sole source of protein for hundreds of years.” I know this was written when chop suey and chow mein were the primary things considered authentic chinese food, but just damn.
