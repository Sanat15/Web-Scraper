Rating: 3/5

This is a novel from Zebra's Men's Adventure line, and this second novel in the series once again disproves the idea that Zebra saved all of their craziest covers for their horror books. (I don't know who the artist was.) The first book introduced a standard Johnstone hero, an ex-Special Forces veteran, Barry Rivers, who goes to war in an eighteen-wheeled chariot of vengeance to protect his father from criminals and drug dealers and leftist commies and all such nasty people. The baddies were experimenting on helpless human guinea pigs, the fight went nationwide, and Barry and Big Joe were soon leading an army of patriotic truckers, possibly lacking only eleven long-haired friends of Jesus in their chartreuse micro-bus. It was all very silly cartoon story, but Johnstone was a wonderful storyteller, always entertaining even when he might not have realized he was making you laugh and roll your eyes a little. C.W. McCall will enhance the story if you want a soundtrack. Barry is back in this one, having won the battle but lost the war when his fiancé was blown up and he was listed as dead, too. He assumes the handle of Dog (he has a dog named Dog) and is hand-picked by the president to be an underground avenging angel for democracy. He's sent to Dane County, Kentucky, which has been dominated for generations by the Anson family, evil rapists and murderers and degenerates, where he flattens bad guys, kicks butt, and chews bubblegum, only he's all outta bubblegum. (Sorry, wrong adventure.) It's more than a little over-the-top and over-the-line, but it's engrossing, nonetheless.
