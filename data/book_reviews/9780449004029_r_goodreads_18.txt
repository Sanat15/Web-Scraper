I couldn't find any actual helpful advice here other than (1) don't rush in the moment the baby cries, in case she self-soothes; and (2) everybody should take lots of naps, never letting the baby get overtired.
The rest of the advice is basically (3) use cry-it-out or else you're a moron:

"If your baby isn't sleeping well, it's your fault as parents. And he'll grow up to be maladjusted, obese, and suffering from all kinds of mental and physical ailments. In fact, not only is it your fault, there's probably something wrong with your marriage and your own unresolved childhood issues.

How should you sleep-train a baby? You have two options:
* cry-it-out, cold-turkey (i.e. don't check on the baby all night no matter how long or loud it cries), and
* anything else.
I don't want to tell you what to do, so let me just summarize my beliefs and you can decide: cry-it-out is guaranteed to work after just 2 nights, while anything else will take weeks and definitely fail. But of course it's up to you. Haha, jk, seriously you should cry-it-out or else your child is doomed to be fat and sad.

The only problem with cry-it-out? If the baby is sick, of course you should comfort her. But if she's not sick, ignore her cries and be sure not to check in on her. But you can't know if she's sick without checking in on her. So here's my alternative: you should indeed check in on her, but only if she's sick.
What? You're saying that's the same thing I already told you not to do? Who's the doctor here?"

~~~

OK, seriously, if you were looking for a sleep research / baby sleep development literature review, this is thorough and probably well-researched.
But instead, it's marketed as a guide to help tired parents get their kids to fall asleep. This it certainly isn't.
