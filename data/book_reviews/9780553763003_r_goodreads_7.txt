Rating: 4/5

Liked it better than the third Fox book, but couldn't keep the characters straight; and, while the only black character is--thank every deity in the vicinity--not a caricature, I didn't like the subtle racism of him being constantly referred to by his first name, while the white male characters are referred to by last name. The "found family" at Fox's farm was nice, but I can see why Stout didn't want to carry it on: too many characters, too much to keep track of. Good choice of whodunnit.

One other thing that raises it above the level of the Wolfe books: not seeing everything through Archie Goodwin's rather misogynistic eyes means Stout could create a physically less-attractive female character who is developed into a really interesting, three-dimensional individual. Don't tend to get that in the Wolfe books. And, having cringed all the way through the book at the relationship between Nancy and Jeffrey, I was very pleased by how the book ended.
