Rating: 4/5

I read this aloud to my 5 and 9 year olds, who both enjoyed this story. We had just heard Meg Cabot's Avalon High on CD, so it was interesting for them to see a different version of the Arthurian legend. 

This book is marketed towards children, but it comes from a different era of children's literature when authors introduced advanced ideas to children. Unlike other "kids Arthur" books I have seen, the content in this book is just barely sanitized, however. The writer does a great job of including a lot of the adult themes of Arthurian Legend in a way that isn't too graphic that it turns off children.

The book depicts TWO one-night-stands that result in a surprise pregnancy -- it is described subtly enough that young children won't understand what was going on. 

It depicts marital infidelity. 

It depicts people being so consumed by jealously that they kill people who previously were their friends. It also contains a level of warfare violence that is appropriate for the subject matter.

Possibly the best thing I can say about this book is that it does not leave the reader with a sense that battle is fun. Battle and war seems messy, bloody and ultimately a bad choice.

The main problem with it being so true to the source material, however, is that it is just as sexist as the source material. While there are important women in this book who even sometimes engage in active participation of the story, it is mostly a story about men being in charge. However, since this is period fiction, it opens up a chance to speak with children about these issues.
