Rating: 4/5

Steinbeck is a master of examining humanity through the intimate lens of a devoted storyteller.

These stories are interconnected (and follow the same characters) but each one explores different (yet similar) themes and has its own narrative arch. I thoroughly enjoyed the structure of these stories and how they fit together as a whole. 

The title story, The Red Pony, was (as usual for a work of Steinbeck’s) breathtakingly written and unbearably heartbreaking! I was practically holding my breath while I read, hoping to stop the inevitable from happen… whether it happened or not, I won’t say. The brutal yet realistic nature of this story will stay with me (and all of its readers) forever. It’s truly an unfortunate tale. 

The other stories were just a brilliant, with equally honest and quietly devastating commentary on life, family, and humanity as a whole. 

To be honest, I found parts of the book quite (emotionally) hard to read at times because of how certain animals were being treated. Those scenes were part of the main crux of the book’s message, so I understand why they were there. They also served to show the reader each character’s personality and morals by their actions in those situations. This is where Steinbeck’s work shines. Every word, action, and description feels intentional and important. He continues to amaze me.
