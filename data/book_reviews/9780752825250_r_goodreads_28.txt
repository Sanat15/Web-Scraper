Rating: 4/5

WWII has sadly been an untapped mine when it comes to historical fiction for me, it makes sense that the greatest episode of warfare in mankind's history would be a source of incredible storytelling.

This book focused on a key battle in Stalingrad where the Red Army clashed with the Nazis in an epic, destructive confrontation that levelled an entire city into ash and rubble, costing hundreds of thousands of lives.

But it was not the movement of masses of men going at each other in explosive fashion that stole the show in this book, the urban ruin of Stalingrad necessitated a different more subtle strategy to gain a bit of edge between both parties, that edge was provided by soldiers giving fire from long distance behind a scope, snipers.

I quite enjoyed the atmosphere of this book, for some reason I expected the writing to be dense but it flowed simply and painted a clear but horrifying picture of a war torn landscape and its effect on the unfortunate souls trapped in it.

Saitsev, Tania and the other snipers were well developed characters and it was a thrill to follow their missions and the skills used in the execution of them.

The final clash between the Russian master sniper Saitsev and his German opponent, Thorvold was built up to be something quite epic, but I wished the execution had been more than it was. Still a great duel though.

By the end of it I was as exhausted as the soldiers on campaign, and I couldn't wait to finish.
