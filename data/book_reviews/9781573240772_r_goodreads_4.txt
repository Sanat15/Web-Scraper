Rating: 4/5

A powerful book about one family's connectedness and resilience. It is somewhat surpassing how the author was granted such intimate access to a family in the midst of grief, as they were losing their youngest son to AIDS after having lost his older brothers. We see the journey of the parents from tolerance to acceptance and celebration. Their love was a constant presence, even when they did not know how to express it. Finishing this book on the day of the Supreme Court decision on marriage led me to contemplate how the lives of Glen and Guy Nakatani might have been different today.
PS. I read this in a Kindle version. The book deserved a better editor. The number of typos was distracting. I hope the paper copy is better in this respect.
