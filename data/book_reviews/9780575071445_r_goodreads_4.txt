Rating: 5/5

The Witches of Karres is one of my favorites. The first couple of chapters were published as a novelette in the December 1949 issue of Astounding SF magazine, and Schmitz expanded it to novel length for hardback publication in 1966 by Chilton (where the editor was Sterling E. Lanier and he had had enormous success with Herbert's Dune, which all other publishers had passed on). Ace published it in 1968 in mass market paperback, and it was the very first of their famous SF Specials line, with a nifty cover by Leo &amp; Diane Dillon. It's the story of a rather strait-laced spaceman, Captain Pausert of Nikkeldepain, who rescues three young girls who happen to be witches. They're actually highly evolved wielders of psi-powers from the planet Karres. They embark on a series of adventures involving pirates and interstellar warfare and all manner of unlikely hijinks. One somewhat troubling point is that one of the girls decides that she's going to marry the captain despite their age difference, but she's far from mentally childish and it's in the fairy/folk-tale spirit of the concept and there is no hint of anything mildly pedophilic anywhere in the story. It's a wildly fun and happy and innocent space fantasy adventure, one of the best the genre ever produced. A version edited by Eric Flint appeared many years later, with references to smoking and such excised, and Flint also wrote three very enjoyable sequels in company with Mercedes Lackey (the first one, The Wizard of Karres) and Dave Freer (who worked on all three, including The Sorceress of Karres and The Shaman of Karres). It's a lovely, fun and funny book, worth reading and re-reading.
