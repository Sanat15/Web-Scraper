Rating: 3/5

3,5 ⭐
I had high expectations for this one since I always enjoyed Le Fanu's short stories.
However, Uncle Silas sadly fell a bit flat for me.
The way he was described, I anticipated the titular antagonist to be a blend between a Mr. Rochester and an Ann Radcliffe Gothic villain. This was not how he turned out to be.
I also found the supernatural elements unsatisfying as well as some plot points, as they ultimately led nowhere. The novel would have benefitted from being shorter.
What was really interesting however, is the depiction of Silas' daughter, Milly, who was really free-spirited and not at all what you would expect of a Victorian girl and Lady Knollys, Maud's (the narrator's) cousin who was an assertive independent woman. I also liked how the author weaved some Celtic elements into his story.
