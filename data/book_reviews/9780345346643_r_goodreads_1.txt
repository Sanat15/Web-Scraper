Rating: 3/5

Honestly, it's not my favorite. It's not a bad book by any stretch, but it's just not something I'd pick again. 

I took particular umbrage with the unrelenting plot. I can see that for some readers, this sort of fast-paced and blurred style of storytelling can be interesting, but this story left quite a few things to be desired. I felt as though all of the mystery/problem-solving was saved for the last three pages, even though action was happening in every chapter. Maybe I'm a lazy reader, but I feel like one-third of this books plot could have been omitted and still have achieved the same outcome.

I will say that by the end of the book, a small handful of the many (many, many) characters were developed wonderfully. I loved to hate Georgie, and I loved to hope for Johanna and Martin and even Detective Bassett. This character development was not for all, which is understandable, but I would have loved to understand Father Walsh and Mrs. Rocco more. They seemed like devices more than characters, which is a loss for me. Sidenote: I loved the awareness of Detective Bassett when he says that some of the characters are useless. Made me feel seen.

At this point in time, 2024, it reads like a period piece. The Era of Mafioso's seems so distant in our current phase of capitalism that I was able to think of it as a sort of noir. The images in my head were black and white, with all of the characters having that Italian/transatlantic accent. As such, I found myself looking up lots of references to slang of the time, and also lots of World War 1&amp;2 info. The references are subtle, almost entirely escapable, but I found that that knowledge gave me firmer ground to stand on in this otherwise shaky mystery.

Fun read for Italians, mafia history nerds, men who smoke cigars, and women who dress like it's the 50s.

2.8 stars
