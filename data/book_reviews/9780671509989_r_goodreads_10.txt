Rating: 4/5

A hilarious English comedy of mistaken identity and good-natured deceit! This is the first work I’ve read by Goldsmith, but it won’t be the last.

A few favourite quotes:

“There was a time, indeed, I fretted myself about the mistakes of government, like other people; but finding myself every day growing more angry, and the government growing no better, I left it to mend itself.” 🤣

“In this hypocritical age there are few that do not condemn in public what they practise in private, and think they pay every debt to virtue when they praise it.”

“When a girl finds a fellow’s outside to her taste, she then sets about guessing the rest of his furniture. With her, a smooth face stands for good sense, and a genteel figure for every virtue.” 🤣

A really fun romp with some subtle statements on class and societal expectations. I enjoyed it!
