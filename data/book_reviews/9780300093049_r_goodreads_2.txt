To think of the Middle Ages as a time of moralizing rejection of sensible beauty does not merely reflect a superficial knowledge of the texts; it is a radical misunderstanding of the medieval mentality. If an example is needed to clarify things, we have it at hand in the attitude adopted towards beauty by mystics and rigorists. Whatever the latitude, moralists and ascetics are by no means individuals rendered insensitive to the attraction of earthly pleasures; on the contrary, they experience this kind of solicitation with more intensity than others, and it is precisely from this conflict between a reactivity to earthly things, and a tension oriented towards the supernatural that the drama of ascetic discipline is formed.
