35 Years Ago Jeff Smith Changed Comics with Bone.

Now having read several negative reviews of this, I must say to my neighbors the Bone haters: if you merely thought this book was "OK" or love your favorite authors less for thinking this is a masterpiece — you are simply in error. This book is better than OK and absolutely a masterpiece, especially for when it was made. It's one of those things that — like Matisse's influence on frigging colored construction paper — you're simply blind to because it's now part of the furniture.

That historical context aside (which is nearly impossible for me to ignore, thinking as I so often do about the lineage of thought and artifact), it's clear that those who think this — as they affirm in their reviews — did not finish the series. 

After finishing the series YET AGAIN, I went back to the start of it to see if it was as good as it seemed and everything that paid off at the very end was promised in that first volume, and in many instances the first issue. It's amazing. If you've never read it, it's the 35th anniversary for a reason. 

You need to have read this, Maus, Watchmen, Sandman (though maybe get that one free online), The Killing Joke, and several others.

While I have you here, have you tried my graphic novel told in film noir photographs, Cold Brewed?
