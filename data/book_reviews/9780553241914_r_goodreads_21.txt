Well-Balanced Triplet of Very Good Short Stories
May 05, 2021

It's been about two years since I've read any Nero Wolfe books. So, the pleasure I felt reading even the first paragraph of the first story in Rex Stout's 1964 short story compilation [[ASIN:B003V4BPSI "Nero Wolfe, Book 39: Trio for Blunt Instruments"]] might have been due to my having been away so long. Nah. It's because Rex Stout is one of the best writers known. Anyway, this compilation consists of three nicely balanced short stories: "Kill Now – Pay Later" (1961), "Murder is Corny" (previously unpublished) and "Blood Will Tell" (1963). I'm rating each of them (and thus the compilation as well) at a Very Good 4 stars out of 5. A few comments about each of the stories:

- "Kill Now – Pay Later:" Very good writing all around. Some of the characters are a bit silly. But, the biggest issue is that Cramer just misses evidence that he should have and, of course, that's what Wolfe gives us at the end.
- "Murder is Corny:" I was actually pretty upset with this book through most of the story. Firstly, it's a nasty one. Second, right at the start, Cramer shows up to take Archie into custody. The problem is he's (Cramer) pretty much gloating over it. This is the 39th Nero Wolfe book (about a third of them are multi-story compilations). So, we KNOW that Nero Wolfe and Archie have found at least 60 or so murderers over the years. We KNOW Cramer knows this and should actually respect Archie. We also KNOW Cramer knows Archie's pretty darn smart (especially since he works for Nero Wolfe). Yet, Cramer thinks Archie was involved in what appears to be the stupidest murder known to mankind. It just doesn't work that Stout keeps writing Cramer this way. Third, Archie's female "friend" appears to be so stupid that I don't know how she's able to pump blood and breathe at the same time. But, at the end, even though it's not happy, we can appreciate what happened and why.
- "Blood Will Tell:" It's not a big issue, but Archie just seems out of character for much of this story. Of course, maybe he's just in shock because Nero Wolfe takes a job without Archie having to force the issue. Also, there are several things unexplained at the end (Stout even has Nero Wolfe ask about them but then passes them by).

So, again, three nicely balanced, very good short stories in one book. Good stuff.
