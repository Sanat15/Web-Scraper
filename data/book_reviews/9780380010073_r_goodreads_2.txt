Rating: 3/5

Advise &amp; Consent is a very uneven read. Parts of the novel are brilliant. For instance, the character portraits of Brigham Anderson &amp; Orrin Knox are so good that I practically felt like I knew them!  Similarly, the principal story--involving the Leffingwell nomination--is generally quite interesting.  However, A&amp;C also contains enormous plot holes. WARNING: SPOILERS AHEAD.  Take Leffingwell's lies to Congress. The book treats these solely as a moral issue, completely ignoring that lying to Congress is also a crime.  Consider also Knox's struggle with whether to accept the President's offer of support at the next party convention in exchange for his, Knox's, support of the Leffingwell nomination.  Even though it is apparently close to common knowledge that the President is very sick, whether or not the President will actually live until the next convention never enters Knox's considerations. 

As a final note, I was genuinely perplexed by the ancillary story of Crystal Danta's marriage to the son of Orrin Knox.  I kept expecting this plot line to somehow tie-in to the novel's main narrative, but it never did.  So, why was it there?  It served only to make a very lengthy novel even longer.
