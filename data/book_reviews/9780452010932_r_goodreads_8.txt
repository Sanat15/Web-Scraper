Rating: 4/5

My first Dostoevsky! I’m glad I read this edition, which includes both Notes from Underground and The Grand Inquisitor, plus a great introduction and related works. I feel like it’s a pretty good snapshot of what Dostoevsky was all about - namely, the discussion of freedom of will and individuality vs equality. Is man essentially evil or good? How does that shape your world view? 

I also liked the defense of Russian Orthodoxy against Roman Catholicism, that whole subject will always be interesting to me. 

I may not agree with him 100%, but Dostoevsky definitely gets you thinking about big stuff while also entertaining you. Overall, I’m intrigued and will be reading more from him. 

Finally, here is probably my favorite passage from Notes from Underground: 

“And although our life, in this manifestation of it, is often worthless, yet it is life nevertheless and not simply extracting square roots. After all, here I, for instance, quite naturally want to live, in order to satisfy all my faculties for life, and not simply my rational faculty, that is, not simply one-twentieth of all my faculties for life. What does reason know? Reason only knows what it has succeeded in learning (some things it will perhaps never learn; while this is nevertheless no comfort, why not say so frankly?) and human nature acts as a whole, with everything that is in it, consciously or unconsciously, and even if it goes wrong, it lives.”
