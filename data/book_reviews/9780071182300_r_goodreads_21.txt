Rating: 4/5

The newest version of this introduction to film studies tries to cover all aspects including economics, genres and history. The focus is however, on the topics for which the authors are known as academics: the analysis of plot construction and style. There approach has been accused as technicist, but although Bordwell dedicate a lot of attention to technical issues, this is never an end in itself. Instead they highlight what specific techniques are supposed to achieve, and in whcih periods and for which genres they were relevant.
Each of the discussions narrative form, style, shots, editing and sound is based on a variety of examples from film history, while one or two films are more closely analyzed at the end of the chapter. Citizien Kane serves as the example for a fim analysis that synthesizes all of these elements.
Thereafter fiction film genres as well as documentaries and experimental films are discussed.

The discussion of film economics (basically American) and film history are more superficial, and the weaker parts of the book.

Given that this is a book primarily written for studying the films used as examples appear to be well selected. Many are from recent Hollywood productions many readers will know, but then the authors introduce examples from the beginning of film until today and highlight in which tradition the more recent films discussed stand. As a result both the "typical" as well as the outstanding works are well represented. One might however criticize that the selection comes with very few exceptions from an American-European-Soviet-East Asian canon.

In any case, a book that can be absolutely recommended, who wants to deepen his/ her understanding of film art.
