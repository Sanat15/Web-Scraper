Rating: 3/5

Huh. I really don't know about this one. I do know that I didn't and don't feel much about it. I didn't believe it, from the beginning, because of its surreal nature. I thought about Death in Venice (much better, and set in the same city) and The Magus (much worse) and even the film Eyes Wide Shut (similarly bizarre).

A couple is vacationing in Venice, and seems to be afflicted, on and off, by a malaise that isn't uncommon between people who've been romantically involved seven years. They run into a weird couple, a man named Robert and his creepy wife who has a mysterious injury.

Maybe it's their malaise that causes them to return to the weirdos, but an "erotic doom" (I'm using the descriptor for Thomas Mann's classic) ends the book. An inevitable one, perhaps, given the weirdness of Robert and his wife, and the moody trajectory of the story, but one that sort of struck me as clunky. The short novel, which is full of tiny minutia - which, if I'm going to be honest, sort of bored me - ends with a dramatic, "shocking" sexual violence that seems oddly outsized.

So what? I said to myself, as I turned the final page. 

Good question, me. What is Ian McEwan is saying here, that conventional love makes one stupid and eventually bored, needing to escalate to masochistic and even fatal male dominance? Because the couple HAD to be stupid to walk back into Robert's home. They are like sleepwalkers, which contributes to the surreal feeling of the book. And to my own detachment, too, I suppose.

I will no doubt mull over this question for some time, and am interested to hear what others think, as always.
