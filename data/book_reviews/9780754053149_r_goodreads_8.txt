Rating: 3/5

Cookson's strengths — her ear for community, her feel for grit and survival, her instinct for female resilience — are present here. But the book's structural imbalances prevent it from reaching the heights of her most celebrated work.

The pacing is lopsided: the first half moves at a crawl, spending considerable time on scene-setting that does not generate enough narrative tension to justify the length before the murder reframes the whole novel.
The romance arc feels under-constructed. The male lead's feelings are never directly shown — they are relayed secondhand through the grandmother's account, which robs the relationship of the earned intimacy it needs to be convincing.

For a Cookson novel, the emotional stakes feel muted. Her best work makes you feel the weight of every choice her characters make; here, that weight is intermittent.
