Rating: 4/5

If Charles Bragg had only been an artist, he would have been worth spending some time with. His pictures--which are best described as everything from barely developed doodles to full-on frameable portrait-worthy grand compositions--are whimsical, satirical, impressive, and entertaining. He adds the tiniest details to some of his grander pieces that are worth looking for. Anyway, it's a fun romp already. 
Then he has his writing, which is obviously the product of a cynical, deadpan sense of humor that spares nobody in his assessment of what is odd, frustrating, bizarre, terrible, but ultimately entertaining about the world. Appearing as it did in 1994, this marvellous collection would certainly be twenty times bigger if it were commenting on the state of reality in our own surreal times, but it is enough. It is certainly enough.
One bit of advice: take your time.
And here is a personal addendum: Bragg is surely one of the people whom I admire so much that I would love to impersonate him a bit. That is to say, one of my pet fantasies is to put together a book such as this one with my own meanderings, musings, pictures and portraits. Wouldn't it be nice?
