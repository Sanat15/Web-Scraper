Grade/interest level: Upper elementary (3-5)
Reading level: Fountas-Pinnell M/Lexile 550L
Genre: Modern Fantasy/Multicultural

Main Characters: Jacob, Peewee and the Black Snowman
Setting: Inner City/Urban environemnt
POV: First Person

Summary:
This story centers on Jacob, a young African American boy who does not understand his culture and heritage. He believes that everything black is bad. He basis this belief off of his personal life experiences. Jacob and his little brother, Peewee, build a snowman in their backyard out of the dirty, sooty snow that they find. They place a traditional Kente scarf around their snowman and he comes to life. 

Jacob tells the snowman how he feels about being black and the snowman tells him why he thinks he is wrong. Jacob does not give in to the snowman’s views that easily. The snowman comes back at night and tells/shows Jacob strong, brave Africans that represent Jacob’s heritage. 

The next day, the snowman guides Jacob to a burning building that Peewee is inside of. The scarf is only big enough to fit around Jacob and Peewee and as they find their way out of the building the snowman melts away and they are left alone with a better understanding and appreciation of where they came from. 

Classroom use/theme:
I would use this to guide discussions in class on themes such as identity, heritage, culture, discrimination and self-esteem. Although it may seem a little bit dark for young readers, I think that it touches on an issue that many students in urban schools can relate to.
