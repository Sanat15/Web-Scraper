Rating: 4/5

3.5ish stars. 

When it is great, it is great. Like five stars. The first chapter/essay is phenomenal. When you go on a diatribe about strip mining it's a little bloated for what it is. There are also quite a few dated references and ideas here. Comparing the thought of Wired and other 90's magazines as emblematic of the zeitgeist. I was a child in the 90s, that may very well have been the case. But he does a lot of forecasting that seems right because of the cycle of society. 

I wonder what happened to his pessimism about public engagement and idealism in politics when Barack Obama ran and won just eight years after this book? Certainly, he seems prescient now with the mess we're in. Perhaps he'd see the populist wave of idealism as an outlier fad. I tend to think it is more of a pendulum swing. Either way, the case for public space and common things is effective even if his prophesies fall short.
