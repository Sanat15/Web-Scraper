Rating: 5/5

To me Shepard often feels as much a poet as a novelist, with a surprising ability to imbue the most squalid scenes with a sense of magic, or even myth. The future that he has created here is almost cyberpunk in its dystopian feel. But, rather than post-modern, his world seems connected to the past, especially the colonial and post-colonial evils in South and Central America. In the midst of high tech warfare and artificially-induced psychic powers, you get the feel of an interlocking series of magical folktales, both in the main plot, as well as in the stories told by various characters. As in many of Shepard's books, the protagonists are flawed (often deeply) and the deeper you get into the story, the stranger and more hallucinatory it becomes. This is my favorite Lucius Shepard novel, edging out Green Eyes by a hair. 5 stars.
