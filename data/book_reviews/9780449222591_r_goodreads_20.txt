Rating: 3/5

This ended up being an alright read for me (and I’m glad it was on the shorter side). The premise itself was fun, and the humor consistently landed. In particular, I laughed at Harriet’s conversations with Dangerfield about Griselda. The humor had a liveliness that the emotional beats could not quite match.
Harriet herself was one of the stronger elements. I loved that she wasn’t a ‘young miss,’ and that we got to see a capable, independent woman navigating her world on her own terms.  That made the situation with her former friends feel even more disappointing. Both sides were so judgmental that the loss of those friendships felt unnecessarily harsh, and I mostly ended up feeling sorry about what they’d once had. 
Susan was another bright spot, just as interesting as the main characters and far more perceptive than anyone gave her credit for. She absolutely should not have been underestimated.
