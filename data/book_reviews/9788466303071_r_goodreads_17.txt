Rating: 4/5

This is a fascinating memoir covering the life of Adeline Yen Mah from her time as a little girl growing up in pre-Communist era China to her emigration to the USA as a young, newly qualified doctor.

In the main, this is not a nice story. Adeline had a terrible childhood where she suffered considerable neglect and abuse from her immediate family. Considered a 'bad luck baby' due to her mother's death a few months after her birth, she had to endure her father's perpetual disappointment with her and suffer extreme cruelty from her stepmother who saw Adeline as a threat to her own relationship with her husband. 

On the surface, Adeline was from a very privileged background; the fifth daughter of a very wealthy business man and entrepreneur, but she was denied the advantages of this by her father and stepmother who singled her out for very harsh and unjustified treatment. How Adeline managed to survive and flourish despite this is a testament to her strength of will and ambition.

Adeline's family members are brilliantly described. Her stepmother is truly despicable and her father, easily manipulated whilst her brothers and sister are untrustworthy bullies at best. A strong theme in this book is the importance of filial piety within Chinese communities which left Adeline continually seeking the approval of her parents and the affection of her siblings even after their ill-treatment of her. 

As well as providing a captivating account of a decidedly dysfunctional and damaged family life, the book is also an excellent narrative about the evolution of China across phases in its recent history and the impact of that on its peoples and on global migration. Recommended for readers who enjoy history and biography in particular.
