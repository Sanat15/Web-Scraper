Rating: 3/5

If you’re already acquainted with the general arguments of postcolonialism and orientalism etc then the book isn’t saying anything necessarily groundbreaking, but I think it’s nice and important this gap in the literature is being filled because as a prolific enjoyer of travel writing and tales of exploration it’s important to be aware of precisely the harms these genres ultimately reify. Lots of really specific detail and examples which might drag on the casual reader but is to be expected from an academic monograph. I wish there had been a bit more analysis on how exploration -&gt; colonisation -&gt; tourism developed as I think this is quite important in linking the three. The end analysis about globalisation felt a bit tacked on just to fit a contemporaneously trending topic and I wish more analysis had been done about more contemporary travel writers. Hugely glaring gap in discussing post-colonial national identity in South American and NOT discussing Tropicália but I digress. It seems like I’m hating but I think ultimately this book is just a sufficient analysis of what has previously been pretty large gap in the literature so it’s nice that it exists. “Competent” is the way I’d describe it.
