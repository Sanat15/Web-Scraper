Rating: 3/5

One of my least favorite tropes among so very, very many is the heroine who pretends to be easy, a slut, a mistress, insert your preferred bad girl term in order to teach the H a lesson, throw up an emotional shield, etc as it is usually so poorly done. There are authors who can pull it off, but not many. Imagine my surprise and happiness when this heroine’s attempt is much more lighthearted and more carefree. She will pretend to be irresponsible to throw her BF in a better light so the BF can receive her inheritance from her disapproving Uncle. Not exactly the best logic, but oh well. Nice touch when the H thinks she is easy because he hears she’s providing solace to a brown-haired lonely guy. The BF’s dog.

We have some four star moments with the very virginal heroine lusting after the H after she sees him swimming nude. I think the term she used was “Greek god”. He hides his light under a bushel of tweed and some glasses. He does a little eyebrow raising and subtle allusions to the heroine’s bad girl status, but his heart isn’t in it.

There is some horrifically bad parenting off page that drives the heroine which is where the book falls down. I wondered if word count issues derailed a better resolution of the heroine’s two bad parents. The H and h get their HEA, but it comes after just a couple of dinners, a late night kitchen raid and a couple of heartfelt confessions. If Mortimer had done more with the H’s past and resolving the horror story that the h endured I would have gone for four stars. 

Very nice banter and some whimsy.
