Rating: 3/5

2.5 stars - I really like the first two books in this series, but there are a number of things that I think took away from the strength of Stolz’s writing in this 3rd one. First, Martin Hastings was the bully in the first two stories and showed such character growth by the end of the 2nd. That growth did not extend to his character in this one, however. Perhaps when 20 years have passed, an author should not return to the same characters without some similar amount of time changing in the books. I was so disappointed by Martin’s regression at the beginning of this one. Second, Martin’s character is not complimented by the change in illustrators (which is not a reflection of McCully’s skill). Shortall’s version of Martin in the first two is of a boy who is much bigger and meaner than his contemporaries, while McCully presents him as a younger-looking and smaller. This is jarring because I expected a more grown up character, but I got a character who is older by two years, yet looks like a whiny kid. Finally, two turns of phrase by Martin are rather startling. Toward the end of the story, Martin &amp; his family attend a music recital in which his sister is playing a guitar and singing. As Martin reminisces about his own playing of the bugle in the 6th grade during his class's spring performance, he says "he made such a mess of it that if the bugle had been a gun he probably would have blown his brains out.” Then, as he observes his sister walk out on stage in her 'lacy white Mexican peasant dress', he says, "She walked very straight, and in the new dress her development showed more than usual." Neither of these statements are necessary for the story’s plot or theme. Indeed, they are off putting. I will be weeding this, #34 in the 2026 Search &amp; Purge.
