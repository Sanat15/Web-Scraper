Rating: 5/5

There has always been something intrinsically likable about Michael J. Fox, and this generous autobiography gives some clues to his immense appeal. Charisma doesn't just happen. Part of it arises from the blessing of having such confidence and self assuredness from an early age, from strong family connections, continuing with his wife Tracy and their kids. Family Ties, indeed. From my first exposure of him in a show I wasn't watching on a regular basis, I knew he had IT, whatever that may be. His account of his disease, its genesis and what he has done to bring attention to it, is presented without whining or self pity. His choice to appear in tv shows playing against type (not mentioned here, but stints on The Good Wife and Rescue Me along with a hilarious bit on the Stephen Colbert show), only enhance him in my eyes. I fairly devoured this in almost one sitting. Highly recommended.
