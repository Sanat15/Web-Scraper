Rating: 5/5

This is my first Eva Ibbotson book but definitely not my last. It's been on my radar awhile, thanks to the recommendation of author Rachel McMillan, and I've been waiting for a free day to read it straight through. That day finally came yesterday. I started the book mid-morning and finished it in the evening, finding it very hard to put down. 

I won't summarize. I'm a terrible summarizer. I'll just say that this story deeply moved me, and several times brought me to tears. Themes of family, sacrifice, resilience, nature, and community. And romance. A wonderful romance that resonates as much for what is unsaid between Ruth and Quin as for what the author puts on the page. Ibbotson has a brilliant way of making emotion shimmer between the lines. It rises up and grabs you by the heart.

If I had any criticism, it would be that there were some scenes where I wanted more--more of Ruth and Quin together, more conversation, more time being "in" the action of the story. But this is just another example of the draw of Ibboton's writing. 

The other thing I'll say is that some of the references to the onset of World War II sound eerily familiar and left me unsettled and sad, wondering if humans ever really do learn from history. For those of you concerned about graphic depictions, this story is fairly safe from those, but the trauma of loss and displacement is there in full force, both in the written story and in what's left unsaid.

Highly, highly recommended.
