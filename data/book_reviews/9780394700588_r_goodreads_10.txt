What would today be called fun facts (except when they're kind of dull, or transparent lies) about Nathan and H.L. Mencken, Nathan's written by Mencken and vice versa, with an intro and conclusion by Mencken under his usual pseudonym, Owen Hatteras. Why was this pamphlet written? Surely neither one felt the need to self-promote, although Mencken seems to have imagined himself as a a well-known writer from early on, and paid a clipping service from the time he was a newspaper staffer. Maybe it was published privately? 

Anyway, some of the more better and/or more ridiculous ones:

  On Nathan

  Among his most widely quoted retorts is that made by him to the newspaper interviewer who asked him if it was true that a disgruntled theatrical manager named Gest had alluded to him as a “pinhead.” “That,” replied Nathan, “is on the face of it absurd. ‘Pinhead’ is a word of two syllables.”

The claret he commonly serves to his guests costs eighty-five cents a gallon, in quarts. He buys the labels separately.

He can down several hindred olives at a single sitting.
  

  

  On Mencken

  He believes in war so long as it is not for a moral cause. He advocates universal military training on the ground that it causes wars.

Once, on receiving an amorous billet doux from a fair admirer, he sent it back to the writer with a Smart Set rejection slip.

When in New York, every night before retiring he eats a dozen large clams.

One of his best pieces of humor is a pun on “non compos mentis.” I cannot print it.
