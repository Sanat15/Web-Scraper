I don’t 100% agree with this perspective, especially counting for behavior management is so fundamentally against my core beliefs about parental discipline. I remember being four years old at the health department waiting for a vaccine, and witnessing another mom “count” her son to obedience. My mom leaned over and said, “See that, it doesn’t work. The kids just learn to wait as long as possible before they obey.” So that was my bias. 
Still, I like the core concepts of emotionless discipline, expectation of obedience, and patience when parenting. I also realized that my previous conception of counting for behavioral management was incomplete. I had likely witnessed ineffective counting for “start” behaviors rather than obnoxious “stop” behaviors. 
There are some useful things here to consider. This isn’t gentle parenting, with lots of careful explanation and reliance on child cognition. It is definitely more of a behavioral approach, with a system designed to provide consistent consequences and avoid escalating conflict.
