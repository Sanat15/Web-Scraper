Rating: 5/5

I treasure my Collette books because, independent of anything else, she is one of my grandmother's very favorite authors, and my grandmother gave me this, her entire Colette collection. I read them all right away, of course, Every week she would ask me, "have you read Mitsou? What did you think?" And every week I would tell her, "Grandma, you didn't give me Mitsou." We argued about it for two years before she finally found her actual Mitsou and sent it to me.

Sadly, though, I can not tell her what I think of it honestly, because the truth is this: every single scene of Mitsou's life as a dancehall performer, and the author's as a traveling actress, is an amusingly sepia-toned echo of my experiences as a stripper (other than the traveling aspect). The freezing dressing rooms, the quick changes, the inevitability of one's own and others' states of undress. The fun and difficulty of the costumes. The camaraderie among people who probably wouldn't be hanging out with each other otherwise. The glamour and sordid decadence the audience seems to imagine of our lives, vs the total ordinariness you actually find there. 

But, of course, these are not the kind of beautiful truths that I can share with her. Maybe someday, a few years after I've stopped dancing, I'll let her in on some of them. She's good at accepting things when they're over.
