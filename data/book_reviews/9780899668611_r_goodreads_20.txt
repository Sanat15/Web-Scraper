Rating: 3/5

This is one of those books that I've always heard about and wanted to read: from the blurb, I was expecting something scandalously akin to Valley of the Dolls but this is actually a much quieter story of a small American town. I can understand that the topics of teenage sexuality, domestic/sexual abuse, and sex out of marriage were shocking when this came out, not because no-one knew they happened but because they're acknowledged on the page. 

All the same, no-one ever told me that so much of this is following school-age kids. I really struggled with the relentless school topics of the school prom, bullying, feeling alienated, dealing with adolescence etc., and ended up skipping some chapters just to move on. In the end, the big topics are often treated with a surface-level of attention, perhaps something driven by what the publishers thought would be acceptable. The end result is that this feels old-fashioned not because of what it wants to talk about and depict but because it's sketchier than the topics deserve. 

In the end, this was flatter and more tedious than the racy reputation led me to believe.
