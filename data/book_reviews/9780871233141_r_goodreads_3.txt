Rating: 5/5

This is the final book in Phillip Keller’s Shepherd trilogy and it is excellent, excellent reading. 
Keller’s purpose is to show the unique perspective shepherds may have to explain the analogy God has drawn for our understanding of His perfect plan for man’s eternal salvation through His Son’s sacrifice on the cross. 
Keller starts the book with provoking thoughts and questions: “‘THE LAMB OF GOD...’ What an unusual title! Among the countless varieties of animals, why was this particular species chosen most often to portray God’s Son? What special significance does it bear?”
To lead us to answers, Keller guides us through the divine drama unfolded from Genesis 3 to Revelation 5 in seven chapters. He calls them seven Acts.
We start by seeing the Old Testament requirement of continual sacrificing of lambs for temporary forgiveness of sins beginning with Adam, Eve and Abel and moving through Abraham, Moses and the kings. This is all a foretelling of the better thing to come. The ultimate culmination of God’s plan is the New Testament singular sacrifice of the spotless Lamb of God who died for our sins ONCE and FOR ALL. 
I highly recommend this Biblically-sound trilogy. It made me pause and re-evaluate long-held assumptions. 👍 Those assumptions can be misleading!
