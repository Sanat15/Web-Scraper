Rating: 1/5

Largely baffling historical novel. At times I admired the plain brutality with which Wesley writes about World War II, but that's about all I admired. This is due to the novel's actual main concerns being incest, child sexual abuse, and antisemitism. Wesley does not appear to be critiquing these things; the narrative depicts them matter of factly with at best a little shrug, which seems to be the emotional setting of the majority of the characters. 

The novel concludes with a Christian funeral of a Jewish man, thrown by the people who claimed to love him; this funeral is attended by his son, a survivor of Auschwitz, who the novel's main character laments was "not improved" by the camp; said main character reminisces semi-fondly about an older relative who was sexually inappropriate with her, and then goes off to fuck her cousin. Unless Wesley is making a very, very sly case for why the entire country of England should sink into the sea, I can at least remain grateful that she didn't make me doubt, at the end, my growing distaste for her novel.
