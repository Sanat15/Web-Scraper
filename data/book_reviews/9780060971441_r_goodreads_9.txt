Rating: 3/5

I was really impressed with Adler's Speedboat which I read a few years back, and finally got around to reading this, her second novel. Both novels are similar in offering fragmentary accounts, but I thought Speedboat was certainly the more radical and cool novel, and of her two heroines - both who write, both who travel - I felt Jen Fain, the journalist narrator in Speedboat, caught my attention a lot more than Kate Ennis here, who is mulling over whether to end the affair with her lover - or was he about leave her?, whilst bordering on a nervous breakdown, which is clearly evident during her somewhat disturbing 'fleeing from her life' trip to Ireland, and the paranoid state of mind she finds herself in after a fender-bender with a big truck. Ultimately, this is a novel about love, and there was much to admire about the rhythm of Adler's sentences, with certain scenes between the two lovers being quite brilliantly done; however, whilst this novel does have more of a narrative plot, I found it cold and disjointed and didn't truly feel Kate's melancholy heartbreak. I think about Speedboat even now, while this one, regrettably, will not be hanging around in the back of my mind for anywhere near as long. Still, I like Adler's style and I'm glad to have read it.
