Rating: 4/5

Inspector Reginald Brough is chafing at the mandatory, age 60 retirement and so jumps at the chance to collaborate with his young nephew, Nicholas, on a book about a series of murders in Rogano during the Inquisition. The murders bear an eerie similarity to contemporary murders, and there is a question of whether Nicholas' unsettling dreams point to him as the murderer, or are they they called up from his past, and is he the reincarnation of one of two brothers executed for the Inquisition-era crimes? Add to this the release of Peterson, the Mineshaft killer, who may or may not be the serial murderer and may or may not have it in for Brough. And then there's the appearance of the mysterious Galen, who claims to be the reincarnation of the second of the two brothers executed for the Inquisition crimes.
 Unfortunately, author Stephen Knight had me at parallel crimes - a sufficient plot for a mystery novel, with an interesting pairing of a retired detective with an eager, if troubled, researcher. Piling on the Peterson subplot, a host of supernatural elements that call for long passages of exposition grind the pace down and give the reader time to figure out who the present-day killer is. Reading this, I thought of that wonderful Sherlock Holmes quote, that a plotter lacked the supreme gift of the artist which was the knowledge of when to stop.
 Knight wrote very well, and the 4 stars are a reflection of that rather than the overly, and unnecessarily, convoluted plot. Originally published in 1979, it was the only work of fiction from the author who is best known for his theoretical "Jack the Ripper: The Final Solution" which proposed the "royal conspiracy" theory that was the basis for "Murder by Decree" and "From Hell."
