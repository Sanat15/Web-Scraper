Rating: 5/5

Edmund Wilson provides a crystal clear critique of some of the late 19th and early 20th centuries important writers. Wilson has a great understanding of a vast array of authors and books that he can effortlessly switch from one to another to illustrate a point. In the essay on Gertrude Stein he picks out that in ‘Three Lives,’ whilst she probably knows little about her subjects, she still manages to catch ‘the very rhythms and accents of the minds of her heroines’. It is a demonstration that Wilson had a clear grasp of the intentions and subtleties of his authors. 
Highly recommended.
