Rating: 5/5

Such a lovely surprise this book was ! A gamble at Pegasus has paid off.

This book takes the form of two-hundred page dialogue of Neopolitans about a infinite number of topics, all framed within the lens of Naples, a city, as I have learned, which enables a certain character of lackadaisical, idiosyncratic vindication. 

The discussion delves into the Cold War, to Kant &amp; Freud, to matters of love, and to the very essence of life - and each time the point returns, in a silly silly way, to what the Neopolitan framing. An phrase that sums up what am I trying to get at is that, if Hitler were based out of Naples, then an SS officer, driving Jews to a camp, would strike up a conversation with a Jew, discover he was a family man, then let him out on the side of the road for an espresso and turn him back to his home. 

I knew little of how strong of a character the people of Naples find in themselves before reading this, and I have learned that the essential character of this polis is one who works only off of running favours, who takes espresso as an activity requiring intense focus, and who might allow the man who robbed his car to come back and collect his mother’s gold chain which fell off during his crime. 

This is the strangest, most brilliant, most insightfully weird book I have read in a long, long, time. It’s hard to put it into words. Wherever you can find a translated copy of this, read it.
