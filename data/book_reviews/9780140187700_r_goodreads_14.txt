Rating: 5/5

I don't know why people called this book "flawed" back in the twentieth century. Perhaps because it has a somewhat sympathetically Marxist tone. However, it's also an excellent picture of what the giant robber barons like Stanford &amp; Co were really LIKE- and the struggle of ordinary people against a corporate monster, too big for true human comprehension, but at best, built to serve the few at the expense of the many. It's a bit Dickensian and also prefigures Jack London, who must have loved it. But it's a good read. The drama never really stops, sweeps you in, and keeps you with it. The characters are quite believable, if a little ragged and sometimes "stereotypical". I am also reminded of Kesey's Sometimes A Great Notion... you get the feeling he must have loved this too. I avoided this one way too long.
