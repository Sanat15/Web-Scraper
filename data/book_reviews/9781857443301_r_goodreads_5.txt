Rating: 4/5

Are you ever really done reading a chess book? Probably not. 

Kasparov is clearly a chess player first and historian second - many of his sources draw from other chess books or his own personal knowledge of chess history. This makes it interesting and engaging (especially when writing about players that Kasparov knew personally, like Lilienthal) but decidedly amateurish when it comes to writing the intellectual history of modern chess.

Willy Hendriks' book On the Origin of Good Moves discusses this in depth, particularly the idea that Steinitz was the first to outline and use "positional" principles in his games.
