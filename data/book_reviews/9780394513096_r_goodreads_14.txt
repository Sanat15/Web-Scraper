Rating: 4/5

[TLDR: Good book. A very human look at the NBA during the late 70s-early 80s. But why no chapters??]

I picked up this book on recommendation from a former professor, and stayed for the thorough, empathetic writing style of David Halberstam. He provided a detailed look at the ‘79-‘80 Trailblazer team, and discussed large-scale themes/changes within the NBA at the time not through the distant observations of an analyst, but through the direct experience and personalities of players, coaches, and league personnel. It’s a constant temptation within sports writing to create heroes and villains, but Halberstam stuck to portraying people just doing their best, and I loved that.

Lots of interesting themes:
- How did players/coaches/fans respond as the game got faster and more athletic as more and more black athletes filled rosters?
- How did the appeal of TV shape the league, for better and worse?
- How did players/coaches cope with rigor of the season on their minds and bodies?
- How does such a peripatetic lifestyle affect the values and self-image of an athlete (and their families)?
- What’s the true effect of a championship on a team?
- And, something of personal interest: how did the NBA of the 70s/80s compare/contrast to the 30-ish-year-old WNBA of today? 

My one complaint is to whoever let this man create this 460-page book and put only 4 CHAPTERS IN IT! That’s insane! Chapter 3 is literally 400 pages long!! Love ya, David, but surely after all of this basketball research, you know the value of a timeout. Seriously, though, with how thorough the research is, the flow of the book would have benefitted from some more intentional organization, at least from the perspective of a modern reader. 🤓🤷🏻‍♀️

Overall, a worthy read (especially for athletes/coaches), though at times a bit thick!
