Rating: 4/5

Ten Men Dead offers very good insight into the 1980 Maze Hunger Strike. While it is a consuming read, it offers a balanced account of not only the hunger strike, but also the political, social and religious climate in Ireland at that time. The author also does a nice job explaining the context of the strike and prision, which I think dispells many misconceptions surrounding the IRA and its command structure as well as the resulting decisions of the prisoners. I also appreciated the level of detail as it put the story into better context for me -- I could visulaize many places easily. I would have given this five stars, but I felt the inclusion of the fictional text took away from the general plot and served no real purpose. Overall, this book is excellent.
