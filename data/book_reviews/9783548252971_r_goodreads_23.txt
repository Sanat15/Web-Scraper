Rating: 3/5

The House of Thunder

This is one of Dean Koontz’s older novels (originally published under the pseudonym Leigh Nichols), and in my opinion it’s far from his best work. I didn’t hate it—there’s a solid mystery here and enough momentum to keep the pages turning—but I never truly connected with the characters, and that ended up dragging the whole experience down.

Susan in particular feels like an enigma in the wrong way. The story leans heavily on her coma, her recovery, and the slow return of her memories, but her past and even her present condition are kept so thin that it’s hard to care what happens to her. And she isn’t alone—most of the supporting cast suffers from the same problem. A handsome doctor, a friendly nurse, and several faces tied to her past show up along the way, but Koontz gives you very little to hold onto. When you barely know these people, it’s hard to feel anything when the tension ramps up.

To be fair, this is a mystery, and part of the design is that the reader has to piece together what’s really going on. That structure can work. But Koontz has done this sort of thing far better in later, stronger novels like Strangers and Dark Rivers of the Heart, where the characters feel real enough to carry the suspense.

Overall, I’m glad I read it, and I can appreciate what it’s aiming for—but I wouldn’t call it a must-read, even for Koontz fans. If you’re exploring his catalog, there are better places to start.
