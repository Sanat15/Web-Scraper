Rating: 2/5

Although this was suppose to be a nature-type book I found that there was a lot of wolf behavior mentioned that just basically confused me more than it educated me on the animal itself. And that is saying a lot, especially due to the fact that there was a page dedicated to providing the reader with more wolf information, wolf groups and a map of both past as well present wolf habitats in the United States.

 The actual book itself had simple words to understand and follow along for a starting reader although the book does start off with a bit of a dark note as it explains the wolf's mate had been killed in a steel trap by humans. Otherwise the font size was pretty big to help with the reading.

 As mentioned previously there were just too much inconsistencies for me on the wolf behavior. They had the gray wolf howling and standing where he can be seen. Although I am sure lone wolves howl that doesn't seem to be the style they would do so since it would lead them to also being exposed, especially to any packs that may actually own that territory. Another thing that didn't make sense to me was the fact that he never had a pack although he did have a mate. This whole latter set-up seems to imply that wolf packs are more likely to be made when two lonely wolves get together instead of the whole pack hierarchy being challenged by lone wolves or those on the inside trying to raise themselves from a lower ranking.

 Out of all of this, though, I have to definitely say the best part was the illustrations. The book is fairly large while the illustrations take up the whole page and show the animals as well as their environment in full detail although beautifully done.

 All in all it was a decent book but one I would be a bit wary about reading to a child unless they like learning more about wolves than just taking a fictional children's book at face value for the information.
