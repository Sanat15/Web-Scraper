Rating: 5/5

ANOTHER BANGER. The main female character is abrasive and judgmental and quick to anger and impulsive and I respect and love her for it. Unlike the scores of male love interests in historical fiction who are proud declarants of their membership of the He-Man Woman Haters Club, Dierdre's hatred of men isn't because some man broke up with her. She hates them because she witnessed firsthand - and continued to witness because women weren't exactly first class citizens in Regency England - what men (were encouraged and allowed to) do to women. Her hatred is connected to a traumatic childhood event *as well as* a real, understandable frustration and anger over how little power women have over their own lives. 

So as many times as I got frustrated with her when she lashed out viciously or pushed the (insane) hero away, I immediately forgave her. She was fighting a wild and desperate fight against allowing herself to be roped into the thing that would dehumanize her and deprive her of autonomy and render her a glorified servant to a man who could easily put her through the same hell that she watched her mother succumb to.

That's why I gave my bitch a break as often as I did. She spent the entire novel being untethered from the things that gave her her sense of self and control. I love it when she FINALLY admits that she loves the hero, but even until the very end no one can rightly say that she was wrong about the power that husbands are allowed to exert over their wives.

The male main character is an absolute lunatic but I could never stay too mad at him either. Mainly because he would fling himself in front of a cannon just to defend the ground she walks on.
