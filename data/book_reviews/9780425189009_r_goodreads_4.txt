Rating: 2/5

I found this book kind of boring. That Conan Doyle took on public, written defense of these real-life cases of perceived (and, it seems, actual) injustice due to police laziness and/or incompetence coupled with possible bad defense is, in itself, interesting. But the whole arguments seem to have dragged on and on with the same points repeated ad nauseum.

One does marvel at the situations. And I did finish reading the book, which combines "The Case of George Ernest Thompson Edalji" and "The Case of Oscar Slater" into one volume. But I think I continued reading more out of a sense of outrage at the long-gone situations than real fascination.
