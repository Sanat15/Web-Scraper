Rating: 4/5

Norwood is a compact, economically written road novel that traces a journey through small town America. It’s about the oddball people the protagonist meets on his way to the big city.

On the surface, many events seem mundane — a man looking for work, meeting strangers, fetching an overdue debt. But Portis uses these ordinary situations to reflect on the quirks of human behavior, conveyed in a deadpan, spare prose that appears simple at first glance. The comedy isn’t in big punchlines so much as in the understated tone, as Norwood deals with the absurdities of everyday life.

I loved this book, particularly for the author’s writing style, and can’t wait to dig into his magnum opus, True Grit.
