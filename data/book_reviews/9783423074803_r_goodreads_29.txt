Rating: 5/5

Three 13yo boys form their own detective agency and blackmail a famous director into hiring them to investigate a haunted house. 

I picked up this kid's book to revisit a beloved childhood series. These boys were So. Cool. They have a gold Rolls Royce limousine WITH chauffeur, thanks to First Investigator Jupiter Jones winning a publicity contest with his big brain. They have business cards. They have color coded chalk for leaving secret messages. They have a snide Bugs Meany-esque rival. They have an amazing-awesome secret hideout, a camper tucked away in Jupe's uncle's salvage yard. Headquarters has multiple secret entrances such as Green Gate One (press a knot in the fence and the boards swing up), Tunnel Two (a pipe hidden behind an innocent-looking grate), and Easy Three (merely a discarded door leaning against some piles of junk...unless you know which pile of junk contains the door's key!) 

This series was the absolute tits. I loved the Hardy Boys too but they were older, and combat-savvy, and could drive, and had a boat. I could actually relate to the Three Investigators, two of whom were smart nerds like me.

Granted, 
  Terror Castle
 doesn't go down now the way it did when I was nine. It's written in stilted, simple kidspeak. It's horrendously dated (the boys are awestruck by the limo's phone; you just give the operator a name and she connects you. A phone! In a car!!). The book is completely incognizant of the fact that its heroes basically commit B&amp;E's for 160 pages. 

Nevertheless: Five (heavily nostalgia-laced) stars.
