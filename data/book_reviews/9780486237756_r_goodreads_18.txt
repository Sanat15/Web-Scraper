Rating: 4/5

Wilkie Collins is among the best of the sensational/gothic mystery writers. His books always have as much an element of human entanglements and class struggle as they do of mystery, and I like that.

In the opening of this particular novel, we find a Mrs. Treverton on her deathbed and pressing upon her ladies maid a letter that reveals a “secret” that she insists must be given to her husband. The maid is very agitated by this mission she has been given, and in the end she hides the letter rather than giving it to Mr. Treverton. The letter, of course, is the key to the mystery and the plot revolves around what it contains and how that will be revealed to various characters in the novel.

It is a fun, intriguing read and rich with entertainment value. There is one character who absolutely won my heart and one who received all my pity. But to discuss specifics would be to ruin the story for others, and I would never wish to do that!

This is an early effort by Collins and not his best work, but quite enjoyable nonetheless and laced with sparks of what makes his later work great. He was under the tutelage of Charles Dickens when writing this, and there is much of that influence that can be seen here.
