Rating: 2/5

While there are sections of this book that are excellent: theologically rich and personally challenging, it does not have a nihil obstat, and why is clear. The author frequently toes the line of heresy towards unitarianism and relativism, and in her writing about transubstantiation, I believe she crosses it. 
However, if a person has a firm understanding of the Real Presence of Christ in the Eucharist (which the author does not dispute, just presents poorly), the message of the Eucharist needing to change those who receive It, and the mandate that they then work to change their world, is an important one.
