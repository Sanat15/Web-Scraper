Rating: 5/5

Although Hornblower is getting older and more mature, he remains anxious, self-critical, socially awkward, and prone to private despair—even while performing brilliantly under pressure. That's why his character is so appealing and contemporary.
Another reason why I find Hornblower novels so compelling is because the reader is drawn into the mental process: weighing odds, anticipating enemy behavior, managing subordinates, suppressing fear. This focus on thinking rather than spectacle (although the descriptions of naval battles and other military operations taking place in exotic countries are detailed and vivid, too!) gives the stories enduring relevance, especially to readers interested in leadership, ethics, or strategy.
Aspiring leaders could learn a lesson or two from Hornblower.
