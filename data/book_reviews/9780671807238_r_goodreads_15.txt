Rating: 5/5

The last time I read Seven Men of Gascony I was a callow youth who had a lot of living and learning to do. I didn't even know what the Schönbrunn was. Now forty-three years later I can still visualize the grandeur of that palace after a wonderful visit to Vienna and laugh at the effrontery of Delderfield's young voltigeurs as they casually help themselves to books from its library to ease the boredom of a hospitalized comrade.

As much as I loved this book back in the day, time and experience have deepened my love and appreciation of this story. This work is a classic.

Delderfield tells us the tale of seven French voltiguers (elite skirmishers and/or marksmen) fighting during the final campaigns of the Empire under Napoleon from 1809-1815. The early part of the book introduces the men, and some of the individuals who affect their lives. 

On various battlefields, as each man meets his fate, we experience their ties to each other and their endurance under adversity as glory slips away from France. Delderfield has many of the great names of Napoleonic fame interact with the characters. He shows these celebrities as men and not just legends. But make no mistake - this is the story of a band of brothers, comradeship and teamwork - not Napoleon or his marshals. 

Yes, we trudge through cannonades and cavalry charges, hot dusty treks through Spain and Portugal and into the Winter of Russia, but the battle scenes are not in-depth accounts. The author provides enough for the reader to get a feel for the heat of battle and what the men are suffering through. The story is about the men.

If you are looking for a story of people being people, of nobility in the darkest of places, and thick emotion, you'll be glad to read this.
