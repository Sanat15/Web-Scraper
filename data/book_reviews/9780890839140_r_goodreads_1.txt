Rating: 1/5

This review is of “Magnolia Plantation” by Beverly Butler.

The Story: This long book-652 pages-takes place in Natchez and Natchez Under-the-Hill in 1833. It is here that Tabitha Clay, 18, the heroine of the book, lives with her father, Abijah, a street preacher, and her mother, Rachel, a homemaker.. (Tabitha has one older sister, Ryma, 23, who left home to become a prostitute). Tabitha has big dreams: to be one of the women who live in the fancy plantations of Natchez proper. To that end, she sets out to attract Nicholas Enright, the wealthiest man in Natchez. Tabitha also attracts the attention of Dr. Michael Long, who works and lives in Natchez-Under-the-Hill.

Tabitha is later asked by Nicholas to stay a month at Magnolia Manor, his plantation. There, Tabitha meets Nicholas’ mother, Martha, whom Nicholas has a difficult-and complex-relationship with. Nicholas proposes marriage to Tabitha, who accepts. 

As time goes on, Tabitha learns a great many things about Nicholas and Martha that make her marriage to Nicholas an unhappy one. She also begins an affair with Mike. Ultimately, Tabitha is forced to choose between Nicholas and Mike. Who will she choose?

Upside: “Magnolia Manor” is, for all intents and purposes, a parable about life that asks a question: What’s more important: what we think we want, or what we actually need?

Downside: I didn’t find any of the characters to be likeable. Tabitha and Nicholas are grasping, scheming, opportunistic, racists. Mike is the only fairly likeable character in the book. Ms. Butler’s drawing of her characters is shallow and uninteresting. 

Sex: There are two love scenes between Tabitha and Mike; both are mild.

Violence: Assaults, whippings, shootings and killings. The violence is mildly graphic. 

Bottom Line: There is nothing for me to recommend about “Magnolia Manor”.
