“[If] it were possible for Me to suffer pain again as I have done before, I would rather suffer as much pain as I ever did for your soul alone rather than that you should be separated from Me forever.”

My main impression is one of sadness that Margery suffered so much in her soul as she grappled with both a probable mental illness and a genuine devotion to her Lord. It comforts me to think of the joy and rest she must have felt once she reached heaven, once she met the Savior face to face and received true and total clarity as to which of her visions had come from him and which had not, which crosses he had asked her to shoulder and which had been burdens foisted on her by illegitimate tongues. What an exhale that must have been for her weary spirit.

  [For] sometimes, revelations are hard to understand, and sometimes things that people believe to be revelations are deceits and illusions, and therefore it is not appropriate to give credence too eagerly to every stirring, but rather to wait discreetly and test if they are sent from God.
