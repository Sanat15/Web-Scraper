Rating: 4/5

Being this is an academic work it is perhaps not surprising that it is a bit dry. The first volume covering the Deutsch Reichbahn's history from the end of the First World War through to the End of the Weimar Republic and the rise of the Nazi Party to power featured a fairly detailed accounting of the mechanics of running a railroad and what the DRB's leadership did in pursuit of improving a railroad. The second volume, in contrast, is mostly focused around the relationship between the Party and the railroad, the interactions of the DRB with the various other railroad companies under the Nazi umbrella (France &amp; Belgium's national railroads, the Ostbahn, and the pseudo-military railroad only partially under the control of the DRB). But mostly the volume focuses on how the DRB management and staff were all too aware of the activities of the SS's racial objectives and activities and the dysfunction of the Nazi political apparatus. The actual operation of the railroad is generally reduced to dry tables of statistics comparing ton-kilometers or passenger-kilometers year-over-year. Likely a great starting point for a Ph.D. thesis on the subject of wartime railroading, but not much to recommend for the general rail-enthusiast.
