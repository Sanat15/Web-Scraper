Rating: 5/5

Loved, loved, loved this book. It was a NYT notable book of the year in 1996, but should have received more acclaim. Taste is so personal, but it's one of the best contemporary story collections I've ever read. Mazur is brilliant. This is a linked collection before linking was in, and focuses mainly on two very different women. It takes you all over the world, and into many different situations, some quite shocking. But Mazur pulls it off without turning you off.

Some passages about loss and loneliness:

"There was a man walking ahead of her on the path ... She could see the air around him closing where he had been, and opening up where he was now, the way the space around Robert, who was gone, was now closing in. She had painted Robert out of his studio, and had filled his space in her bed with silk."

"What is it we hear when the noisy ones who surround us are hushed? We hear our hearts beating. It's not only our skin that we feel, then, but also our pulse, clocking off the allotted integers."

I hate comparing writers to each other, but she reminds me of Anthony Doerr. Both have that scientist's power of observation backed by an artist's creative spirit and humanitarian heart. Highly recommended to litaray fiction lovers.
