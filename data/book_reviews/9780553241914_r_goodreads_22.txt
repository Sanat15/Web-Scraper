Rating: 3/5

"Kill Now, Pay Later" is one of the few sad stories in the Wolfe series. It's rare we feel sorry for the victims, which may be more about us than the genre, but this time we do, and likely only because it's someone somewhat close to Archie and Wolfe, followed by a distraught family member. The resolution to this one may seem to be typical in the long history of the mystery genre, with an Ellery Queen-like "dying clue in his own blood," but it's more like Stout is poking fun at that sort of thing, since the discovery of the clue is almost after Wolfe solves it anyway.

"Murder is Corny" is likely memorable today because of the Chaykin/Hutton adaptation, though the recipe for summer corn is also intriguing. What does "hottest possible temperature" mean? Sadly, the Nero Wolfe Cookbook does not elaborate. The show does a great job bringing out the potential humor in this scenario, but the short story does a good job resolving the emotional effect of the revelation of the killer. The episode sort of glosses over that in favor of the corn side of the story, but the more emotional resolution here should not be missed.

"Blood Will Tell" is likely the best of the three, as many have said, mainly because it is so Archie-centric. He basically does the whole kaboodle himself this time. The hook is very bizarre this time, which also helps: Archie gets a bloody tie in the mail (reminding us of course of Wolfe's tie used as a murder weapon years earlier) and an unusual call shortly thereafter asking him to forget the whole thing. Both of these incidents are key to bringing down the culprit in a very ironic way. I almost said "instrumental," but I stopped myself considering the title here. You're welcome. Decent stories all around, probably not too many people's favorites, but good and various just the same.
