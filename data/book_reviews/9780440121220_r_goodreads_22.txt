Rating: 4/5

A re-read of this collection of stories by a favorite author, Daphne Du Maurier. Each of these stories reads more like a novella than a short story. There is a complete plot in each, rather than a moment or slice of life. For the most part, they don’t have great value as philosophical themes or moral teachings, but they have quite a bit of entertainment value, which is also a valid reason for reading. Not that there are not lessons that can be learned from them, just that it seems secondary in all but perhaps the last entry.

Don’t Look Now - a rather tense story that begins as a holiday, with laughter, and grows increasingly darker as it progresses. 3.5*

The Breakthrough - A story with a bit of a sci-fi vibe. I thought it was quite well done, because I found myself caring about what happened to a character that might have seemed rather minor until the end. 5*

Not After Midnight - An unsocial school teacher takes a vacation to Crete to paint. While there he comes in contact with a nefarious fellow visitor and becomes involved in watching some very questionable activities. Very suspenseful, with an open ending that leaves one pondering what has really happened here. 4*

A Border-Line Case - I figured this one out almost immediately. This one is a case of paternity. I was not surprised by the outcome, other than I did not expect her to sleep with him before she realized he was her father. I think what makes this work is that it is a well written a character study, even though the outcome is almost pre-determined. 4*

The Way of the Cross - A motley group of English visitors is on a trip to Jerusalem, where each meets with a kind of comeuppance, except the lone child in the group who seems to be the only one focused on the ancient history of the place. There are a lot of moral lapses in the company, and a few experience true repentance. There is a casual kind of unconcern for their fellows and a lot of petty gossip and zinging that does damage by being overheard. 4*
