Rating: 5/5

I had previously read Koestler's The Sleepwalkers. In The Sleepwalkers, about the life and work of Copernicus, Kepler and Galileo, Koestler's concern is how these scientists created their ideas that lead to the scientific revolution. The Act of Creation is a study of not only scientific creativity but also creation in art and humor. It is a hugely ambitious work that incorporates large swathes of psychology, physiology, animal behavior into a synthesis that attempts to explain human creativity. Koestler's develops a hierarchical system ranging from the cell up to the thought matrices we use to navigate life. Creativity happens when these thought matrices merge and rearrange into a new configuration that supplies greater insight. An expert in psychology or physiology could probably poke a dozen holes in Koestler's arguments, but as Koestler's canvas is so vast the picture remains. Like a great work of art it can make great demands on the reader, but it may also spur their own creativity.
