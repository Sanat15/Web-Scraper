Rating: 3/5

This 1983 Methuen publication is a collection of four plays by Wodehouse, with an introduction by David A. Jasen.

The Play’s the Thing – A brilliant, creative and wonderfully conceived farcical comedy. Originally written by Ferenc Molnar, Wodehouse has adapted it with his inimitable stamp of humor. 3.5 stars. 

Good Morning, Bill – A three-act comedy based on Hungarian Ladislaus Fodor’s work, Wodehouse, in addition to writing this play, subsequently also wrote a novel using the same plot. Personally, I found the novel, Doctor Sally, more humorous than the play. 2.5 stars.

Leave it to Psmith – A three-act comedy created in partnership with Ian Hays in 1930, Wodehouse subsequently wrote a thoroughly satisfying Psmith novel set in Blandings Castle subsequently in the same name. As far as the play itself, I must admit it was an improvement over Good Morning, Bill, but it dawned upon me that perhaps, stage plays, when seen, will give far more satisfaction compared to when they are read. 3 stars. 

Come on, Jeeves – The fourth play in this collection comes with the author’s note, describing his relationship with Guy Bolton with whom he had collaborated on multiple theatrical plays, including Come on, Jeeves. Wodehouse subsequently wrote Ring for Jeeves based on this play. While it was fun to see Jeeves in action – how can he ever fail to give satisfaction – my preference leans heavily on the side of the novel. 3.5 stars.
