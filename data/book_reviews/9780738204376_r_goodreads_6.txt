Rating: 4/5

Wise covers a lot of ground here from the place of nonhuman animals in ancient Babylonian law to the sign language skills of chimpanzees, and it's pretty consistently fascinating. While I agreed with him before I even started the book, I am now even more convinced not only that 1) many nonhuman animals should be granted legal rights to bodily liberty but 2) that the courts--as they have done in previous cases involving enslaved persons, fetuses, and others--have the power to rule in these animals' favor even without any changes to existing law. I find it infuriating that 25 years later, US courts (with the exception of a few dissenting judges) have remained unwilling to take this step.

That said, perhaps a small missed opportunity is to explore the potential role of legislative change as well in establishing legal personhood and rights for nonhuman animals. And not his fault, but I'd love to see the updated version with the last 25 years of research on animal cognition--I think there'd be not only more to say about chimpanzees and bonobos, but other species (to whom he only briefly alludes) as well.

I don't know if this is just the hardcover or what but there were kind of a lot of typos. Normally that wouldn't be a big issue but some sentences were missing quotation marks and/or words that made the meaning, as well as who said what, actually confusing to follow.
