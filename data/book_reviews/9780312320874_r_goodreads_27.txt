Rating: 2/5

I was somehow disappointed from this reading. I've heard so many times talking about this book like something different from the "usual Indian fiction" and how intense and deep was this novel, that I maybe formed it in my mind like something different. I think that the plot is good: an unmarried Indian woman, after having took care of her family after the death of her father, finds herself different from what she would have been. She is still living with her younger sister - who has her own family but seems like she can't let her elder sister go, despite her being really nasty and unbearable with her - and decides to move. I liked the idea that the book was set on a train, especially if we think it is set on an "only women coach". What happens then is that the novel is no more a novel with a tissue linking all the stories of the women Akhila meets. It looks more like a collection of stories, like the women she met just pass through the coach to narrate their lives and then depart, hardly remembered. The women really not face each others, do not discuss about what they are saying. One of the stories really fall apart from the usual Indian cliché (Prabha Devis' one, which remains my favourite), but the others look or really inconsequential, or really sad in the manner that if there's something that is should go wrong, it will. I noticed that the author is also quite "obsessed" by sex, so this is not treated like one of the subjects of the novel, but more like a straining to make the whole thing interesting, putting it unrequested also in the final part, making it like something really unreal and spoiling the whole thing.
