Rating: 5/5

This is an extraordinary book. It's an equal blend of history and drama. More than just the telling of the story of the Battle of Stalingrad, the author writes an emotional, detailed story that not only provides a chilling look at the horrors of war, but also explores the mind and spirit of the soldier. 

The author writes with such depth and creativity. I recall one line in particular: "[Nikki] held the rifle away from him. Look at this thing. Metal and wood, that's all it is. But it's also a door, an opening that the devil and death and all that hates life can march through."

The actual duel between the two snipers, which is a large part of the background of the story and which is used to summarize the content of the book, was fascinating but surprisingly and disappointingly short. The scene of Tania's surgery was shockingly blunt and gruesome. I loved how Vasha gave his Order of Lenin pin to an ordinary soldier in a quiet and posthumous gesture.

The perspective changes between the four main characters let the reader see equally from both sides. The fact that the story ended with Nikki, the only fictional character of the four main characters, and not the two hero snipers leaves the reader really feeling the pain, despair, and suffering of the average soldier.
