Rating: 5/5

This is the western for all those readers who don't think they like westerns. It has bad guys (&amp; gals), and it has good guys (&amp; gals), but mostly it has regular people who are sometimes and in some ways bad and some good and mostly just living their lives as best they can from day to day. I wasn't alive in the days of the wild, wild west, yet I imagine the reality of that time &amp; place was much closer to that portrayed in this novel than in any movie I have ever seen. (I have a sneaking suspicion that the makers of the HBO series &amp; movie Deadwood likely read Doctorow's book, published in 1960, and loosely drew the characters, story arc, and plot lines from it. I enjoyed that series a lot, but it doesn't hold a candle to this novel.) 

The story is told by one man named Blue, nominally the unelected mayor but mostly just the keeper of ledgers of the town of Hard Times, and it unfolds from start to finish over about 18 to 24 months. The beginning is a doozy and not for the faint of heart (lots of rough language, lots of violence) -- if you've never imagined how impossibly lawless and every-man-for-himself the tiny communities of the western territories likely were, Doctorow draws a vivid picture. The middle is a deep character exploration of all those residents who survived &amp; remained and of the new arrivals to the town; what drew them &amp; kept them there; how they understood or failed to understand themselves and each other; and how they faced &amp; were affected by the challenges of each changing season. You'll likely love some of the characters and downright despise others, but you're guaranteed to care about them either way. And hold your horses &amp; pour yourself a stiff drink for the ending, because it will break your heart. Honest: I think I died just a little on the last page.
