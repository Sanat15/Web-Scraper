Rating: 3/5

3.5-Dhuoda, wife of Duke Bernard of Septimania as the Carolingian Empire was tottering due to fraternal fighting, wrote this manual of conduct and advice for her son, William, who was given hostage to Charles the Bald, and her other new born (whose name she did not even know at the time), that Bernard took away with him in compensation for William. Dhuoda often pleads ignorance, but this is in line with contemporary practice, she was clearly very well versed with the Bible, was able to call upon a variety of Patristic and verse classics with ease, and in general she is a very good exemplar of the results of the Carolingian educational project. The book is excellent to orient one in Carolingian culture, as Dhuoda intended for it to give a comprehensive account of conduct for her own young children (she notes that she would have written much differently if they were older), so forms a good introduction. One will learn more about how to cultivate virtue and personal piety, and how to properly pray after a very dogged attempt to teach them. But throughout, the book is suffused with the sadness and anxiety of a mother worried sick and missing her children, no amount of chronological distance reduces the thick pain that flows through it. I challenge one to read Book 10 without leaving dejected. Dhuoda's family indeed did meet tragedy (Bernard got executed, William ended up a rebel and got killed for it, it was the little baby here that carried on the family line), and she herself probably died not long afterwards. All in all a worthy medieval classic to understand Carolingian culture and education, and an important reminder to call your mom.
