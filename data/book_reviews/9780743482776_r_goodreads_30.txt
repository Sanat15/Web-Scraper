My review 7/6/25:

 “If music be the food of love, play on.”--Duke Orsino

Twelfth Night (or What You Will) (in other words, whatever!) (though it was written to be performed initially on Twelfth Night (re: Twelve days of Christmas, a fun party) (1602 ) by William Shakespeare, is a romantic comedy. If Hamlet begins in grief and ends in tragedy, Twelfth Night begins in grief and all’s well that ends well in laughs and seemiongl;y random couplings. It’s a play that plays as several of his plays with gender--cross-dressing Viola who is the focus of romantic attachment by Duke Orsino and the Duke’s former love interest, Olivia.
 
After the twins Sebastian and Viola survive a shipwreck, neither knows that the other is alive. Viola gets a job (by cross-dressing, so now Cesario) with Count Orsino, who sends Cesario to woo the Olivia on his behalf, but Olivia falls in love with Cesario. Viola, in the meantime, has fallen in love with Orsino. Oops, triangle!

The mad confusion of love and grief is complicated by hilarity from hypocrites (Malvolio), fools (an actual fool!, though Sir Andrew Aguecheek ialso qualifies), and confused lovers everywhere. I always though it was one of his funniest comedies, with lots of silliness in it. Maybe not one of the “great” plays, but if you can use a laugh, and who doesn’t now?

Some funny bits: 
*Sir Toby (a drunk), uncle to Olivia: What a plague means my niece to take the death of her brother thus? I am sure care’s an enemy to Life. (having her grieve messes with his drinken vibe)
* Maria chides Sir Toby for public drunkenness, wraning him that his niece Olivia is mad at him for it.Toby says he is drunk drinking toasts to his niece, which he will do every day all day, and anyone who does not do so is a knave.
*Toby tells Andrew to “accost” (hit on) Maria. Andrew approaches her: “Good Mistress Accost, I desire better acquaintance.” Maria responds, “My name is Mary, sir.” (oops, Andrew doesn't know what accost means)
* Malvolio thinks he will be a great man and says this famous line that enrages everyone in the play as pretentious: "Some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon them." He thinks he will woo Olivia and win her! 
*Sir Toby Belch to the teetotalling hypocritical puritan Malvolio (or, “ill will”): "Dost thou think, because thou art virtuous, there shall be no more cakes and ale?" The partying group enacts revenge on Malvolio for his contemptuous behavior (including his goal of winning Olivia) by sending him a letter as if written by Olivia expressing her love to him and asking him to come to her, if he indeed reciprocates in love, smiling constantly, wearing yellow stockings and cross-gartered (all things she hates and are the opposite of his gray demeanor). So that’s very funny and of course it doesn’t matter that someone says, “If this were played upon a stage now, I could condemn it as an improbable fiction.”
