Rating: 2/5

It doesn’t help that this book wasn’t what I was expecting. The author’s intention is to compare the history of food in England and France and points out first thing the stereotypes of, especially, English food (boring, boiled) and manages to insult it right off the bat without a corresponding dig at the French. This uneven start moves into one of the most boring introductions to any non-fiction book I have ever read. 

I shouldn’t give the impression that there isn’t interesting info contained within, but the book is dated (in a chapter on food dislikes, he barely touches the subject of allergies) and in some ways badly written with grammatical issues throughout. It is understandable, especially at the time of writing, that early food history sources were minimal and much of the book focuses on more modern times, especially after the advent of the magazine (which created the food columns and articles) as well as on how the restaurant (or equivalent) changed the world of eating and food. 

There is much info included here, and it may be much better suited to a student of food history and theory, more interested in the history itself than the food details. This is definitely not a passing read.
