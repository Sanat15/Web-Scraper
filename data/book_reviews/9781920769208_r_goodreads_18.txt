Rating: 1/5

2017 Recipient of my Poison Apple Award: Looks juicy on the surface. One bite and you are stuck chewing 900 pages of purple, perfumed self-importance.

A prison-escape story set in Mumbai should be catnip. An Australian fugitive, heroin habit, slums, street medicine, the underworld, and a climb into the orbit of organized crime. That is a clean, hard engine for a 350 to 450 page novel.

This one takes that engine and bolts it to a parade float.

The problem is not the plot so much as the prose. It is gaudy, flowery-to-the-nth, and endlessly impressed with itself. The book does not trust scenes, dialogue, or the reader. It tells you what to feel, tells you again, then tells you a third time in a fresh coat of metaphor.

Karla, the love interest, is Exhibit A. Instead of letting affection build through lived moments, choices, and talk, the narrator repeatedly lunges into breathless worship. It reads less like romance and more like a man narrating his own perfume commercial.

  Example of the vibe: “Her eyes… the green of water lilies after the rain.”

That is not a sentence, it is a scented candle with a law degree.

As the pages pile up, the melodrama thickens and the self-righteousness starts to hum. At its worst, the book becomes a long, overwrought tribute to the narrator, noble sufferer, poetic warrior, spiritual tourist of his own heartbreak. At its best, it is a repetitive valentine to Mumbai, delivered with the subtlety of a marching band in a library.

By the later chapters, the language turns into full sugar sludge. I did not need a bookmark. I needed anti-nausea meds.

  Another representative flourish: “His voice… like ocean” and “I floated on the dark water of sorrow.”

If you love maximalist, incense-heavy, swooning prose that treats every glance like a cosmic event, you may have a religious experience.

If you prefer a story that shows its power instead of chanting it at you, this is a 900 page cautionary tale about what happens when a great premise never meets a firm editor.
