I received a copy of this book via Net Galley in exchange for an honest review. 

2 stars

The story begins with a feud between two professors, Pat O'Shea and Everett Adams. In the beginning you do not really know why these two apparently hate each other but learn later it has to do with theft of a piece of equipment. The O'Shea pursues Adams in an attempt to confront him and both men end up going missing. You are introduced to a host of characters quickly. Anabel O'Shea, Vee Adams, Celia Adams, Cecil Wahl, the provost, and two detectives. The story seemed promising but, honestly fell flat for me. 

The characters are not well defined and it's hard to relate to any of them. The writing style is very different. This may be due to the fact that it was originally published in 1963. The POV jumps from one character to the next with no real warning or rhythm. I felt myself starting to skim at times and had to force myself to focus. There is a mystery within the story that is caught up with the odd twins Celia and Cecil but it never truly explains what that mystery is or why we should care. I liked that the author made Anabel O'Shea into a strong female character in her own way but she came off cold and unlikable for the majority of the story. 

It was an interesting concept for a story but did not feel that anything really got explained in the end and the climax was a bit disappointing. At the end, you are basically told that the detectives know nothing about the rest of the mystery and neither will you.
