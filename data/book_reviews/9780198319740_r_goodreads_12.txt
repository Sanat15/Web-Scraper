Rating: 5/5

Twelfth Night is the first play of Shakespeare that I read. I was then a child and was too young to appreciate Shakespeare, but I remember loving it. However, my first read as an adult was a disappointment. There was something vacant and bare, or so I thought. Years later, I made another attempt at reading it, and was glad to find I still love it. I don't know what happened at that intermediary reading. But now I know that Twelfth Night is my favourite Shakespearean comedy. 

Touching the themes of love, desire, deception, and mistaken identity, Twelfth Night is one of Shakespeare’s more complex comedies. The play consists few different stories: the love triangle of Duke Orsino, Countess Olivia, and Viola was the dominant. The confusion caused by mistaken identity was too funny to resist. There was also a separate, amusing story where a cruel trick of the heart was played as a means of revenge. All these stories were cleverly and neatly tied. 

I loved the characters in Twelfth Night. Viola and Countess Olivia are my favourites. The play is full of satire, humour, and witty prose. The writing is beautiful, though there is less lyricism. The comedy is also full of action, which I enjoyed. It was a fun read, overall, and I loved every moment of it.
