Rating: 2/5

I thought the book was well-written. William Hoffman, the coauthor, is a writer who was invited to accompany the expedition; Jack Grimm is an oilman and Titanic enthusiast who financed the trip. They did a nice job of interspersing the tale of the Titanic and its survivors with the story of their search; however, it ends abruptly with their discovery of a large propellor off the Great Bank of Newfoundland. Due to costs and the fact that the Gyre had been chartered to the U.S. Geological Survey, they had to return to port.

Since I found (from online research today) that Robert Ballard discovered the Titanic about 4 years after this expedition, it seems like the book was written too early. It would be helpful to know how their findings link to Ballard's expedition and discovery. The lack of conclusiveness detracted from the noteworthy journalistic style.
