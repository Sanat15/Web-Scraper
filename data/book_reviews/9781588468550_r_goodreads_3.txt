Rating: 2/5

This wasn't a poorly written book, but I couldn't really enjoy it because it felt like it was the third or fourth in a series. (I think it must be written for people who play the tabletop RPG.) While enough context was given in the book itself that I was rarely actually confused by events, I did feel like I was missing a LOT by not going in knowing more about the characters and world. I'd played the computer game Vampire the Masquerade: Bloodlines so I'd met two of the characters, but that didn't feel like nearly enough. This book has many characters and touches on a lot of lore.
For me, too much was left unclear What was the timeline with Becket's friend being lost, left, and the way to his release discovered? When, in relation to the present, was Jenna's mind messed with? unexplained Did Beckett never try to figure out what was going on with the ancient city? Why was the Abyss-thing chasing Beckett's "taint," and why couldn't it directly affect him? Why did Fatima have a unique connection to her Antediluvian? Most importantly, what was the purpose or reason for vampires? or unfinished What will happen on this "final night of the kindred?" Will Beckett, and the world, survive the withering and mass warfare and woken Antediluvian or two? And aside from the fact that the ending was cliffhanger-ish, I also found it existentially disappointing.Beckett's final epiphany is that the past doesn't matter and we shouldn't focus on it. Really? Really?
