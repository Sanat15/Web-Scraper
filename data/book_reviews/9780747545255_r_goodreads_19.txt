Rating: 4/5

“Funny how we take it for granted that we know all there is to know about another person, just because we see them frequently or because of some strong emotional tie.”

The obligatory opening scene to any good scary story was set for Mary Crane one dark and stormy night when she pulled up to The Bates Motel. On the run, lost, and out of options, Mary’s misfortunes were only beginning when she met the motel proprietor, Norman Bates. She arrived with the intention of getting some rest and a hot meal, but she left with quite the butchered experience…

I’m a sucker for slasher type horror, so I thought it was about time I got around to some of the OG slasher classics infamous for nursing nightmares and inciting fear for generations. As expected, this book is shocking, violent, and bloody. I was honestly surprised by a couple of the twists, and I thought the story was really very good. It’s a short read but a fast-paced, brutal evaluation on the darker side of the human mind. The author certainly takes advantage of mental health stigmatization and what the average person cannot begin to fathom. I guess the main thing I gleaned here was this: You never really know someone, do you? 🫣
