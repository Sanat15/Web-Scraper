Rating: 1/5

Good grief, Dorothy L Sayers is the ultimate bore. She has both class and intellectual snobbery down to a fine art. In this book she excels herself, managing to waffle on for page after page about Einstein and philosophy and the perils of marriage, none of which have the slightest thing to do with the plot (assuming there is a plot) but which she clearly thinks make her look intellectually superior. They don't - we can all read articles in magazines and then paraphrase them. Parrots can spout big words too. Imagine going to dinner with her - it must have been purgatory.

I only attempted to read this because it's one of Martin Edwards' choices in his The Story of Classic Crime in 100 Books, but I'm afraid I cannot share his appreciation for this tedious person who clearly thinks that she's writing the Great Novel of her time when she's not even managing to write an average mystery novel. Worse even than her ultra-snobbish Wimsey horrors. Never again. Never again.
