Rating: 3/5

This is the final collection in the seven-volume series that Eric Flint organized (with just the novel The Witches of Karres to go), containing the rest of Schmitz's short fiction not already collected (minus the one collaboration with A. E. van Vogt, which I guess I'll track down at some point). It also includes the short novel The Eternal Frontiers.

Flint arranges it in several sections--beginning with some general SF adventure stories, including "Captives of the Thieve-Star," a sort of first draft of the Witches of Karres story. I liked "The Big Terrarium" and "Left Hand, Right Hand" in this section as well.

The next section is the "ubermensch" themed stories, of which I mainly found "Spacemaster" the most interesting despite being nothing but dialogue/monologue. "The Ties of Earth" is a novella here, but it rather ended up dragging for me.

The third section involved his "dark stories," per the editor. Despite being best known for lightheartedness, etc., these usually have rather dark endings (or dark for Schmitz). These were all OK, but nothing really stood out to me.

The fourth section were his "crime stories," three of which weren't even SF/fantasy (looks like some were originally published in Alfred Hitchcock's Mystery Magazine). "Crime Buff" was the only interesting one to me, though "Where the Time Went" and "Faddist" were at least vaguely amusing/silly.

The final section was the short novel The Eternal Frontiers. He wrote a politically complex background to set up the premise of multiple parties investigating mysterious things happening on a distant planet. Interesting enough, but I think I need a bit more reason to care, especially with the surprisingly optimistic ending that I didn't quite buy.
