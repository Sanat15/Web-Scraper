Rating: 4/5

This is a great book for beginners, not just to investing, but financial literacy in general. Dunnan starts from the very basic level and moves her way into much more complex investment vehicles. 

My only issue is how dated it feels. This was the 10th edition, and I believe that was the final version of this book. It claims to be updated for the “new economy” but really feels old since it makes no reference to the Great Recession and lacks modern technological developments. She references the Lehman brothers, but they went under from the housing crisis. She also discusses brokerage fees heavily, but there are many zero-fee options available today. There is obviously no mention of cryptocurrencies. Lots of sections recommend getting resources from the library or asking your broker to print you out a research report. This is just not relevant today. 

On the whole though, this book has good information and presents it in a non-biased way. I do think she shouldn’t have made so many stock/bond/mutual fund recommendations. Some of the companies she mentioned are not big players today, just 15 years after publication.
