Rating: 3/5

I loved Hero. She is an intelligent, brave heroine who likes to keep her thoughts to herself. I tend to find stories about people who are mute or choose to be mute fascinating, and she does not disappoint. I also loved the interweaving of fairy tale and real life. I did get bogged down by Mahy's divisions of real life and true life as headings for the sections of the book. I think it would be a lot stronger if she left it to the reader to decide what the difference is between Hero's real life and her true life. I also had difficulty at the beginning with the setting of this book. It takes several chapters to realize that they are in New Zealand, and I didn't think of New Zealand when I started to read it. I didn't know that Mahy is a New Zealander. The family could be in any English-speaking country with universities and subdivisions. I lost the beginning of the novel because I was trying to decipher where they were. I was disappointed in the ending, which is why I only gave this three stars, I won't give anything away, but I thought it was weak. I also love fairy tales. Overall, a decent book, but because of the ending, I'm probably not going to give it to people to read.
