Rating: 2/5

Summary - amazing narration, terrible story.

I was looking up more information about the author, when I stumbled across the definition of "lamprey." It turns out that lampreys are jawless fish that live parasitically off other marine life. They latch onto a larger fish, then use their teeth (really ugly teeth) to dig into the other fish and suck on its blood and flesh. They are ugly and repulsive.

It is hard to think of a better analogy to the main family in this book. The Lampreys are presented as a charmingly eccentric, sometimes wealthy, sometimes not, family of English aristocrats. But they annoyed the hell out of me. They were not charming, they were parasites. They lurch from economic disaster to economic disaster, expecting some rich auntie to die or a wealthy brother to bail them out of their financial hot water. None of them actually has a job or makes any effort to stabilize their finances. When they have cash, they splurge, gamble, and generally have not one care about tomorrow. When they are broke, well, it's just somebody's *duty* to come to their rescue. 

The older brother they expect to rescue them this time is not a terribly pleasant fellow. But heaven knows, he has no duty to provide for these parasites. Why is this brother presented as the unreasonable bad guy here?

I won't discuss the plot, but it was a sub-par mystery for someone who cut her teeth on Agatha Christie. The only reason I am giving this mess two stars is due to the superlative narration by the always-amazing Anton Lesser. His ability to create distinct characters of all ages, all accents, and both genders with his voice is incredible. I would listen to this man recite a cookbook.
