Rating: 4/5

Diana Wynne Jones truly operates on a different plane… no-one else combines the innocent charm of children’s books with such inexplicable and complicated logic systems (except he who shall not be named). If anyone asked me during the book what was going on at any given time, I don’t know if I would have been able to answer. Yet, (perhaps as Claire said?), there is an intuitiveness to Jones’ stories that lends itself better to children’s minds, and while I will never be able to properly explain in English words what a Bannus is or what the Wood is, the magic of Jones means that the strange revelations do make sense, the story does make sense, in some other unformed part of the mind where words and reality’s logic has not taken root. I was reading this before sleep and afterwards, my mind slipped into that thought-dream state where I was not fully asleep but rather persuaded by the internal logic of unreal thoughts, and my thoughts were all confused thoughts of people entering the Bannus and reconfiguring. Finished in the morning.
