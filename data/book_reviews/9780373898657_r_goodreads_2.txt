Rating: 3/5

This read like Picnic, an old fashioned Hollywood movie set in an aw shucks small town, when an outsider comes in, disrupting and rearranging the static lives of the small town folk. The heroine is, funnily enough, engaged to her cousin and they share the same last name. Well okay, he is very very distant cousin, no hanky panky taboo liaison here. But they do share a rather bland, brother-sister affection for each other instead of red-hot passion. On some level, the heroine knows this is the wrong man for her as she keeps putting off their wedding date for the past six years under one pretext or another. Everybody in the small town knows each other, shares the same memories and traditions, go to the same church, frequent the same library, fish in the same pond, celebrate at the same Fourth of July picnic etc. Our hero is a New Yorker who buys the heroine's beloved historic farmhouse, settling there with his socially awkward son, who the heroine tries to help transition to his new life. The resentment and antagonism she feels for his father, naturally, are her hidden, repressed desires that she has never experienced before and doesn't know what to do with. It takes a tornado for these two to finally get together and they live out an aw shucks happy ending complete with an adjusted, lively son, an adorable English Sheperd dog, and even the replanting of a willow tree that had been torn out by the last storm. It's hokey but sweet.
