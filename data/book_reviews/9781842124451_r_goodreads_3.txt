Rating: 5/5

This book was written in 1978, and I imagine that there have been many advances in our understanding of the colonization of South America since then. However, for someone who knows very little about this period in history (like me), it is a wonderful introduction.

Oh God, such suffering and brutality. Once the idea took hold that somewhere there was a region rich in gold, just there for the taking, expedition after expedition set out to find it. The conquistadors were ill-equipped to spend months in the jungle, and most of them died on each expedition, with only a few stragglers limping back home in a half-dead state with nothing to show for it. On the way they brutalized the indigenous inhabitants of the region mercilessly and sadistically. And despite the lack of gold (mostly, anyway - in the land of the Muisca they found a fair amount, to the extreme detriment of the Muisca), this went on for at least two generations.

I learned about the end of Sir Walter Raleigh, who really didn’t deserve his fate. And the true story of Lupe de Aguirre, played so well by Klaus Kinski in the move Aguirre: Wrath of God. The real story is equally as bonkers and disturbing as the movie.

I originally picked the book up because it was signed and looked nice, and I am like a magpie for signed books. But I thoroughly enjoyed it, and intend to read much more about this period.
