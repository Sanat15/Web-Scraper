Rating: 4/5

When Worlds Collide was a surprisingly good novel, so I had rather high hopes for the sequel. It picks up the story where the first book ended on the surface of Bronson Beta, and most of the characters are the same.

Our heroes were not the only survivors of the doomed Earth, but unfortunately not all of them were such nice and wholesome folks as Tony, Eve, Hendron &amp; co. Thus, in addition to the struggle to survive in a harsh environment and the mysteries of Bronson Beta and its original inhabitants, we get a human conflict. The bad guys are just bad – we don’t really get to know much of them, except their vague political agenda (totalitarianism).

Although some of the main characters do not believe in a Christian God, the story takes a very biblical turn. It didn’t bother me too much and I suppose it was almost inevitable, but I could have lived without it.

Most of the book was really enjoyable if you like this sort of old scifi, but the ending bothered me. It seemed very rushed, and any teenager could have come up with a better, more plausible and more exciting finale. With a full-bodied ending my rating would have been higher. Also, the serialization of the novel manifested itself in the annoying repetition of the words “perhaps a million years ago” (or something very similar). 

In addition to Eve, this time we get a couple of additional female characters, but one of them is labelled a “moron” without any real justification, and for the most part they don’t get to do that much. The Earth was a man’s world, and so is Bronson Beta. In addition, fairly early on one of the characters turns out to be much more than everybody had thought, but after that potentially very significant revelation, the character again disappears from the pages. That was rather disappointing.

Despite these shortcomings, those who read and enjoyed When Worlds Collide really should also read After Worlds Collide.

3.75/5
