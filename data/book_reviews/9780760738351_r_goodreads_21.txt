Rating: 5/5

This is a profound reflection of what it means to be a lover of God while yet in the world. I can find myself shying away from certain brands of mysticism because they seem to imply a detachment from the beauty in the world. Merton argues that it is futile to attempt to find peace and closeness to God by simply disengaging from other good things around us. Rather, we are to enjoy beauty as part of our wider, all-consuming love for God. As a creative artist, I’ve struggled with concepts like sacrifice and denial, as they can seem at odds with highly activated senses. Merton challenged me to deny myself in a way that doesn’t force me into any false quandaries. I left challenged and more excited about being a believer who indeed makes sacrifices, but by losing myself in God’s love and beauty, not a self-loathing rejection of good things in an attempt to feel better about myself. 

There is a lot in this book, and the parts that moved me most are only a small part of the whole. This isn’t hard to read, but is a book that should prompt a lot of helpful musings about our spiritual identity.
