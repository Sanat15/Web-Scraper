Rating: 2/5

Ruiz Navarro is a widower with a young child and a mountain of debt. Julia Flores' family works for the Navarro's and she's been in love with Ruiz since they were children. 

I prefer my romance couples to be on somewhat equal footing, balancing each other out. This doesn't happen here. Julia is a strong, intelligent woman. 'We need to do this. We need to do that. We need to get to work!' Ruiz comes off like, 'You do what you need to do. And can you raise my daughter while you're at it? I've got places to be.' In short, Ruiz is a load. I believe a part of Julia recognizes he's a load but she just can't stop loving him. While very little happens in this book, Ruiz really isn't doing any of it. There's a secondary character (already forgot his name) who is having all the adventures with cattle rustlers and treasure seekers. And it's Julia who has to come rescue everybody from the bad guys, not the other way around.

On the plus side this isn't one of those I Hate You/I Love You romances that aren't my thing. I also want to give credit to the author for featuring Hispanic characters as the main leads and not as side characters or villains as they are unfortunately almost always are in historical romances. Too bad Hebby Roman junks up the text with all the high school Spanish she can remember. It's all por favor this and muy bien that.
