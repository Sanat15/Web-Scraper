Rating: 2/5

Nice try but no cigar. This book strains hard to be funny, breezy, and it does succeed here and there, in the dialogue, especially between the main character, Scotty, a washed-up true crime book writer, and his slacker friend Mungus. Scotty's wife has just left him, he's got no writing assignments lined up, and then his best friend Molly, the hugely successful syndicated advice columnist, dies in a hit-and-run accident. Which Scotty is convinced was murder. But who would benefit from Molly's death? Well, the person who gets her high-profile gig, of course. And it's not clear who that will be, since the newspaper's new owner decides to set up a competition to find the next Molly. 

I think it was intended to be madcap, but I found the alternations between farce (Scotty and Mungus burglarize Molly's office, Watergate-style) and the pathetic (Scotty bemoans his writing block and general lack of success) and the merely incomprehensible (Scotty's affair with a married woman, his insistence to barge into people's offices accusing them of murdering Molly) exhausting. Scotty and Mungus' cheerfully a-moral life view veered into the misogynistic for me ("Bring us some girls") and then at the end Scotty became holier-than-thou. It all didn't make a lot of sense to me.
