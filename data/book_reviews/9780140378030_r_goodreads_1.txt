Rating: 4/5

Generally speaking, young adult literature irks me. It isn't an author sharing in the poignant simplicity of childhood. Nor is she delving, unfettered, into her own psyche. It's the literary equivalent of a "very special episode": Don't be anorexic! Embrace your sexuality!—Um, here's a marijuana reference, because I'm hip. That's how you know I'm hip.—Don't drink and drive! And so forth.

This book isn't like that at all.

It represents the true midpoint between childhood and adulthood. Mahy writes from a personal place, so her book is honest, respectful, strange, complex, beautiful.

I've always loved her children's lit. I love this book, too.

Recommended to all.
