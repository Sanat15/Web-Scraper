Rating: 4/5

Kazuo Ishiguro claims this is “not my best book", but I really enjoyed it, old chap! 

When We Were Orphans gives us a true unreliable narrator. Thank you, Ishiguro, show the kids how it's done! This is one of the tropes most misunderstood by #BookTok. That being said, I doubt social media is ever going to rediscover and blast this book into the best-seller list because frankly, it's a little off-putting, like its protagonist. 

Main character (and main orphan!) Christopher Banks isn't lying to us while he tells us his tale, nor is he truly deluded. Banks has a skewed perspective of events that's exacerbated by missing information. Putting the pieces of his history together made for a fun read. I enjoyed spending time with this gentleman detective, a socially awkward dude who is supremely unconcerned about his awkwardness. You're always trying to get a read on this guy, and that's thrilling. 

The last quarter of the book is a real vibes departure from everything that preceded it. I can see this is where Ishiguro is taking critical hits! Imagine going from P.G. Wodehouse to J.G. Ballard. 

The mystery's reveal is a "wait, what?" sort of situation, but easy to roll with. It may be a stretch for readers, but is accepted by our narrator. That's how we get the real reveal, the detective story was a character study all along.
