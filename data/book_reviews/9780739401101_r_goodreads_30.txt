Rating: 1/5

The structure of this reminded me of Asimov's retroactively glued together Robot/Empire/Foundation stories, before the glue dried. At first you have a bunch of loosely connected short stories, then two, more or less independent longer ones, about Cugel at first, then about the magicians of the last aeon.

Except that while Asimov is a mediocre writer with somewhat clunky worldbuilding, Vance is a horrible writer with a totally inconsistent world. The whole thing reads like a fable, at first it reminded me of Robert Silverberg's Majipoor. But then you realize it's not a conscious choice, it's that Vance is unable to write a consistent world and believable characters, even though it's clear he wants to.

It's really great to read about the Vancian magic at first, but the sense of wonder fades quite fast, and you are left with a world where everyone's ability is 100% plot dependent, every character speaks with the same, nifty, overphrased voice, the titular dying of the Earth is mentioned all the time but has little effect.
Everyone is outsmarting each other constantly with the most inane, mundane and retarded way possible. Every person is a piece of shit, but not in the good way. And worst of all, the magic is all over the place, once it solves everything, the other time the magician is totally helpless in a situation, where by all rights, he should be immune.

If written well, this would be a great world to write stories for. Vance was not the person to do it, though.

As a sidenote, Vance gets a lot of flak for having women only as target for rape and leering. This is not true at all. There are strong, independent women all throughout the stories, it's just that they are written equally shitty as the men (and other beings).
