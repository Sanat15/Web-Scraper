Rating: 4/5

The back cover of this book promises a wry, macabre tale of simple living, brutal murder, and a reasonably happy couple. There's a killing, not quite a murder, more like a Patricia Highsmith kind of offing, with plenty of malice but no intent. Still, there'll be a body in the septic tank before long.

Also, like Highsmith, the eponymous Girls, are a Lesbian couple. Two men, their foils, are a homosexual couple. It's all rather matter-of-fact. A fifth character, Mrs Marshall, does not reveal her sexual preference, but the author tells us in a Foreward that she is the only character taken from real life. And she is spectacular. I only wish Mrs Marshall had a few more scenes.

The author also tells us, to start Chapter 12, that This is not a mystery story. Indeed we see everything happen first-hand. And, he continues, You know well enough by now that the watcher was Bob. And yes, we're pretty sure that's him. But there's a tension, nevertheless, if only will someone be caught, and how so.

The story unravels, and the author intrudes. He explains things:

No horror can be invented by a writer of fiction which has not already happened. Who could invent the Holocaust, the Vietnam War, Laos, Idi Amin, the death squads of Argentina and San Salvador, the Fifth Brigade of President Mugabe and the prison camps of Iran where political prisoners are drained of blood to provide transfusions for fifteen-year-old soldiers? Literature follows and feeds on history; it does not lead.

Still, I was unprepared at the end, when the writing changed from the third-person to the first. But that did tidy things up.
