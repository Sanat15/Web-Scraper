Rating: 4/5

This book is about a young African-American boy named Jacob who hates being black. He complains that his family is poor and he feels oppressed and disadvantaged because of his skin color. One day, Jacob and his brother make a snowman that is black because the snow had been trampled on by so many people. To add the final touch, Jacob’s brother, Peewee, finds a colorful cloth in the trash that he turns into a scarf. Little do they know that the scarf is magical and it is called a kente, which brought magic to the people of the Ashanti tribe before they were sold into slavery. As the kente is placed on the snowman, the snowman comes to life. Jacob asserts his opinion that “black is bad” to which the snowman gives many opposing viewpoints. Jacob is still unconvinced so the snowman visits him that night and shows him many images of strong, brave Africans who should make Jacob proud of his heritage. The next day, as Jacob is looking for a gift for his mother, he catches a glimpse of a burning building which the snowman claims Peewee is inside of. The snowman uses the magical kente to lead Jacob inside the burning building so that he can save his brother. When he reaches his brother, the kente is only big enough to cover the two of them, so the snowman melts away and the boys come out together. Jacob finally feels happy to be part of his family and feels good about himself. The illustrations are dark and appear to be watercolor paintings. The author brings the issue of racial discrimination to light and gives a different perspective to the reader. I would read this to a class during black history month.
