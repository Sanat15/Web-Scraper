Rating: 3/5

For a book written 10ish years after the first printing of LoTR and 10ish years before The Silmarillion, Carter's stab at dissecting Tolkien's sources is not bad. A lot of the info he presents probably would have been valuable in that time when people were still asking who this Tolkien fella is. Today, most of what he presents is part of the common knowledge for anyone even mildly interested in the world of Middle-Earth. 
I think this book has value as an artifact of the time, if nothing else. It's a glimpse into the burgeoning fandom that we see today, an insight into what it must have been like for someone to pick up Tolkien as an unknown author and be blown away. And then feel the need to grasp at more through analysis. There's a lot of excitement and wonder at LoTR in this book, backed by a lot of suppositions and unknowns for info we can now easily find with a quick search on our phones. 
I enjoyed reading this, even if it didn't grow my understanding of Tolkien and his world.
