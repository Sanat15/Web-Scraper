Grade: 62% (D)

This tries to set up a new era for the team by introducing its core roster Impulse, Superboy, Robin, Wonder Girl, and others but the execution feels uneven and stretched. The early issues spend a lot of time shuffling between characters and setup rather than building momentum, and while the intention is clearly to establish dynamics, it often comes across more like scattered introductions than a cohesive story. The Deathstroke threat does eventually bring structure, but even that arc feels inconsistent in pacing and impact, with key moments happening quickly and without enough buildup to land emotionally.

There are flashes of stronger material like the Superboy twist and some of the interpersonal tension between the younger heroes and the Justice League but none of it fully develops before the story shifts again. The result is a volume that feels like groundwork more than payoff. Even the more dramatic beats, like character injuries or confrontations, don’t always carry lasting weight because of how quickly the narrative moves on. By the end, the foundation for future stories is there, but this first volume doesn’t quite stand on its own as a satisfying read.

Recommend? Maybe, but it's mostly setup, with little payoff in this volume.
