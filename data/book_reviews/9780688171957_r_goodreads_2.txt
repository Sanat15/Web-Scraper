Rating: 5/5

This book is an absolute marvel of paper engineering. Ten phobias get the full sculpture treatment combined with some evil-genius level perspective drawing. Some people won't be able to finish reading this.

Dentophobia--Fear of dentists. The dental drill spins as it moves towards the reader. It spins.

Aerophobia--Fear of flying in an airplane. The window view rotates from pretty-green-countryside to the engine is on fire!

Ophidiophobia--Fear of snakes. (So would Aerophidiophobia be the fear of Snakes on a Plane?)

Claustrophobia--Fear of enclosed spaces. This page doesn't open all the way. Freaking brilliant.

Mysophobia--Fear of filth. You can lift the toilet seats...if you can stand to touch them.

Glossophobia--Fear of public speaking. This flat display of distinctly unimpressed faces glowering at the reader is the most mundane of the bunch.

Arachnophobia--Fear of spiders. And the sixteen-inch tarantula that opens when you turn the page brushes your hand unless you know it's coming and take precautions.

Acrophobia--Fear of heights. The paper sculpture is relatively simple but the perspective of the illustration does a remarkable simulation of looking straight down from a skyscraper roof.

Coulrophobia--Fear of clowns. This clown offers the reader a lollipop.

Necrophobia--Fear of dying. Does an impressive simulation of looking up from the bottom of a grave while men in suits shovel dirt on the reader.
