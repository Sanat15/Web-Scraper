Rating: 5/5

Superb book. Written in plain English, easy to read. 

The first half of the book is his first autobiography, incredible. The second half is Arnold’s thoughts on training, diet etc. not necessary but interesting. 

Written by Arnold at the height of his career, 29/30 years old - incredible to think as you read through this book. 

Key takeaways: 
- Arnold used visualisation regularly. 
- His ridiculous self confidence, born out of the work he put in - not naturally given. 
- Arnold acted like a winner before he was a winner. 

Key highlights: 

The secret is contained in a three-part formula I learned in the gym: self-confidence, a positive mental attitude, and honest hard work.

I knew I was a winner. I knew I was destined for great things. People will say that kind of thinking is totally immodest. I agree. Modesty is not a word that applies to me in any way—I hope it never will.

I was working to create the greatest, most perfect body anybody had ever seen.

It doesn't do any good to go through training like a blind man, to just go through the motions. Motions mean nothing. You have to realize what is happening to you. You have to want results.

The mind must carry through. If a man stands there and thinks for one-tenth of a second, "Maybe I can't lift it," it's gone. He will not make the lift.

Positive thinking can be contagious. Being surrounded by winners helps you develop into a winner.

I rushed to the Mr. Olympia contest. I entered the dressing room the way I'd been going in everyplace lately, like I was just taking over. (Top G mindset)
