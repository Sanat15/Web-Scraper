Rating: 4/5

"McGahern brings us the tonic gift of the best fiction, the sense of truth - the sense of a transparency that permits us to see imaginary lives more clearly than we see our own." —John Updike

It’s interesting how male vs female authors portray the family sagas. McGahern’s prose is quiet, brutal, real, straightforward.

With a slow paced prose, the story enfolds around a family of 5 children with a patriarchal and controlling father and his second wife. Despite the violent outbreaks, I couldn’t not notice how family oriented the novel is and how the father’s authority tends to diminish throughout the story while the children get more independent partly thanks to his second wife.

The house itself is somehow a character, that hides the insecurities, luck of communication, and failures of the father and is a retreat for children who come back again and again. 

The book was shortlisted for Booker prize in 1990.
