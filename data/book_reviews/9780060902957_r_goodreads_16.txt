Rating: 3/5

Simone Weil is, for me, one of the most fascinating people who ever lived. She's like Alexander the Great, Joan of Arc, Napoleon, or yea, even Donald Trump, as a figure whose life would seem 100% implausible and over the top, too wild even for fiction, if she weren't real. Her oddities can overshadow the fact that her learning was marvelously impressive and her style quite magnetic; she is always worth taking seriously even if she was clearly still an immature thinker in many ways and she is often given to impossibly bold, sweeping pronouncements. Of course she does not adopt an orthodox Christian viewpoint and there are many things that such readers will not agree with, but this collection is worth reading for the insights into literature, the Bible, beauty, and contemplation. It is in many ways an essential library item. However, the more I revisit this, the more I can't help but get the impression of Weil as a way overzealous grad student who thinks she knows it all and makes massively provocative claims that can't be substantiated. I mean, you can't just drop the phrase, "God eats us" as factual without any further extrapolation and expect us to nod heartily in agreement.
