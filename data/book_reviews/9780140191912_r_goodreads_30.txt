Rating: 4/5

Book 1 is a lucid and well developed theory about the nature of creativity in the arts and science.

Book 2 is a poorly conceived attempt to extend these ideas into biology and psychology. The analogy is often strained and the science has not aged gracefully. There is a long section dedicated to beating the dead horse of behavioralism, for instance. Here the editors let AK down. Presumably, this is the reason the book has gone out of print. There are useful insights, such as his anticipation of the concept of encapsulation, but AK would have been better advised to address these subjects in a separate book. He seems to have doubts himself, writing at the end of chapter V: "The reader may consider some of these conclusions trivial, others perhaps as rash generalizations."
