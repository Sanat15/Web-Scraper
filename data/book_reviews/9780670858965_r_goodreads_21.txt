Rating: 4/5

Beautiful.

GGK writes between this line of imagined and history. His books tend to fall into the fantasy genre, despite the fantastical elements being very subtle. 

Here, he reimagines the lives of 3 key players with different faiths. You can either read into his books and depictions or take it at face value. Either go with the disguised cultures or see the intricacies. 

You come to care deeply about all three characters as religious and political tensions escalate towards a holy war.
How can one pursue friendship, love, and loyalty, when personal bonds clash with societal conflict?

It has what I enjoy: political intrigue, intense personal relationships spanning family, love, friendships, comrades. 

However, I always find a slight disconnect with GGK’s characters which means that, whilst I think they are multi-faceted, I feel like I’m observing rather than invested and involved. 

I also have to admit that whilst I enjoy GGK’s prose, it’s not a favourite of mine. It’s evocative and flows well, but looking back, nothing stood out to me. Maybe I just shouldn’t compare authors to Ken Liu when people say elevated prose.

Probably my third favourite GGK.
