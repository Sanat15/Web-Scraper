Rating: 4/5

An ambiguous treatment of resignation. You see the endurance gained by resigning oneself to one’s circumstances; but also in places resignation in this novel is interwoven with a naivete that causes the dire circumstances themselves. Markandaya’s characters are sympathetic, and you hope for them, but also you dread the innocent pitfalls you see them walking into. There is a Steinbeckian pathos in this description of India’s rural poor of the mid 1900s. Also it humanizes the insecurity and impossible choices of poverty. The characters’ find resilience in their attachment to place, among other things.

Markandaya gives the dialogue an exotic formality, which confers extra dignity and humanity to her characters. As this is the opposite of how many authors use twisted and unpolished argots to bring disadvantaged characters to life, it was very effective.
