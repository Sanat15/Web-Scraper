Rating: 4/5

Just a quick review: this book contained a collection of essays and speeches from Camus’ life that revolve around liberty, life, resistance, a history of rebellion, and death. Many of the selections were political, discussing pressing conflicts of the time (Civil unrest in French Algeria, despotism in Hungary, Nazi occupation of France during WWII, etc.). My two favorite pieces were a collection of some of his publications for a French Resistance newspaper, as well as a longer essay arguing to eliminate the death penalty. The resistance newspaper collections, originally published under Nazi occupation, were written as “letters to a German friend,” and explored themes of guilt, rebellion, intellectualism vs. nationalism, and much more.
