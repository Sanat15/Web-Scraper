Rating: 3/5

This book intrigued me because of the topic of persecution of Chinese Christians. It was evident that the author’s research was thorough and that he presented each harrowing scenario in eye-opening ways to a person like me who lives comfortably in a country that allows for my freedom of speech and religion. This is fiction however, to read this book just to learn about what happens in China to those who proclaim to be followers of Christ was worthwhile. And readers also get a perspective on the cultural dichotomy of life in China vs living in the US. I appreciated the message that challenged you to figure out if you are willing and prepared to die for your faith knowing of the hope you have in heaven.

Interspersed with the fictional storyline is a glimpse into heaven and the final war on earth (Armegeddon) as Alcorn interprets it. This is always a big issue for me when I read Christian fiction. Interpretations aren’t truth and only God knows when and how these events will take place. So for me, I’d rather leave the heavenly interpretations out of fiction because it seems a bit dangerous to present it as the way heaven is and could be misconstrued by readers as fact. I was put off my the sections where ancestors who had already been resurrected were in a role of commenting on the action of the novel watching down from a portal and speculating the identity of the last martyr. So if you hold to similar doctrines as Alcorn, you may be more inspired by these sections.
