Rating: 5/5

When Madeleine Houser, a successful novelist, relocates from New York City to Kansas, she finds that writing anywhere may not be as easy as she believed. With a looming deadline, Maddie desperately needs to find a way to concentrate. Taking the advice of her friend and neighbor, Ginny Ross, Maddie sets up a temporary office in a quaint, but currently unused bed and breakfast, owned by Ginny’s friend, Arthur Tyler. Finally finding the much-needed peace and quiet to write, Maddie steadily progresses toward her deadline as she continues to utilize the B&amp;B – and strikes up a surprising ‘pen pal’ friendship with Art. As the friendship grows through their daily messages, neither Maddie nor Art know what to expect. Is love possible without meeting face-to-face? Will misconceptions and lurking pasts stand in its way?

The second novella in Zondervan’s A Year of Weddings – a collection of wedding-based novellas by best-selling inspirational romance novelists – “A January Bride,” by Deborah Raney, is an enjoyable and light read. By no means is the plot a surprise (with the giveaways in both the title and the collection’s premise), but the story does have its quirks. The character interaction and misconceptions are delightful and I thoroughly enjoyed my chance to read this novella. Though it’s part of a collection, it can definitely be read without the other novellas, and I would definitely recommend it to anyone with an interest in inspirational romance and a free afternoon. Again, I greatly appreciate BookSneeze for providing me with this free copy and the opportunity to review it honestly.
