My orange tree is not the biggest in the world. Its soil is poor, dried up by misery and violence, but its branches are free, capturing the sun and dreams. My orange tree is not the most beautiful, but it captures the life and happiness that the earth has rejected. The juice of its fruits erases the blows' marks and transforms the tears into a rainbow.
My orange tree grows in my heart; listen to my sorrows. It is lonely and abandoned in a neighborhood where only the precariousness and reality of adults grow.
When life exudes resignation and sadness, my orange tree perfumes poetry. It will never be big and beautiful; it will always be different, but it will grow and always be in my head. And its fruits will always taste of wonder and innocence, the bitter taste of adults. So my orange tree feeds on my tears and joys, and I engraved my childhood.
