Rating: 5/5

have been fascinated recently by hockey culture because it is so homogeneous, homosocial, and homophobic. a macho sport that swallows up thousands of young men every year, chews them up, spits them out, leaves them emotionally stunted and warped without skills or a support network outside of the isolationist cult that has used them up. the focus here is on canadian minor and major junior league hockey clubs. throughout the book but more specifically in the last chapter robinson places the blame on the commodification of players and the league's financial incentives to allow and ignore abuse.

despite being written 30 years ago the insights remain really relevant- see the 2018 WJC trial. 

- players bought and sold, uprooted by the draft system and sent hundreds of kilometres away on their own with their dreams and futures placed entirely in the care of their coaches; taught that they're disposable and that physical, emotional, and sexual violence committed against them is normal and a requirement for them to make it in hockey; comparisons to other hierarchical all-male structures like the military and churches where young men are similarly systematically vulnerable to abuse.
- hazing rituals
- objectification and humiliation of young women (i just learned that they call the girls who sleep with hockey players "puck bunnies" because they get passed around from player to player like a puck??); gang rape and physical abuse
- community attitudes; deification of teenage boys and sweeping all destructive behaviours under the rug; living vicariously through these young boys
- socioeconomic and racial barriers of entry; harassment and discrimination against players who stand out
- retaliation against people who stand up for themselves (former employees fired and blacklisted, players traded and dropped, girls forced to move out of town) while abusers get off with little to no consequence. 
- "players first" initiative as an ineffectual policy that does nothing to address the structural reasons why coaches are able to so easily abuse the young boys in their charge and that completely disregards the young women who suffer at the hands of players

really made me want to reread beartown i think that book nails it
