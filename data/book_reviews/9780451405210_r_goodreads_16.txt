Rating: 3/5

This was my favourite book of this series so far. 

Nicola starts off as a very angry, young woman. To be fair, I can see why, but it gets a bit wearing after a while. Gilliam approaches the whole situation in a refreshingly un-medieval way, where he actually respects a woman. He does marry her against will, but he doesn't force her to consummate the marriage, despite the customs of the time. It might seem strange to like him for not being a rapist, but so many of the noblemen in these books are unconscionably vile to the women, abusing them every which way, that it's nice to come across one who doesn't.

I like Jos in this book and how his journey mirrors that of Nicola's. And, as ever, the world of these characters is vividly drawn without overwhelming the story with unnecessary detail.
