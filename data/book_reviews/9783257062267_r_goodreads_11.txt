Rating: 5/5

Overall a very interesting and straight storyline, I couldn’t put it away. I love the flashbacks, because they subtly put the story into context and create depth and meaning to the characters and their actions. :))
The author manages to create unique and interesting characters, who all are - likeable or not - relatable to some extent, and I was able to understand their motivations and sympathise with them even though the reader is experiencing the entire book out of the perspective of one main character. Each one has a purpose to the story/ in relation to the protagonist, and because they’re not entirely realistic this really creates the Charme of the book to me. Almost everything makes sense within the fiction - I only didn’t like the ending of the plot much, the way, Lew turned out to be in the end. It was a lame explanation, and I would’ve enjoyed it much more if for example there had been a more peaceful plot twist. 
It was written in a humorous yet serious tone, and it plays with the naive and logical way of how Sascha sees the world. He himself is a very suitable main character; he has an interesting background, is smart, Jewish, and not unattractive. He achieved something, and he fell. He is like a hero but the villain at same time, while Lew seems to be the hero and turns to be evil. (Lame) 

Highly recommended!!!!
