Rating: 3/5

I don't know why it never occurred to me to look up the first Harlequin Presents ever published, but I did and to my surprise, it was very affordable! So when you look at those red and white contemporary HPs in the grocery store, remember: it all started with this one in 1970.

That said, this is a pretty mild start to an imprint that is notorious for its over the top shenanigans and jealous/possessive alpha heroes. The heroine discovers her husband was cheating on her when he's found dead with his mistress in a car wreck. This is right after she suffered a miscarriage, by the way. After recovering from heartsickness, she volunteers to pose as a sort of rent-a-guardian to two small children who are on their way to Greece to live with their uncle: the hero, Leon.

TL;DR he ends up marrying her so he doesn't have to hire a nanny when he sees how good she is with the kids. They end up in a loveless marriage of convenience where they argue over very important things, such as whether women ought to be independent or whether it's right for him to switch his nephew with a stick. No, I'm not kidding.

GATES OF STEEL is a pretty gentle HP. It's also a rarity in the sense that it's one of the early Harelquins and the FMC definitely isn't a virgin. I think if you enjoy marriages of convenience with a lot of back and forth over terms and boundaries, you'll like this book. I wish Leon had been more actively into the heroine, though. Until the very last couple pages, it didn't seem like he cared much about her at all. 

3 stars
