Rating: 3/5

There's a lot going on here, for a relatively slim book: a complex family, happy and unhappy and disintegrating and adhering all at once, and also an entire gothic novel plot that builds and resolves in a sliver of space between the more pedestrian family fights. I'm not convinced it all hangs together. I'm not convinced it doesn't, either; there are certainly plenty of thematic overlaps. (This book has a lot to say on mothers and the state of motherhood.) Prickly, complicated, and I'm still not sure if we're meant to take the narrator as reliable or not. But worth reading.

(There is also an amusing to me bit at the end where Mahy feels compelled to explain in some detail how a word processor works, because I guess this is technology she assumes not all of her readers will have encountered? Also as far as I can tell the protagonist is using a computer that doesn't have a mouse. Honestly, both of these seem a little surprising for 1995; I wonder if Mahy wrote the book somewhat earlier than it was published?)
