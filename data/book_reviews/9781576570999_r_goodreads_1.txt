Rating: 3/5

Happy Spring! It’s a few days late to do a book associated (though loosely) with Spring, but I was trapped in toilet hell so I couldn’t do it on the proper day. Anywho: I picked this Shivers since, well, nature theming. Was it a better one of the series? Definitely the best I’ve read in months from this franchise, even if it isn’t a series high. The first hundred pages of this book is a straightforward but moderately fun and enjoyable curse story; kid does something after being warned it could cause a curse, curse ensues, and he resolves it accordingly. It’s nothing impressive but pretty ight, and there’s some dark moments and effects of the curse that helped carry things along. The writing and proust is surprisingly some of the best I’ve seen from this series, and I have no clue why. Mr. Spenser just locked in, I presume. And in general, this is pretty inoffensive for a majority of the book… and then it shits the bed. The final sixth of the book has the main (who was by far the best Shivers protagonist thus far by the way) revert back to Neanderthal intelligence when he makes the dumbest decision I’ve seen from any character in this series. Actually brain dead. It goes into a lame add-on that is pretty vague on what’s truly happening, which is a bit annoying though I think there’s a dumb moral muddled in there somewhere about life’s ups and downs, and just serves as useless padding to the average one-hundred and twenty page threshold these oldies (as in kids horror series) love to cross. So yeah, bad close to a pretty alright story. Overall, 7/10. It’s good, albeit a familiar predictable plot and leaves off on a relatively rough note. There was, perchance, a curse in the jungle.
