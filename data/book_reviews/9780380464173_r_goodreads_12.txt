Rating: 4/5

If you love Dorothy L Sayers's writing and adore Lord Peter Wimsey (and there are many who do), this little book is a joy. The author has used the family history scattered through the novels and what must have been a very entertaining correspondence with DLS herself, to compile a heraldic and family history of the Wimseys. It is all the greatest of fun, especially for a number of good jokes that are smuggled in, where one Wimsey or another, like Macavity, wasn't quite there, such as the controversy over who wrote the works of Shakespeare. 

The most charming part of the whole conceit is the truly diplomatic way in which the author (who as a Herald himself is the authority on these matters) deals with a solecism made by DLS in one of her novels. In Clouds of Witness the 13th Duke of Denver is (shock, horror) wrongly described as a Peer of the UNited Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland, when the creation of his dukedom is far too early for that. Who better to propose an elegant and unique explanation than C W Scott-Giles, Fitzalan Pursuivant of Arms Extraordinary? A great chap to have on your side.
