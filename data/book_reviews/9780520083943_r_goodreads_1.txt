Rating: 4/5

"Read the whole book" Joseph Horowitz exhorts in the introduction, worried that people will just be looking for a musical, socioeconomic, or political read on American Wagner productions from the 1880s onwards rather than an examination of all. I have now read the whole book and think it probably could have been 2-3 books, as much of the economic/commercial side of opera production, the Italian vs German opera star system, the historical records of unrecorded music, the unique place of women in the audience and on the stage, the American insistence on art as moral good only, and the immigrant culture that shaped New York's theatregoers are all fascinating and just when one got going the book switched to another theme. A great overview, and I suppose it's down to me to go through the bibliography now.
