Rating: 5/5

There is a modern tendency to describe The Golden Rendezvous as “Die Hard at sea.” This is charming but backwards. When Alistair MacLean published the novel in 1962, Bruce Willis was seven years old. If anything, Die Hard is The Golden Rendezvous in a skyscraper. MacLean simply had the good sense to set his thriller somewhere far more suitable: a luxury ship in the middle of the ocean, where no one can leave and help is very far away.

The story follows ship’s officer John Carter, whose job description does not normally include dismantling elaborate international crime plots. Unfortunately for him, a group of cool, highly organized villains seize control of the vessel during a voyage involving a fortune in gold. Their plan is clever, ruthless, and—like all villainous plans—based on the comforting assumption that nothing will go wrong.

Naturally, everything goes wrong.

Carter spends most of the novel wounded, outnumbered, and quietly sarcastic while trying to unravel what is really happening on board. This is classic MacLean: a dry, intelligent hero who simply gets on with the job while the situation becomes steadily more impossible. Meanwhile the villains remain impressively calm and professional, which somehow makes them more dangerous than the usual shouting maniacs.

What makes The Golden Rendezvous so enjoyable is its momentum. Every chapter introduces another twist, another clue, or another complication. Threads appear everywhere—odd behaviour, unexplained motives, pieces of information that don’t quite fit—and MacLean neatly ties them all together in the final act.

Yes, the plot occasionally wanders into the delightfully improbable: stolen gold, nuclear threats, and a hero performing athletic feats that would normally require several weeks of medical leave. But MacLean is such a confident storyteller that the reader never stops to question it.

You start the book intending to read a chapter or two. A short time later you look up and realise you are halfway through.

Which, in the world of thrillers, is about the highest compliment possible.
