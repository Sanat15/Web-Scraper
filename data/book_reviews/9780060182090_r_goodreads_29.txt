Rating: 4/5

In some ways I enjoyed this more than Old Glory, Raban does not come over as misanthropic, but the journey itself was less interesting than an exploration down the Mississippi.

Raban leaves England (and his wife, though amicably) to stay in a series of locations in the USA, mainly New York, a small town in Alabama and Seattle, where he ended up living. The most interesting section relates to Guntersville in Alabama where he rents a small cottage, borrows a friendly dog as protection, and gets to know the local dignitaries. These include the founder of a company making fire retardant suits, local school students and the Mayor who ran the local car dealership.

He is fascinated with Macy's in New York, which had recently gone upmarket and promoted a rural English toff look. He spends time with Korean immigrants in Seattle and explores their challenges of adopting American attitudes to the family or marrying only other Koreans.

Raban's journey took place in the late '80s and one can now look up some of the people he met or businesses he encountered and see what happened to them. (The management buyout of Macy's didn't go well.)

Funniest part: being fined by a car rental company because his borrowed dog had eaten the passenger seatbelt. I've no idea if Raban had a dog later in life but he seemed much happier with one than without.

Overall a good read giving a slice of life in the USA at the end of the Reagan era.
