Rating: 1/5

A marriage of convenience? Characters in academia? World War 2? Vienna? The Morning Gift had so much to offer and though I did not expect much, I hoped for an enjoyable, light read.
Done right, it could have been. 
Done right, it might have been a beautiful romantic story that intertwines music and paleontology and Vienna.
While several factors played into the one star rating, the biggest thing for me was how big of a theme sex plays throughout. I've come to expect something...more from Ibbotson. But whether it is the main character being pressured into it from her boyfriend, her misunderstandings about a one night stand, or even the general inundation of it throughout the pages (everything from bugs not mating to older women shuddering over the memory of their wedding night to the heroine's almost obsessive commentary on Freud) the book is just full of the act and thought of sex. It isn't romantic. It is more of 'Oh, you'll know who you love based on who you can sleep with.'
Not my cup of tea. 
Ruth Berger, the heroine, is a Mary Sue who I at least appreciated until the end. Then I simply gave up on her. The whole 'he sent me a necklace so he must not want me to be his wife!' thing was ridiculous. Also the fact that she manages to not talk to Quin and resolve their differences after that. Also that she decides to go have her baby and like, what? Not tell him? Not tell her parents? Not tell his lawyer? I'm totally for her having the kid but it made no sense that she would just drop everything and hide. This girl faced the Nazis, for heaven's sake.  
Quin had potential but he has to many Mommy/Daddy/Grandpa issues. Also, I got irritated with all the references (even after he was married to Ruth!) of his behavior with other women. 
I hated Heini. I hated him from the start. Their relationship kind of reminded me of Skip Beat!, Vol. 01...

Anyway, while the writing was charming as usual, I hated the last quarter of the book.
