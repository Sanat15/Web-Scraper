Rating: 3/5

Finally got around to reading Alan Dean Foster's Spellsinger, as it is a book and series I've been aware of but had not read for decades. Unfortunately, its existence as a part of a series was my biggest issue with the novel. Spellsinger is definitely just the first act of a larger story, and was obviously planned as such from the start. While we get a few glimpses into the activities of the main antagonists of the story, they do not encounter the protagonists at all in the first novel, with the exception of a brief vision and a solitary emissary.

That being said, I am interested in seeing where the story goes. I enjoyed the means by which Jon-Tom, a pre-law student, musician, and part-time janitor, was transported to the fantasy world. The development of his "spellsinging" abilities was fun, both in the mechanics and the results.

The characters are likable for the most part. The standard sword and sorcery tropes are largely present, as filtered through an anthropomorphic animal lens. Humans blend with these evolved animals (mostly) seamlessly.

The character flaw that was the most difficult for me to look past was the tendency toward objectification of women by Jon-Tom and Mudge, one of his main companions. It was the element of the book that put me off the most, and almost caused me not to finish. Just before I gave up, though, Foster held a mirror to this behavior through the introduction of the character Flor. From that point forward, I became more personally aware of the didactic approach taken with the story, not only regarding sexism, but racism and classism as well. While Jon-Tom's attitudes changed only slightly by the end of the book (and Mudge's not at all), I'm hoping to see further character development over the course of the series.
