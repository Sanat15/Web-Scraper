Rating: 5/5

This is one of the best books I’ve read this year, much better that Ward’s The Call of the Distant Mammoths. To those reviewers who miss the point, and complain that the Gorgons are not more discussed, I’ll just say that the book is about the Permian Triassic extinction. The exact dividing line is hard to find, but the Gorgons were only in the late Permian. Gorgon fossils mean the era is end Permian, close to the extinction event. The black and white pictures show geologic features and rocks, because that is what paleontology is all about, looking at rocks! The evocative part is Ward’s self journey into his own past and future. The added focus on South Africa history, politics, and crime, reminds me somewhat of Paul Theroux. The sub story about the 19th century Boer farmhouse and cemetery is poignant. Seeing his personal and writing growth in the 10 years between the two books is remarkable!
