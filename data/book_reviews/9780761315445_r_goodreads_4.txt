Rating: 3/5

This book is about baby George who can talk fluently and intelligently from the moment he is born, although he doesn’t reveal this fact to any but his older sister, Laura. She is very excited to find out she has a baby brother who is much cleverer than her – or in fact most adults. George insists that no adults find out straight away how clever he is as they won’t understand, not like children. George, not wanting to shock his parents starts to drip feed words to his parents to start to show them his intelligence. They are very shocked at first – but get used to the idea of having a child under one who can ask questions, say when he wants to go to the toilet and request certain meals for dinner. The book peaks at Georges 1st birthday when he decides to reveal to his friends and family his true intelligence. All his family are very shocked, his parents are proud – and his sister knew how clever he was all along! This book really captures the imagination of all children who have had a baby brother or sister born into the family, as the special bond between the siblings is apparent through out. I also like how the book makes the parents out to be the ones not able to cope, when the children can as all children will enjoy this concept. The book is also humorous as George, a 1 year old baby is dictating instructions for his own party. The suspense is built through the books as you wait to find out what George will reveal next, and what other people’s reactions will be. The book ends with Laura and George talking about what George could do when he is ‘grown up’, to which George replies, “I’m going to write funny stories for children.” Which obviously, he does!
