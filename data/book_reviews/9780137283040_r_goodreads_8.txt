Rating: 3/5

Higgins was a journalist, lawyer, and writer best known for his noir crime novels, including the well known "Friends of Eddie Coyle" which I read and thoroughly enjoyed a couple of years ago.

So I opened up this book, which is a memoir published in 1989 of his 40 year love/hate relationship with the Boston Red Sox (who then still hadn't won a WS since 1918), and his relationship with his father and grandfather who took him to Fenway Park as a kid and introduced him to the intricacies of the game . . . 

It's a good (not great) book, diving deep into Red Sox players and lore that filled his childhood (and his adult life with his own children), but it was the front inscription that I found most amazing: In a child's script, "Father's Day 1989. Because you always take us to baseball games . . . and you're a great coach. Love, Matthew [then age 9] and [printed] Christopher [then age 6]"

Wonderful memories!!
