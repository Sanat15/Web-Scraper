Rating: 4/5

I read this book thirty years ago when it was originally published. I remember thinking, "Well, this could never happen!" How very wrong I was.

It's an engaging story: educated (and educator!) white woman, in her 40's, with two grown children and a lawyer husband decides not to have a third child and elects to have an abortion - which, at the place and time of the abortion is legal. The state legislature passes a law shortly after her abortion that states that a woman who has an abortion can be charged with capital murder.

Howard Fast throws in all sorts of social commentary: suspicion of outsiders (especially northerners), prejudice against educated people, antisemitism (in a very big way), small town v big city. The prosecutor practically froths as he moves through this trial, counting on a local jury to help leverage his name into big-time politics.

It's very plausible, and therefore chilling. If you read it, pass it on to a younger woman and recommend it.
