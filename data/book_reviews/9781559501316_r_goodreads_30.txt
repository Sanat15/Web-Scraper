Rating: 3/5

Jesus Christ on a pogo stick, if you thought Crash or 120 Days of Sodom pushed the envelope, buckle up because The Gas doesn't just push it—it sets the whole damn thing on fire and dances naked in the flames. Charles Platt unleashes a psychedelic fever dream where a lab accident floods Southern England with an aphrodisiac vapor that strips away every last shred of human decency. What follows is a careening road trip through a countryside turned into a writhing, blood-soaked carnival of every conceivable (and inconceivable) taboo you could cram into 200 pages. Incest? Check. Bestiality? Double-check. Necrophilia, cannibalism, and enough sadomasochistic slapstick to make your therapist quit on the spot? Oh, hell yes. It's like if The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy got gangbanged by the Marquis de Sade and birthed a mutant baby that's equal parts hilarious, horrifying, and "what the actual fuck did I just read?"

Platt tosses every sequel-worthy situation and boundary-shattering kink into the blender—zombies? Nah, but close enough with the gas-fueled hordes. Cults? You bet. Apocalyptic chases? Non-stop. It's over-the-top graphic in ways that had cops raiding publishers back in the '80s (true story: 3,000 copies seized like it was printed on unicorn hide). But here's the kicker: as a die-hard fan of extreme lit who devours Bukowski benders and Bataille bloodbaths for breakfast, even I couldn't enjoy this ride. It's memorable as a root canal from a chainsaw-wielding dentist—seared into your brain forever, but you'll be gargling mouthwash for weeks. 

Read at your peril if you're chasing shocks. Skip if you're faint of heart (or stomach). Me? I'll stick to my safer extremes next time. What a wild, wretched beast of a book.
