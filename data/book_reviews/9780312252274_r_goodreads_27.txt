Rating: 4/5

First, the cover. What a cover. Gloved hands, in costume, masquerading as a mermaid. Those hands, that cover, captures the essence of this novel better than any words.

But I’ll try some words. Santos-Febres captures a universe I’ll never experience in real life. The news reports the statistics of growing up trans, but this book helps a reader to a far deeper exposure to the pain and exploitation of vulnerable trans children. But it’s not all pain; there is the joy experienced when families form, friendships are made. There is joy when a child, finding her trans community can be free to her nature. 

Sirena Selena was originally published in 2000, so more than 20 years ago. It’s written by a non-trans, non drag queen author. With those caveats, the book may stray from today’s nomenclature and understandings. In the novel, drag queen and transgender women are combined, meaning the drag queens are self described trans women, with the drag community the only place they can feel at home. Today it seems there is a lot of conflict between the drag and trans communities. I am no expert on this, but it seems like there is now a lot of backlash by trans women directed to the drag community and vice verse. I’m happy to be schooled if I’ve misspoken. That all said, this book is not dated in showing how trans and drag women are marginalized, abused, and perhaps the most at risk people in the world. 

I was mesmerized by the prose. Seduced by the imagery. And ripped apart by the tragedy. This is a good book. My only fault with it were a few oddly discordant chapters where I was confused by the author’s intent. But those moments didn’t distract from the overall strength of the story. 

Sirena Selina is told through multiple perspectives and points of view and that too a bit of adaptation, but the style worked. I strongly recommend this novel. It is my choice for Puerto Rico in my read-around-the-world challenge.
