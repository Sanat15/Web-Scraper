2 stars, Metaphorosis reviews

Summary 
There's been a murder in Castle Perilous, or just outside it. But Lord Incarnadine is away handling other matters, as are two of the more powerful guests, and it's left to casual golfing buddies Thaxton and Dalton to work out what happened.

Review 
As it happens, I know exactly when and where I first read this book – on a flight to Copenhagen in May, 1991, according to the boarding pass I found in it. Unfortunately, I can see why it didn’t stick in my mind.

DeChancie clearly has fun with his Castle Perilous books, but in this book, he’s having a lot more fun than we are. Despite starting with a murder investigation, the murder is entirely peripheral to the plot. Or, it would be, if the book had a plot. Since it doesn’t, what we get is a bunch of characters randomly wandering around, haphazardly interacting with inhabitants of various universes and then, with no visible difficulty, abruptly wrapping everything up thanks to magic.

I think that there are three plot threads, but I frankly found them so dull and so similar that even just after finishing this slim volume, I find it hard to remember. They do follow familiar and pleasant characters, but there’s so very little tension in any of it that the book is more of a time filler than a destination. While I give DeChancie credit for giving bland golfers Thaxton and Dalton a chance at the spotlight, they turn out to be just as dull there as they were as supporting actors.

Whether you’re a Perilous fan or a newcomer, I recommend skipping this book and leaving it to the truly devoted fans who just can’t get enough of the series. I’m apparently even less one than I recalled.
