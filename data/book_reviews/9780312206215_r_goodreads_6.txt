Rating: 4/5

In a phrase, too much of a good thing. At almost three hundred pages, it's too long for a pulpy thrill ride and it gets to the point where it's working against its premise of setting an adventure on Route 66. Because there's a time when every reveal has been revealed and the love story has been consummated and yet the author still has more local color and historical facts and car talk he wants to put in, so we just kinda keep going with the heroes having more great sex and narrowly escaping from a few more showdowns with the bad guys. There's one bit where a gas station blows up and incinerates an entire ghost town and it only takes ONE of the bad guys out because we still have another 1/4 of the book to go!

Maybe that sounds like a minor issue. There are, after all, books that are four hundred pages or five hundred pages or even more. Lord of the Rings is arguably one 1000+ page book. I'm just saying it felt self-indulgent that the story reached a point where it seemed natural to wrap up and the author kept it going because of his own conceit that he wanted to do the entirety of Route 66 without enough plot to go for all that page time. It kinda reminded me of Man of Steel, where you get one huge city-destroying fight and then another huge city-destroying fight and it's just like "I get it, I've seen what it looks like for Superman to be punched through a building, message received."

So that's my criticism. It's a fun ride, but it's a long haul. A bit like any road trip--however much you enjoy it, the real relief is having it come to an end.
