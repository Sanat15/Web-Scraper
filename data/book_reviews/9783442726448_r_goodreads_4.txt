Rating: 4/5

If it were about 100 pages shorter, it would've been perfect.
Edward Haggard is a doctor falling apart after a pretty intense affair with a fellow surgeon's wife. It starts at a funeral, where he meets her, and ends with another death...
I knew now there'd be no easy relief from the pain aroused from within, the pain I'd foolishly thought almost extinct. (...) Time, I'd thought, would lay these inner ghosts. But no, apparently not.
It's one long, deeply unsettling monologue. As you read, you start building your understanding based on Haggard's version of events. Then, with a single sentence or one strange detail, everything unravels, and nothing feels reliable anymore. His obsession with this woman runs so deep that he invents a condition, Haggard's disease, just to justify loving her spirit in someone else's body.
Haggard is sophisticated, broken, and crazy. I didn't like him, but I couldn't stop reading.
