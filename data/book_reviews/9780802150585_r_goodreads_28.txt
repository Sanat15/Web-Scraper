Rating: 5/5

I've had this book for years - hidden away on a shelf, and finally read it end to end. Sorry I waited so long. A "greatest hits" of Japanese literature from about the 8th to the 18th century. Short selections - a good nightstand reader. Kudos to the editor for the translations. Amazing to look across time and an ocean and a completely different culture to find thoughtful people feeling and thinking the same things that we feel today. I was impressed with how much emotion can be packed into a 17 syllable haiku. Loved "An Account of My Hut" - reminiscent of Thoreau in his cabin at Walden, yet 600 years earlier. A new poetic concept - chained poetry. In 1488 the three greatest poets of the age gathered together at a retreat. One would write the traditional haiku, the next would write a two line "chain" and the 3rd would write a haiku that uses the chain as an introduction and then takes the thought in a different direction. So the first two verses can stand on their own, and the last two verses can stand on their own as a separate poem. At that gathering they composed 100 verses like this - 50 included in the anthology. I hope to travel to Japan and so this was my introduction to some of their culture. The same editor has done a second volume - 18th-20th century. Ordered from Amazon and on its way!
