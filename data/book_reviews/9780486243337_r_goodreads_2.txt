at a hotel, you take your key, go up to your room and settle the bill in the morning before leaving. i thought that was how it would be for me too.  then the door closed behind me and i realized the room had seen me before.  

as i walk along the long corridor, the silence of another hotel mingles with the sound of my footsteps. 

i know that moment when mike enslin stands outside room 1408: inside, there is a bed, a wardrobe and a perfectly prepared, perfectly ordinary night.  and yet i also know that once the door opens, the air will grow heavy, time will fold in on itself and the room will behave as though it knew its guest long before he arrived.  

the same thing happens in this old hotel in the middle of venice. as the brass key turns in the lock with that high, sharp sound, for a moment i can no longer tell which door i'm standing in front of.  it opens and this hotel room and room 1408 overlap within the same image.  

i step inside. they've covered the walls with new wallpaper and tried to bury the dampness of venice beneath the smell of fresh paint. but the room doesn't believe in its own transformation.  a heavy, rotten smell seeps from behind the walls, from somewhere just above the ceiling; the crime committed here is still alive beneath every new layer of polish.  

the bed has been made, the lamp turned on, everything i might need for the night carefully provided. and yet, after only a few seconds, i sense that none of these objects are here to serve me. the lamplight doesn't illuminate me, the whiteness of the bed promises concealment rather than cleanliness and even the bedside table seems to be standing silent guard between me and the door. 

 1408 had used mike’s pain against him. this room seems determined not to let me go without first smearing me with the crime rotting inside it.  

i turn towards the door, but i cannot make out the number.  for a moment, the brass digits read 1408; then they fade and dissolve into the nameless door of this room in venice. behind me, the lamplight flickers; the white bed begins to yellow and the dampness rising from the walls mingles with air that is suddenly hot and dry. one room pulls me into my own past, while the other brings its past crashing down upon me.  

i no longer know which door i need to open to get out.  mike couldn't escape his own mind; here, the hotel will not let me escape the crime it has hidden inside itself.  

as i follow the rotten smell drifting through the room, i move closer to lord montbarry’s death.  the walls seem to have held his final breath inside them for years, slowly releasing it from beneath the new wallpaper, through the cracks in the ceiling and between the polished pieces of furniture.  

ferrari disappears into that same silence.  one moment he is there and the next he is gone. it is as though he turned down one of the corridors and the hotel closed the door behind him.  as the emptiness he leaves behind grows, i begin to believe that what the building is hiding cannot be reduced to a single death. 

and the emptiness left by ferrari leads me all the way to the pages lying before countess narona.  in the very first line, the lord, the baron, the courier, the doctor and the countess stand side by side, as though they've stepped out of the corridor i've just crossed and taken their places inside the play. narona hasn't even given them different names. she has merely pushed the death behind the curtain and the disappearance into a scene.  

as i turn the pages, i think i'm moving forward through the play, only to realize that the same night has begun again exactly where i left it.  the bed is still made, the lamp is still burning and the key is still in my pocket.

kapıya dönüyorum ama üzerindeki numarayı seçemiyorum.  pirinç rakamlar bir an 1408’i gösteriyor, bir an sonra silinip venedik’teki bu odanın isimsiz kapısına karışıyor. arkamda lambanın ışığı titriyor; yatağın beyazlığı sararıyor, duvarlardan yükselen rutubet sıcak ve kuru bir havaya karışıyor. odalardan biri beni geçmişime çekiyor, diğeri kendi geçmişini üzerime yıkıyor.
