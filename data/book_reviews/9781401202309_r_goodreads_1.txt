Rating: 4/5

The edition I have read here is the 4" x 7" mass-market paperback from June of 1972 published by Paperback Library that includes two issues of Green Lantern (#78 July 1970 and #79 September '70) reproduced in black and white. There are several different books with almost-identical titles that's led to some bibliographical confusion. These are from the run during which GL&amp;GA teamed up to fight for social justice and civil relevance instead of just against supervillains. The cover price had jumped from 12 cents to 15 cents around #70, and the title wasn't doing well, and they introduced this radical change to try to save it. It was a noble and brilliant experiment, though it eventually failed. This reprint even has a slug-line above the logo on the title page that reads: "Comix that give a damn," and the CCA seal isn't on the cover. (Though the cover does promise "Two complete novels," which is stretching it a little.) The two stories are written by Denny O'Neil, and drawn by Neal Adams, assisted on the first by Frank Giacoia and the second by Dan Adkins. In the first, Black Canary is attacked by a motorcycle gang and comes under the influence of a Native American shaman, and the second further explores the mistreatment of the Native Americans in a story that offers a little hope though the odds seem overwhelming. The format is interesting, as only a panel or two are reproduced on each page, though the lack of color leaves it looking a bit barren despite the enlarged image. It's a quick, fun read, quite striking in the context of its era, though it all seems terribly naive here six decades on.
