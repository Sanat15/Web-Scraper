Rating: 4/5

This is not a blend of genres that I would normally apply to a single book, but this volume is a mashup of T.S. Eliot and Raymond Chandler, a thing you don't see every day, and with good cause.
While it's a treat on many levels, it's also as wild and disconnected as either of the two, only multiplied by a plethora of cameos. With Mary Astor as a dangerous dame and Sydney Greenstreet looking for the Grail, the story is as complex as a killing someone with a tuna. You can do it, but it's not the best way, and nobody will be happy with the results.
The notes at the end of the book are worth the effort. I do wish that the creator had proofread his lettering a little better, as a gag loses its some of its punch if the word is misspelled.
