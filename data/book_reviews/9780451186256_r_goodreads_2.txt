Rating: 2/5

As much as I enjoyed the first book in the series, this one was disappointing. I loved Mary the heroine, although she was described over and over again as being so tiny, I started picturing her as a Little Person, or whatever the current PC term is. Not that there is anything wrong with that, but since her love object was Black Jack, a huge, tall strapping guy, I was concerned that when they eventually got together, she'd end up dying in childbirth trying to bear his giant offspring.

Anyway, the fact that my mind wandered off in this direction was an indication that I found the plot less than exciting, and the oncoming Big Misunderstanding, which the author splendidly avoided in the first book, was telegraphed almost from the first page and proceeded to annoy me as these things always do.

Finally, the worst part about A Change of Heart is that Jack is portrayed as such a whoremonger, that if Mary didn't die in childbirth, she would surely have succumbed to one of the many STDs coursing around in Jack's polluted bloodstream. I really don't understand why this aspect of the character was emphasized SO much, it made it very hard to root for this couple. Mary deserved better.

I need to go back and update my review of the first novel to include the "Threw back his/her head and laughed" tally. The author is completely besotted with this phrase. In real life, how often does anyone actually throw back their head and laugh? Well, in Candice Hern's regency world, they do it every five minutes. And these are very short books!
