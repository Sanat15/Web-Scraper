Rating: 4/5

In my quest to read James by Percival Everett, I decided to read The Adventures of Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn first because I'd never read them.

Tom Sawyer hasn't much of a plot, but it introduces us to Huck Finn as the homeless vagabond of a boy who inhabits the alleys and woods of Tom Sawyer's anytown USA and is one of Tom's besties. Tom's the star, but his story is somewhat boring.

What makes Tom Sawyer interesting is Twain's witty, lively, and at times lyrical descriptions of the people, the places, and the times: How they lived and spoke and treated each other and carried on. They're a simple lot, prone to extreme superstition, hero worship, knee-jerk religiosity, and narrow mindedness. Twain grew up among them, in Tom's very town and in that very time, so we trust that he's got the inside scoop.

Tom Sawyer, we can see, is destined to become a good citizen, marry Becky, and graduate into the staid adulthood that awaits him in his hometown. Huck Finn, not so much.
