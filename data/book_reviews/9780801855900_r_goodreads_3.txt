Rating: 3/5

Tournier takes his reader along in the meanders of his thoughts with this novel. I admit, personally, getting lost in all the contradictions raised, in all the inversions, whether explicit or not, and in all the signs and other symbols that I have no doubt not always been able to decipher, especially those that ended up annoying me. The novel's beginning is inviting, drawing the reader into its ambiguous hero, who holds both the pedophile ogre and the universal mother. However, my interest waned throughout the reading, when I had the impression of receiving lessons, sometimes in history and sometimes in philosophy, as Tournier praised the body of boys, when everything under his pen had been theorized and thus justified. For me, the novel lacks unity, and if I recognize the writing qualities of its author, I would say that he sins by an excess of erudition that he never ceases to display. Therefore, I am far from the rave reviews usually given to this work and only give a relatively average rating of three stars.
