Rating: 4/5

Oh John Harrison, I have become a fan, I have to admit. At first I wasn’t sure, I enjoyed Cloud Road but felt you were leading me through to something. Then came A journey to the land of time, and you did something I had dreamed of doing and writing a book about, and now Where The Earth Ends, heading down to a continent that has fascinated you for some time. This book was the first of the series, Elaine is still around, and that was part of it, I feel like I know you, and am now interested to hear when you are healthy and happy, and still gallivanting around.

Starting in the desolate Argentinian pampas, heading down to Antartica and then back up Chile, Harrison chronicles the history of this cold, wild region with obvious passion, alongside his own trip as he tracks down people and places that have important significance or peak his interest.

There is a simple easy charm to Harrison’s writing that is uniquely his. It’s not probing and grumbling like the old hand Theroux, or quite as irreverent as John Gimlette, but almost like your favourite uncle who has an interest in history and thinks he’ll have a go at it, Harrison jumps in. It’s not really like that, Harrison is a member of the Royal Geographical Society and an experienced traveller, especially around the polar regions. But there are moments where you share his joy and wonder at the little things, and others where you laugh at the humour and share the compassion, particularly as this is a region of the world that I have a passion for myself.

So now I just have Forgotten Footprints to get hold of and read until something else comes out, and I hope it’s soon, I would like to know how Harrison is getting on.
