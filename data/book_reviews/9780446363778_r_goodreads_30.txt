Rating: 3/5

This bio tells the story of Jim Morrison. This is not an interpretive bio, it is a reporting of his life from childhood to the end.

While the authors relied on interviews, these are not documented in footnotes. There is a lot of dialog, most likely from the memory of those interviewed. There are a lot of B &amp; W photos. 

The best parts for me were his childhood and the summary of the speculation on his last days.

The characteristic that stands out is his cruelty (particularly) to women, to his family which seemed to give him a decent childhood, and to his band.

This story poses a lot of speculation (aside from the mystery of Morrison's death): If what we know today about addiction and depression were known then, would it have made a difference? If the times were not so sexist, would these women have put up with him? Would he have been a star with today’s entertainment system that is not likely to put up with problem crowds and no show performers?

The book was OK, a distraction from this week’s tawdry news. It’s on most lists of the best Rock &amp; Roll biographies so I expected more.
