Rating: 5/5

MY VERDICT:
A historical mystery set in 7th century China. Deeply researched, brilliantly plotted, and utterly unlike anything else. As good as the genre gets.

I have recently read The Chinese Maze Murders as a buddyread with Mallika.
You can check all our posts, where we shared our notes (full of spoilers of course).
But I had not yet put together my concluding thoughts until today.
It’s a fascinating and unique historical mystery.

I came to Robert van Gulik in 2024 through his translation of the 18th century Chinese mystery Di Gong An in Celebrated Cases of Judge Dee, and The Chinese Maze Murders is the first book in his own Judge Dee series.

He launched it after being asked, following the translation’s success, to find similar Chinese stories that would appeal to both modern Chinese and Western readers — an impossible task he solved by simply writing his own.

Van Gulik was the ideal person for it: a sinologist with a Ph.D., an accomplished calligrapher, a speaker of Mandarin and Japanese, and a man married to a Chinese woman. His credentials show on every page.
