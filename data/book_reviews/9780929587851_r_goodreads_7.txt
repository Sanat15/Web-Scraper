Rating: 3/5

This was a doctoral thesis published in 1971. The comparisons to the "modern" era, then, end in the late 60's. 

All readers will recognize at least some film titles, like King Kong, The Public Enemy, Animal Crackers, Duck Soup, and Mr. Smith Goes to Washington. However, as expected with a doctoral thesis, it goes into some films that have been lost to the public memory but are still important and groundbreaking (and probably sitting on the shelves of Cinephiles). Titles like Fury, The G men, I Am A Fugitive From A Chain Gang (which I discovered was the inspiration for Cool Hand Luke, one of my favorite movies), Gabriel Over The White House and others. 

The book is divided roughly into genres or areas of interest. Gangster films...G-Men films...the role of the woman...treacherous government/benevolent government...shyster good guys/bad guys...and others.

There's not much to "review" as far as content, because it is a thesis, not a narrative. If you have an interest in American films from 1929-1939, and how movies did or did *not* reflect America at the time (a "not" would be watching a grand musical/synchronized swimming showpiece, the kind parodied by everyone from the Muppets to Blazing Saddles, while jobless with everyone you know jobless), then you should grab a copy of the book, if for no other reason than to put a playlist together. 

I myself scanned the filmography (interesting bit of trivia, "filmography" must not have been acceptable or even in use in 1971, because the films are still listed under "Bibliography" at the end of the book, then subheading "FILMS") and will watch what I can find.
