Before I started reading books, this is honestly exactly how I imagined horror novels. Ghosts, mysterious wealthy people, bags of bones, eerie lakes, lonely houses, writers with personal tragedies, and that feeling that something is quietly standing in the corner of the story, waiting for things to get worse.

And the most interesting part is that Stephen King seems completely aware of that.

At times, it even feels like he is gently making fun of himself. The main character is also a writer, and sometimes he comments on what is happening in a way that feels almost like a wink to the reader. As if King is saying, “Yes, I know what this looks like, and I know I’ve done this before.” And that self-awareness makes the novel more enjoyable, because the book never takes itself too seriously, even when the themes are heavy.

The atmosphere is probably its strongest part. The small lake, the house, the silence, the grief—everything is written in a way that makes the tension come more from the setting than from the supernatural elements themselves. This was never simply a ghost story. It feels more like a story about loss, loneliness, and the way the past sometimes refuses to leave you alone.

The problem for me was that despite all of that, the book never truly hit me. It was enjoyable to read. There were moments that felt powerful. There was also that familiar sense that King can turn the most ordinary place into something deeply unsettling. But when I closed the final page, it did not leave me with that feeling some of his books do.

It is one of those books that works, but does not stay with you for very long.

It is not weak. It is simply one of those novels that works while you are reading it. You sink into it, move through the story, enjoy the atmosphere. But after some time, you begin to forget the details, and all that remains is the general feeling that you read something good, but not unforgettable.

Maybe that is exactly why Bag of Bones felt like a very typical Stephen King novel to me, with both his strengths and his familiar weaknesses. Well written, atmospheric, slightly self-aware, but not one of those books that will stay in my mind long after the ending.
