4.5/5

Going through the book in one sitting took me through a wide gamut of thoughts and feelings. The confluence of time and subject make this an extremely interesting and insightful documented journey that will never be again. Araki was bold in tackling such a polarizing subject and succinct in his execution. I'm glad art like this is still being distributed in the world.

Details

This book leaves the art and interpretation up to you as the only writing you will get are the first few pages at the beginning of the book. These 2 sections, non written by the artist, set the historical tone and grant a reflection about the nature of the photographer interfering in photographs by just being there to take the photograph. This last point being especially important for the context of this book.

One of the things that stood out the most to me about one of the writings was that the women involved in the sex industry at the time were not seen, and did not see themselves, as victims of poverty or exploitation, but just as regular workers in a popular industry. I was expecting some of the pictures to feel very vulgar or shocking but actually found them, for the most part, quaint and natural; which on later reflection made less skeptic about what I pointed out from the writing.

I could go on about my experience with the book, but you will probably have a different take and feeling. I will just say that you can get through it in about 2-3 hours of which you will be transported to a Japan that will never exist again, which much less foreigner oriented places, and an industry that has since ceased to exist in that fashion.
