Rating: 3/5

In the first couple of chapters, this murder-mystery seemed simple-minded. I thought this book was merely a vehicle for Mailer to show-off his similes ("as if" occurs 109 times). That was a misjudgment. The plot turned out to be convoluted. Maybe a better word is messy. Mailer needed to cheat to make sense of the mess: coincidences, convenient suicides, far-fetched alcohol-induced blackout activities, and in the end the bad guy confesses all the details to you while holding you at gunpoint. To top it off, the killer's motive turned out to be an undersupply of  analingus .
