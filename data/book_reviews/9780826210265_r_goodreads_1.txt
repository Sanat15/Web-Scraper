Rating: 3/5

A LOVE AFFAIR WITH LIFE &amp; SMITHSONIAN is really an autobiography – in the narrow sense of the word. That’s not all bad as Edward K. Thompson’s life story is worth reading; but at times it’s in danger of becoming just a tale of office politics. There is, nevertheless, enough meat to make reading worthwhile, particularly the tales of Truman, Churchill, and MacArthur; and, of course, Henry Luce, who had a sense of humor. Who knew? Otherwise EKT is reserved in judgment; he rarely names names for any potentially juicer tales.

Bottom line: it’s a good read by a key personality in the history of publishing and journalism in the U. S.; but it’s also a lost opportunity for what could have undoubtedly been a much richer account if EKT had related his incredibly unique experience to the larger context of the people and times in which he lived.
