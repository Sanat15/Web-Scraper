Rating: 4/5

"...the crowd suddenly felt like a vast, hostile creature that could trap you, nudge you into a corner and drain the life from you."

Joel Lane's novel begins with the story of Neil, a student at Birmingham University, his life evolves around politics, gigs, sex and clubs. After an argument with his lover, Neil ends up at a bar and meets a stranger. He follows him down a canal to where he is attacked viciously disfiguring his face. His recover takes him through a journey where he is dealing with the trauma of what happened to him, search for his attacker, isolation and coming to terms with his life as it is now.

I've now read a couple of Joel Lane's books and what stand out for me is the way in which he wrote about the city. In the novels and short stories, the city becomes a character in itself. It's impact on the characters is vast and in this novel a focal part as to why Neil has so many demons he is trying to face.

The story itself is raw and quite unnerving. Often the narrative slips into the the past which gives the present a darker atmosphere making certain scenes more impactful.

Although this story at times was heartbreaking when seeing how the characters navigated their lives around their experiences, I did enjoy the writing in this novel which isn't surprising. I have more books to read by this author and can't wait to get to those.
