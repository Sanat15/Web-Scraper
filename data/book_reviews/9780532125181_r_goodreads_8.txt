Rating: 5/5

The Voyage of the Space Beagle is a fix-up novel of four stories originally published 1939 - 1950. The novel version has some characters and linking material added but stays pretty true to the original publications. Black Destroyer and Discord in Scarlet were published in John W. Campbell's Astounding magazine in 1939, M33 in Andromeda appeared there in 1943, and War of Nerves first saw print in Raymond A. Palmer's Other Worlds magazine in 1950, the same year that Simon &amp; Schuster released the novel version in hardcover with a Lew Glansman cover. The first story, Black Destroyer, has been often cited as the single story that most likely marks the beginning of science fiction's "Golden Age," and also was the inspiration for the film Alien in 1979. I thought the Space Beagle was the single most likely inspiration for Star Trek, including Nexialism and Vulcan philosophy. (Though the Space Beagle did have an all-male crew of a thousand that were chemically controlled... it was written in the 1930s after all!) Van Vogt was a prolific writer and has many well-known titles to his credit (Slan, The Weapon Shops, Rull, The Mixed Men, etc.), but The Voyage of the Space Beagle remains my favorite by far.
