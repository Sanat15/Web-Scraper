Rating: 2/5

Treading on with my Shivers journey, I picked up its HorrorLand knockoff: “Lost in Dreamland.” Whilst heavily flawed, I enjoyed my time with it. There’s a nice theme of sibling friendship expressed that you don’t get often from kids horror, which was nice to see. The actual happenings at Dreamland are pretty cool, even if it’s not reinventing the wheel with its ball-drops (aka reveals). The ending is quite nice too. And uh… yeah, not a lot to say positive past that. Post-page fifty, the pacing slows dramatically and it’s very jarring; the padding there and all-round is very noticeable. The book feels like it’s having an identity crisis, whether it’s trying to be a Sci-Fi plot, mob/tough guy/action plot, or a kids horror book without logic, and it’s jarring, again. The writing isn’t the greatest (with all due respect Mr. Spenser) and the resolution felt far to quick. It’s like the story did what it wanted (good), and decided to self-destruct/get-the-fuck-outta-there ASAP (not good; the climax is anti-climatic AF). And all-in-all, there’s a lot of leaps in logic and whilst it might make sense for a kids horror book to have that, let alone fantasy (duh), I’m talking just hard to believe shit like Dr. Ferret being a dense and deaf piece of wtfness and that this all went on for that long, which is apparently four years. I can only suspend my belief so far. However, with all of these nitpicks and criticisms out of the way: this book was still pretty fun. Overall, 5.5/10, rounded to three whole stars. Surprised how little this read like HorrorLand.
