Rating: 4/5

Wholly entertaining, heartfelt and prescient - I loved this! Follows on from the previous book in a slick, efficient way and Titmuss’s metamorphosis into even more of a manipulator feels logical … and scary. Every character feels fully realized and there’s a lot to like about them, particularly Fred (my beloved) and Jenny - two honest people in a swamp of greed. 

Where in the first book his power-hungry grasping sprang from pure spite, here Titmuss is the archetypal 21st-Century Tory, so blinded with ambition and his own mythology that he (literally!) bulldozes everything in his path to power. However, he’s not a caricature; Mortimer portrays his insecurities with care and surprising empathy (perhaps more than he deserves), giving the most accurate portrayal of narcissism I’ve seen (in fiction) for a while. Absolute flashbacks to my last ex though …
My only complaint is how abrupt the ending felt; it’s too sudden and too clean, considering how dirty Titmuss gets. However, I’ll chalk this up to Mortimer’s faith in human nature. For, surprisingly , this isn’t a cynical novel; it’s warm with empathy and hope throughout.
