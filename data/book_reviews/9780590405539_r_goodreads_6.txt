Rating: 5/5

Grade/interest level: Primary (K-3)	
Reading level: 2.7 (Lexile 550L) 	
Genre: Multicultural, Modern Fantasy

Main Characters: Jacob, Peewee, the Black Snowman
Setting: Inner-city
POV: Jacob 

Jacob Miller is a poor, unhappy boy who lives in a small apartment with his mother and little brother, Peewee. Jacob has no pride in his African heritage, and equates being black with being second-class. He comes up with a mantra, “Everything black is bad,” based on his surroundings, from his home, to his peers, to the white snow that turns into black slush during the wintertime. Peewee, however, wants his brother to think differently, and convinces him to help build a snowman, even if it is made of black slush. To complete their snowman, they wrap an old cloth around it, and to their surprise, the snowman comes to life. It turns out the cloth was a magical African kente containing the spirit of a storyteller, who proceeds to tell Jacob and Peewee of their noble history, including Timbuktu scholars, Bornu horsemen, and the Ashanti tribe.

I would use this book to address issues revolving around self-esteem. Even though life may seem unfair at times, everyone can find positive elements from which to create positive reinforcement. Also, it is through remembering ones history that strengths and self-worth are found.
