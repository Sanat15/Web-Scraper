Rating: 3/5

In the 1990s, a college history professor recounts his mid-1970s married and family life and adultery, interspersed with his working draft of those years of his life work—a biography of James Buchanan (all this in reply to a request from his regional historians association for members’ “memories of the Ford Administration.”) Entertaining at times but slow at others, with a bit of a forced and uneven feeling from the odd structure perhaps. Sometimes very good Updike, when I have always liked him a lot, in the recreated places and times, detailed sensory descriptions, striking observations and pointed asides, and strong characters (the professor one of his least likable I can recall). The Buchanan sections are interesting on his early life and affairs and the long political run-up to the Civil War but at times too erudite in a showing-off kind of way.
