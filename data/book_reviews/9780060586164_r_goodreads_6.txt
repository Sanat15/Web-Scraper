Rating: 3/5

My first encounter with Elmore Leonard was through the small screen; in Timothy Olyphant's exceptional portrayal of Raylan Givens, lawman, from the acclaimed FX series, Justified. This was great television and equipped with one of the most riveting bromances of all time, that between Raylan and his frenemy, backwater criminal Boyd Crowder. The relationship started here, in a collection of short stories, under the heading Fire in the Hole. The tale is largely identical to the first few episodes of the series, so there's very little new, but with this and the other seven shorts in the assortment we do get a fine taste of Elmore Leonard.

The initial tales in the line-up proved to be the best, successfully launching us into worlds filled with flawed characters, dicey situations, sardonic commentary, and a surprising amount of worldly wisdom. You can practically trace the roots to Chandler, pinpoint the intersections with McMurtry and, perhaps, Grey, as well as chart the many direct influences of Elmore Leonard in the work of Michael Connelly and Robert Crais. He's certainly part of the pantheon, which many of my crime-reading friends have been insisting and, hence, nudging me toward.

So here I am. This was good. I'm suspecting a novel would be better.
