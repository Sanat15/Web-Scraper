Rating: 5/5

I finally got around reading my copy of this wonderful Special Edition! And I found it incredibly enlightening and helpful to highlight the core differences between the play and the musical adaptation. Learning the backstory made the experience almost hilarious for me: Shaw was messy for having planned the entire play around his previous "partner" as the main role. 

The play centers on a woman whose identity is being controlled by an eccentric, intellectual man (one who displays traits we might today identify as neurodivergent and perhaps even queer-coded.) It really makes me wonder if this was the author’s way of putting his own worst traits under a microscope,—given that the story was loosely based on his real-life obsession with his leading lady.

​Furthermore, after reading Pygmalion, I agree that the musical stripped the leading roles of their individuality. I find it hard to believe they edited out scenes that are absolutely necessary for understanding the plot. Personally, I feel the romantic route taken in the musical could have been far more interesting if they had kept these original interactions. While I understand the need to make time for musical numbers, they shouldn't have devalued the character dynamics. Their "friction" (which the musical passes off as romantic or sexual tension) would have been much more powerful if the original, sharp dialogue had remained intact.

​​That said, comparing the text to the film (as I can't reference the 1956 Broadway act, as much as I love the cast album and would cut my left tit to see Julie Andrews perform the role of Eliza), I have to admit that Rex Harrison did a goddamned wonderful job of bringing Professor Higgins to life. I could almost hear his voice while reading; I even found myself attempting every kind of "British" accent imaginable as I went along.
