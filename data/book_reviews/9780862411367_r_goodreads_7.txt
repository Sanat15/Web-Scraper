Rating: 5/5

I haven't enjoyed a book this much in a while! I really loved Quest for a Maid when I was younger, and was excited to find more Hendry. 

This is the sort of children's fiction I love. Excellent atmospheric historical fiction, with quality writing, fun and humor, but also never shies away from showing some really dark elements of life. There are a lot of things I think most children's books nowadays would avoid (e.g. period-accurate attitudes/slurs, violence/blood, foul language, etc.) but I never felt that the book was necessarily condoning these things, just showing things like they were. I appreciate this sort of storytelling - restricting children's literature to being happy clappy rainbow stories where everyone has modern sensibilities (not that I'm against stories like this existing) gives children a very narrow worldview.

The characters are all very vivid and human, and the people you're supposed to like are likable. Even the villains, while they could be just bad people (like Duggie), are shades of real people rather than caricatures. Duggie and Minister Rose were great examples of characters that were despicable, without any justification or redemption, but never felt like they were just straw men. For example, Duggie is just an angry person, who takes out his resentment on people weaker than him, and you get the sense that he just can't admit that any of his problems are his own fault. Even if he's never particularly sympathetic as a character, he evoked other resentful people I've (and I would imagine most of us have) known.

Overall, I really appreciate Hendry as an author and really loved this book.
