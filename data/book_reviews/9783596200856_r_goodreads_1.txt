Rating: 4/5

3.75 stars

This story, sometimes titled A Man and His Dog, is about the affection between a dog and a man. The dog, named Bashan, displays tenacious loyalty to the man who adopted him, saving him from an unenviable fate. It is easy to recognize Thomas Mann in the narrator who recounts the development of his relationship with the dog. 

This story left me with mixed feelings, although my overall impression was positive. I wish the narration had been slightly more dynamic. I hesitated between three and four stars, but something moving and deeply humane about this story made me round up my rating. Its appreciation of nature and its praise of the simple joys of life, such as walking with your dog, inhaling fresh air, and beholding picturesque landscapes, are finely rendered and feel contagious. It made me smile more than once.  

Mann painted a vivid portrait of genuine friendship between animal and human. Like most relationships, theirs is not perfect. It is joyful, it is touching, and it is somewhat problematic at times. Mann can be self-absorbed, ignoring his dog and not always valuing Bashan's faithfulness, but he does care about his dog. Bashan can feel abandoned and depressed. But the dog's appetite for life is too strong to succumb to melancholy. His enthusiasm leaps off the page. Like the narrator, you soon grow fond of Bashan. The writing is lyrical and observant.
I should mention that the story has fleeting moments of animal cruelty. To be fair, the narrator did not view them as such when writing it circa 1918.
