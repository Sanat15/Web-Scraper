Rating: 3/5

This was a pretty good story, but the writing was simplistic -- it made the characters flat &amp; the dialogue wooden. While there was a distinct lack of adverb flogging (always a good thing), everyone was constantly addressing each other by name -- "Where have you been, Jennifer?" "On the terrace, Michael." "Jennifer, I told you never to go there alone." -- as if the dearth of adverbial tags would somehow confuse the reader. Maybe the author was trying to pad her word count? Who knows. 

That being said, it was entertaining enough for a quickie read. Jennifer's origin (rescued from death by abusive miners, then running off with gypsies before finding her way back to the family estate) is similar to Sarah's upbringing in Meridon, but her extreme childhood poverty is unusual in gothics -- there's no governess/companion/poor relation trope in this book. Also interesting is that Jennifer enjoys being admired by men, particularly as she does her gypsy dancing. She's a virgin heroine, but she's very aware of her reaction to Michael; though she's firm about wanting sex with marriage, she doesn't resent having hormones.

So...it was mediocre, but mediocre in that entertaining pulpy way. I've already got two more books by this author, so I'll read them both. I just hope they're less wooden in the dialogue &amp; more expansive in the characterization.
