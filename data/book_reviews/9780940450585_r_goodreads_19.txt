Rating: 5/5

Hemingway said that all modern American literature began with Mark Twain's "Huckleberry Finn." I suppose he hadn't read Grant's "Memoirs." Here is the clean, clear prose of a well-directed intelligence. The intelligence that won the Civil War. And the story of how he did it. Eyewitnesses say that Grant in his field tent would begin to write orders for battle, penning page after page, allowing each to fall to the tent floor until he was finished. Then he'd gather up the sheets and, without second thoughts or revisions, hand them to his aides to carry to his subordinate generals. He knew what he wanted to do and he knew how to say it. And the generals who read his orders understood what they must do. Read this book and you will understand Grant, and why it was Grant and no other general who could end the Civil War.
