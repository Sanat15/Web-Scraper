Rating: 4/5

The best thing about this book is that it made me watch the Hammerklavier performed on YouTube, which was revelatory and recommendation enough for this book!
A sort of In Search of Lost Time if Marcel Proust had been incredibly more succinct and worked in vignettes only concerned with music and his father. I suspect that these vignettes, a joy to read that they are, are actually more profound and serious than I can credit them for. Sometimes the book is greater than the reader. Or does a profound work by its very nature carry with it a sense of the unknowable? Does any work that grapples with memory and time offer anything other than plangency?
We exist and we remember existing and we are aware of things in the world. I know that this book exists, where it is on my shelf and that I will revisit it many times in the future.
