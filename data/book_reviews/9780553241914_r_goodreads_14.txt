Rating: 4/5

This is a collection of three novellas from 1961-63. They share a couple of common of themes. One is that all of the victims meet their end by collisions with blunt instruments.

The other is that the sexual revolution that accelerated dramatically with the approval of the birth control pill in 1960, as noted in the Nero Wolfe mystery of that year, Too Many Clients, continues to have unintended consequences, sometimes deadly ones. Some women of social standing are becoming a lot more promiscuous. Some guys, like ladies-man Archie, are taking this well and enjoying the benefits. Others are definitely not.

“Kill Now – Pay Later” involves a beautiful secretary who may or may not have been involved with one or more executives at her company. It is very well plotted, 4 stars.

“Murder is Corny” involves a beautiful farm girl turned Manhattan supermodel. Since moving to the big city she has had at least 30 suitors and a dozen marriage proposals. This is also very well plotted. Archie is framed for a murder and the entire Wolfe household comes very close to being blown up by a parcel bomb delivered to them. They are saved only when Wolfe senses the danger and cuts short his daily time with the orchids. 4 stars.

“Blood Well Tell” involves a beautiful architect’s wife who is playing the field without a care for the hearts she is breaking. This one also attempts an intricate plot, but I don’t think it pulls it off quite as well as the other two. 3.5 stars.

I noticed only a few new observations on daily life and events in this period. Wolfe is reading Shirer’s excellent The Rise and Fall of the Third Reich in one story. Closed-circuit television surveillance is mentioned as an available, though expensive, consumer product for the first time. I expected some reference to the Berlin Wall (1961), the Bay of Pigs (1961), the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962), Vietnam, President Kennedy, and/or his assassination by this time, but there has been none yet.
