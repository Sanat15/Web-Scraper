Rating: 3/5

DNF. 

I got this book because I'm trying to find every extant written experience of someone who lived through the broad range of experiences of African slavery in the West--living free in Africa, getting kidnapped, getting sold across the sea, etc. 

Primary sources which include all of those experiences are rare. This is only the 4th book of that kind I have found. The others are: Baracoon by Zora Neale Hurston, in which she interviewed Kossula (Cudjo Lewis); The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano by Olaudah Equiano; and the Biography of Mahommah G. Baquaqua by himself and Samuel Moore. So I was very excited to find this book. And it does, indeed, contain an account of Ottobah Cugoano's enslavement and transport, etc. But that account is VERY short and doesn't have a lot of details. It's not nearly as rich in that sense as the other books. 

Of course, that's not surprising. The title gives away that Cugoano's purpose in writing was not to share his life story, but to persuade people that slavery was evil, and get it outlawed. That is a high and lofty, laudable goal. But it makes this book not particularly interesting to my purpose. Of course, seeing the actual arguments that were being used at that time to take slavery down--arguments that actually succeeded in taking slavery down--is fascinating. If I were studying that particular aspect of history, this would be a very valuable book. But, as it is, I stopped listening a little ways after Cugoano told his life story. 

I listened to the audiobook version read by Chukwudi Iwuji. He did a wonderful job.
