Rating: 3/5

This is a novel based on what might have happened when Lord Byron and the Shelley party met up in Switzerland in 1816. In that sense it’s an historical novel, but more of a character study than a novel. If you’re not into several pages of reflection of some small incident (repeatedly) you may find yourself as bored as I was with this novel.

The fact that the author’s name is larger than the title indicates that the author was really the selling point for this novel. I found it speculative and somewhat tedious. John William Polidori (Lord Byron’s actual doctor that summer) is presented in character with some of his surviving diary, but also as very interested in sexual organs, which he studies closely many times, and is self-pitying to an annoying degree.

Historically Polidori did die by suicide not long after that summer. He penned “The Vampyre,” his most famous work, after the summer gathering. He was, his diary makes clear, jealous of Byron and also his attention lavished on Percy Shelley. There are some good parts to this novel, but as I note elsewhere (Sects and Violence in the Ancient World), it was pretty much a slog to get through.
