Rating: 4/5

This book is the first in a trilogy, called either the "Free Lance series", or "A Knight's Tale".

The story is entirely told in first person, from the perspective of the knight himself, Free Lance. He is an unbonded knight, a jousting specialist, a free lance.

Telling the entire story in first person just about works, the Barnaby Grimes books do it to better effect, but it was still a quick an enjoyable tale, full of reprehensible yokels and bloody skirmishes.

It really was quick, if you were reading it aloud, it would not take an hour - despite being 125 pages, very small pages. Part of the reason for this is that the book is chocked full of illustrations from Chris Riddell - the book benefits more from the illustrations than the storyline in my opinion, which I would not normally say for the Stewart/Riddell partnership, usually they share the glory. Almost every double page has some substantial illustration, all of which are excellent, as anyone who has read any of the Edge Chronicles will have come to expect.

So to summarise, quick, fairly amusing and very well illustrated. Not the very best the duo have to offer, but worth a short read if you can find a copy.
