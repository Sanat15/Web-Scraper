Rating: 3/5

For this fourth book of the Sackett Series, Jubal Sackett is next, set in 1620 concurrently with the prior book ("The Warrior's Path) in L'Amour's epic family saga. Jubal is heading west across the Appalachian Mountains as a pioneering adventurer driven to explore. 

Early in the story, Jubal meets up with a native named Keokatah and they travel together like Natty Bumpo and Chingachgook in Last of the Mohicans. His adventures take him on an exploration/searching mission and we're introduced to various Indian tribes and lands in their pre-European invasion form; a wonderful first half. Unfortunately, the second half falls into the common L'Amour trap where the protagonist's internal conflict between his innate wandering ways versus the pull of settling down is boringly dominant.

Verdict: This is the first of the Sackett series that has a true western feel to it. If we can separate the book into two halves, the first adventure half is very good, while the second half defending the valley is just okay and actually quite silly in two spoilery ways I can't share.
