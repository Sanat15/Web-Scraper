Rating: 3/5

Since undersea science fiction is rare, I bought this trilogy and hoped for the best. I read Undersea Fleet first, thinking I’d read the other two later. Now I’m not sure.

The book was published in 1956, and I read the version by Ballentine Books. The mid-1950s was a time when the science fiction writing world began turning from short stories to novel length books. Publishers couldn’t get them fast enough, and authors struggle to meet the demand. 

This book shows signs of hasty writing. I presume its ideal audience was young boys, from age 10 to 16 or so. Neither the characters nor the setting receives much description, leaving young imaginations to fill that in. Even the action is quite slow at first, though it picks up at the end. 

I’m a former submariner, and the descriptions of the subs and their workings leave a lot to be desired. Again, I assume the authors—both masters of science fiction writing—didn’t take the time to do much research about submarines. 

To describe a nuclear reactor as “chuckling away” (p. 115) or to worry that moisture and condensation will “rot out” a submarine (p. 144)—these and other errors detracted from my enjoyment of the story. 

Still, for a 1950s audience of young boys, there’s the futuristic setting, the high adventure and action, and enough plot twists and turns that this book probably met its audience’s need.

I’ll give it three stars, but it will be some time before I turn to reading the other books of the trilogy.
