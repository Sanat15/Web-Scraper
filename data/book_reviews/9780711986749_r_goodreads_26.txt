Rating: 1/5

This book appears to be written on post it notes while the author was riding the bus. There was no editor involved in any part of this book's creation, probably because he didn't want to decrease his profit margin. There is nothing new he shares and some absurd statements are made such as portraying Winnie as this jolly man, 'chunky and hunky", who was just who Freddie needed. Are you kidding? The man was physically and emotionally abusive! And when Freddie passed away, with great dignity in the face of immense pain and sorrow, the words after he describes the body leaving the home are "Freddie Mercury had left the building!" Did he honestly think an analogy to Elvis was funny? How pathetic that Freddie may have thought of this man as a friend. Peter, if you were such a great guy, why didn't you encourage him to attend rehab instead of boasting about how you could procure the best drugs? Sorry buddy, that's an enabler not a friend. And promiscuity is a sexual addiction not just something you do because your gay. Unfortunately, Freddie had far too many like you in his life, and it never worked in his favor. This was just another book written as fast as he could, as evidenced by minimal punctuation and grade three grammar, vying to be the first out the publishing gate to beat all the other 'friends' books about Freddies's life. God bless Mary Austin, the woman who showed him nothing but unconditional love.
