Rating: 3/5

The second book in The Cheetah Girls book series is somewhat of a step up from the previous book.

In this one, we follow Chanel as she is given control of her mother's credit card. She goes BERSERK with it and ends up ordering way more than she should, which gets her into a lot of trouble. Meanwhile, the Cheetah Girls are excited about a possible management deal from Jackal Johnson.

I do prefer this one over the first book, but only by a bit. I liked following Chanel, even though she isn't half as spicy as Adrienne Bailon's portrayal of the character. The first movie did a great job adapting the storyline from this book as I did want to see the relationship between Chanel and her mother. Her mother is less supportive of Chanel's dreams in the book, as she wants her to get more serious with her career. But I do like how both mother and daughter, who are both riddled with problems with one another, can come together in the end to sort out their differences.

I also enjoyed the first recording sessions with Jackal Johnson and how Dorothea (Galleria's mum) really comes out to play with the hyenas. I love her in the movie too, and I love her in the books. The problem is still with the painfully cheesy dialogue ("see you around like a doughnut" for example), as well as the fact that I don't think Galleria's songs are... good. There, I said it. She can't write songs. But, in the books, she's fantastic and whenever people listen to them perform her original songs, they love it. So maybe I just need to hear the songs with a melody? I don't know. 

Still smiling a lot when I read these books though - they might not be great, but they're certainly popcorn entertainment in book form.
