One of the first classic fantasy tales from the '40s. Reads like H G Wells in part but obviously very American. Explores popular myths - Norse, Medieval, Celtic with time travel element not a million miles from Quantum leap as the main prog jumps from one problematic reality to the next. Great to doff my cap out of respect to a contributor that grew the fantasy genre along with Howard, Leiber and Co. This big fat book collects most of the best stories, though I do prefer to seek out smaller single publications now, as they don't flop around so much in bed. Did I say that right?
