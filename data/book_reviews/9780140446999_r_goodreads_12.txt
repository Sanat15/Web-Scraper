Rating: 4/5

A good introduction to Descartes' philosophy. Overall I enjoyed it, although I found Descartes to be inconsistent. He outlines a method he alleges will allow people to discover capital t Truths, and then proceeds to not actually follow the method. For example, the first rule of the method is to never accept anything as true if you do not know clearly and distinctly that it is so beyond doubt. This involves questioning widely accepted beliefs and even things established as truth by academics. He then goes on to support his belief that God made nature by saying it's"a view that is widely accepted among theologians" and justifies other opinions in similar ways. It's kind of frustrating to read. Can bro really not see the hypocrisy in his argument? People should question authority but never the Church. 

Despite the crippling effect this refusal to seriously question Catholicism has on his argument, Descartes still has something to offer readers today. The Discourse is written in a clear and extremely straightforward way by philosophical standards. It was easier to get through than I expected and I am interested in reading the Meditations too, if only to become a better thinker myself by determining where his arguments fall through.
