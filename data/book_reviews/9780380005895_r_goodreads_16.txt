Rating: 2/5

A typical classic for me....meh. 

Once again I fear that I missed the deeper meaning, or maybe it meant something different to people in 1928, I don't know. Maybe there is no deeper meaning. Maybe it was just a mediocre story written by an overly praised author. There's a reason most people haven't heard of this Pulitzer prize winner from 1928, because it's meh. The writing was ok. The story was boring. The characters were unrelatable. I just was not into it at all. 

We trace the lives of five seemingly random individuals who happen to be crossing a rickety bridge at the exact moment of its collapse. The collapse is witnessed by a priest who is determined to pin some kind of scientific, spiritual, or cosmic meaning to the whole event. He spends the next six years trying to make heads or tails of it and compiling a massive volume of interviews with anyone who knew the victims. Well, it's all for not in the end as you'll see. 

"There is a land of the living and a land of the dead and the bridge is love, the only survival, the only meaning." 

Whatever that means.
