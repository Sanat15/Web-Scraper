Rating: 5/5

An amazing account of two Americans caught by the Communists after the takeover in Beijing. The writing takes the reader on the Ricketts' thought process through their initial arrest, denial, self-criticisms, and final agreement with the Communists. 

I thought that some of the views held by the Ricketts are relevant today, especially as the US continues to interfere with Chinese affairs globally. Of course the Chinese state is very different from in 1949, but some characteristics and worldviews persist.

*A note for Beloiters: the Ricketts were at Yanjing (Yenching) University, where Lucius Porter (who married the daughter of Beloit's first president) lived. Lucius was born in China, came to Beloit for college, went on to divinity school at Yale, and taught philosophy and religious studies at Yanjing. He was even interned by the Japanese when they took over in the late '30s. Lucius returned to the US after the Communists seized power and it became apparent he could not stay in China. He taught at Beloit and was a runner, even competing in hurdles into his late 70s.
