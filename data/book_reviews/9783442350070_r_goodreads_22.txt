Rating: 4/5

A beautiful romance full of love and family. 
While Ruth felt a little young to me, I had to remind myself that this was the 1930's and young ladies didn't grow up as fast as they do today. Society was different. And the ten year age gap between her and Quin, while not a problem, I did find myself ageing him down a little. But it was obvious from the start these two were soulmates, which makes some of the things they do very frustrating, particularly in the last act. Grrr, just talk to him.
Heini was horrible, so self absorbed and ugh.
But the starling metaphor in here was very clever and subtle; you'll see what I mean.
The plight of refugees and outcasts is once again a subject very close to hearts, and it's interesting seeing different view points and methods of helping. How would Ruth have managed if she had not had help and support doesn't care thinking about. And I certainly learnt a few things that my history has never taught me. A bit of an eye opener to be honest.
This is a romance novel in every sense of the word, with some social commentary, but that's not the focus. Hold those you love close and tell them you love them. Don't waste precious moments.
