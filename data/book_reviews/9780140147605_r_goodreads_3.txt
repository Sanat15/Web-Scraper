Rating: 4/5

This is the second book in Kee’s Irish Nationalist trilogy and it was the volume that I had anticipated the most. I had ancestors from counties Kerry and Cork who left Ireland in about 1866, right about the time of the Fenian rebellions in North America and Ireland. I was hoping for in depth detail of the reasons for the rebellion and the aftermath.
The book, like the first volume, is well researched and written with minimal bias. This is laudable because the Fenian rebellion became almost a comedy of errors in its planning and execution. It would be easy to scoff and site the episode as an example of the foolishness of the Irish or the lack of unity among the people but Kee resists that temptation admirably.
Did I get the depth I was hoping for? Well, not really. The Fenians are covered briefly and then the author moves on. Still, I recommend this volume as the most complete narrative I’ve encountered on the overall subject matter.
