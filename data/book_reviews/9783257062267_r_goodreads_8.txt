Rating: 3/5

As weird as when I read it the first time ages ago. The musings about time, space, science, consciousness and morality are pretty interesting, the character who performs them definitely less so. A rigidly moral person conflicting with a completely amoral one from a frame of friendship can be interesting, but Sasha is so incredibly stupid that the conflict doesn't really go anywhere. Lev's betrayals and manipulations are so transparent that one kind of feels Sasha deserves what keeps happening to him, except the happy ending.

As a historical document of a certain time, place and mindset the book is pretty successful, and although you can already see hints of the author's hard right turn coming up, especially in its treatment of women and non-white Jews and Arabs, it's pretty even-keeled overall.

The usual Dutch obsession of being as crude as possible about sexual matters, but that's all from the perspective of crude characters so it can be excused I guess.
