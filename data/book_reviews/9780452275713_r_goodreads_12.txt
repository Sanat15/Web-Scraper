Rating: 4/5

This is the first book I’ve read by E. L. Doctorow, and it was his first, written in 1960. He captures a change to the Zeitgeist, in that this novel is very much a move away from the traditional western. There were aspects that reminded me of the 1973 film “High Plains Drifter”, to the extent I wondered whether the makers of that film had drawn some of their inspiration from “Welcome to Hard Times”. It’s not a perfect comparison though, as the novel has a deeper strain of pessimism. 

So, Hard Times is a one-horse town in the Dakota Territory, and as the novel opens it is visited by a violent psychopath, “The Bad Man from Bodie”, who robs and rapes the inhabitants at will. Most of the townsfolk are too afraid to intervene and those that do are shot dead. Before leaving the killer sets fire to the town’s wooden buildings. Afterwards most of the survivors head off elsewhere, but a motley group stay behind to rebuild the town, led by “Blue”, the unofficial mayor. Blue is a well-meaning but flawed individual. He and his fellow townsfolk are quite successful in bringing the town back, but there is the ever-present thought that the terrifying Man from Bodie may one day return. The story builds to an exciting climax.

It’s very much an allegorical novel, but to avoid spoilers I won’t say more.

A very impressive first book. A big thank you to my GR Friend Howard, whose review first brought it to my attention.
