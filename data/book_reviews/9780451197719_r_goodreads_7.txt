Rating: 5/5

How absolutely delightful!

I frankly loved how they first met. No, not the unasked-for kiss, but when they formally met one another and recognised one another as the strangers they had once been.

Then Richard slowly realising how much he enjoyed teasing Caitlin, yet apologising so beautifully when he realised that he had overstepped his mark.

And Elizabeth - lawd what a shrew! She was the epitome of starchiness and propriety - how dare she criticise Serena even BEFORE she was married to Richard! I have to admit, I totally hated just how much Richard was utterly improper with Caitlin, even while he was still formally engaged to Elizabeth. That left a bad taste in my mouth, though the final scene with them together was brilliant! I can't believe how Diane Farr described Elizabeth's anger running away with her so effectively, that she cried off! HA!

And speaking of Serena and Edward? They are SO cute together - especially Edward's long unrequited love, all so wonderfully described. That was adorable!

Perhaps the weakest portion of the book was Egbert's part in the suspense side of the story. I wanted something a bit meatier as to how things could've progressed that far. I didn't buy into the resolution of that part of the story either.

Despite some of the weak points of the story, I still thoroughly enjoyed it. Diane Farr writes wonderfully well, and she lets her readers come to their own conclusions - I love that she respects our intelligence! - and doesn't needlessly explain every single gesture or word spoken.
