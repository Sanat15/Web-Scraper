Rating: 5/5

While not as immersive as False Ambassador (and its sequel Mappamundi), Memoirs of a Byzantine Eunuch is still a Christopher Harris historical so it delivers the expected - great atmosphere, a cynical but still a bit naive narrator, a take no prisoners storyline - all based on real characters and events - and of course lots of scenes not for the squeamish on both theological and prudery grounds, from the inquiries into the nature of eunuchs, all done with apparatus that purports to measure the precise degree of arousal, to a brutal and quite obscene mockery of transubstantiation where the depraved Emperor Michael orders Zeno - who as protegee of the Patriarch plays the role of clergy in Michael's dark masses and all around blasphemous actions - to transfer the sacredness from the mummified purported sacred organ to the aroused one of Michael's current favorite for purposes that are definitely not left to imagination in the novel...

And so it goes - the timeline moves fast and the events seem compressed though they take place over quite some years, but overall a very good book if you like its kind
