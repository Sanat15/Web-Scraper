Rating: 5/5

The calligraphic collection at the Sakip Sabanci museum in Istanbul is amongst the best I have seen, surpassing the collection on display at the museum of Turkish Calligraphy at the Bayezid complex, also in Istanbul. The museum collection offers a survey of Turkish calligraphic arts from the 15th through the 20th century. [My favorite calligraphic piece on display at the Sakip Sabanci museum is a Fer'men written in Arabic dating back to the 15th century, the only piece of its kind I have seen anywhere]. Letters in Gold: Ottoman Calligraphy from the Sakip Sabanci Collection serves as a beautifully produced mini-catalog of the collection. The book includes an introduction on the Turko-Islamic calligraphic arts, followed by an exposition of 71 pieces found at the collection, and ending with a chart the surveys the genealogy of key Turkish calligraphers. Almost all the major names in the pantheon of Turkish calligraphy are represented. The book itself is lavishly produced, and the quality of the calligraphic plates are among the best I have seen in any book. It belongs in the collection of any and all serious enthusiasts and practitioners of Islamic and Turkish Calligraphy.
