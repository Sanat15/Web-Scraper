Rating: 3/5

A. L. Rowse, in case you're not aware, was one of the most peculiar English intellectuals of the last century. An intellectual snob, a brilliant historian, a man getting progressively weirder; "...I used to be a homo; but now, when it doesn't matter, if anything I'm a hetero." It is almost impossible to expect clear and unobtrusive history from him, and maybe there is, but it's definitely not here. It is as if he written this small anthology to spite someone, and/or to amuse himself. It's occasionally entertaining, and interesting, and also monstrously boring, especially when he gets his hands on some personal archives belonging to Bess of Hardwick.
