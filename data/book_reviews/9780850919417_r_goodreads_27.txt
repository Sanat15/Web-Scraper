Rating: 4/5

fun fact - the copy of the book i own is from when my sister was in year 8 (2005), and hers was secondhand too.. safe to say it’s been in the family for a while 

i picked this up on a whim because i wanted something short to fill my time, and im really happy i read this. it’s a beautiful story, written from a realistic 14-15 year olds voice. its tragic, its sad, its funny at points, but overall its about trauma. 

it’s always a difficult situation when it comes to families, especially when you’re so young. the inner conflict of loving but hating, and being so extremely hurt from someone who was supposed to be there to keep you safe and love you unconditionally is shown really raw in this story. 

but at the same time, it’s more than that. seeing her friendships, and the value kindness has (without even knowing what an impact it can have on someone), and the idea that absolutely no one has a perfect life despite what’s visible on the outside. 

i always like diary entry style books, and i also like books based in australia, so i liked the way this was written as well. 

only downside is that i wish we got more of the aftermath. i would’ve loved to see an epilogue of what happened to her after she spoke to her father. did her friendships flourish or did they lose interest after she became ‘normal’? did her teachers remain close to her? what did she end up doing with her life? what about her mum - did she change when she started speaking or did she run away with the stepdad? lots of questions we will never have the answer to
