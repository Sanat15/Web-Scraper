Rating: 2/5

***Some questionable material for younger readers***

 This was a find in a thrift store that promised to be interesting but rather turned out to be a lot less than expected. And part of it is the fact of the compilation of works that were included with a larger proportion being poetry involving the owl of some sort - usually in a negative way as our ancestors used to view it - and the rest as fables or even possible real life encounters.

 For illustration purposes the book is just as eclectic in its illustrative theme with various sources being curated thus having the book with some very much realistic portrayals and others such as Edward Lear's more cartoonish smoking owl. Fortunately for the curious there is an index in the back that provides the reader with more information about the owl and its piece being portrayed just in case they would like to know more.

 Otherwise to whet the appetite for owls, especially as they have made their introduction on the world stage, it was a fairly decent read but not one I would recommend for serious owl fans.
.
