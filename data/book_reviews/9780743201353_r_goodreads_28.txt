Rating: 2/5

I like to pick up a Doig novel occasionally, when I'm looking for a rich historical story with unforgettable characters, western setting, great writing and interesting plot. This book did not live up to the anticipated quality.
The setting is Montana. Settlers have moved into this northern area, drawn by the beauty, water, and grass. It's a perfect setting to raise cattle or sheep. But there are rifts - the Scottish contingency is divided into the Highlanders ( Erskins, Duffs, McCaskill) border the Williansons and their Double W brand (for the brothers Wes and Whit.). Throw in is a Duff female (Susan) who is a talented musician and suffragette, and a black cowboy called Monty. These characters embark on a story of color lines, human values, the cost of passion, KKK and western gallantry.
The book wasn't succinct - the narration lagged. Some characters came to life (such as Wes and Susan. But Monty didn't fit his role in the book. The ending was a surprise, but not one I appreciated. It was just kind of disappointing to me.
