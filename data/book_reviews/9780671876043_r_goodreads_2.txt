Rating: 2/5

Yeah, this contains some good stories, but you can find them in many other, much better anthologies. Just because a story wins awards doesn't necessarily mean that it's any good. Connie Willis took over as editor, but her introductions are bland. This anthology wound up being a chore to get through.

Selections:

* "Introduction" by Our Editor. Fairly short introduction, mostly written as a tribute to Isaac Asimov, who edited the series starting in 1962 and continued until his death from AIDS (contracted from a blood transfusion). Also includes Our Editor's happiest moment at the Hugos.

47th Convention, Boston, 1989
* "Kirinyaga" by Mike Resnik. This novella started the Kirinyaga series. This was a controversial win, since it not only showed the conflict between religion and science, but was about a colony of Blacks, written by a white guy. It's a compelling, disturbing read ... but disturbing in a good way. It gets you thinking.
* "Schroedinger's Kitten" by George Alec Effinger. By the time you get about six pages in, you've figured out the trick to this novella and just want to get it over with.
* "The Last of the Winnebegos" by Our Editor. The title is a lie. The story is actually about the last of the DOGS. It's a devastating story that made me weep. I first read it about 20 years ago and have never forgotten it.

48th Convention, The Hauge, Holland, 1990
* "Boobs" by Suzy McKee Charnas. This is actually an urban fantasy and not a sci-fi story, although I don't think the phrase "urban fantasy" was around in 1990. An eighth grade girl grows breasts, gets her period ... and discovers she's a werewolf. WARNING: At first, she eats dogs.
* "Enter a Soldier. Later: Enter Another" by Robert Silverberg. Although an interesting concept, there is no ending. Yes, that might be the point, but mainly this was just an argument between Socrates and Pizarro.
* "The Mountains of Mourning" by Lois McMaster Bujold. This was the original short story that inspired her novel Barrayar. This in turn was part of her series, the Vorkosigan Saga. That means there's a lot left unsaid or not explained in this search for a baby killer. The best character is the horse, Fat Ninny. 

49th Convention, Chicago, 1991
* "Bears Discover Fire" by Terry Bisson. Quiet but fascinating little story of, well, bears figuring out how to make fire. There's also commentary about how the elderly are treated.
* "The Manamouki" by Mike Resnik. This is another Kirinyaga story, but it's not as disturbing.
* "The Hemingway Hoax" by Joe Haldeman. I have no idea why this was so popular. It goes on forever, and has no definite ending. And you need to know all about Ernest Hemingway.
