Rating: 4/5

3.5 stars. Colson is a high profile person because he used to work for President Richard Nixon. Then he was jailed for his involvement in the Watergate cover up. Around this time, Colson became a Christian. Many people suspected his conversion was fabricated in order that he might make parole from prison sooner. I don't think anyone still suspects that to be the case. Colson started Prison Fellowship Ministry, an outreach to people living in prison. 

This book tells a bit about why and how Colson got caught in the Watergate scandal, and then about his conversion. Most of the book is inspirational stories about how other Christians live out their faith, and how faith in general, can and should make a difference in our lives and the world around us. This book was first published in 1983, so there are sections that felt a bit outdated. For example, one chapter tells about Myrtie Howell, who corresponds with prisoners by writing letters. The version I read had an update from 1996 that tells where all the people mentioned in the book are today. I liked that Colson included stories that didn't always end up wrapped up in a tidy bow. Life doesn't work that way, and often times living out our faith doesn't mean rainbows, sunshine, and lollipops in the end. 

There is a study guide at the end of the book, which allows for reflection questions or reading this with a group.
