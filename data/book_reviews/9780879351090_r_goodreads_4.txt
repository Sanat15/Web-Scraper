Rating: 3/5

This short book, 79 pages, details some of the DeWitt Wallace Decorative Arts Gallery (Williamsburg, VA) clothing collection from eighteenth century, which was on exhibit in the mid-1980's. 

I was confused by the title, thinking that this was a collection of clothing that was actually worn in colonial Williamsburg. It's not. Most of the pieces were acquired in Europe, mainly England and France, for this collection. 

The basics of undergarments and how different clothing pieces and accessories were used is explained. Most of the clothing is from upper class society, but the book does touch on the clothing of lower-class people as well as slaves from the time period. 

Pages 9 - 48 detail women's clothing. Pages 49 - 71 detail men's clothing. There are 7 pages at the end of the book that explain children's clothing. 

Not all of the pictures are colored. There are quite a few in black and white which takes away from the overall sense of the piece in my opinion. For example, there is a black and white picture of three pairs of silk stockings, and then on the last page of the book that same picture appears smaller, but in color, and the effect is very different.
