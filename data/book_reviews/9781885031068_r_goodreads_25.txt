Rating: 3/5

I have a feeling Hughes wanted to do Conrad one better in this tale of storm at sea and how men cope with danger and strain. Early on he writes: "The days of Conrad's Typhoon are past: the days when hurricanes pounced on ships as unexpectedly as a cat on mice." And then of course, an unexpected hurricane hits the Archimedes and nearly destroys her. "A High Wind in Jamaica" and "The Fox in the Attic" being 2 of my absolute favorite books, I guess I was bound to be somewhat disappointed by this one. The narrative is ungainly, with a long description of how Ao Ling became a Communist and ended up on the Archimedes with false papers interrupting the flow of the story. This said, there is a lot that's good about it. For one thing, the prose, with descriptions like "the cold water in their clothes began slowly to explore their warm skins." But above all, I relate to the unsentimental view Hughes has of men and human destiny. As in "A High Wind", one of the main characters meets a swift and meaningless death. In this case it is Ramsay MacDonald, chief engineer, who falls into the sea out of sheer exhaustion just as he has decided to retire after surviving the hurricane. The tragic irony of his fate is compounded by the fact that his captain and other close colleagues are convinced he committed suicide and leave it at that.
