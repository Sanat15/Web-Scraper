Rating: 4/5

"Inanimate objects were often so much nicer than people, she thought. What person, for example, could possibly be so comforting as one's bed?"

Now this is exactly why I love Barbara Pym, she thinks like I do. Her spinsters are rarely sad and lonely creatures; instead they are very observant and perceptive and congratulate themselves on not having men or children to clutter up their lives. Because that's what this book is all about. An insensitive middle-aged husband turned by a pretty head, an unmarried curate thinking of marriage to make his life simpler, a clueless Vicar, and various young men looking for fun. The setting is Oxford, and we also have our share of busybodies and gossips, male and female. There is also a wife who can't see what's going on under her nose, and wins the day by ignoring the rumors. And the title makes me chuckle every time I see it.

This was Pym's first novel, written in 1939 and unpublished til after her death. Thanks goodness it was rescued, for it gave me a few hours of enjoyment and is full of her sly humor.
