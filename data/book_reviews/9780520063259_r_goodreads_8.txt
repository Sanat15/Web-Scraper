Rating: 4/5

The author is one of 36 children and the only one to be sent away from his herding life in Tanzania to a government school, chosen because his father felt that he could be depended upon to return to the family after being exposed to a very different way of life. He learned to read and write and to speak English, Kiswahili, and German and his education made him an asset to the family when selling their cattle or dealing with hospitals and the government.

With awe-inspiring flexibility, Saitoti went on to live in Europe and the U.S. and earned degrees well beyond those available to him at home in Africa. Through his hard work, he became in turn a ranger at the Serengeti National Park, a Maasai warrior, the "star" of a National Geographic film about life in Maasailand and a lecturer and author.

Some of the most interesting insights come from his explanations of traditional sayings and from the relationships he has with his autocratic father, his father's wives and his various step-siblings. As a nomadic people, the rules come from tradition, not laws and police officers, and it's fascinating to see how they work.
