Rating: 1/5

I wish I liked this book more. I like the intention and general principal: women need a spirituality that works better with the life/body they inhabit. This book is very Judao-Christian, very heteronormative, very centered on the abuses (chemical, physical, emotional, spiritual) that women suffer in the larger patriarchal world and male-dominated religions. I get it; I was raised in a super Christian, heteronormative, sometimes abusive family. But I've already worked through a lot of that. If you are just starting your journey away from, or your exploration into the why's of, that normative system/life, this might be a great book for you. There are certainly a few gems of wisdom scattered throughout. The book is super repetitive, slightly reductive, and the second-to-last chapter is a (repetitive) review and then a treatise on how to better get along with men. For real?! To write a book about women's spirituality and end it with a chapter on how better to encorporate that "new" perspective within your relationships with men is circular, or a huge undoing of the message in the rest of the book. I would distill this book down to less than 20 or so pages of useful/inspiring information. Don't waste your time.
