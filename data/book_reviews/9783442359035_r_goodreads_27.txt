Rating: 5/5

I'm a big fan of Cornwell but am the first to admit his books (particularly the Sharpe novels) can be a bit...same-ey.

This is most definitely NOT the case with Stonehenge.

Here BC world-builds a setting that is almost alien in terms of society and mores.

I really felt that the characters WERE from a different time far, far in the past and had no comprehension with how we understand the world today.

One of my biggest literary bug-bears in a historical novel is an author populating stories with characters who exist in a period of time that held radically differing attitudes to gender&amp;sex/race/religion/law/honour/equity/parenting/taboos/music/art/medicine/fairness and then cops-out by giving them 21st attitudes that are clearly at odds to those universally held beliefs that were the norm in that period.

(Are you reading this Griff Hosker?)

It was a breath of fresh air reading Stonehenge as here the characters didn't behave as modern day people who looked at their world through the prism of 4000 years of future development.

In this respect this book is quite a remarkable feat of writing.
