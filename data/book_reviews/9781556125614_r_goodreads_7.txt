Rating: 3/5

This was a really nice little book on the Eucharist as the center of Christian life. Hellwig might be a Roman Catholic, but what she says is very much in line with Calvin and Nevin regarding what happens in the Eucharist. Gone are the arguments that the elements themselves become something else or something more and now it is in the act of eating that Christ is made known by the Spirit. Also, she has a lengthy exposition of the Eucharist as a covenant renewal rite, and yes, she even used the words "covenant renewal" and "renewal of covenant." 

Her central thesis, though, is that the Eucharist is not meant to be an end, but a beginning. Gift does not flow from God to us and then stop, but a new cycle of gifting and receiving is to begin, gift, grace and food flowing out of us and our lives into the lives of one another and then to the whole world. Her prose is dense and opaque at times, which I found caused my mind to wander. It was an act of concentration to really pay attention to each word sometimes, but the effort was worth it.
