Rating: 1/5

Clearly, I am not the target audience for this book. It must *have* one, because there's a whole series of books about this Raisin woman, but it isn't me.

Which is a pity, because I would like to find a new English mystery series to read.

Now, this book *is* an improvement over certain other books I've reviewed recently, in that it has a discernible plot and is reasonably well-written. What it does not have is a main character I consider engaging. There's a pull quote on the cover that describes Agatha Raisin as "a glorious cross between Miss Marple and Lucille Ball, with a tad of pit bull tossed in."

Wild exaggeration, that.

Really, Agatha Raisin is a short-tempered semi-retired publicist who somehow overlooked the fact that she was still married when she tried to marry the guy next door to her new country cottage. She spends a lot of time worrying about that relationship, and also about the effects of aging. I'd have more sympathy with the latter, I'm sure, if I had ever spent a lot of time and effort on dolling myself up for display back when I *wasn't* staring middle age in the face and going, "So?"

Ahem. There was also a mystery in there. She talks to a bunch of people, investigating it, most of whom are very or extremely rude. In the end, though, the mystery is solved by police analysis of some cat hair and coincidence. Boring, really, even though the nice wife of the parson wound up having to shoot the murderer.

So, nothing to see here, folks. Move along.
