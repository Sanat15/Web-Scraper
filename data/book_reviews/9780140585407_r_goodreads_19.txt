Honestly
I haven’t finished this book yet. But I've decided that I'm done with it.

I don’t think poetry books need to be read straight through from beginning to end. It makes more sense to skim, find one or two poems that catch your attention, read them out loud, sit with them for a while, bask in their beauty, then move on until you find another one that hits. Just repeat that rhythm. The beauty of poetry is that there’s no pressure to follow a plot or worry about missing something.
About this book, I’ve realized that I really like D. H. Lawrence, especially after reading Lady Chatterley's Lover. There’s something so striking about him. Sensual, romantic, deeply aware of the body and emotion, and all of it bleeds into his poems. (I clearly have a weakness for romanticism.) His poetry moves across a wide range of themes: love, desire, religion, death, and even animals. There’s even a period where he writes a lot about tortoises, which sounds odd, but is so unique. What stands out most is this constant sense of yearning. (OH HE'S YEARNING YEARNING).
