Rating: 4/5

After introducing David, a 12-year old devout Christian, Stephen King moves on to his more usual spooky territory: various people are traveling on a lonely Nevada highway and are picked off for minor traffic infractions by a local town cop. One car is driven by a young professor and his wife, another target is an RV with a couple and their two young children, including David. And for good measure he stops a motorcyclist who happens to be a world famous author. (King describes this world famous author as a bit of an a-hole as an inside joke for his readers.) Things rapidly go downhill as the cop delivers his captives to the town jailhouse. Just for the hell of it he kills two of the people and hangs them on hooks in his office.

King used the plot device he's used in other novels - Good vs. Evil. It is similar to The Stand in that a group of good people band together to battle the forces of evil. If I remember correctly the good power was embodied in an old lady sitting on a porch. In Desperation, King goes straight to the top for his "good power" - the Christian God, bypassing Jesus and any other spiritual humans. God talks to David, the young boy, quite a bit; almost as much as he talked to Moses in the Old Testament. I got curious and googled "Is Stephen King an atheist?" Google AI said that when interviewed King said, "I choose to believe in God." He does not practice any formal religion. I mention this because I found it unsettling that King uses God as a character in this story. Other authors have done it so I suppose why not King?

Desperation almost squeaked in as a 5-star read for me. I've noticed in other reviews that readers loved the beginning and middle but complained that the ending was weak. I liked the ending. But then again I liked the ending of the HBO series "Game of Thrones" and I was really an outlier on that one.
