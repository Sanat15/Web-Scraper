Rating: 4/5

Though I read this for a class, I pretty much enjoyed every page of it. It's a bit out dated, as it lacks the relatively new discovery of the acceleration of the universe's expansion and dark energy. However, it does cover a surprisingly large amount of physics.

From the advent of 'modern' physics with Newton to Einstein, it covers the wide scope that is gravity. Zee goes into cosmological abstracts like cosmic strings and down to the extremely small, such as quarks and super string theory. Gravity is an important concept virtually on all scales, though as he discusses, this turns out to be the greatest difficultly in understanding it. 

Zee's prose is excellent and pretty lively, which is great for the lay reader. I should also mention that this book does not go into technical detail (I would have enjoyed this, but most others would not) which makes it quite accessible. He also tells anecdotes and some history that readers may find entertaining and a nice break from science, though they are always there for a purpose. 

The diagrams are a bit old and could have been better, which was slightly disappointing, but not detracting. There are also numerous references to the creator of the universe/god/Him which I didn't find appealing, but that's also not too big of an issue. 

Zee also gives some neat insight into the theoretical physics community in general, which nicely humanizes them. If you like science and are interested in the history of gravity and why it's still a topic of mystery and discussion, then this book is probably about as good as it gets.
