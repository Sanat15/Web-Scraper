Rating: 3/5

This is a very Kay Thorpe story. What does that mean? Heroes may or may not be cheaters. Heroines are doormats. Their conflict is ridiculous. The writing is compelling.

In this story the poor character/morals of the H/h propel the plot:

Heroine is tacky – I’ll do anything to keep my father out of jail

Hero is ridiculously impulsive– You must have sex with me now. You must marry me now. I’m going to fulfill my inheritance and destiny in Venice now. I’m going to kiss my former mistress now. I’m going to fly to England to bring my bride back now. I’m going to buy a house in England now. 

The author ends the story there but I’m guessing the whiplash change in plans and sentiments will continue apace. Their carbon footprint will be enormous.

I didn’t take this hero too seriously when he told the heroine he couldn’t guarantee he wouldn’t cheat. I read it that he didn’t want to admit the hold she had over him. 

Disclaimer. I do like this author even though all of her heroes are stinkers and her heroines are idiots. Her characters act from the basest of motivations – but try to dress up their bad behavior. (Case in point, heroine “doing anything” for her “father”) Heroine was attracted to H and wanted to have sex with him. Hero is just as captivated with the heroine, realizes marriage will strengthen his hand against his grandfather and he goes for it. They are a matched pair and deserve each other and their dubious HEA.
