Rating: 4/5

When I first read some Deryni books back in the 80s, I complained that they were too close to reality to please me. Now that I have read more, this was wrong. Kurtz is great writer whom I didn't read enough of as they were coming out, but luckily that can be fixed.

This third and final volume of the first trilogy is probably the strongest. The character work is very good, and the world continues to grow as we learn more and more about it. This series was a rare fantasy novel that wasn't a Tolkien-clone that involved a quest to stop evil-doers. It is a story of a Kingdom, a young king learning to rule, and a fight between, the known and unknown through the lense of racism. The other aspect of this series I really like is the presence of a strong religious element. Most fantasy book may have religion, but usually it is either pasted on, used as the villain, or just present. Religion in this series is a living and breathing religion with its flaws and its successes. 

The writing only has a few flaws. Kurtz tells far too often what the color of each character's eyes are, and the biggest one is the main villain in never fleshed out. He's just a really bad guy. We learn nothing about him or his motivations.
