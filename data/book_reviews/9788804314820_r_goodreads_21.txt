I'm sorry, but even after a second read I didn't manage to find this a compelling novel. Twenty years ago I gave up half way, and now I haven't gotten much further. Mind you, I acknowledge that this is an extremely interesting piece of literature. The extensive self-analysis of protagonist Zeno Cosini is worth the effort just to see how an older man (he is 57 when he writes this down) mercilessly fillets himself: his weaknesses, addictions, hypochondria, etc. are exposed in great detail. The sometimes feverish, neurotic narration involuntarily brings to mind Gogol, Dostoyevsky and Pirandello, and at one point the "self-tormenter" of our time, Karl Ove Knausgaard, also enters the picture, although that is a comparison that does not quite hold. After all, Zeno is an unreliable narrator through and through, who tries to talk himself out of everything, and is therefore primarily out for self-justification. He even bends himself in such a way that it becomes comical. Hence perhaps in many reviews the references to the inherent humour in this novel. Another relevant layer: the regular references to psychoanalysis (which, by the way, would lie at the basis of this writing) and to medical science in general, but all with a distinctly sarcastic undertone.
When I reread the above, I keep wondering how come I couldn't fully enjoy this and didn't finish it. For once I have no answer, I don't know, sorry.
