Rating: 1/5

‘’I’m fat’ said Mrs Large’ at the very beginning of this story to her three small children. Followed by ‘We must all go on a diet’. I don’t know why the children need to go on a diet just because she thinks she’s fat. I also don’t know why her idea of a nice healthy supper is watercress soup and water, or why everyone feels so terrible after doing more exercise. Oh wait, it’s probably because they’re in calorie deficit from only eating watercress soup. There’s lots of weighing themselves on scales until in the end they all bump into each other in the kitchen in the middle of the night sneaking cake and conclude that elephants are meant to be fat.
Honestly NO IDEA what the takeaway is meant to be for small human children. 

I immediately regretted agreeing to read this but was locked in by a persistent three year old. It was very difficult to edit into something I found not offensive. I’ve generally considered myself anti book censorship and think you should discuss the issues in books you find problematic with your child. Joke’s on me and I now understand the opposing argument. I just didn’t WANT to start in on ‘different people have different natural body weights but it’s also impacted by what you eat and how much you exercise and sometimes people feel bad about their weight because -‘ yuck. He’s not even aware of weight yet and I don’t want to give this subject floor-time. Also there’s just so much nonsense in this ‘diet’ to unpack. It’s the kind of 80s parenting that is now considered abuse and I feel newly suspicious of Mrs Large in other previously enjoyed adventures such as Five Minutes Peace.

I don’t think this book should be banned but I do feel strongly that it’s rubbish.
