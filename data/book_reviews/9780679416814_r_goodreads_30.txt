Rating: 3/5

The joke of the premise is so omnipresent as to come across surprisingly subtly: that is, a New England history professor responds to a local journal's call for impressions of Ford's presidency with 350 pages of sex and neurosis and a failed manuscript of a completely different president's biography that never really come close to useable. Within that framework, Updike paints an interesting portrait of a man who, as free-thinking as he wants to be in his academic life, can't let go of the dominant narrative of his life even as it deconstructs around him. I wish I knew a little more Deconstructionist and Post-Modern theory to get a little more out of the later narrative of book, but it probably wouldn't be any less dry.
