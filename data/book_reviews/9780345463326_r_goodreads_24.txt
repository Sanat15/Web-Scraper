Rating: 3/5

3/5⭐️

I picked this up after really enjoying The Genesis Code, so I went in with pretty high expectations. It didn’t quite meet that expectation, but I still had a solid time with it overall.

One thing I respect about Case’s writing is that he doesn’t over explain things. He trusts you to keep up, to connect the dots yourself and that makes the whole experience feel more immersive. You’re not being walked through the story, you’re in it, trying to keep pace with everything as it unfolds.

Which is why it’s kind of interesting that the protagonist often feels like he’s operating with a very low level of awareness. The book expects the reader to be alert and engaged, but the main character himself can come across as surprisingly naive at times. He’s meant to be capable and experienced, yet he keeps making fairly obvious mistakes while trying to stay hidden from people who are actively hunting him. That disconnect started to stand out more as things went on, especially because the writing itself is so careful not to spoon feed anything. It’s like the reader is being asked to think three steps ahead while the character occasionally forgets to check what’s right in front of him.

That said, the core idea of the book is still really strong. It’s one of those ‘ordinary guy in way over his head’ stories that just keeps escalating and it does a good job of maintaining that sense of momentum and unease as everything unravels.

And I do think the setting helps a lot too. Late 90s/early 2000s thriller energy just hits differently. There’s more space for things to happen, fewer safety nets, less digital intrusion everywhere you turn. It makes the whole situation feel more open and unpredictable in a way modern thrillers sometimes struggle to replicate.

So yeah, I didn’t love it as much as The Genesis Code, but I still enjoyed the ride.
