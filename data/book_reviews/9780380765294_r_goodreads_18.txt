Rating: 5/5

Whoever said that life was fair? Certainly not Elaine Guinivere Morgaine Borne Marshall, also using the nom-de-schoolmistress: Mrs. Courtney. Her attempt to lead the life of a lady in English Regency Society - the ton - was an unmitigated disaster, her marriage an even worse catastrophe. So instead of leading the life of a lady of quality, she heads a school, Morton House, which attempts to instruct young ladies in accepted ways of making their entrance into society, all the while hoping that her stewardship of the school will allow her students to avoid the pitfalls of her own life. Of necessity, she frequently resorts to disguise so that society families do not discover the truth of her background. Unfortunately, the best laid plans of mice and men - or scandalous schoolmistresses - do not run smoothly. Her latest pupil, Emmaline, is a rare handful, but Elaine soon unravels the events that have caused her newest pupil to behave and dress as she does. But in addition to Emmaline, Elaine has to contend with the advances of the girl’s all too attractive - and single - brother, Lord Corvin.
Complicating everything is the unprincipled roué who hopes to compromise Emmaline into marriage in order to gain control of her inheritance, as well as Emmaline’s stepmother, who has her own nefarious agenda. Mixed into all this is Daniel, Viscount Corvin and his determined pursuit of the elusive woman who unbeknownst to him, is really the scandalous schoolmistress. And even though Elaine knows that she can never reveal who she really is, she strives to deny her feelings for Lord Corvin. Can there be a happy ending to this travail? (I have read both editions of The Scandalous Schoolmistress. This review is of the 2021 edition.)
