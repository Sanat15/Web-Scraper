Rating: 3/5

An enjoyable look at the Supreme Court by a justice on it for many years. I enjoyed his dicussion of certain cases or types of cases he worked and his discussion of the presidents he served under. He had the highest opinion of FDR. And Nixon and LBJ were probably the people he had the lowest opinion of. 

One of the fascinating stories he mentioned was a conversation he had with FDR where he pointed out Supreme Court justices are not required under the constitution to be lawyers. FDR thought this provided him a great opportunity to put a non-lawyer (with common sense) on the court and planned on recommending Senator Bob La Follette to the court when the next opportunity arose. However, no vacancy opened up before FDR passed away. 

Justice Douglas didn't have a high opinion on the ABA and thought their opinion on who should be selected as a justice wasn't worth paying attention to.
