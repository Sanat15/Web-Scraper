Rating: 4/5

This is the seventh volume of a long-running anthology series that presents stories based on Larry Niven's Kzinti, a race of war-prone felines, and set, of course, in Niven's universe of Known Space. Think Klingons except with panthers or lions. This seventh volume contains three stories, A Darker Geometry, a full-length novel collaboration by Gregory Benford and Mark O. Martin, and stories from two writers new to the series, Hal Colebatch (a novella, The Colonel's Tiger) and Paul Chafe (a novelette, Prisoner of War). The novel, especially, refers to characters and events from the previous books, and all of the stories range kind of far into the recesses of Niven's Known Space universe. I think it's necessary to have read some of Niven's other work first, particularly Ringworld, to have at least some knowledge of Known Space. The Colebatch goes in a unique direction (back in time to colonial India), but he carries off quite well. The collaboration explores The Outsiders, though I thought it might contradict some of what Niven had established in other works. I enjoyed the Chafe, too. Once again, the Stephen Hickman cover presents the Kzinti as looking much more like Tony the Tiger than I'd pictured them, and this time he looks like he's fighting the blob in a vacuum. A fun series, overall, and a terrific auxiliary to Known Space!
