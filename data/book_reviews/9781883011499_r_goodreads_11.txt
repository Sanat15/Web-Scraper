Rating: 5/5

A selection of five novels well described by the title. Three I'd read, two were new to me. Jim Thompson, Patricia Highsmith, and Chester Himes are famous in the field that encompasses psychological thrillers, hard-boiled detectives, and noir. Jim Thompson's The Killer Inside Me (1952) is considered his masterpiece, the story of a serial killer told from the inside. Brutal and chilling, it shows a world the reader doesn't want to see. Several of his novels have been made into films including this one. The Talented Mr. Ripley (1955) spawned a series of sequels by Highsmith and inspired a hit movie with Matt Damon in 1999. Arguably her best novel. Chester Himes created a series of violent, over-the-top novels featuring mythic Harlem detectives Grave Digger Jones and Coffin Ed Johnson. Three were made into films, but not The Real Cool Killers (1958) included here, the second novel in the series. If there's a genre called "murderous humor," Himes invented it. All three of these books are essential for noir cultists. David Goodis was an author I'd heard of, but I hadn't yet read Down There (1956), as good as any of his books and made into the French film Shoot the Piano Player. His novels' protagonists are always a little off-center, which is saying something in this group. Pick-Up (1955) by Charles Willeford was a revelation, just a couple of corners away from perfection, but sucked me in and broke me anyway. It can hold its own in this massive company. The story of two lost and shattered souls trying to stay afloat in this world by holding on to each other. If this is a fair sample of Willeford's writing I want a lot more. Crime Novels: American Noir of the 1950s is an amazing collection in which each story will knock the reader to the ground. If you go in for that sort of thing.
