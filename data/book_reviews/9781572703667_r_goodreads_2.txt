Rating: 3/5

Agatha Christie's last novel.
Published in 1973, 3 years before her death at 85 years of age, this was better than you might imagine.
However, it simply isn't her best work for that very reason.

The mystery isn't interesting and the conclusion to the said mystery is even less interesting. Even by Christie's standards, the plot was far-fetched and muddled.
 Tuppence finds a code (letters underlined) that a young teen wrote in his favorite children's novel. She deciphers it and finds out that he thinks a woman didn't die of natural causes. And she may have been a German spy. Keep in mind that all of these people have been dead since the First World War. The only people she can even "interview" are in the local retirement home. And yet, somehow the descendants of the murderers might still have ties to organizations that are a threat to decent people and democracy. In fact, British intelligence officers encourage Tommy and Tuppence to look into the matter. Seriously?  
That part of the story really showed her age.

And yet.
Tommy &amp; Tuppence were still wonderful characters. I truly loved seeing them one last time.
They'd lost none of the flirty sparks that made them my favorite Christie couple. Their interactions were still a great representation of what a fun relationship looks like in its golden years.

Read it for Tommy and Tuppence.
