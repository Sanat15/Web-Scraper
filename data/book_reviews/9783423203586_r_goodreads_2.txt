Rating: 1/5

Am I, too, an Ursinologist?! 🧸

Because I love all things Pooh I was intrigued, but I have a much harder time seeing Pooh as the Great Magus of astrology and alchemy (etc.) than I do seeing Taoism in the classic script (see previous posts The Tao of Pooh and The Te of Piglet). 

With the repeated use of such words as “clearly” and “obviously” and phrases such as “this leaves very little doubt,” I DID become doubtful and a little offended at his treatment of the audience: is the author trying to trick us, or trying to be funny? In the end I simply let most of it go over my head (since I don’t otherwise study the Tarot, for example). Do you think our Bear of Very Little Brain would be pleased with me? I, for one, don’t think he would be disappointed! 

Take away: anyone can twist anything into supporting whatever it is they already believe or want to believe. But I will concede to agree with this: “Any interpretation hostile to Winnie-the-Pooh is self-evidently illegitimate” (86). 😅

This was a pretty fast read, however (perhaps bc I just wanted to get it over with), and included lots of the original art (which I love to see). But the author’s arguments did not make sense, did not convince, and on the whole read like gibberish; frankly I don’t think it was even meant to be a serious work. 

1/5 ⭐️ 

If you want to delve into Pooh Bear scholarship (yes, that’s a thing!), then you can join me and we’ll have these conversations! ☺️ Until then I’ll just keep regretfully wishing A.A. Milne had written a book about all the hidden meanings within his Masterpiece himself, so we wouldn’t have to put up with this sort of nonsense!
