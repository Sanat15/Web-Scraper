I’ve enjoyed other books by this author, but this one didn’t measure up for me. It was an entertaining story yet it felt as if it were contrived by the author when she was an adolescent. It was as if her adolescent-self had dreamily written a story that included 
  
    every
  
 plot ploy and characterization she had ever read in all her youthful reading of gothic and regency romances. So much of it was thin, convoluted and unbelievable, it just seemed very amateurish. And a, supposedly sensible, heroine who, conflictingly, acts naïve, stupid and silly gets on my last nerve no matter which author does it!

I would recommend first reading this author’s Frost Fair Fiancé and A Christmas Betrothal, both of which were quite good. On the Twelfth Day of Christmas was also a fairly good fluff story. 

(I appreciate that this author writes fairly “clean” traditional Regency stories. I did not make note of any expletives or God’s name being used in vain, although it is possible I missed something.)
