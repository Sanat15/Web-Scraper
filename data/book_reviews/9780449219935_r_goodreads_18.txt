Rating: 2/5

Isabella Chadbury, having accidentally witnessed an orgy with lots of loud and boisterous sex, is firmly convinced that all men are sex-starved boors. Determined not to marry, she ends up being labelled a heartless flirt. When the Chadburys' neighbour's son Lord Harry, on leave from the Peninsular War, finds that their respective parents have decided to get Isabella and Harry married, he decides he will teach the flirt a lesson - by pretending to be a fop. Meanwhile, Harry's younger sister Lucy is falling in love with Harry's friend James, and a spurned suitor of Isabella's is hatching a dastardly plot...

This one had seemed promising at the start, but it didn't deliver. Harry's parents are caricatures, Harry and Isabella are neither of them the sort of people I could root for (and Isabella's gullibility in the second half of the book is very surprising, especially for a woman so suspicious of men). What I would have liked, really, would be more about James and Lucy: they were a sweeter, gentler, nicer couple, whom I liked much more than Harry and Isabella. And one could see something of the love between these two: between Harry and Isabella, there was no attraction, no chemistry, nothing believable as love.
