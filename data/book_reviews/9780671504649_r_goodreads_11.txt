Rating: 4/5

In a story spanning just a few months, set with one foot in Oxford and the other in a village in southeastern France, Tremain's troupe of characters loop in and out of each other's lives. 

Though the cast is large and the characters complex (strange even), Tremain ensures we understand and feel for each of them. Through their tangled loves and dreams, we see the essential messiness of 'la condition humaine': its pain, its cruelty, its joys and its hope. 

As always with Tremain, the writing is sure; the reader senses they are in safe hands. And though the style is wonderfully poetic, it's still direct and incisive. 

Perhaps the reason I'm giving it only 4 stars rather than 5 is because the larger number of characters means we are less involved in their lives than had we had just a couple of main players. But dont let that put you off! It's a fab read!
