Rating: 1/5

This book was a remarkably easy read, taking me a refreshing few hours to finish. Presenting itself as a series of letters from young Leigh Botts favorite author that later morphs into his private journal, it documents the years of Leigh's life as he undergoes everyday hardships that slowly change and mature him. Throughout the duration of the book, he struggles to cope with the lunch thief, his mom's working hours, their humble house and neighborhood, and most of all, his parents' divorce.
Personally, I did not enjoy this book, mostly because I didn't find Leigh remarkably likable. One thing I will say in this story's defense, however, is its unafraid and unfailing honesty. Beverly Cleary is dealing with a fragile issue - divorce - and she handles it extremely well. There isn't a satisfying end to the book, but life is often like that. No happy endings, just time, going on and on, like always, as if nothing had happened that should have affected the normal flow of things.
