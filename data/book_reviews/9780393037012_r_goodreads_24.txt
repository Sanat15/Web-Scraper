Rating: 4/5

"Master and Commander" was a book I had wanted to read for a long time, especially since I had seen the movie and enjoyed it. Written by Patrick O'Brian it is considered to be one of the best examples of naval combat during the early 1800s.

Jack Aubrey has just been promoted to Master and Commander of the HM sloop of war the Sophie. Along with his Ship Doctor Stephen Maturin and his 1st Lieutenant, James Dillon. Based off the real-life exploits of Lord Cochrane, this is a fun read for those who want a good feel for what life on a ship of war was like during these times.

The Sophie will go through many ordeals as Capt Aubrey tries to get the sailors in shape. Daily life and chores, how the officer's mess is run, many of the ideas central to running a warship of this period will come to light. The naval engagements are very accurate and obviously O'Brian knows what he's talking about.

While I did enjoy this book, I am going to admit the sheer amount of 1800's Naval jargon will be thrown at you and sometimes it is difficult to keep up with what in the holy hell is going on. I am speaking as a military historian, albeit of modern conflict, and as a former US Marine. Perhaps those in the US Navy would have a better understanding but so much has changed since the Age of Sail that there is sure to be terminology that no longer applies. This often confusing and nebulous terminology was the only reason I didn't give this a 5 star.

So yes I liked it but was not blown away by it. I shall try another book in the series and see what I think going from there.
