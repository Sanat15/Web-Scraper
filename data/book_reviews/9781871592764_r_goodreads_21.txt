Rating: 3/5

The genius of the title, of course, being Dalí himself. I do think he was a genius -- a prolific, self-aggrandizing genius. As you would expect, parts of this are outrageous, aggravating, &amp; confounding, at times making me feel like I'm being punked by a joke played decades ago (diary entries span 1952 to 1963) but also revealing the true artistic breadth of an ideosyncratic polymath, imo. I wouldn't necessarily call this an enjoyable read as a whole (some felt too dense for me to parse including some of his self-described paranoiac-critical analysis) but other sections were illuminating, entertaining, &amp; sometimes even made me laugh. It feels mundane to call it an unusual read because what else, really, would you expect from Dalí?
"Since early childhood, I have had the vicious turn of mind of considering myself different from ordinary mortals. Here, too, I am being successful." (26 May 1953)

"We are eating muscat grapes. I have always thought that a grape held very close to the ear should make a kind of music. So at the end of the meal I have the habit of putting a grape from the bunch in my left ear. The coolness delights me, and I am already thinking of how to utilize the mystery of that delight." (22 August 1953)
