Rating: 1/5

First published in 1993, Martin Duberman's "Stonewall: The Definitive Story of the LGBTQ Rights Uprising that Changed America" is a disappointing novel describing the events leading up to and through the Stonewall raid that would mark the beginning of the modern civil rights movement for the LGBTQIA+ community. The book focuses on the lives of 6 different individuals who would play a partial role in those events in 1969 and tells the book almost as a biography of them which doesn't fully help the story. The actual events at Stonewall are relegated to a small segment in the latter part of the book with a very small segment of it describing the events leading to the 1st anniversary of things into 1970 and then ends. With this reader reading the book over 30 years after its publication originally a lot has changed something that is partially evident in the introduction and epilogue which have been updated since then when the book was re-released in 1993; however, this reader would've liked to have seen more focus on the actual events as well as the progress/challenges that still exist over 50 years since.
