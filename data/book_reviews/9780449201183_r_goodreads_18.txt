Rating: 3/5

This is only the third romance I've read from Chesney, and I'm slowly starting to wonder if her romances are supposed to be satirical.
 

This one was a lot weird. There were so many branches to this plot that I honestly couldn't give a synopsis if I tried. In the simplest of terms, the heroine pretends to take a lover in order to make her husband jealous and turn him away from his extramarital amours and back to his marriage. Despite the length of this book, that's about 0.01% of the goings-on.

I was recently knocked off my feet by Chesney's romance novel At the Sign of the Golden Pineapple. I don't mean that in a good way. I believe Chesney has a gift for making the first 99% of her book somewhat romantic, and then dropping a one-liner during the conclusion that makes the ending marriage uncomfortably dark. Comedic effect? Probably not. Genuine cynicism? I really hope not.

That's pretty much exactly how I felt with Lady Lucy's Lover. A fluffy, mindless romance with a hard back-hand to polish it off (see below). I'm really interested in reading at least a couple more of her romances. Not exactly for the entertainment of it, but to see if this pattern continues. 

Lucy: "Would you beat me Simon?"
Simon: "If you tried to leave me, yes"

...Awww.
