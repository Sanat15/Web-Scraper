4.5 stars

short review for busy readers: Lovecraft’s longest work. Remarkably gripping for a ‘case study’ mostly told and not shown. The setting of colonial Rhode Island fits the necromancy and witchcraft theme wonderfully well. A fantastic short novel for anyone who likes ghost or academic paranormal stories in the English and early American tradition. 
 
in detail:
I wouldn’t categorise this short novel as a horror story. More a “horror story viewed from across a meadow whilst attending a picnic on an oddly cloudy day”. 

The story of the 18th century necromancer, Joseph Curwen and his descendant Charles Dexter Ward is certainly gripping in its parallels and mystery, but at times becomes rather frustrating as many of the incidences which would make for real horror are either vaguely indicated, or the author of the ‘Case’ doesn’t go into them out of ‘propriety’s sake’. 

Was this gentile coyness Lovecraft taking the easy way out, or was he attempting to copy the mores of the times – indeed, the 20th century part of the novel takes place roughly at the same time the novel was written (the 1920s, making it a contemporary novel)? 

I don’t know, but it very much lessens the horror when you can only shadily guess as to what happened and who did what and how, when everyone involved is walking around with a finger in front of their lips in a ‘shhhh’ or 'zipped lip' gesture. 🤫🤐

As is typical with split timeline stories, the historic one about J. Curwen back in the 1700s is far more engaging than the modern one concerning Ward. 

I would have been content to read an entire novel just about Curwen, as the colonial atmosphere a la “The Legend of Sleepy Hollow” and his connection to Salem, MA and the notorious witch trials were a delight to read. (And I just like the 1700s. Guilty.) 

Charles Dexter Ward is…yeah. 

This is my first Lovecraft and I enjoyed it far more than I thought I would! Looking forward to reading some of his other works now.
