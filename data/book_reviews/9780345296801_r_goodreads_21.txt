Rating: 3/5

An interesting story told without particular flair or thrills. I read this to get a better idea of the Dying Earth genre for tabletop game I'll be running soon, and there's certainly a lot of really imaginative setting ideas I'll be stealing inspired by in designing my campaign. But there is little to this story outside of plot vehicles for exploring more of the setting; even to the point of repetitive descriptions of hunting/running from fantastic animals that seem to do nothing but pad time and break up descriptions of long travel. Farmer seems like he can write interesting characters; the cast all have clear and unique voices; and the character Sloosh ends up being an especially characterful expositor. But there's little interest in making the characters interesting in themselves, or in having their dynamics change in novel and interesting ways. 
The use of far future species with specific character traits and dispositions worsens this: The Yawtl, a fox-like species who are all disposed to thievery and greed, who are "less intelligent but more cunning" than humans, and who seem genetically predisposed to grudges and treachery—you'd hope your xenofiction sounded less like real-world race science, y'know? I was hoping that the perception of Yawtl as all thieving villains would be challenged like many of the other beliefs of the characters throughout the reading, but alack and alas it was simply framed as an uncomplicated truth of the setting.
