Rating: 3/5

This was a lovely book - it flowed nicely, it was an enjoyable read. My only major problem with it is that it didn't leave much of an impression on me. It's been all of a day, and I already feel like I'm starting to forget things about it. 

There were some issues I had with its narrative, but to talk about it would pretty much require revealing spoilers, so... 

The characters of the androgynous twins were interesting, but seemed to go no where. They existed on the periphery of the narrative, and then, near the end, abruptly turn up dead in a dry well, with little bearing on the narrative. Similarly, the character of Corey, the woman who is cross-dressed so that she can work as a travelling photographer, exists, is given some build up, but then just disappears from the narrative.

The overall narrative seemed, to me, to revolve frustratingly around Zacarias, who was, in my opinion, the least interesting of the characters. Some of this could be because his notes, fluttering across the countryside may or may not be the "impetus" for the entire story, but I still wasn't particularly interested in him - besides, the narrator was too omnipotent for it to be from the recollections of Zacarias, who spent much of his time too enamoured of his own expeditions to really notice the needs or desires of those people around him. 

Zacarias' "sainthood" was interesting, but again, one of those moments where the story just swung around him. 

I don't say this to imply that I didn't enjoy the book - I really did, and the writing was beautiful - I just didn't feel particularly drawn in by it, and it's not going to stick with me in the long term. I do feel like I'm being a little unjust, but my subjective opinion is that this book felt nice to me, but I wanted it to be more than just nice.
