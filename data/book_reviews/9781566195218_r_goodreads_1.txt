Rating: 4/5

This is a lot of fun. Not fall-down laughing funny, but amusing and simultaneously educational. Reading amusing judicial opinions from the year 1257 isn't something that most people can say they've done, so there's a coolness-via-obscurity factor, but the real gems are the more recent opinions. Some judges clearly get frustrated and bored on the bench, so when an opportunity arises that allows them to stretch their creative muscles, they seize the day.

My favorite case here has to be Ex Rel. Mayo, in which a gentleman brought suit against Satan and his accomplices, alleging various kinds of trespass and other tortious conduct. The judge had some gentle fun with the obviously insane plaintiff, noting that it was not clear from the record that the defendant had been duly served, nor was it readily apparent that the court had jurisdiction over the defendant. 

Also great: a California state appellate opinion affirming the dismissal of a suit brought by a kid who lost a spelling bee. Excellent stuff.
