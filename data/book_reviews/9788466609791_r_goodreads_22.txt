Rating: 3/5

This little book of early LOTR literary analysis was written after the trilogy was published in the US, but before The Silmarillion was published at all. Several times the author comments that this or that is a theory, but that "we'll know more when The Silmarillion is published." It felt like I'd gone back in time and was waiting with bated breath alongside this author for the release of the prequel to my favorite series.

The most valuable chapters are 10 through 12, followed by 13 through 16. In chapters 10, 11, and 12, the author traces the lineage of the fantasy novel from ancient epics through medieval romances to the (then) present day. In chapters 13 through 16, the author identifies Tolkien's sources and notes specific instances where he was inspired by them for the plot, character and place names, and props like the Ring. I added several of these ancient works to my reading list; reading about those old stories felt like finding hidden treasure.

I docked a star because three long chapters are devoted to retelling (!) the plot of the trilogy "in case someone hadn't read it." Seriously? Who would want to read about the books without reading the books first? Anyway, I read a page or so, and then I skipped those chapters. I've read the trilogy at least 4 times and studied it for a college class; I certainly don't need a retelling.
