Rating: 5/5

This is Singer at the peak of his powers. This short work encapsulates a crucial period in his life when he had to make decisions about his love life and career. He made lots of bad decisions. But he trusted his instincts and always came out okay in the end. This is largely due to his upbringing by a strict rabbi who rejected all things secular. The young Singer held on to many of the beliefs his father had instilled in him and it made him the think hard about everything around him. The way he summarizes the incredibly complicated political situation that existed in Poland during this time is amazing. We may think we live in precarious times given that we put a demagogue in the White House and suck up conspiracy theories like pap. But we are far from times like those that Singer describes where everyone is against everyone and suspicion is so high that nothing can get done. As far as his love life goes, Singer was no saint. But he is at least honest. He often succumbs to the temptations of the flesh. But given the mileu he is living in, I don't blame him for trying to go for the gusto in a world that offers little in the way of comfort and security. This is a wonderful book.
