Rating: 4/5

The "journeying boy" of the title (a title taken from a poem by Thomas Hardy) is a boy from another era (the book dates back to 1949), rather moody, at least until some of his behavior is explained, alternating between a mixture of precocious thoughtfulness and adventurous recklessness. It all begins with the search for a guardian who is to accompany the boy on a holiday with Irish relatives, leaving from London, where he lives with his father, a famous physicist. At first, things are a bit incomprehensible (Why does the guardian send a telegram declining the assignment but then prepare to leave? Why does the father lie at a certain point?), but then things gradually become clearer and the complication is also created by the presence of two rival gangs with the same goal and therefore, on the one hand, they hinder each other and, on the other, make the story more tangled for the reader. At one point, there's a surreal scene that takes place on the train, which I was convinced was part of a dream: the guardian wanders through all the compartments of a train and encounters several rather bizarre characters: a dwarf, a Chinese lady with a white monkey on a leash, two Indians playing cards, a black man dressed like an Edwardian dandy smoking a cigar, a gentleman wrapped in bandages like a mummy... in short, a complete circus, complete with animals in tow, in a scene somewhere between A Fish Called Wanda and Big Fish.
Cute, also read in one of my beloved green and white Penguin editions.
