Rating: 4/5

*Note: I have only read Miss Lavinia's Call, although I realize now it is for a whole collection.

This is another trodden-down-maiden-auntie tale, reminiscent of Aunt Crete's Emancipation- but Aunt Crete is way better! Miss Lavinia has a different quality to her, to be sure: firmer, more resolute, but she has to be, as her nephew is dependent on her instead of the other way around. However, the plot with her lover I felt was hard to take. Would he really have done that? I don't quite understand his reasoning. Would a man really let the lady he loved suffer, just because he didn't want her to nurse his broken body? Especially when he was rich, and could have hired a nurse, while Lavinia just kept him company? Wouldn't he have felt that he could at least protect her from poverty and exploitation and slave labor and her brother and sister-in-law's meanness, even if he couldn't get out of bed? Men, you'll have to help me with this one! 
However, the ending is satisfying and triumphant, which I always approve of!
