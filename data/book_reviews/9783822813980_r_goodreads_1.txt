Rating: 3/5

Taschen photographic books are usually very good and this one is no exception. It looks at London through three aspects, the streets of the metropolis, some of the homes that belong to perhaps the more affluent residents and then at closer detail within some of those homes.

In the streets section, modern London is represented by one of the pods of the London Eye, full with viewers, that looks across the city while the other images wander around the city with my favourite being a shot of a street that I visited often, especially when living close by in Bayswater. That is Portobello Road with a view of its closely packed antique stalls and another that resonates more with me is one of Brick Lane in the East End for that was opposite where my late wife Linda lived and it also hosted a thriving market every week. There are also plenty of views of Notting Hill Gate, W11, because quite a few of the houses featured in the following sections are located in that now desirable area.

And in the Interiors section there is a wide variety of styles, mostly very swish and mostly very modern, although there are one or two that look back on, say, a Victorian style. To see so many views of interiors could be deemed uninteresting but the opportunity to see so many fascinating interiors does take some of that feeling away.

As for the details section, many of the images are close ups of some of the items already spotted in interiors already shown, such as the large Buddha head originally seen in the background of an interior, so this details section is probably the weakest of the three.

Overall it makes an interesting insight into these aspects of the city and as Jane Edwards states in her foreword, 'This book is a window on the private worlds of a handful of Londoners whose homes reflect the city in all its spirited diversity.' It sure is!
