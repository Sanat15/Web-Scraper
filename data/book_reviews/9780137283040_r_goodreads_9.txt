Rating: 2/5

Disappointing.

George V. Higgins is one of the greatest genre fiction writers of all-time so I thought a book about his life-long passion for the Boston Red Sox (the chronically disappointing Red Sox not the big money juggernaut Red Sox; the book was written in 1989) would be interesting. It was just ok.

Higgins spends much of the book explaining baseball to his readers and although he consults with Pete Palmer he seems out of his depth when talking about statistics. No, Wade Boggs and his failures to drive in 100 runs from the lead-off spot was not the 1980s Red Sox' biggest problem. He also relies on the opinions of a few retired Red Sox who naturally tell us how the game was better back in their day.

I would like to have read much more about his personal history with his favorite team (the best parts of the book) and not as much about the shortcomings of Jim Rice.
