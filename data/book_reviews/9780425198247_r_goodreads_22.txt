Spoilers: A very dense installment in the series—in more ways than one. Anita’s ardeur continues to wreck her life; as she fights it, it gets worse. This is the novel where Anita FINALLY realizes that she’s in love with Nathaniel. Nathaniel, Damian, and Anita become a second triumvirate on accident and it shakes the power in all of them + Richard and Jean Claude. The longest / most painfully negotiated scene between Richard, Anita, and Jean Claude truly takes ages to get through due to Richard’s self-loathing and Anita’s stubborn foot-in-mouth reactions. They get there in the end but it’s a slog for the reader. This book is about sex, way more so than the other books in the series so far. We meet Requiem. We discover Malcolm’s Church of Eternal Life vampires don’t take blood oaths. We meet Wicked and Truth, and Anita binds Truth to her. The murder mystery in this one is very back burner and doesn’t get resolved—strippers are being hunted by a rogue pack of vamps (it’s Vittorio, he is not caught). 

Other musings on this book:

Anita’s ability to call out Richard for his blatant homophobia while ignoring her own in regard to women drove me insane in this 2024 reread. Again, I know she has a personal reckoning later and it gets resolved, but wow is it painful to read and supposedly “normal” in the context of this novel. 

I still continue to grate on the Madonna / good Christian girl vs. whore / slut dichotomy. I know these were written by a woman who was raised in a different time—1970s and 1980s, not millennial or whatnot—so the mindset is very different. And the books themselves were written in the early 2000s when the larger culture was also thinking about things in a much more repressed way. Anita walked so today’s authors could run. But it still gets me, and I like to point it out in case other readers think I’ve missed it and/or aren’t bothered.
