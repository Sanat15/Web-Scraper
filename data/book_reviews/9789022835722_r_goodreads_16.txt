Rating: 4/5

Read in the Hebrew translation.

Turning the last page on the diaries and letters of Etty Hillesum my only sentiment was grief over such a beautiful soul. Etty (I feel that I can call her that having spent a week with her, sharing in her innermost thoughts) was a mature and deeply spiritual woman. She wrote her diaries for herself in order to put to paper the barrage of emotions and thoughts she experienced and the existential issues that plagued her in every aspect of her life: her loves, her friends, her family, God and the meaning of it all. And through it all there is her great desire to become a writer. One is only left to imagine what a deeply engaging writer she would have become. 
We follow Etty through her diaries, and later through the letters she wrote from Westerbork, the detention center for Jews in Amsterdam in which they awaited their deportation to Auschwitz.
The war gradually seeps into her writing. First the German soldiers walking through the streets of her beloved city, then the limitations on Jews, finally their being hunted down by the Dutch police, and ultimately the atrocity of Westerbork and Auschwitz.

 Etty didn’t survive. She is one of the six million.
