Rating: 3/5

As has been noted, Alan Cooper is one of the "founding fathers" of interaction design and "About Face: Fundamentals of Interaction Design" is his magnus opus that shaped the nascent field. The ideas in the book significantly influenced the principles and practices of modern interaction design.

It's often the case that the best "textbooks" are almost mysteriously dense - so terse yet so packed with useful information that you are still learning on the third read. I think an opportunity was missed here to create such a work. The entire book could easily be condensed into a 100-200 page monograph focusing more on the main theoretical ideas, with implementation details outsourced to a website that could easily be updated with the rapidly evolving technological landscape. Likely not even a consideration when originally published, but in the decades since much more feasible. 

So while verbose and repetitive in parts, the key ideas introduced here are central to the field of interaction design and it rightly deserves to be considered a classic.
