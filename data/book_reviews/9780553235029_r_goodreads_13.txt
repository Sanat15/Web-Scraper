Rating: 3/5

Such an interesting reading experience. I was fully halfway through the collection before I found a story that I fully enjoyed, Sherwood Anderson's The Untold Lie. Before the beginning of Part Two, I was struggling to even appreciate the form of the "short short", as the editors describe it, at all. I think that largely holds true after having completed the book, as only a handful of the 38 entries within were anything that I care to think about again, let alone recommend or claim to like, at the most basic level. The short short is, if you can believe it, simply too short to really convey a message or tell a satisfying story. If ambiguity is where you like to live, then I would recommend this collection, as I feel my main complaint with the various entries, beyond stylistic preferences, was that the feeling left with me after finishing the short short was most frequently confusion or frustration.

My favorite quote from this collection comes from Paula Fox's The News of the World: "Words are nets through which all truth escapes."

My favorite entries include the Sherwood Anderson mentioned above and Augusto Monterroso's The Eclipse.
