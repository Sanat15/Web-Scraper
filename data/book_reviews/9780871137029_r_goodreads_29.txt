Rating: 4/5

This is less a review of this excellent piece of reportage of the hunger strikes in Northern Ireland than my feeling about the strikes themselves. This book made me feel deeply sad about the waste of life on all sides, and particularly angry at those people, both leaders of the IRA and the British government, who allowed these people to do this to themselves. But also not finding these people heroes, some of them (particularly Francis Hughes) did profoundly awful things. This is one of the strengths of this book, interspersing the account of the strikes with some of the horrors happening outside the prison. A small bit of hope, this book was written in 1986 when the conflict seemed hopelessly never ending, but things did get better.
