Rating: 4/5

A most interesting read. Over five hundred pages of well researched data. I can understand now why Walt Brown makes so many references to this work in his 'Master Chronology of the JFK Assassination.'
Mark North's 'Act of Treason' was first published back in 1991 and provides a fascinating chronology through 1960 to 1964. The sub-title of this book asserts this author's theme :-'The Role of J. Edgar Hoover in the Assassination of President Kennedy.' Not that we should be looking for this extreme right wing, pug nosed, cross dressing little runt hanging out of a window in the Texas School Book Depository. I think to any reader on this subject it should be blatantly obvious that Hoover and LBJ were primarily involved in the prevention of any truth emanating from the events in Texas. North propounds that through electronic surveillance of Mafia operatives in 1962, Hoover became aware of assassination plots against the chief executive being arranged by New Orleans 'don' Carlos Marcello. (John H. Davis 'Mafia Kingfish') These plans were never forwarded to the Attorney General or the Secret Service. Also never disclosed was Oswald's role as FBI informant.
A highly recommended and thought provoking read accompanied by a wealth of documentation.
I do not agree fully with Josiah Thompson's 'Six Seconds in Dallas' treatment of ballistics/wounds, which are adopted in this book, but to be fair, that side of things is not the remit of 'Act of Treason.' Yet never explained in North's thesis is any role of CIA and their involvement in the Oswald 'legend' or Mexico City, or the instant involvement of military controlled actions at Bethesda.
