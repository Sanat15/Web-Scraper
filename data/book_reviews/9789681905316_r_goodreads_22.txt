Rating: 4/5

The good: this book is like a portrait of 20th century Mexico City through the eyes of a woman who Forrest Gumped herself into influential periods and events. It opened my eyes to the competing ideologies that have shaped Mexico. Unions, Spanish republican refugees, Catholic priests, communists real and imagined (refugees of McCarthy era purges), artists and movie stars all make an appearance. Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera have a not insignificant cameo, so that’s cool.

The bad: Laura Díaz herself is kind of dull. She’s a witness to history, which I guess is kind of the point. She never engrosses herself in the ideologies that destroy the lives of her friends, so she becomes the sole survivor. And that makes her boring. It can also feel a bit slow since nothing is really driving the plot forward, it’s just another year with Laura Díaz!
