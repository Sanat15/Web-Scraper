Rating: 5/5

If Joseph Conrad had tried ayahuasca --- that is how "At Play in the Fields of the Lord" resonated with me. Mathiessen brings the reader into a dark jungle populated by a host of conflicted and morally flawed protagonists for whom innocence is long gone and all existence is cynical and absurd. Survival is neither sought nor prized. Power is the sole pursuit against the inevitable, all-consuming void.

The epigraph, a quote from Hermann Hesse reads :

"The way to innocence, to the uncreated and to God leads on, not back to the wolf or to the child, but ever further into sin, ever deeper into human life".

Matthiessen is a superb writer and an acute observer of both the natural world and the human condition and he knows how to plumb the darkest heart of humanity. He can also, when you are seeking light, offer you a silver moon and the last unplucked eye of a bloated corpse until you question the very concept of salvation. If there is a natural history of the Soul it might very well be written by Mathiessen.

As I read i thought of the week I spent trekking in the jungles of Malaysia, in particular when I was squatting to defecate and the relentless trains of ants incorporated my form as just another obstacle in their endless marching. Drops of water from the forest canopy fell. Sweat never stopped and I realized the specific small irrelevance of any single organism in such a dense web of life.
As I read the last page of "At play in the fields of the Lord" i returned to the first, for the best books end as they began thematically, like Robert Jordan laying down on the pine-needled floor, like a snake eating it's tail, like Kurtz dancing in the flames.
Like the moon rising into darkness again.
