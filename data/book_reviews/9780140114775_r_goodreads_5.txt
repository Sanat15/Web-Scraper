Rating: 5/5

A classic peep into the traumatic period of India’s partition. In many ways the novel is a true reflection of the modern times as well. While reading the novel I remembered Frank Kafka’s quote- ‘One idiot is one idiot. Two idiots are two idiots. Then thousand idiots are a political party’. Like the novel Pinjar, it captures a period, where as a bystander who watch helplessly as poor innocent people find themselves trapped in a hopeless situation created by a few with vested interests. The novel is a strong commentary on how greed and politics corrupts absolutely.
