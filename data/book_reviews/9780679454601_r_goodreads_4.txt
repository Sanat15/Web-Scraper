Rating: 4/5

I can’t decide if this book was brilliant or just okay, but it probably says something that I’m still dwelling on it days after finishing it. The author writes about her love-at-first-sight romance with Jim Beaman in Iowa City, early 1990s. She completely poured her heart out – I think every conversation they ever had, every inside joke, every thought she ever had about him, is included. She went through the Iowa Writers’ Workshop, and there’s no question she can write. 

As Mary learned within weeks of meeting Jim, he was an alcoholic and cocaine and heroin addict. She was far from a kid when they met, and part of me was resistant to the entire story, because getting involved with someone whose entire adult life has been ruled by addiction is clearly a terrible idea. About a year into their relationship, fraught with ups and downs when Jim’s addictions always took precedence over his feelings for Mary, they became engaged. Shortly after Christmas, they broke up, reconciled over the phone, and the same afternoon Jim shot himself. (This is all in the book jacket… no spoilers.)

The rest of the book is Mary’s story as she tries to come to terms with the idea of never talking to Jim again and the broader question of where people go when they die. She dabbles in various attempts to maintain contact with him, which slowly take over her life and reason; eventually her friends intervene and she is briefly committed to a psychiatric hospital. Both of these events contain the same excessive level of detail as her relationship with Jim. At times this made me impatient, and at other times had the effect of completely immersing me in her experience. After being released from the hospital she slowly works through things on her own, including her own challenging childhood experiences, and specifically adds that she no longer gets involved with people with drug or alcohol addictions. It was all surreal… probably not the best book to read with a 102 degree fever.
