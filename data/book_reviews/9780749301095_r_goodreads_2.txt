"A singular exception [...] a woman is often acknowledged the absolute sovereign of a great kingdom, in which she would be deemed incapable of exercising the smallest employment, civil or military" 

Gibbon, Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire

My first Antonia Fraser history book - An experience filled with pleasant surprise regarding the level of research that went into it. My prior knowledge of Fraser was limited to her writing popular history books

Centered around Boudica Supposedly Boudicea is the spelling that the British are most used to - thanks to Queen Victoria - I have only seen Boudicca. A better familiarity with Tacitus than British History, I suppose. Though Fraser does spend time talking about how the different spellings arose, as well as what they meant for the development of The Warrior Queen., the historical figure who led the 60/61 rebellion against the Romans, and Boudicea, the legendary character, who represented moral qualities that were commendable, Antonia Fraser confronts the development of these two ideas, and how they arose in response to the political and social climate of the time.

From this, Fraser highlights the themes that occur throughout different civilisations - that of the 'Shame Syndrome', by which the courage of the woman is contrasted with the weakness of men around her, the attribution of masculine features to a woman i.e., the 'Tomboy Syndrome', and the chaste/promiscuous paradox.
