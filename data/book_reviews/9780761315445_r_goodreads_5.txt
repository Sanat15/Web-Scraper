Rating: 4/5

George Speaks by Dick King-Smith is about a baby boy, George, who starts to speak at only 4 weeks old. He does not speak typical baby ‘goo goo’ but in an adult and even pompous manner. Laura, George’s older sister found out he could speak but was to keep it a secret from their mum and dad. Laura help George improve matters of been a baby by helping holding up a book so he can read, helping him use a potty and to get different foods other than milk such as Bacon Casserole and Chicken and Mushrooms. George slowly shows his intelligence to his parents, not wanted to shock them and on his 1st birthday he decides to reveal to his friends and family his true intellect. His parents were proud of him, the rest of his family were bemused and Laura knew all along how clever her little brother was.
 The book can really capture the imagination of children especially if they have a baby brother or sister themselves. Even reading now I imagine how I would react if I had a baby sibling who spoke so young. Alongside the story are sketches which are very clear and detailed. They add to the humour of the book, for example, an illustration of his family shocked when he gave his speech on his 1st birthday.
 An appropriate age range would be between 7-9 year olds. I would use this book for independent reading or even as a class book at the end of the day. I thoroughly enjoyed this book and loved the humour within it, with George in the end giving all the orders to his parents and saying that children are much more sensible compared to the grown-ups. I remember reading this at school and re-reading it over and over again and even now, with a different perspective I still thoroughly enjoyed it.
