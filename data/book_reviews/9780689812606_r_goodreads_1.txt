Rating: 2/5

Molly Wigand has crafted a world where infants understand free market trade, where communities barter with each other's refuse, and she portrays a fictional post-racial America where frozen dairy treats are peddled on street corners. It is a quaint, picturesque fable, but narratively leaves a lot to be desired. For example, it isnt until the last pages where the junk sale, alluded to in the first pages, actually begins. The promised premise of a junk sale is not delivered upon until the closing arc, and when it does present itself, it does not pay off. The only satisfaction a reader can ascertain from this bespoke yarn is the clever word play of the title, as we do indeed progress from junk, to "sweet," back to junk. All in all, this Rugrat story evokes the feelings of the warmth of childhood, but leaves much to be desired.
