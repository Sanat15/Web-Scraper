Rating: 2/5

Kenneth Thomasma's nonfiction novel "Naya Nuki" felt lifeless. "Naya Nuki" is the story of a Native American that escaped slavery and ran for miles in an attempt to reach her people the Shoshoni once more. Naya Nuki has to survive the cold wilderness of northwest America and the dangerous animals and people that dwell in it. The character development is made fairly obvious within the few main characters that are mentioned, specifically Naya Nuki herself, though I found it so obvious that it was fairly dull. There is very little dialogue and more so an inner monologue that is voiced in third person, which I found somewhat unsettling. The writing style was not my favorite. It felt too professional to be enjoyed as a personal read and more so like an essay. The thematic depth felt very shallow. The imagery was simple and lifeless, important moments felt short-lived and pointless. However, the pacing was fairly consistent throughout the book. Yes, the important moments felt shorter, but otherwise, the pacing was fair. Personally, I felt very bored reading this book. It felt like I was reading an essay on a Native American rather than a unique telling of the story. It was very factual and less thriving in life and emotions, and there didn't seem to be a solid, clear climax in the book.
