Rating: 4/5

Barbara Pym is an English author, who, in the 1950's, wrote a series of social comedies. Jane and Prudence is not her best known, but if some of her other books are better than this one, I have GOT to read them.

The plot is somewhat formulaic: older minister's wife is playing matchmaker for her younger friend. Jane met Prudence when she tutored her at Oxford. But Jane does not fit my idea of an Oxford graduate. She is sweet, but somewhat scatter brained. On page 145, a young man comes to her door looking for old clothes for sale. Jane makes a few nonsensical comments and the young man backs away. "He thinks he has come to a private mental home, thought Jane, the patients are not dangerous and are allowed to walk the grounds". That's our Jane, crazy, but not dangerous.

Prudence is a 29 year old who has had many love affairs but none have ended in marriage. She also happens to have a thing for her older married boss.

Jane tries to get a relationship going between Prudence and the local widower, Fabian Driver. It's a comedy to see if it will work or not.

This book was very enjoyable for me because of its humor and satire. These characters are very entertaining; I'm glad they are in the book, though, and not my neighbors!!

Pym's better known books are Excellent Women and A Glass of Blessings. Her book, Quartet in Autumn, written in 1977, was nominated for the Man Booker Prize. I see more Pym in my future.
