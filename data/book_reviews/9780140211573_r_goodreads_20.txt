Rating: 4/5

These are tragedies that broke my heart.
This book made me break down many times. It brought me back to the darkness of childhood trauma, and more than once I felt furious, even disgusted, because I projected so much of my own hatred toward my parents onto the stories in the book. I became too emotionally immersed in it. (I know this is unhealthy in some ways, almost like reopening old wounds and hurting myself again, so there were moments when I had to stop reading in order not to fall back into that darkness. Still, I’m grateful that once I became financially independent, I managed to leave that toxic environment. Even though those memories still follow me, sometimes swallowing me whole when I least expect it, I will continue fighting against them.)

Putting aside my personal emotions, this is an incredibly valuable and insightful work, and I’m deeply grateful to R. D. Laing for writing it.
Reading this book also reminded me of a thought I once heard: many children only begin to take mental health seriously because they were raised by parents who most desperately needed help themselves, but refused to confront their own condition.
While reading, one question kept returning to me: when people talk about deeply distorted or even pathological parents, has anyone considered that some of them may also be suffering from severe psychological disorders themselves, perhaps untreated trauma, delusion, or other forms of mental illness...
