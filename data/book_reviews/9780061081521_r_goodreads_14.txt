Rating: 3/5

This one was all over the place. The heroine Sylvan is the daughter of an overbearing cit who only sees her as a means of solidifying his place in society. She decides to leave home and serve as a nurse on the battlefields in Brussels. Unfortunately, this leaves her with PTSD and a ruined reputation, despite her intact virtue.

The kindly Duke of Clairmont offers to employ Sylvan to care for his wheelchair-bound younger brother Rand, who had been injured in battle. When she accepts and meets her patient, sparks fly. Rand is full of rage and grief. Sylvan absorbs the brunt of his surly attitude with aplomb and manages him perfectly. 

I thought the start was great. There is also a little magical realism thrown in with the ghost of the first Duke haunting the estate, which I liked. But after that, things lost momentum and went a little off-track.

There is a marriage of convenience between the MCs, a murderer on the loose, and a marriage-in-trouble caused by a weird argument that felt OTT. Sylvan, who started the book so strong, melted into a wimp, and Rand also seemed to have a personality transplant midway through.

This book contains multiple descriptive love scenes which lean into purple prose.
