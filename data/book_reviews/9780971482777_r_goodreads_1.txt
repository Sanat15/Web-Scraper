Rating: 3/5

Clifford Pickover is one of those writers for whom literary quantity exceeds quality, yet his wide-ranging intellect and boundless creativity make up for any and all of his stylistic shortcomings. In that regard, he reminds me very much of Philip K. Dick, who admittedly wrote much more science fiction than Pickover, primarily known as a non-fiction author. This similarity is nowhere more apparent than in the present volume, The Lobotomy Club. As in one of PKD's novels, here we have characters on a fast-paced, barely-hanging-together romp through an altered state of consciousness, wrestling with religious themes, and confronting a possible alien invasion. In typical Pickover style, the cause of the altered state isn't mystical or psychedelic so much as it is mathematical, topological; the CMS, or Cerebral Mobius Strip, is "a special topology and network of neurons" which the protagonists manifest. A fun, provocative book which shows Pickover as a budding sf writer.

"Neoreality and the Quest for Transcendence" by Clifford A. Pickover
