Rating: 3/5

I had previously given this book five stars, however, after rereading it, I have to lower my rating to three stars.

This book is a bit of a narrative mess. The accusation described in the description, about Phano's parentage, doesn't even come up until page 165 of the book. We get glimpses of why the character of Phrynion is so awful, but the story really needed to focus more on this. Most of the book focuses on rituals and culture, not actual story.

Phano is a passive character in her own story. Much of the important information is told to her instead of her finding it out herself. 

Another key missing is Theo and Phano's relationship. As the narrator, she often says she loves him, but we never see why. I wouldn't expect a large romantic part of the book, but something on it would have added to the story.

I'm not sure why I gave this story five stars before. I do consider it worth a read for historical fiction fans, but it's not worth going too out of your way for it.
