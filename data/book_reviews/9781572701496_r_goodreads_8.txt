Rating: 2/5

While there was nothing I actively disliked about The Religious Body, I didn't find much to enjoy, either. DI Sloan was interesting but not compelling. The portrayal of religious life rang true in some ways, not in others. Ultimately, the mystery was implausible for me, hence the spoilered rant below.

I didn't buy the twist that Ranby had been masquerading as Sister Anne. Sloan notes that nuns are trained not to notice, but that just doesn't ring true to me. In an enclosed order like the one in this book, the nuns must grow very accustomed to each other, knowing one another's pattern of movement, shoes, even scent. The little things that one sees day after day, becoming part of the fabric of one's reality. Like how you can recognize loved ones through a crowd, because you know their haircuts instinctually, and the way they hold their shoulders. Tiny, imperceptible things that nuns still would have noticed even when their coifs acted like blinders and they were in silence. I couldn't help but think of Ranby's scent, too; nuns would not wear any perfume, and I imagine their hygiene products were simple. Ranby must have just smelled different, you know? And he was certainly not used to walking in a full-length wool habit. I think someone would have noticed he walked differently as he came by--that his step was not that of Sister Anne's. I guess what I'm saying is, that while Aird's portrayal of the religious community was realistic in many ways, she underestimated the level of mundane intimacy those in the religious life have with each other, which makes the twist unrealistic.
