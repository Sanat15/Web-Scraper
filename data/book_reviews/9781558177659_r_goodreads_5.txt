Rating: 4/5

Another great story by Ms. Crenshaw. She makes the unusual choice of focusing her tale on a servant girl (no secret aristocrat or marriage to a nobleman) and a baseborn highwayman. Her characters are what makes the book such a great read.THEY move the plot forward, not the other way around.
Jane is a product of a society will not allow for advancement or even a modicum of grace if she’s found to be less than a model citizen. The line dividing the gentry and the lower order was so cruelly sharp that you could see why the Colonists rebelled.
She’s accused and tried by public opinion without anyone even knowing the circumstances of her situation. It’s her desperate decision to flee that puts her in the sights of Keith Cutler the Highwayman. 
This is a story of a man who falls in love with a woman in a portrait and has to come to terms with the fact that she is more human, more vulnerable and yet, much stronger than the beautiful girl who was the muse for a painting.
It’s the story of a woman who struggles with trusting ANY person after the vile way she was treated by those who should have cared for her. She has no joy in realizing she loves a highwayman, a man who treats life like a game and who flirts with the uncompromising system of “Justice”. She just wants to be safe and be the decision maker of her own choices, be they good or bad.
Historically accurate, fully developed main and secondary characters, and a story that leaves you wiser for finishing it. What more do you need?
