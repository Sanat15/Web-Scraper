Rating: 5/5

'Athena' is both a sad and comic ode to uncertainty. You always question the characters' true identity similarly to the forged/genuine paintings the story formally revolves around. The narrator, Morrow/Freddie Montgomery, is never fully stable or trustworthy. Being a morally murky individual and an ex-convict, he ha suffered many losses in his life but what he hasn't lost is his self-irony; he never pities himself.
We see the reality through Morrow's eyes but how real is it? Are his memories genuine? Is some kind of transformation is taking place inside of him? Are other people just playing their roles, concealing their true selves? And what is 'true self' in the first place?
Banville's writing astonishes, amuses and mesmerises. To me it mattered much more than the story itself.
We watch grief-stricken Morrow turning into a self-conscious and ironic observer of his life and we explore its darkest corners with him.
