Kobliner's book provides some standard advice on personal finance. It is short and structured so as that it can be used as a quick resource. Books like this quickly become obsolete in different ways as tax laws and other plan details change over time, but are good to sit down with especially when young and starting your professional life. 

I perused this one quickly right after completing college, borrowing it from my brother who read it during one of his economics course. 

Some of the basic points:
1) Start building wealth early—time is your biggest asset—the power of compounding interest 
2) Track your spending and align it with your goals—basic budgeting pointers
3) Eliminate high-interest debt aggressively
4) Prioritize financial basics before investing, such as debt reduction, health insurance, etc
5)Save consistently, even if amounts are small6) Use retirement accounts early and fully—401k, Roth IRA, etc
7) Build and protect your credit score
8) Choose financial institutions carefully: FDIC-back banks, minimize fees, prioritize convenience
9) Make smart decisions about major life expenses, e.g. Renting vs Buying,
10) Invest simply and rationally: diversification, index funds, long-term view
11) Be proactive about taxes and benefits
12) Financial discipline &gt; financial complexity

Overall, Kobliner’s core message is that getting control of your finances early via disciplined habits and avoiding costly mistakes will pay dividends. It is basic and safe advice for most middle or upper-middle class individuals.
