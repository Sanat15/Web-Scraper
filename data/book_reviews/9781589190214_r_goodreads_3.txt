Rating: 1/5

It is clean (no swearing, etc.) and I obtained it on my Kindle during a free promotion. Those are the only two good things I can say about this book. The subtitle is “A Joe Box Mystery,” but there is no mystery. Crime, yes, but not mystery. There is a semblance of a decent plot, but the dialogue is hokey and the characters are all one dimensional and the plot often gets sidetracked by stories about the hero’s cat or his “Granny.” There is also a recurring “conflict” with a particular police officer that has no basis or point. The protagonist—the aforementioned Joe Box—is a private detective with thirty years experience, but he has no appreciable skills other than being able to deliver a roundhouse kick to a 20-year-old goofball. His idea of tracking down the bad guy is to sit and wait for him in his office. Literally. Well, he did also go buy a second gun, which the gun store owner ends up giving him for unexplained reasons. But when the final battle occurs our hero decides to only bring one of the two guns. It doesn’t matter, though, because the cartoonish bad guy apparently cannot be beaten by mere bullets. The Christianity of the good guys is overt and prevalent throughout, but it is a charismatic Christianity lacking a clear presentation of the Gospel (despite acceptance of the Gospel being essential to survival in the plot) in favor of a Christianity able to sense the future and part the fire of a burning building. I cannot predict the future, but I do know that good Christian literature exists and this isn’t it.
