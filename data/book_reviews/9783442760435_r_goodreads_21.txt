Reminded me a bit of Angela Carter, but either missing something or diluted. Perhaps as might be expected the title tells us what is absent from the text. However it is an amusing, page turning fantasy adventure. The regular presence of Venice reminded me that I have never read invisible cities, while a heroine called villanelle that I have never watched killing eve . The setting and episodic advetures in the era of Napoleon put me in mind of the film the duel  though there is much less dueling in this.

The book is in four chapters, the second is called "The Queen of Spades", I wondered if it would reference Pushkin's standard European Gothic story of the same name, but it might I suppose, very faintly, and quite possibly accidentally.

In a forward to the book Winterson notes that she wrote it in 1986 as a mirror to the "boom-time of the Thatcher years, click-race of yuppies and city boyd, rich-quick, never count the cost", but in the way of mirrors it can be seen as celebrating the dreams of indulgence and risk of those years, as well as the losses of those who couldn't keep up in the clock race.

Some phrases are repeated; "I'm telling you stories. Trust me.""Passion is between fear and sex" in many variants, mostly 'x is between fear and sex', until I was waiting to read that accountancy is between fear and sex, which, of course, it is.
