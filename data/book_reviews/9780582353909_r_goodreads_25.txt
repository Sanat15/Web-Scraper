Rating: 3/5

A play from the 17th century—the true spirit of the Renaissance era pouring out of its lines.

Marlowe's Dr. Faustus is indeed nothing short of a tragedy. A man of average and unassuming upbringing builds a respectable and admirable image; yet, how could he possibly find contentment in such banality?

It is through a pact with a sweet fallen angel—Mephistopheles—that Faustus would attain forbidden knowledge and power. However, I think we all know how self-serving and grasping demons can be.

Marlowe wrote a script that sustains itself on paper. Even without a visual representation of the play, I felt the soul, intensity, and drama so characteristic of the works produced during this time.

Overall, a wonderful and impactful representation of the spirit and wariness toward the ever-growing humanistic mentality during this period, and the dogmatic, flawed, and human side of the religious institution. 

3.3/5 🪽🌑
