I read this book on vacation. Hass's is a learned, avuncular style. Reading it, I felt like I was in the hands of a man who knew his way around poetry yet didn't reek of academia or condescension (not that those two always travel together... they just use the same agency, as a rule).

I especially enjoyed Hass's memoir-esque essay and the one on images, where he shared his expertise on the haiku form. There's a lot to learn from haiku. Even if you truck in BIG poems as a poet yourself. And especially if you're a bit wordy (ahem).
