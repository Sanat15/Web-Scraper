Rating: 4/5

Joan Smith is one of my favorite traditional regency writers. She is to go to author when I need some light and cozy story. Reading this book, as often with her stories, I felt like watching a play: delightful romp, amusing comedy of manners with laugh out loud moments. 
Location: Wildercliffe and Swann Hall estates in Cotswold country.
Cast of characters:
Lord Fenwick, top of the trees gentleman who came to the country to escape a filly chasing after him.
His friend Mr. Swann who puts foot in mouth more often than not and looks like "an unmade bed" but otherwise amicable gentleman.
Lady Sykes, distant relation to both gentlemen, a scheming widow with wild and priceless imagination.
Lady Pargeter, a former housekeeper turned mistress of Wildercliffe and now a wealthy widow.
Miss Lonsdale, her niece and a former schoolteacher.
Put the characters together in a country setting and you get:
-delightful bantering, flirtation and budding romance between Lord Fenwick and Miss Longsdale
-battle of wills between Lady Pargeter and Lady Skykes where swords are crossed, daggers are drawn
-and much , much more.
Although not my favorite of Joan Smith's regencies, I would definetly recommend it to all JS fans.
