Rating: 3/5

3.5 stars. An interesting, sad novel about William Dublin, a late 50s successful biographer who is currently struggling with his latest book on d. H. Lawrence. Dublin has been married for a number of years to Kitty and has two grown up children. Things become unstable when he has an affair with Fanny, a very young sexually experienced woman in her early 20s. Their affair begins in Venice, Italy. Dublin had thought the affair was over after a falling out at the end of their one week in Venice. However Fanny is a very persistent young woman.

Dublin has a daughter of a similar age to Fanny who is a little lost, but independent. Dubin’s marriage to Kitty, whilst satisfactory for many years, is now in difficulty as Dubin lets his work on the biography of Lawrence overtake his private life. His affair with Fanny affects his relationship with Kitty. He finds himself being very secretive and continually lies to Kitty in order to be with Fanny.

A well written, clever novel that includes comments on a number of famous individuals, including Lawrence, Mark Twain and Thoreau.

This book was first published in 1979.
