Rating: 5/5

The Rooms of Heaven reads like the beginning of a storm when fat raindrops slowly and deliberately freckle the pavement, the rooftops, the hard surfaces of the workaday world. The book is measured out in these substantial raindrops (or teardrops?)--each drop a memory hitting the puddled surface of the writer's consciousness and rippling out to wash up onto the page in little scenes from the writer's life. I loved the organic structure Allen uses to share the story of her love affair with and loss of Beaman. Her memoir explores love, its tragic loss, addiction, co-dependency, insanity, spirituality, and the afterlife. I especially enjoyed the book after taking a class with her last week at the summer writing festival in Iowa City. I hope to read more work from Mary Allen!
