Rating: 4/5

“I know what you are, and you shall not have my daughter.”

After her brother dies, Emily Hotspur is her aging father’s only remaining child. She is the family heiress and her future happiness as well as the Hotspur name and property, which date back generations, depend on her choice between possible marriage partners, one of whom is handsome, popular, but dishonest and a scamp. Everything is on the line and everyone but Emily knows the score. 

“It was natural that this girl of his should learn to love some youth: and how terrible was the danger of her loving amiss, when so much depended on her loving wisely!” 

I have read many of Trollope’s novels, and in most of them I fall in love with the characters. Those beloved characters and the relationships between them normally form the foundation of my engagement with Trollope’s plots. In this short novel, however, I didn’t connect emotionally with any of the characters. I still enjoyed the novel. It was quite good. But the primary engagement device was tension. Rather than wanting to spend time with the characters, I needed answers to questions. What would happen to Emily? What would become of her family? Could her dishonest suitor be redeemed? 

“It may be said of him, both as to his moral and physical position, that he was on the edge of the precipice of degradation, but that there was yet a possibility of salvation.”
