Once upon a time in China there was a poor boy named Chen Ping, who worked for a rich man. Although he did whatever was asked of him, Chen Ping could never satisfy the rich man. One day on his way to cut wood, Chen Ping accidentally dropped his axe from a bridge, and it fell into the river far below. How will he be able to chop wood without his axe?

The best way for a Westerner to understand Chinese folktales is to read a great many of them. One at a time they might seem strange to Western eyes, and the folktale motifs are also unfamiliar. This tale is a classic, and very popular. Think of the concept of yin and yang, and the need to maintain balance in the universe. In Chinese lore, the phoenix and the dragon keep the universe in order, and reason prevails.

Demi's delicate illustrations add a lovely dimension to this old folktale. Each view of the sky shows a beautiful phoenix watching over Chen Ping, and views of the water show drawings of dragons swirling in the splashing waves and rapids. Ages 4-10.
