Rating: 4/5

Camus is the voice of a generation, not by choice or destiny, but by bravery and unwavering belief in liberty and art, both of which are wrapped in the human spirit. 
This work certainly fleshes out Camus character and philosophy more than any other of his writings. I loved the collection of letters to a German friend, published during the occupation of Paris by his own underground anti-Nazi newspaper, proclaiming the doctrines of freedom and responsibility for a unified Europe. I enjoyed the Algerian resistance works, however it did feel like a lack of historical context and understanding limited my reading experience. Reflections on the Guillotine was incredible, touching on the morality of the masses and control of the state, fighting not for criminals but for human beings and the right to life. Live bravely, create dangerously.
