1250pm ~~ With this book I complete my 2022 project of exploring the life and works of Vita Sackville-West. Of course I know there are other titles out there, both by her and about her, but I am satisfied with the eight books I dipped into both at Project Gutenberg and in print. 

I expected to like this particular book more than I ended up doing. The idea was intriguing: to trace Vita's life through the gardens she called her own over the years. Of course we start with her childhood at Knole and end with her glorious year-long project at Sissinghurst. 

In between we read about the gardens in Teheran, at Long Barn, and we also take a little tour to a few of the English gardens VSW visited over the years. And there are some outstanding color plates as well as black and white photos throughout the book.

So why was I a little disappointed with this mostly interesting book? Well, the author tended to gush about both the gardens and the human subject of her book. And there were lists of flowers every little bit, which of course you would expect in a book about gardens intended for readers who are also gardeners. But it gets old, especially if a reader is a bit ignorant of what so many of the different flowers look like. (Not to mention any names, of course, but that would be me. lol) These two factors had me skimming through many of the pages, but overall it was an enjoyable and informative book.

The author relied a great deal on letters between VSW and her husband Harold for her text, and also quoted from VSW's poetry, which was a treat to me since I liked her poetry better than her novels. This really is a thorough work, and I've loaned to my mother, who so enjoyed the book Sissinghurst last year. She is already oohing and aahing over all of those pictures!
