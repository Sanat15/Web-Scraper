In an era where the concept of a moral compass feels unneeded, and even unwanted, I welcomed Ms. Weil’s voice because I wish I had such a compass throughout my journey. This short volume is split between six letters addressed to Father Joseph Perrin and several short essays. Most of her writing dwells on her thoughts for God and what a virtuous life means to her.

Given that her letters were written in 1942, roughly eighteen months before her untimely death, I wondered about her mental health. Further, her comments are devoid of remarks regarding intimate relationships. She does mention a jealousy for her brother’s talent, but that is the extent of commentary for her personal connections. I also thought about Ms. Weil’s perspective; who among us at age thirty-three is capable of such humility and clarity of expression. Unfortunately, our social gravity now exerts an unremitting force toward the exaltation of the flesh rather than the betterment of the soul. Her writing offers an alternative.
