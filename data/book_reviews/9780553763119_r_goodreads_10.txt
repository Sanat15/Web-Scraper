Rating: 4/5

It isn’t often that Inspector Cramer of the New York Police Department shows up at Nero Wolfe’s doorstep asking for a favor. But then Cramer has never encountered until now a case this perplexing. A man named Leonard Dykes, a clerk working at a prominent law firm, has been fished out of the East River. His apartment had been picked through before the police arrived, depriving them of all clues save a list of names that correspond to no known people. With no insights to offer, Wolfe turns Cramer away. Then, a few weeks later, a prospective client shows up asking Wolfe to investigate the recent murder of his daughter. And among the evidence he brings for them is a letter mentioning one of the names on Dykes’s list.

Thus begins one of the most challenging cases in Wolfe’s career. With nothing to connect the two murders but a name and a missing manuscript, Wolfe and his legman partner, Archie Goodwin, begin investigating Dykes, hoping to find more. That each lead proves frustratingly unproductive only adds to the tension in Rex Stout’s nineteenth book, as the resolution of the case only seems to get further away the longer the men investigate. It’s one of Stout’s best yet: a novel by one of the great mystery writers at the peak of his powers, with fantastic set pieces and a seeming resolution that proves to be anything but one. It’s a great example of why the author enjoys the reputation that he does today.
