Rating: 5/5

The foundational novels aside (Mandingo and Drum), this third novel in the Falconhurst series, Master of Falconhurst, takes us into the intricacies and peculiarities of the daily routines and functioning of the slave-breeding Plantation in the mid 1800s, with its power struggles and shockingly wicked occurrences. 

The dynasty created at Falconhurst by the Maxwell family continues to evolve through the eyes and life of the slave Drummage, son of Drumson, depicting the challenges of Plantation life under the overseer Maxwell clan, and Hammond’s daughter Sophie, as well as growing abolitionist sentiment from the Yankee northerners, and what soon is to become the American Civil War. 

Again, this series is compelling, as it plays to the obscene and violent sensibilities of story telling commensurate with this period of history, which make it so engrossing that the reader can’t help but be anxious to know what happens next. A brutal ending serves as the ghastly climax.
