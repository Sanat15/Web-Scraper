Rating: 5/5

I'm finding that with the neglect of African American historical documentation, that sometimes even historians have to resort to the novel to fill in gaps. This book is one case that personalizes the sacrifices of not only the men, but also the women &amp; families of the Buffalo Soldiers. Not only does in reinforce the contradictory contribution of African American Soldiers killing Native Americans to make the West safe for Whites to settle, attacking the fortifications killing Cuban soldiers at San Juan, Cuba, making Teddy Roosevelt's Rough Rider's assault on San Juan Hill successful, but it tells of the sacrifice &amp; traditions of their families who moved from remotest posts all over the West to support their men. Through several generations of one military family, it describes the overlooked contributions of these soldiers. It details the events of 372 Infantry Regiment in the final push to break the back of the German army in the trench warfare on the plains of France in 1918 through the experiences of two African American Lieutenant brothers, veterans of the Spanish American War &amp; second generation Buffalo Soldiers. It also paints a vivid picture of the ingenuity, the loneliness, the community &amp; the grief of women who love these men. It reminds us all that African Americans have paid the price of freedom in blood since the beginning of the republic.
