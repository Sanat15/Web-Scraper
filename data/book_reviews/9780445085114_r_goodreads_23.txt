Rating: 3/5

The Conquering Family is non-fiction that reads like fiction. I know that is often claimed nowadays in publisher’s blurbs to try and reach a larger audience than pure non-fiction aficionados but with The Conquering Family it really is true. Costain takes what is known and fleshes out the description. (“Reginald was a fat little fellow who walked pompously and oozed self-importance from every pore.” p272) George RR Martin claims the author, Thomas B Costain, as one of his inspirations.

Costain covers the history of the Plantagenets, who ruled England from 1154 to 1485, in a series of four books. This is the first and covers the reigns of Henry II, Richard I (aka The Lionheart,) and Bad King John. There was enough scandal and gossip surrounding this family (locked up Queen, kidnapped King, murdered Archbishop, rebellious sons) to compete with a soap opera and that is just from the known history. There are also the myths that have sprung up around them, like that of the Fair Rosamunde and the maze, which Costain relates because they are entertaining but then proceeds to disclose what scholars really believe. Before you think that the book is just about royal shenanigans, Costain also takes the time to describe the lot of the common people, whether they were part of the growing town populations or the majority peasantry who worked the land, he highlights the evolving justice system (an accidental by-product,) and speaks of the dissolving divisions between the Normans and the Saxons to form the English nation.

With the exception of the Magna Charta, Costain quotes sparingly from the source material choosing to relay just the truly bizarre or enlightening; he knows his audience is the layman. This is an older book (1949) and my copy was literally falling apart as I read it but if you come across one in an old book shop or garage sale it is worth picking up. You can learn a lot of history from The Conquering Family but its primary purpose is entertainment.
