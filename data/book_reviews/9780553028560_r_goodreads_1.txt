Rating: 3/5

This is at least the second time I've read this book, as I read it in the early 1980s when I was much younger and not learning to be a writer.

The plot is set in the early days after the Battle of Midway in early June and before Guadalcanal in August 1942. B-25 bombers are taking high losses for minimal results. Along comes Captain Whip Russel - we never learn his first real first name - with a better idea. Put as many .50 cal machine guns as you can in the nose and side of the bomber, then blow away everything in your path - fighters, destroyers, troop barges, etc. 

As expected, there are early successes. But as time progresses, the Japanese adjust. I don't want to spoil anything, but let's just say that things don't go all Whip and his men's way. The final scene was quite a moral dilemma, which I thought was quite interesting for being published in 1976, just after the US Vietnam War had concluded.

I like the description of the setting in the first chapter. It makes you feel like you're there. However, there's parts of the novel where the dialogue is a bit painful due to what I'll call overacting. The relationship between the two main characters is based on a past that is revealed as the book progresses, which was fine. 

The action is great. I've never been a pilot, but I got a sense of what it was like to be a B-25 bomber pilot in WWII. I had the pleasure of having a ride in a B-24 Liberator as a cool anniversary gift a few years back, so that helped give me an even better sense of what the novel is depicting. One that I didn't have when I first read this book a long time ago.

Overall, "Whip" was a good leisure read. I think that there are a lot better war books, particularly some of the ones that were published in the Bantam war non-fiction series in the 1950s-1980s. Mind you most of those are biographies/autobiographies, but that's the stick I'm measuring this novel against. Not a bad read.
