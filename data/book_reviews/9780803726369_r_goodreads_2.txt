Rating: 3/5

Another great YA book from the time before YA supposedly existed. This was Rosemary Wells' first novel, and was based on her own experiences at boarding school. Maybe Wells was still too emotionally close to the material or was still finding her voice, but this book, while powerful and probing at times, is a bit of a mess. I looooooved it when I was 10, don't get me wrong. It had so many great things: boarding school, rebellion, a loner girl, a bad girl, secret journal writing! And as an overly-sensitive girl child from a middle class white family, I related to Rachel enough that it didn't even occur to me that she was unlikable. 

Reading it now, however, I think the ending is jarring, the illustrations belong in a book for younger children, and much of the story could have been developed more fully. And Rachel clearly suffers from panic attacks and I wish that had been addressed. But the sensations and impressions of anxiety are acutely depicted. Issues of honesty vs loyalty and conformity vs individuality are raised but not resolved.

Still, an honest look at coming of age in the 1960s and 70s, and a damn good YA novel, especially considering YA didn't even exist back then.

LOL moment: "Today we had a class instead of gym. They call it hygiene, but it's just the same old sex and drug stuff they give in public schools, which means, of course, Fallopian tubes and marijuana."
