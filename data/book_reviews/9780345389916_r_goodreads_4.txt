Rating: 4/5

I don't know if this one has a wide audience, but I really enjoyed reading it. It was written in 1974, but holds up surprisingly well as an exploration of how women make their way in the world. Not a whole lot happens here. Much of it is the interior life of our protagonist, Jane Clifford, a single, PhD in English, who has an uncertain future with a two year appointment as a visiting professor at an unnamed Midwestern university. She's having a long-distance affair that's going nowhere with a married Art History professor, a decent, mild-mannered fellow. Her grandmother's death precipitates a visit home to the South- a place also not identified, but I'd guess the mountains of North Carolina. This a book that has some memorable scenes in it. One of them is Jane and her mother going through her grandmother's things, talking, drinking and getting tipsy. Another is when Jane ends up in New York City in order to rendezvous with her lover. There's a hilarious scene in which she goes shopping at Saks. The Odd Woman was nominated for the National Book Award. But it seems largely forgotten. It does move slowly, not exactly in tune with the short attention spans of the digital age, but I think it's well worth reading. Godwin is just a marvelous writer- her intelligence and understanding of human nature shine through on every page.
