Rating: 4/5

I'm glad I read this. It's a "thing", and a big one, and part of my education as a reader and writer. I don't expect to say anything new in my reaction to this 2001 masterpiece, so I'll keep it brief.

For me, this epic family drama, famously endorsed by Oprah much to the concern of the author (he worried her sticker would put off male readers), is a mix of brilliance and digression. 

When it's good, which it frequently is, especially when focussed on the Lambert family, it is SO good. When the author digresses into 1) long, long lists, or 2) detailed backgrounds of side characters that only show up once, and fleetingly, or 3) anything at all that happens in the cartoonish Lithuania story, he lost me. In these instances, I found it bloated and overwritten, and felt annoyed. Then he'd reel me in with incredible scenes and conversations and observations and descriptions. It was on-off like that, the whole 600 pages. Sometimes I was bitterly lamenting the Dickensian verbosity, other times I was rapturously marking brilliant passages.

My reading of this novel was like a dysfunctional relationship, now that I think about it, much like the ones featured in this book.

I think, at the end of this journey, I walk away with a great deal of admiration for what Franzen accomplished here. More admiration than annoyance, though I still couldn't care less about what happened in Lithuania.
