Rating: 5/5

An excellent resource, and a must-read for anyone looking to write about the American West from 1840 to 1860. Packed with information from primary sources, covering a myriad of aspects of the overland experience, this tome features dry parts but also fascinating, interesting, and downright funny stories. 

I loved the writing style, especially in the beginning, where Unruh would make a (generalized) statement of fact, following it up with specific examples of overlanders (usually citing diaries or other autobiographical memories of the overlanders themselves). While it may seem a tad repetitive, it successfully serves as wonderful evidence-based writing.

The author includes much information regarding the interaction between overlanders and the Mormon settlements (much eye-opening information, I might add). He is careful and hesitate to condemn the Mormons for their allegedly unjust treatment of overlanders, though he admits to their singular hatred of Illinoisans and Missourians. However, aside from a brief comment such as appropriate kudos to those making a settlement in such inhospitable conditions, I do not remember a section devoted to the Mormon emigration enterprise. It left me wondering whether the majority of Mormons traveled along with all the other '40s overlanders, or they had a special emigration experience left unmentioned by this otherwise exhaustive treatise.

But enough of the criticism: I am extremely happy to have this book as part of my library!
