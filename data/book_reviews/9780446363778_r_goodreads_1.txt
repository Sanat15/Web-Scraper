Rating: 3/5

My rock journey continues with a book that’s been voted the top rock and roll read on Goodreads. I’m stepping out of the chaotic and mystical realm of Led Zeppelin and diving into the even more chaotic universe of Jim Morrison, also known as Mr. Mojo Risin’ and The Lizard King.

  

First of all, I have to mention that Angels Dance and Angels Die: The Tragic Romance of Pamela and Jim Morrison still reigns supreme. It's worth noting that Jerry Hopkins, the author of No One Here Gets Out Alive, wrote a foreword in Angels Dance and Angels Die where he expressed regret for not adequately highlighting Pamela Courson, who was Jim Morrison's long-term girlfriend and 'cosmic mate.' Honestly, I wasn't shocked because it's pretty well-known that the wives and girlfriends of rock stars often get overlooked and sometimes even disliked (justice for Yoko). At least he’s being honest about it.

I'm doing my best to keep this review from turning into a tribute to Pamela Courson. To quote Taylor Swift, this is me trying. 

I first learned about Jim Morrison and The Doors through Oliver Stone's film 'The Doors,' featuring Val Kilmer and Meg Ryan. Older classic rock fans can be pretty annoyed when rock biopics attract younger audiences. They often feel that if someone watches a movie first and then becomes a fan, it somehow makes them a "fake" fan. We saw this with Bohemian Rhapsody, which brought in a ton of new Queen fans, but many of the old-school fans dismissed them as not being real fans. This kind of gatekeeping is part of why rock has taken a backseat to pop and rap these days, but that's a whole different topic to dive into.

Lana Del Rey, my favorite female artist, has a line in one of her songs that says,  "No one's gonna take my soul away; I'm living like Jim Morrison."  I love you Lana, you're my problematic queen, but I don't want to live like Jim Morrison. I want to live like Keanu Reeves.

Danny Sugerman, who co-wrote this book, has an intense fascination with Jim Morrison that I found unsettling. He even refers to Morrison as a god. 

These are the kinds of sentences I've been subjected to:

  My personal belief is that Jim Morrison was a god. To some of you, that may sound extravagant; to others, at least eccentric. Of course, Morrison insisted we were all gods and our destiny was of our own making. I just wanted to say I think Jim Morrison was a modern god. Oh hell, at least a lord.   

It’s strange how many dudes idolize Morrison and treat him like a divine figure. Sure, he was hot and talented, but he was just a human being. I was a huge Backstreet Boys fan as a kid, but my enthusiasm was nowhere near as extreme as Sugerman’s, and he’s a grown man. 

Dear  Slim  Jim, I wrote you, but you still ain't callin' I left my cell, my pager, and my home phone at the bottom.

  

Sorry, I had to. 

If I were to magically travel back to the 60s and run into Jim Morrison, I wouldn't turn him down. But let's take a step back and have some self-respect. There's no need to be so thirsty, Danny. 

I won't discuss Jim's early life and rise to fame since you can easily find that on Google. Instead, I want this review to focus on his relationships and inner demons. 

Sober Jim was known for his intelligence, talent, and gentlemanly demeanor. Sadly, he didn't stay sober very often. When he drank, Jim transformed into a wild beast, losing all control. In his drunken state, he morphed into a completely different person, a madman who danced with danger. He made history as the first rock musician to be arrested while performing on stage. He was one of the most iconic rock stars of the 60s, alongside Jimi Hendrix and Janis Joplin. All three would meet a tragic fate, dying at the same age within months of each other. But more on that later. 

Jim Morrison had a wild romantic life, hooking up with countless women, including Linda McCartney before she got with Paul (watch The Linda McCartney Story, it's so good). But the one woman he always returned to was Pamela. They had a deep connection and saw each other as soulmates, but their relationship was anything but stable. Jim struggled with fidelity, and Pamela had her share of flings too. At one point, she was so fed up with Jim that she jetted off to Paris and had a fling with a rich French count. When in Paris. 

  

  

  

I found the women's narratives in the book more captivating than those of Jim and the band. In The Doors film, Pamela Courson has a dramatic confrontation with Jim and his journalist mistress, Patricia Kennealy. In reality, Patricia visited Pamela, and they spent hours getting high, drinking wine, and talking. During their conversation, Patricia reveals that she had an abortion of her and Jim's child, to which Pamela responds that she should have gone through with the pregnancy, despite Jim being unreliable and unlikely to provide any support. This comment upset Patricia, who insisted that abortion was her best and only choice. That's what I call trauma bonding. Jim Morrison must have had some incredible skills in the bedroom, as it's hard to fathom why these women would tolerate him otherwise.

That same day, Jim and a few buddies swing by Pamela's house, and they kick back with some card games - just a typical hangout with your main girl and side chick. Eventually, Patricia and Jim doze off in the living room, and when Pamela discovers them, she gets upset. They assure her nothing happened (at least not that time). Pamela is still feeling high and a bit upset, and a friend suggests that she should take some vitamin pills, drink some orange juice, and get some rest. 

THIS WOMAN HAS BEEN STUCK IN A TOXIC RELATIONSHIP FOR YEARS, AND SHE JUST DISCOVERED HER MAN SLEEPING WITH ANOTHER WOMAN. AND YOU SUGGEST SHE JUST TAKE SOME VITAMIN PILLS AND DRINK ORANGE JUICE? 

Jim wraps his arm around Pamela and comforts her, saying, "It's all in the family.' Did he suggest that Pamela and Patricia are now sister wives? THE AUDACITY OF THIS MAN. 

  

I went through a phase where I was really into The Doors and Jim Morrison after diving into classic rock. While I don’t listen to them as much now, I still appreciate their talent and impact on music. It’s important to note that Jim Morrison had a dark side, which many fans tend to overlook. He treated women like crap. However, he also had a sensitive and artistic side that he didn't reveal often. 

Jim became a member of the 27 Club on July 3, 1971. For those unfamiliar, the 27 Club is a term used to describe a group of iconic musicians who tragically passed away at the age of 27, including legends like Jimi Hendrix, Janis Joplin, Jim Morrison, Brian Jones, Kurt Cobain, and Amy Winehouse, among others. The original trio of this club includes Jimi, Janis, and Jim, who died within a few months of one another. Jim's final moments were in Paris, where he was laid to rest in the renowned Cimetière du Père-Lachaise, attended by only Pamela and a handful of others. Many renowned figures, including Edith Piaf, Oscar Wilde, Balzac, Bizet, and Chopin, have found their final resting place in that cemetery.

There are numerous conspiracy theories regarding Jim's death and his secret burial, but I won't delve into those. I hate conspiracy theories, especially the tendency for people to claim that celebrities either fake their deaths or are murdered whenever they pass away. Celebrities are human beings, just like everyone else, and they can face health issues and unfortunate events. I’m puzzled as to why the authors of this book chose to suggest that Morrison might have faked his death. Anyone who believes he lived long enough to celebrate his 28th birthday, given his heavy drinking, is simply not seeing reality. Jim left everything to Pamela, but tragically, she passed away from an overdose just three years later, also at the age of 27.

 "To some, Jim was a poet, his soul trapped between heaven and hell. To others, he was just another rock star who crashed and burned. But this much is true. You can't burn out if you're not on fire."  - Johnny Depp, from When You're Strange (2009) documentary
