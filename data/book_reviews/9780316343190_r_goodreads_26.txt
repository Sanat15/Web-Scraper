Rating: 5/5

This might not be everyone's cup of tea but I adore this book. It's about Helene's younger years as a playwright, nearly starving in New York, the people, the ratty apartments, the thrill of the stage, and finally finding her niche. 

I gloried in all the little details of life back then-- the life hacks, especially. My personal favorite was how to get a handkerchief look as if it had been ironed when you have no iron, and at this time, ironed clothing was a must look for respectable people, especially if one was going on job interviews for any sort of job.

Helene (yes, I'm calling her by her first name) has the knack of making you feel she's letting you into her life, telling you all her secrets as if you were a trusted friend. Only later do you realize that she's omitted most of the really personal details. 

But that's okay. She's a storyteller, keeping me both spellbound and sputtering with laughter. Even after death she is entitled to her privacy.
