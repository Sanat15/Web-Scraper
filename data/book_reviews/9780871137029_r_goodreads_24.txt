Rating: 3/5

This book was really interesting until it just kept going. I got bored a little over 2/3rds of the way through :P

"He has chosen death:

W. B. Yeats, The King's Threshold

The tale is of the IRA and the conflicts of British rule over Northern Ireland. I picked it up from a hostel book exchange because I always love to learn about places I'm visiting (or nearby) and am certainly glad I picked this one up, even if I didn't feel the need to finish it (I read a summary of how it ended and the aftermath). The biggest problem was that it's just the same 3 plot points repeated with different people over and over...

However, I found the fight for freedom and the sacrificial nature of the young men rather striking. Hunger strikes are something I had never heard of before. The main issue was that these "terrorists," as labeled by the British Government for their murderous and destructive crimes, demanded recognition as political prisoners. However, as many people know, Margaret Thatcher was not one to compromise, and ultimately never did. It resulted in 10 men sacrificing their lives through the 1981 prison hunger strike and virtually no change in the Northern Ireland push for independence. It did spark a nationalist revival in the country but that has still never produced much.
