Patricia Highsmith's novels all seem to have that one common happy thread running through them - psychological tension. And how well, or differently, she accomplishes it seems to vary. The Price of Salt, or Carol, is the fourth I've read by the author and I've given 4 different ratings to each.
Young Therese Belivet, enduring a dreary dead end job at a large department store has her world rocked when a beautiful older woman, Carol, catches her eye in the toy department. Therese becomes obsessed with Carol, to such a high degree we start to wonder about her sanity:
'If Carol had to go home now, Therese thought, she would do something violent...'
Highsmith builds on that tension as the relationship develops - young Therese's insecurities and neuroses are manifested- she lives to please Carol, who we aren't quite sure about, until later, on a cross country road trip when the story picks up steam. The book was published in 1952 under a pseudonym and for yesterday's reader, this story probably was quite risqué and bold, but by today's standards it feels worn and familiar. For me it has the feel of an historical abstract, a study of sexuality, sexual identity and societal attitudes in the 1950's.
That's not to take anything away from Highsmith's wonderful lyrical prose, rich with symbolism, which i thought was a contrast from her more usual style-sparse, clean , cut to the bone.
I hear they made this into a pretty good movie with Cate Blanchett which I'm not quite curious enough to check out.
