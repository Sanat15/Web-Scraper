Rating: 5/5

Michael J. Fox’s Lucky Man is an extraordinary memoir that blends humor, honesty, and resilience in a way few autobiographies manage. From his early days chasing acting roles to the heights of fame with Family Ties and Back to the Future, Fox paints a vivid picture of the highs and lows of Hollywood life. What makes this book truly remarkable, though, is the raw and courageous way he shares his journey with Parkinson’s disease. 

Fox doesn’t sugarcoat the challenges, he describes the fear, denial, and eventual acceptance with a candor that is both heartbreaking and inspiring. Yet, throughout the narrative, his trademark wit shines through, reminding readers that even in the darkest times, laughter and perspective can be powerful tools. His reflections on family, gratitude, and the meaning of success go far beyond celebrity memoir territory; they feel deeply personal and universally relatable. 

Reading Lucky Man feels less like flipping through a celebrity’s life story and more like sitting down with a friend who’s willing to share the lessons they’ve learned the hard way. It’s a book about perseverance, humility, and finding joy in unexpected places. Michael J. Fox proves that being a “lucky man” isn’t about avoiding hardship, it’s about embracing life fully, even when it doesn’t go according to plan. 

An unforgettable, uplifting read that leaves you with admiration not just for the actor, but for the human being behind the roles.
