Rating: 1/5

I have to agree with the other reviewers - this was a hot mess *and* it was boring. 

It started off so well with the architect hero looking at the gorgeous interior designer heroine with contempt. Seems she left her home at 17 when her father married her stepmother. Stepmother accused heroine of pushing her down the stairs, causing her to miscarry. Heroine's father sided with stepmother (who is also the hero's cousin) and heroine left to live with her maternal grandmother. The hero thinks she's a hard-hearted career woman who coldly ruined her stepmother's only chance of conceiving. The hero has tracked her down because her father has had a stroke and she should be reconciled with him.

That's the most interesting part of the book:
 
Heroine's father regrets what happened with his daughter and they are reconciled quickly. Stepmom is a psycho, but she's never around so all that tension drains away. Hero seeks out heroine and takes her to new houses he's having built. He gives her a commission to decorate one. There's a wannabe OW who is also an interior decorator and she teams up with stepmom to make heroine look bad in the eyes of the hero, but that never goes anywhere. Then cousins from England show up so the family estate can be lived in by someone other than the stepmom. 

Heroine's father dies.Step mom goes away after not inheriting anything. Heroine's sad, but a wannabe OM takes her out and tries to cheer her. Hero is having none of that. He takes heroine away in the middle of her date to see a house he designed. I love yous. HEA 

I'm skimmed a lot of the interior decorating and architecture descriptions. We have everyone's point of view - the hero, heroine, nurse, stepmom, OM, the English cousins - for no discernible reason. This was an early story of Margaret Way's and it shows.
