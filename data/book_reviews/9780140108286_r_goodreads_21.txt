Rating: 3/5

A friend recommended this book when we were talking about easy and cosy reads. That same week I found a whole bunch of Mary Wesley’s at an op shop for cheap. Mary Wesley didn’t write any novels until she was in her late 50’s and this was her most popular and well known one. 

The Vacillations of Poppy Carew is about Poppy at a crossroads in her life. The reader is introduced to pretty, flighty, strange Poppy at the time of her father’s death and the end of a long abusive relationship. She learns more about her dad as the story goes on and the reader learns more about Poppy and her ex. 

The side characters make the story shine, Poppy becomes friends with the undertaker and his cousin, they’re loosely connected to some old friends of her dad and then they’re connected to a pig farmer that falls in love with Poppy. It’s convoluted and chaotic and fun. It’s also quite dark, some of the characters are despicable and some are very callous and flippant. 

Wesley has an odd writing style, she’s so impatient and short, quite cold. Her sentences are staccato and sharp and she never lingers long in a scene. Her characters have some bizarre world views and make really racist/sexist/ weird remarks. There’s a stormy and disturbing trip to North Africa. Somehow though ,the overall tone of the book is like a Richard Curtis film, very English and silly and vain and witty. 

I’m intrigued to try some of Mary Wesley’s other novels but I’m unsure which one.
