Rating: 4/5

This was a fascinating book. The author was the grandson of the richest man in the world, spent a half century in the center of global finance and geopolitics and ended up with no achievements to point to. 

In light of his unbelievably privilege upbringing, it is remarkable that David R turned out as well as he did. He comes across as intelligent and remarkably conscientious. No chore is turned down for 80 years unless he thinks his name will prevent its completion. Unlike his brother Nelson (I reviewed his biography too), David appears to be motivated mostly by the desire to make the world better rather than become its all-powerful ruler.

His method of doing that appears to be jetting around to talk to work leaders and forming think tanks or boards of rich people. There are very few instances of him actually doing the work of carrying out a project or making sure that all the details have been taken care of. As a result, almost all of his grand projects didn't work. Chase Manhattan Bank (he was CEO in the 1970s) was a shambles of an organization when I came into banking shortly after his retirement. The collapse of the IT systems in the early 1970s was completely his fault and the area had reported to him before he became CEO.

So what did he achieve? In the private sector, his ability to pull together rich people enabled the construction of several major buildings. In foreign affairs, he went everywhere and talked to almost everyone. (And clearly had a ridiculously high security clearance). From my perspective, there's no one achievement one can point to - but we won the Cold War and the continuity of David Rockefeller on his Chase plane showing up in some remote capital year after year must have been an ingredient in the victory of the United States.
