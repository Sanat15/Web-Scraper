Rating: 1/5

The Animal Hour, Andrew Klavan [Pocket Books, 1993].

The Animal Hour is a thriller with two main parallel (and converging) plot lines. On Halloween morning, a recent college graduate, Nancy Kincaid, shows up at her workplace, in the office of a high powered NYC attorney who seems destined for the governor’s office. Kincaid is not recognized by her co-workers and is sent away by an office manager which triggers an identity crisis. She discovers a handgun in her purse, and a voice in her head urging her to “kill him.” After shooting a panhandler who was harassing her in the park, Kincaid is chased by the police, ditches the weapon and is captured and placed in Bellevue. Next, she escapes after killing a doctor and taking a nurse hostage. She retrieves her firearm and goes on the run. Meanwhile, in Greenwich Village, a poet and his brother are suspects in the murder of the real Nancy Kincaid. The characters finally violently converge against the backdrop of a Halloween parade. The premise of the book is intriguing enough, but the execution is flawed and the ending barely explains what was even going on. 

***
Andrew Klavan is a prolific writer of thrillers. He is the author of True Crime, adapted as a film by Clint Eastwood and Don’t Say a Word the basis of a movie with Michael Douglas.
