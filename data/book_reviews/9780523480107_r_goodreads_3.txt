Rating: 1/5

Was really charmed to stumble across such an obscure novel that found new life as a Tor ebook. Whoever Jan Jennings is (I suspect they are a man but I can't know for sure), this is the only novel they ever had published.

Which is probably because it's so goddamn boring.

There are FAR too many characters in this for a book this short, and none of them are distinguishable beyond surface-level gimmicks. Twin vampires that have incest sex! TWO different vampire priests! A vampire hunter that's really fat! A couple weird ethnic stereotypes! They appear in almost every chapter and disappear just as quickly, but you're clearly supposed to remember all of them by name.

The protagonists correspond about plot points that almost always happen off-page, speak vaguely about threatening people that are doing threatening things far away, and drift aimlessly around the world to crash at their various mansions and conspire about... stuff. There is no buildup of tension or sense of momentum. The closest thing the book has to big villains aren't introduced in earnest until more than three-quarters of the way through the book and one of them is dispatched, like so much else, off-page.

Vampyr feels like a sketch or outline of a much thicker and more detailed novel that the author couldn't be bothered to give any weight or texture to, which is just as well because if it were any longer I might not have finished it.

If I have anything nice to say about this it's that the writing style isn't terrible, and the ending had some genuine pathos, even if it felt a little mean-spirited.
