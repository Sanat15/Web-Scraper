Rating: 5/5

Bill Music is the hero of this depression-era tale set in the south. When I am reading the beautiful (and, I swear, *perfect* is not over-stating it) prose I wonder why this isn't as widely read as The Grapes of Wrath (which I hated...) Granted, it doesn't narrate the change our country saw that Steinbeck iconically depicted, and focuses instead on a couple of men in one place at one time, but the rhythm, characters and writing here are fantastic and you will find yourself thinking in the vernacular of the characters..."I was fixin' to...", etc. 
And darned if you can't almost hear the soundtrack from O Brother Where Art Thou playing in your head while you read.
