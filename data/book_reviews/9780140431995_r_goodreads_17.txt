Rating: 3/5

simultaneously progressive of its time in the name of feminism but also ridiculously backwards by contemporary standards, mary wollstonecraft’s a vindication of the rights of woman is a philosophical look into the feminism of a radical of her time. understandably divisive, i found myself both agreeing with and disagreeing with many of her points, but, despite this, thoroughly understand how key this novel was and still is when debating the ideas surrounding equality for women. reading it with two mindsets: what was believed at the time of the publication date and what is believed now, centuries in the future, is the answer to reading the mind of wollestonecraft, for you will find a range of her points resonate, and others leave you startled at how they were ever commonplace amongst the early feminists. undoubtedly a good read.
