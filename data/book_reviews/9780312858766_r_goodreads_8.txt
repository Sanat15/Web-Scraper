Rating: 3/5

Set on the world of Persephone, Dreaming Metal is a quintessential cyberpunk tale exploring the blurred lines between high-level "cool" hardware and the emergence of true artificial intelligence. It follows two primary threads: a pair of underground performers utilizing advanced robotics and a young programmer caught in the political and social crossfire of a world divided by class and technology.

There is no denying that Scott is a gifted prose stylist. Her writing is dense and rhythmic, capturing the neon-slicked aesthetic that fans of the genre crave. But for me, the novel suffers from a classic cyberpunk pitfall: The Jargon Trap, something my own editors real me back from. While the book draws immediate comparisons to William Gibson’s Neuromancer, it also inherits that seminal work's "drowsy" pacing. Scott leans heavily into technical vernacular and world-specific slang without providing the necessary "paint" to visualize the concepts. When jargon is used to add flavor, it works; when it replaces world-building, the reader is left adrift. Halfway through the book, the technical density begins to feel like a substitute for narrative momentum, making the act of reading feel more like decoding than enjoying a story.
