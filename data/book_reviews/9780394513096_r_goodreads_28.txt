Rating: 5/5

By far the most academic and scholarly hoops book I’ve read. Halberstam was a sage. 

It was a blast following the Portland Trailblazers through the 1980 season. It catches the NBA in a fascinating time - just as the old league was giving way to the new, with a young Earvin Johnson and Larry Bird taking the torch. When games were still shown only on the weekends and tape delay (after karate tournaments and chess championships). When cocaine was taken as freely as players drink Gatorade on the sidelines now (before they knew it was a highly destructive drug). Just makes you wonder how much better these guys could have been and how much longer they could have played if it weren’t for the drugs. 

My main take away was just how grievous screw up it was giving an up and coming Moses Malone away for virtually a bag of Skittles. What were they thinking? Had the team been healthy and they retained him, the Blazers seriously could have had Bill Walton, Maurice Lucas and Moses Malone COMING OFF THE BENCH. Then the Jordan debacle. Then oden. Then Brandon Roy. Ugh. Poor blazers fans.
