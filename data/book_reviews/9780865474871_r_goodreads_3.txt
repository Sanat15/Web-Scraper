Rating: 5/5

I remember first reading this when I was in community college years ago, and underlining many passages, memorizing quotes, and finding it to be a very challenging, encouraging, and fascinating read. At that time I was struggling to find my voice as a writer, and this book was one that really helped me find my voice, as well as books by other writers such as C.S.Lewis, George MacDonald, Robert Frost, John Steinbeck, and Jane Austen.
Re-reading it again for the third time reminded me of why I love writing, that good writing is very, very, hard work, and that a writer must write every day, whether it is a thousand words, or one little paragraph written after a very tiring day of work when you just want to crawl in bed and call it a night.
Some of my favorite moments in the book is when L'Engle quotes George MacDonald, the struggle and joy she experienced when writing her iconic book A Wrinkle In Time, her belief that there is no sacred or secular either/ or, and that whatever the artist worldview, their belief will come out in their work. I also enjoyed the many references to great writers, composers, painters, and other artists that L'Engle found to be of great help as she was pursuing her artistic vocation.
L'Engle gives a very valid point about not comparing your own unique gift and voice to the great artists that have come before, but to build off of them, find your own voice, perfect your craft, and to surrender to the work itself. When working on different creative projects, the transcendent experiences I have had, has filled me with wonder and great joy.
I once met a lady who knew L'Engle, and she spoke very highly of her. I am glad that she wrote this book. This is my favorite quote from it, “The discipline of creation, be it to paint, compose, write, is an effort towards wholeness.”
