Rating: 4/5

really interesting book from frank herbert about the intersectionality of dystopia, imperialism, and religion. the philosophy and ideas presented here feel more like a supplemental piece to dune, building on some of the religious themes there but the ideas maybe being too lofty to be contained within this relatively short novel. that aside, its still a very interesting and engaging story. further shows herberts interest in anthropological studies of extra terrestial life and how it intersects with religion and the imperial complex. there’s a lot of good insights into anti war and anti imperialism ideas, though they definitely are supplemented by me already knowing some of herberts ideas on the subjects from dune. the last quarter of the book is a bit messier and different from the rest, but that is just cause its the loftiest part of the ideas. lots of interesting stuff here, one to return to at some time.
