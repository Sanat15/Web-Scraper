Rating: 5/5

Love is the music that persuades us to dance in another's arms. To some it is as long as symphonies full of violins. To others it is a quick drum beat made with bare palms. Two whistled notes might make one person jump and whirl like a top. For someone else, love is a more intricate tune. This book is testimony to that.

D. H. Lawrence lets us into the most private moments which apart from death anyone can have. He makes us look into that hut in the woods where these lovers lie. And in the mud, too. Oh, but the flowers! There are so many and such different kinds. 

It's not an easy read. Some passages, I'll admit, took me several tries. But it is worth the effort. Ours as the readers. His as a writer. Lawrence claims he wanted to call it "Tenderness." That surprised me at first. Then, I thought it's true. "To me," Lawrence has said, "it is beautiful and tender and frail as the naked self is." He didn't mention any music. That's for us to imagine.
