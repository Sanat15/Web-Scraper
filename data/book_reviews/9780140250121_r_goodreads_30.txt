Rating: 3/5

Unfortunately I think this turned out to be not the classic that one expected it to become at the time. (In a league with Wodehouse and Conan Doyle.) It is nice reading and funny, but not hilariously. The quotes from Keats and Shelley and Wordworh (the sheep of the Lake District) are fun but not that exciting and a bit forced. And referring to ones wife as She Who Must Be Obeyed was probably not a bright idea in the seventies, now it definitely seems to be a bad move.

I liked the story about the rape best. Rumpole represents the alleged perpetrator and the way he cross-examines the victim is rough, and his daughter in law does not like it.
