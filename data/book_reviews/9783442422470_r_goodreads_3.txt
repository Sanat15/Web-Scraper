"Museum," he said. "Explain this word museum. What is the museum, a tomb?"

Anne Rice remains an underrated author, especially when it comes to historical fiction. The reason is that she wrote about vampires, witches, and mummies, but I've rarely read another author capable of transporting you to another world, making you notice the subtleties of a particular place or era, and convincing you that you're truly meeting historical figures.

The Mummy does all of this, with the added advantage of being set in one of the richest and most fascinating settings imaginable: Egypt. Featuring characters such as Ramses the Great and Cleopatra, it's as close to perfection as the genre gets.
