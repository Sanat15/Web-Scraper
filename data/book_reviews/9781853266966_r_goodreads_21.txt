Rating: 3/5

Catton is a wonderful storyteller and a careful historian. But I can't seem to get past the part at the beginning where he criticizes politicians who were unable to come to a reasonable compromise. Pardon me, but what reasonable compromise can be made in the case of slavery? Free half the people and carry on enslaving the other half? Let every slave have every other month free? Maybe continue to enslave half of all black Americans and begin to enslave half of all white Americans? 
I bet we could save a lot of law enforcement and judicial expense if we just worked out a reasonable compromise with serial killers, too. They stick to a reasonable number of senseless murders and we don't interfere unless they go over, say, one a month, for example. Or, even better, they only kill below a certain latitude. 
I hadn't recovered even a little from the compromise criticism when I found Catton mildly disapproving of the brutal attack on Sumner on the Senate floor. Clearly a more reasonable solution, such as arming BOTH men with sturdy canes, should have been at least considered. 
It's terrifying to think that we undoubtedly have our own gaping blindspots, just like Catton. I can only hope that we are, indeed, progressing. But I can't help fearing that we only keep exchanging one for another throughout the generations.
