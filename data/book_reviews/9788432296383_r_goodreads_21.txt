The Dante Club takes a fascinating idea and builds a literary murder mystery around it. In post-Civil War Boston, a series of gruesome murders begins mirroring the punishments described in Dante's Inferno. The only people who recognize the pattern are members of the Dante Club, including real historical figures such as Henry Wadsworth Longfellow, Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr., and James Russell Lowell. As the body count rises, they race to identify the killer before Dante's growing reputation in America is destroyed along with more lives.

The murders are elaborate, unsettling, and cleverly tied to Inferno. I enjoyed watching the clues unfold and piecing together how the victims connected to the controversy surrounding Dante's translation. Dan Teal proved to be the most interesting character in the book. His Civil War trauma, obsession with Dante, and distorted sense of justice created a villain who felt more complex than a standard serial killer.

My biggest struggle was keeping track of everyone. The cast is enormous and many characters occupy overlapping roles within Harvard, the Dante Club, publishing circles, and the investigation. I spent a good portion of the audiobook trying to remember who people were and why they mattered. At several points I wished I had a flowchart sitting beside me.

The literary side of the novel also created some distance for me. Readers with a strong background in Dante, nineteenth-century poetry, and literary history will likely get much more out of this story. I often felt like I was missing important context and references. I frequently felt like I was struggling to catch up instead of feeling immersed in the intellectual discussions.

The audiobook narration made that problem worse. John Seidman does not differentiate the voices clearly enough for a cast this large. His character voices often blended together and his accent choices sometimes felt distracting rather than helpful. I regularly found myself rewinding conversations because I was unsure who was speaking. The audiobook would have benefited from stronger vocal distinction or a full cast production with so many historical figures and suspects moving through the story.

I can appreciate the ambition behind The Dante Club. A historical mystery built around Dante's Inferno is a fantastic idea. The murder investigation kept me engaged until the end. The combination of dense literary references, a crowded cast, and challenging narration prevented me from connecting with the characters as much as I wanted. Readers who love literary history and classic literature will likely find much more to enjoy here than I did.
