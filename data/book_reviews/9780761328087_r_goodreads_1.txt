Rating: 2/5

I'm very confused about the intended audience for this book; the writing style cannot possibly be meant for anyone with an intelligence greater than that of a common houseplant (approximately fifth grade and lower) but the author does not shy away from discussing rape and bestiality. [return]Also, the author seems to be one of the new breed of male-hating ultra feminists. [return]Aside from that, the mother-daughter bonding subject is nauseatingly thrown to the fore-front of the stories with all the subtlety of a Hallmark card. I love the idea of there being more fiction books emphasizing the importance of mother-daughter relationships, but mentioning the "circle of mother-daughter unity" every other paragraph is annoyingly Disney-esque. I almost expected Leda to start singing "we are one you and I/We are like the Earth and sky/One family, under the sun" (Lion King 2).[return]One final nitpick, in Leda's story, she states that Zeus lay with her as a quail, but in Persephone's story (as well as in commonly accepted mythological cannon), it's stated that Zeus came to Leda as a swan.
