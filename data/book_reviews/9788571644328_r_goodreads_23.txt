Rating: 4/5

A fine line between living and wasting life 
Book review: Cem dias entre céu e mar, by Amyr Klink
By Fernando Torres Carrera. April 8, 2018.

A brazilian young man engages into a personal goal: be the first person to cross the Atlantic Ocean on a tiny rowing boat. Acknowledging the wilderness of the former and the fragility of the latter, people close to him consider his entrepreneur as madness. Death is the most probable outcome expected from a six thousand kilometer and a hundred days navigation from Luderitz, in Namibia, to Praia da Espera, a Bahia’s beach in Brazil in 1984, when neither GPS or advanced communication technologies were available.
The assumption of danger fades aways little by little as Amyr depics the history surrounded by interesting coincidences and curiosities. Alternatively, the author turns the reader’s idea upside-down, from a no-go adventure to a enriching experience. Everything, including the selection of the boat, the nutritional plan, and the ideal route, makes part of a convincing scheme to explore the most what life has to offer. The hard work for accomplishing the travel pays off every time Amyr come across a natural wonder, like a huge shining whale, or come up with an insight about his feelings and inner motivations.
	Love can be felt every time he vividly describes nature surrounding him. Although some excerpt may be heavy-going explaining navigation techniques, it’s impressive how Amyr Klink’ narrative is delightful and inspiring. His book gives evidences that human being could be happier just by being simpler and choosing the right values to live with.
