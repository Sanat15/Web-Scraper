3.5/5 stars. Before coming to Morocco I heard that Paul Bowles was the quintessential author on all things Morocco, so I set out to read The Sheltering Sky, his best known work. After a somewhat disappointing read based on my expectations, it took me until now to give Bowles another shot with The Spider’s House.

The Spider’s House used two narrators: an American author who lived and wrote in Fez, and an illiterate teenage boy trying to find his way in the world. The drastic difference in their worldview expressed in front of the same backdrop of 1950s Fez was a great way for Bowles’ to express ideas about Moroccans, ex-pats, colonizers, and the way people view each other. I found myself questioning my own relationship with Moroccans at times, while also nodding along in agreement when Bowles seemed to hit the nail on the head.

I particularly enjoyed the way Bowles painted a picture of 1950s Fez. The way of life and the belief systems present jumped off the page and took me to an era of Morocco that is far from the one I know. Overall a solid read and one that will probably push me to read either another novel of Bowles’ or potentially a collection of essays or short stories.
