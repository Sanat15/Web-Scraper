Rating: 3/5

I read this book prior to an upcoming trip to Vietnam to get a better perspective of its more recent history. It takes you through the onset of French colonialism in the 1800’s through the war with the French and what is termed “The American War”, up to the 1990s and the loosening of communist doctrine and the beginning of a more open country with a freer financial system. The family depicted are of the Mandarin class; so from a privileged background, but the family members were on both sides of the conflict with some joining the Viet Mhin and others remaining loyal to first the French and then the Americans. The author at one point while the Vietnamese war was still ongoing job was to interview Viet Cong Viet Mhin who had been captured to help the Americans better understand why the north maintained a more loyal and motivated base than the south. This is the only part of the book that delves into how the peasantry viewed thier existence. The part of the book which deals with end of the war, and the collapse of Saigon was excellent. What comes out in spades is the culture of corruption, particularity in South Vietnam. It was very much worth the read, but I wish it had more of the peasants perspective.
