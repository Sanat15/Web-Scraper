Rating: 5/5

This is one of the few books I've ever read which I could truly describe as a "suspense novel." Usually, when people tell me a novel is suspenseful, what they really mean is that it is a thriller, a chiller, an action tale, etc. For example, the well-worn image of a burning fuse causes tension and apprehension...but suspense? Not unless you don't know how long the fuse is. This novel is suspenseful in the first section because of what you do not know in 1952; the second section of the novel, set in 1925, is suspenseful because of the shattering future revealed to you in the first section; the final portion of the novel, set in 1965, ramps the suspense up because of both what you know and what has remained hidden, so that the climax is as devastating to the reader as it is to the young girl who finally understands the enormity of sin she witnessed thirteen years earlier...and the part she played in it. Although Mrs Disney has at times been criticized for introducing plot elements that reveal a mystery prematurely or interrupt a mounting sense of dread, in this book she delivers an almost flawless performance.
