Rating: 3/5

I was a big fan of these books when the first one came out. I loved the cover image and the story that unfolded. The third books bothered me a same way Erik Von Daniken did. The scientific possibilities started to give way to conspiracy rheories about alien political manipulations that were true. This book built upon that, taking readers to a whole new, yet mostly human world. I finished the first three books in the 80s, by the way.

It was a refreshing surprise to see that there were two more books…and this one delved into AI. It was a remarkable contrast to look at the well-controlled and imagined AI of 1990 (the year I presume he wrote this novel.) There we’re some interesting issues posed then dropped for the sake of plot. It took a bloody long time to get the story going too. The ending has surprising action and philosophy that I can appreciate. I do feel as if it requires the humans to set up the deus ex machina finale, and that was underwhelming. 

It appears that I have the fifth book on the horizon. We’ll see how his continuing saga holds up roughly 40 years later.
