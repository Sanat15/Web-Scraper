Rating: 3/5

A somewhat baffling novel in which a precocious, sociopathic eight-year-old boy with mother issues falls into a downward spiral after his mom stops moving them around and attempts to settle down in one place with one man (i.e., Pedro). Phillip is having none of this, to the eventual severe detriment of Pedro. Later, Mom tries again to root down, this time without a man, until lo and behold, Phillip's father reenters the picture. Phillip is conflicted about his father's sudden presence in his life and consoles himself with various intoxicating substances, on his own and in the company of two reprobates a few year his senior, both of whom (like Phillip) speak in a manner not normally employed amongst their age set. The situation with Mom (now pregnant) and Dad inevitably comes to a head, with 'Pedro' egging Phillip on in his quest to finally resolve his existential crisis. I won't give away all that happens, but let's just say...well, nothing...never mind. This novel seems to belong to a certain time and place in American history: namely, Southern California in the 1980s, at the height of that decade's renowned materialistic excess. It stares down the barrel of that excess into a pool of emptiness, from which peers back the hunted eyes of a world-weary eight-year-old boy. Bradfield has a lot to critique and he does it in an elaborate way. The characters all feel archetypal, each of them representative of one approach a person could take in response to the finger trap of American-style capitalist society. I can't say the style appealed that much to me but it's certainly a unique novel.
  I no longer wanted to live forever. I wanted only to burn intensely in the sun and extinguish, seedless and unforlorn like some unpopped pinecone.
