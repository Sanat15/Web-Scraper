Rating: 3/5

This is the second book with triangle-with-brothers I’ve read by Laurey Bright this week. If Marrying Marcus is nice and predictable then this one goes into psych mode with a clinically depressed h. 

Sorry to compare but can’t resist.*Spoilers*
*In the other book the H is the older brother who has always been in love with the (neighbors since childhood) h while she puppy worships the younger brother and patiently waits for his return from the US where he’s gone to study.
*Here the h/H meet after she marries the much older brother…and it’s instant connection/rapport that later turns into an attraction, which they both try their best to snuff out. After an almost sexual conflagration they don’t meet again for years – intentionally.

*In the other (easy) story, the om returns with a fiancée and breaks the h’s heart and the H happily picks up the pieces and pushes his own agenda and corners her into marrying him&gt;&gt;hea. 
*Here the husband/brother dies and the H is full of anger and hostility as the husband has painted his wife as a flighty, unsupportive wife who flirts with other men. All lies and workings of a sick twisted mind but the H now wants to make her confess to her said sins and then make her pay. 
The h is understandably confused and hurt as she cannot understand why he's being so mean to her.
The h’s in a fragile state of mind – not just because of her widowhood but also because of a delayed reaction to the subtle abuse she’d suffered over years. The husband has been physically disabled in an accident and so had transferred his insecurities into belittling his wife. The H takes her to his island retreat (or some such place) – the idea is to let heal and then attack!

The h comes across as apathetic and depressed and the H’s insinuations add to the angst. There’s a doctor on the island who acts as an informal medical/psychiatric support. I think that the author put him there just so the H would be let off from getting her actual medial help - and so stay on the island. She needed psychiatric evaluation and help, but is given sun, sand and intermittent unkind and hurtful comments instead. 
And yes, time as well. In a way, it's good that the story pans out over almost a year (the island time and later) because otherwise it would have been a greater farce.
