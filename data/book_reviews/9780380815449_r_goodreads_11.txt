Rating: 4/5

What was it like to be a child during World War II? To go weeks without word from your father, worrying about where he was and what dangers he faced? To live with rationing, air raid drills, and the constant fear of enemy spies? This novel, written for a pre-teen audience, pulls readers into the daily life of a Brooklyn boy in 1943, offering a vivid glimpse of childhood on the American home front.
Through Howie’s eyes, the war feels both overwhelming and immediate. Convinced his school principal must be a German spy, Howie’s suspicions grow when he spots the man slipping into a neighborhood house. His investigation reveals that his teacher’s job is in jeopardy—sending Howie on a mission to save her.
The dialogue rings true to both the era and the voices of boys his age, while period details immerse readers in wartime life: ration books, shortages, women stepping into new roles, and news reports tracking the war’s progress. Against this uncertain backdrop, Howie and his friends cling to the small ways they can make a difference, seeking control in a world where so much is beyond their reach.
