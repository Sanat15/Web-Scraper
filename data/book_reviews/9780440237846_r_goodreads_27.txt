Rating: 3/5

Historical fiction set in the Dominican Republic in 1960-1961 during the dictatorship of Rafael Trujillo. According to the author’s introductory note, her parents were born in the Dominican Republic and fled the country in 1960 when she was ten years old. She decided to write historical fiction about those who stayed in Cuba. 

Main character Anita de la Torre is a twelve-year-old girl who is initially unaware of the ongoing political oppression in her country. Her father and uncle are involved in the revolutionary movement seeking to overthrow the regime. As the story opens, her uncle has disappeared. Anita’s mother gives out the necessary background information by answering Anita’s naïve questions about what is going on.

Anita writes daily in her diary, which forms the structure of the narrative. She also writes of her crushes and normal preteen to teenage worries. The secret police are looking for dissidents and her remaining family must go into hiding. Her diary entries reflect her growing awareness of the dangers her family is facing. 

The book is targeted at middle grade readers. It is particularly good for its target audience, but adults may find it rather basic. I suppose it depends on the reader’s level of knowledge of the history of the Dominican Republic. Another well-written historical fiction for an adult reader about the Trujillo dictatorship may be found in The Feast of the Goat by Mario Vargas Llosa, which I highly recommend.
