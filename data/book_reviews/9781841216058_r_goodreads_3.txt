Rating: 5/5

A very interesting historical relic from the year 2000.

Five stars because it achieved what it set out to do: create a memento from the turn of the millennium in a way that feels profound and timeless. It nods to technology without dating itself, while also acknowledging the need for human input, symbolised by the compassion and hope of a child.

As a child, I found the story strangely melancholic, maybe due to its solemnity, but now in hindsight I am able to enjoy it a lot more.

I'll also mention that the rhythm of the poetic narration was really well done, in a way that is strangely rare in modern English language books that are written in rhyming verse.
