Rating: 2/5

MacEwan states in his preface that this is a work of historical fiction, due to their being so little factual information about Marie Anne Gaboury surviving.

To rate this book as fiction it is quite bad--the dialogue is poor, and the characterization of Marie Anne and Jean Baptiste is weak. As written, it is difficult to believe that Marie Anne was an important figure in the Red River settlement in her own right--she seems a little foolish standing up in canoes and obsessing about turnips. Very little of the mettle ascribed to her seems evident in MacEwan's characterization, and parts of the ending are bizarre. If you are not genuinely interested in learning more about the Gaboury-Lagimodiere family, you should skip this book!

As a historical document--well, I would have preferred more robust footnotes to help me discern the fact from fiction.

However, I am grateful for MacEwan for attempting to stitch this story together. As a 5th generation descendant of Marie Anne and Jean Baptiste, who lived most of my life in Winnipeg, I will take what information I can get! It was exciting to read the names that pepper Winnipeg's regions and street names and to learn something of the Red River valley's history, and that of my family.

While I would recommend Maggie Siggins book about Marie Anne for its accuracy miles ahead of this one, this book provides some context and "possibility," even though a great deal of it is embellished beyond belief.
