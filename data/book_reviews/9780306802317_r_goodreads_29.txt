Rating: 2/5

Hell in a Very Small Place, more like Hell in a Very Large Book. Prior to reading this book, I had a genuine interest in the Battle of Dien Bien Phu and its consequences in the fight for global power between East and West. I had always heard about the battle as a massive, strategic French defeat that functionally ended the French Indochina War, but I knew very little beyond that. 

Unfortunately, the book did not provide an analysis of the sweeping, long-term consequences of Dien Bien Phu, but rather focused on the minutiae of the siege itself—from gun and mortar positions to play-by-play radio traffic between commanders. That is not to say such analysis is not important. The problem is that it is boring, cluttered, and impossible to remember. 

Perhaps, though, the writer was a victim of his timing, as HIAVSP was published only a decade after the battle itself and before the completion of the United States’ own foray into Southeastern Asia. 

In short, unless you are interested in the finer details of military battles, don’t waste your time on this one.
