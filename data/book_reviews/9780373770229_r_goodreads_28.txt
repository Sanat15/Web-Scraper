Rating: 3/5

I have a fondness for Sheik-Romance books. They're always a bit cheesy but totally up my alley.

I like the plot of this, predictable as it was and there was a happy ending which I'm always a fan of. I liked that it was a country in the middle east and it stuck to that, I find often these kind of books have become totally westernised and it doesn't read true.

I hadn't read the first two books though and there was lots of random mentioning of people from other stories - which I found weird as a few secondary characters weren't given names. Phillipe's Father and Uncle, for example are referred to as 'Phillipe's Uncle' and 'The Old Sheik'. Very odd.

The writing was a little bit off for me too - there was a bit of repeated information, or one character suddenly wouldn't know something which they should as it was mentioned before and there was exposition where it wasn't really needed and none when it was. I think it made it a bit jarring to read. As did the fact that it's an older book (I assume - haven't actually checked the publication date) - it feels very dated, from the fashions mentioned (or example; his very thin gold watch' made me think it was written in the 80s).

I also didn't particularly like Gretchen. I know she was really young and inexperienced but instead of sweetly naive, she came across as wilfully ignorant. 

I was going to say I liked Philippe - but then I remembered how much of a dick he was to poor, doormat Gretchen and how he was all about Brianne, who was his 'world'. I also didn't believe his 180 later on and his declaration of 'we're just friends and that's all we've ever been'. Yeah, only because Briann'es married. I very much felt like Gretchen was his consolation prize and she seemed ok with that!? Bah.

I like this tale well enough but I'm not rushing out to read the rest of the series.
