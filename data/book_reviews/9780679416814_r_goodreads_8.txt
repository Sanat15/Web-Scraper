Rating: 4/5

bit of a tough one. but also a genius construct. the gist being this: alf, historian, is asked by a northern new england historians association to write something of a memoir about the administration of 38th president ford. alf, historion, has also attempted, once, to write a new biography of 15th president buchanan. the chapters alternating between the biography and alf's recollection of the ford administration. those last ones filled with the usual updikian musings on infidelity, lust, sex, beauty, furniture and politics. sporadically present day alf interjects within the biographical parts. and alf's extramarital affairs seem dictated by random facts of buchanan's days. when finally, in the last two chapters, both worlds bleed into each other. ending at the brink of civil war whilst halting a divorce; equilibrium of sorts, for the late updike at least. finish this novel took so long because the biographical parts about buchanan are very thorough, filled with names, places, dates and footnotes within footnotes. at times boring, at times a fun austen larp. what is most interesting here is the keen interest updike has for buchanan; the research he has done for this is must be extensive. alf is obviously an updike stand-in here. updike the biographer. but alf stands in also for updike as the historical figure of a seventies man, living in new england. two books written to be one, done in a way not many writer can pull off this well. the pudding of proof updike resides at lonely heights.
