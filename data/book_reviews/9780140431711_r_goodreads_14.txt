Rating: 4/5

Nostromo is a very fine book that has a great deal to say on the subject of American Imperialism. The novel takes place in the fictitious country of Costaguana of which will lose the remote province of Sulaco to an independence movement tied to an American corporation that operates a silver mine in Sulaco. The events are very similar to those which occurred in 1903 when an American backed independence movement in Panama seceded from Colombia so that the US could complete the Suez Canal without interference from the Colombian government based in Bogota. Thus the setting of Nostromo is very close to being Panama at the beginning of the 20th century but Conrad borrows elements from other countries in the region. As a result Conrad's analysis is not properly anchored in any real set of events Conrad wants to express his thoughts on Garibaldi and his followers. For this reason he gives us an expatriate Italian patriarch who fought with Garibaldi. The problem with this is that at the time the largest Italian expatriated community in Latin America was in Buenos Aires. There were essentially none in Panama. Sulaco appears to have a substantial middle class and a large number of intellectuals that participate actively in the independence movement. The Panama of the era had a very small middle class and very few if any intellectuals. 

What Conrad does very well in Nostromo is to make Europeans and North Americans reflect upon themselves as Imperial powers and upon the Imperial process. The novel has a great set of characters who are described and developed with great finesse. The plot is well designed. The resolutions of the issues facing the various protagonists are admirably handled.
