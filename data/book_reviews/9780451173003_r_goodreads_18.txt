Rating: 3/5

really enjoyed this one. liked how Richard Hawke deals with all the other suitors and out cons the cons man. Liked Cassiopeia, unlike other regencies, her reticence actually has a believable reason behind it. Loved all the side characters (loved John Turk and his relationship with Hawke) (really enjoyed Digory and Lady Letitia) and its satisfying to see that all characters get their own ending

Hawke baits Lord Fauxbridge, deliberately publicly shamming him of owning a plantation and slaves. He wins half of Lord Rowcliff's fortune (by cheating, because "it is much too serious to have risked everything on the vagaries of chance" though he promises the return the money after Hawke marries) then makes Lord Aetherston back out of proposing by saying Cassiopeia's line only breeds females. He pays not one cent to her brother, instead cons him into paying for his own passage to North Africa, but neglects to mention this fact to Cassie. She is at first afraid of him, but starts to love him, until she thinks he outbid everyone else to buy her from her brother. eventually everything is revealed (including that hawke was a self-made man, grew up not knowing who his family was, was sold into slavery once (and why he would never buy nor sell another human being) 

Perry renounces his Viscount-hood and marries Cassie's widowed Scottish lady's maid (who gave him a merry chase first, after stabbing him first) John rescues the vicar's hidden away daughter, Cassie's step-mother elopes off to France with a man 5-years younger than her, and Digory gives Lady Letitia an adventure of her own by taking her on a smuggling run
