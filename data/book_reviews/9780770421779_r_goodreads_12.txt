Rating: 5/5

"Bless me or not, Lord, just as you please, for I'll not beg."

For the better part of this book, I believed Hagar Shipley was a bitchy woman, bitter and unhappy, with most of her problems brought on by her own decisions and inability to let go of her false pride long enough to connect with others. When you make an unwise marriage just to show your father you won't be controlled, you get what you ask for. At ninety, she needs help from others, but resents it, and is nasty and complaining.

While arguing with her son about being placed in a care home, her mind wanders to the past and we get her story of a difficult life.

By the end of this book, I had to admire her spirit and her courage. Like the stone angel on her mother's grave, she was chipped and weathered by time, yet she refused to topple. She spoke her mind til the last, but did manage a few kind words and deeds, even though it was foreign to her nature. 

This book will remain with me for a while, because my own mother was a lot like Hagar. Everything was a fight and had to be resisted. When she had to go into assisted living before she died, I told her it made no sense to be mean to the people trying to help you. If you're nice to them, they'll be nice to you, was my advice. A few months later, she told me I was right, it worked! Wouldn't you think that, at the age of 90, you would have learned that already?

This is my second Margaret Laurence book, and I'm a dedicated fan. A Canadian author that was once very popular, she's been largely forgotten in recent years, but if you haven't read her yet, do yourself a favor and give one a try.
