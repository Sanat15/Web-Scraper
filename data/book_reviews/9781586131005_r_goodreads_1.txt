Rating: 3/5

An interesting mix of Buddhism, Taoism and Chinese superstitions. Those who have not read the texts it derives from will find it enlightening, others might find it confusing (especially without exposure to some Chinese culture, which the author clearly warns at the beginning of the book), but those who have other Buddhist and Taoist texts will find nothing startling original (the author also states this at the beginning of the book). It is more of a clarification of Buddhist thought for a particular sect than anything startlingly original, and for such a purpose it works very well.

I cannot understand, however, the need of the Chinese government to repress the followers of this sect, as none of its teachings are particularly subversive, in fact, the Buddhist and Taoist teachings could do nothing but benefit society in general. Especially useful, in a communist society, would be the negative aspects of material seeking taught so plainly here.

A good curiosity read, but I'd honestly recommend "The Dhammapada" or "Tao Te Ching" over it.
