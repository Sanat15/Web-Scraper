Rating: 4/5

This is the third Margaret Mahy book I’ve read, and I just love them even when they aren’t 100% perfect. Her books usually have two parallel but interacting storylines. 

One storyline will be contemporary and deal with a child’s real life challenges of family dynamics, life stages, sickness, and even death. The second storyline will be supernatural and the main character will face her real world problems in a different context. 

But the supernatural storyline is never just a simple allegory for real life problems. Both the natural and supernatural storylines could stand on their own, but they are both deepened and strengthened by the combination with the other.

In The Other Side of Silence we follow a girl named Hero who stopped speaking several years prior to the beginning of the story. In what she calls her “real” life, Hero is one of four imperfect siblings of a best-selling author who writes books on how to raise perfect children.

In what Hero refers to as her “true” life, she climbs through the treetops of her neighbor’s property, the reclusive and mysterious Miss Credence. Hero watches both Miss Credence’s odd behavior and her strange house, whose one tower’s main window is painted over in white.

While I really enjoyed The Other Side of Silence, it had a couple issues that kept it from being a 5 star read. Margaret Mahy introduced a lot of really good story elements, but she didn’t fully explore them all. Mahy rushed at the end to try and tie everything up, but if she just made the book longer, she could have really knocked it out of the park.

Even with the drawbacks, I still loved it, and I highly recommend it to the reading public!
