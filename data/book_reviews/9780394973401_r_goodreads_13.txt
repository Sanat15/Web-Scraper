Rating: 3/5

It's interesting that I think this is one of the few Bears books I've read that explicitly points out how the parents need to change, too, not just the kiddos. I appreciate that the outside advice of "don't try and scare kids out of a habit" is very clear against both Papa *and* Mama Bear; both of them were trying to deter Sister's nail-biting rather than redirecting it, which is something we know now is much better neurologically. (Your brain doesn't like abandoning pathways, but it can be convinced to create new ones.)

Wildly not about the money-driven aspect of this because we have enough capitalism, thank you, but the lesson was solid. It has aged mostly well--although wow all the kids at school are the worst. (Also, don't be a punk, Lizzie Bruin. I thought you two were friends!)
