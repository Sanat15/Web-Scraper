Rating: 5/5

De Balzac downed six cups of joe 
Rolled up his ample sleeves
Spent the whole night composing yet
Another masterpiece

This time about a miser who
Cares more about his gold
Than his unhappy wife and daughter
A-quaking in the cold

Of a dark room and a stone heart
And calculating eyes
Computing every speck of dust
Upon the legs of flies

A man whose only passion is
Accumulating wealth
Who of the fox does imitate
The cunning and the stealth

Amassing sixteen million
By wily sleight of hand
Acting like a simpleton 
Without a single franc

Little thinking the gold he hoards
Weighs down his daughter’s heart
Made of a far more precious metal
Unimproved by art

The old cliché will never die:
True love cannot be bought
If money makes the world go round
Without love—it will rot
