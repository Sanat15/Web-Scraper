Rating: 4/5

Funny and clever, and never a page that's dull. Also packed with useful observations on life and how to live with others. Toronto lawyer Farb finds himself enmeshed in the devious schemes of a number of clients on either side of a real estate deal, while also crossing paths with a couple of concentration camp survivors and thinking wistfully about his estranged wife. The deeper characters in the book have quirks or outright flaws. The shallower ones are not quite as shallow as they first appear, although they remain shallow. A satisfying moral tale about greed. Also features observations of legal ethics and Jewish culture in Toronto. Read it first about 30 years ago shortly after publication; it holds up very well and turns out to be better written than I remember (only a few quibbles).
It's surprising that Torgov's books now seem to be generally unavailable except in Kindle editions and at exorbitant prices on Amazon; perhaps for the current market he is too good humoured as well as being an older, upper-middle class male writing about his natural habitat.
