Rating: 3/5

Happy to be finally finished with this long, long slog of a book… Did I mention it was long? This is the memoir of William Tecumseh Sherman, the great Union general of the Civil War. His name and reputation still remains anathema among many Southerners, even 160 years after his military campaigns through the South.

In the book, Sherman documents in tedious detail what he did in his military career, going back many years before the war, and continuing on for years afterward. Sherman was certainly an able general and a devoted military man, driven by duty, honor and the maintenance of his reputation as he saw it. But for such a giant memoir, there is surprisingly little written about what the General thought or felt. He was an intelligent, educated man, but decidedly not a philosopher or even a particularly entertaining storyteller.

There is much to be learned from this memoir—its historical value is unquestionably great, but entertaining literature it ain’t.
