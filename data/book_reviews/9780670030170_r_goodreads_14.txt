Rating: 3/5

Mann's book which looks at the history of queer Hollywood from the early days to Stonewall is filled with a wealth of details that never coalesces into a coherent narrative. While I was able to finish the book it was a slog, because of Mann's style. The book is perfect for someone who is looking for pithy details about some of these obscure individuals but lacks any overall structure or style.

"The story of gay men and lesbians in Hollywood-and how they shaped their industry and art-only really begins here, in California, when they settled down among the cowboys and con men and began spinning the illusion that would transform a nation. "Came the dawn," wrote one scribe of the early days, "lavender and gold...from all walks they cam to colonize the land of dreams." 4

"It was run by Jewish immigrants who desperately sought middle-class status for both themselves and their industry, who wanted nothing less than to codify the American dream." 15

"Like the Jews, the early Hollywood gays also aspired to a certain social respectability. Unlike the Jews, however, most of the gays we can identity...came from comfortable middle-class backgrounds." 21
