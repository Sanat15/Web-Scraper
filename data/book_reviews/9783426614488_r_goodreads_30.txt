Maeve Binchy's Circle of Friends stunned me last year when I read it, and I was hoping for a repeat. In many ways, the characters and community in this are very similar to those in CoF, so I loved that. They feel lifted straight from life in all their humor, awkwardness, bitterness, pettiness, or compassion.

The plot hinges on a very unlikely event, which might be what kept me from loving it as much as CoF, although stranger things do happen in real life, so I can't complain too much. Plus, large sections of it reminded me of Anna Karenina, particularly Anna's soul in torment when she's living with Vronsky. Maeve Binchy has a gift for showing natural consequences of moral actions without a whiff of preachiness.

That being said, there was something that occurred just a few pages from the end that was really irritating for being totally unnecessary. I'm sure many people would not have an issue with it, but it soured the ending for me and kept me from giving it five stars.

Still, I would recommend it for fans of Dorothy Whipple, and vice versa. There are few 700 page books that would have me reading like my life depended on it, but that's the magic of Maeve Binchy's writing.
