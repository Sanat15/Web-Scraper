Overall this was a good read. It's the first full book I read about the Moorish civilization in Iberia, so for the time being, I do not have baseline to compare it or make a good judgment of its content. I appreciate the author's attempt to be impartial, and to see the history of Moorish Iberia from the perspective of both Moors and invading Christians.
One caveat however is that the author seems confused about the word "Moor". Sometimes he uses it with the meaning "Berber Northwest African", and other times he seems to generalize it to "Arabs" as well. He's also seemingly confused about the identity of the Muslim invaders who ruled Andalusia for about 8 centuries. In chapter 5, he seems to think that they were Arabs from Arabia (even though 2 or 3 chapters earlier he wrote that the bulk of the army, including the commander Tarik ibn Ziyad, were Amazigh, aka Berbers), and wonders how they learned to govern a country so efficiently since they were originally Bedouins from Arabia. The answer is really simple and on the nose, and has also been confirmed by modern genetic studies in Iberia and North Africa: they were culturally Arabized Berbers. Many North Africans, Moors included, had already urbanized at least 1000 years before they converted to Islam and invaded Iberia, and had good skills with administration and managing a city. They ruled in the name of Islam and Arabdom, but no matter how much they tried to hide their "lowly" origins, and claim "noble" descent close to Muhammad, the prophet of Islam, nothing will change their true identity.
