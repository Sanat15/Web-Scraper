Rating: 2/5

A Paperback from Hell... Vampyr is not. It is instead - or tries to be, anyway - a tale of paranormal romance between a wealthy and powerful vampiress and a widowed human hematologist. Val, our main vampiric character, has sought renowned blood disease researcher Dr. Theo James with the hope that he will discover what makes vampires, well, vampires. Dr. James, himself suffering from a rare and terminal form of leukemia, sees an opportunity to discover a cure for his disease as well as other cancers. Win-win, right? Until it all goes off the rails because love and stuff.

But seriously... even taken for what it is, Vampyr still suffers from several major flaws. The motivations of both the main characters, which initially seem to be core to the story, are rather easily abandoned for the sake of plot convenience. For this reason, among others, I found it hard to invest in either Val or Theo or care much about how their relationship could/would work. Character building was also a major shortcoming. Val and Theo are wooden, at best, while all of the supporting characters - the vast majority of whom are vampires - come across as little more than caricatures. The last major issue I have concerns the action - both in and out of the bedroom. Nearly every significant event, including the "final showdown", occurs off page. There are hints and innuendo, but little else.

That being said, the author does devote some serious word time to building a vampiric world that contains several unique aspects and, for the most part, avoids relying on "established" lore. A lot more could have been done to explain how the various factions gained prominence or fell from power (yep, I'm thinking a fang-y version of GoT), but what was given is still quite interesting.

In the end, I found Vampyr to be a meh sort of read. It lacks the horror (or even the thrills), the spice, and the emotion to leave much of an impression.
