Rating: 3/5

The history of the universe is written into a single pebble; thus opens Hikaru Okuizumi’s (b. 1956) novel The Stones Cry Out. Set after the Second World War, its main character, Tsuyoshi Manase, is caught between memories of war and his family life, unable to make any real connection. He seeks refuge in stones, turning to geology as a way of searching for meaning and value beyond human measure. 

Okuizumi’s style is, above all, enigmatic. The war references are reminiscent of Junpei Gomikawa (
  Menselijke voorwaarden
), and I generally appreciated the uncertainty of where the story was heading. At the same time, I found the style somewhat plodding, and by hurrying through the years, the narrative loses focus. In the end, it left me touched, intrigued, and disappointed.
