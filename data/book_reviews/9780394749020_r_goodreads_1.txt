Rating: 5/5

This book discusses Douglas’s Court Years, 1939 to 1975, in detail. I understand this is vol II of his memoir. The first book covered his early life and career.

The book is well written. The legal discussions are easy to understand by the layman. The writing is clear and concise. Douglas served on the Court for 36 years, making him the longest serving Justice to date. He was one of the first Justices to come from the West (Yakima, Washington). He was on the Court during some of the famous landmark decisions. He served in the military during WWI. Douglas was a law professor at Columbia and then at Yale. FDR appointed him to the SEC (U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission) in the 1930s. In 1939 FDR appointed him to the Supreme Court.

Douglas was one of the first environmentalist Justices. He was a big fan of Rachel Carson. Unlike the current popular trend by Justices to base their opinions on textualism or originalism, Douglas’s style was unusual. He was known to rely on philosophical insights, observations about current politics and literature, as well as the usual Judicial sources. I enjoyed the stories and insights about beliefs and work habits of the various Justices from the 1930 to 1960s. I knew little about the Justices of the thirties and forties. If you are interested in the Supreme Court, this two-volume autobiography will be an interesting read.

I read this as a hardback book obtained from Amazon. It is 434 pages published in 1980 by Random House Publishing Company. The book has lots of photographs.
