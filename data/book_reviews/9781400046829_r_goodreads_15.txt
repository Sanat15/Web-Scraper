Rating: 4/5

Pairs up well with Everyone Who Is Gone Is Here: The United States, Central America, and the Making of a Crisis, but with a narrower focus on two intertwining stories of desperate immigrant family and corrupt builders in NYC. In terms of craft and in-depth research, it calls to mind work of contemporary John McPhee. Breslin, though, has the far more acerbic tongue, which benefits from judicious and dry employment. Other than the odd column here and there, my first reading of Breslin. The words match the rep. Glad I bought copy of Library of America edition of his work. Looking forward to reading more, much more. 4 1/2 stars.
