Rating: 4/5

My sister Loni loaned me this book. When she was in Boston in June, I heard her address a crowd of 400 people in the Grand Ballroom of the Copley Plaza (yes, of course I’m bragging!). All of the participants in that Hemangioma conference were given a signed copy of Dr. Folkman’s War. At some point I began to read this book, was riveted, but then life took over and I set it aside. When I got to Jamaica, I picked up where I had left off.

This is the amazing story of Judah Folkman. It was written before his death: he died in January, 2008 at the age of 74. The book documents his life from his earliest days, his exceptional curiosity, persistence, obsession, creativity, humanity, and comfort being outside the mainstream. In fact, he had an idea about cancer cells that was ridiculed by most people in the medical and scientific communities for years: he thought that cancers grew larger via their blood supply; therefore, a cancer could be controlled by blocking its blood supply. 

Cooke presents a great deal of technical information that could easily be incomprehensible to most laypersons – yet clarity rules. Repetition also works well. The book moves chronologically and unfolds like a mystery. The suspense/ drama that constituted these real life successes and failures (failures weren’t viewed as such because there was always something “interesting” or “noteworthy” that could be gleaned from them) is palpable here. As readers we KNOW that Folkman does come up with something revolutionary in the end, but we don’t know the how or the when; we’re compelled to keep reading to learn how this brilliant man and his team figured out the answers to their endless queries. Bonus: much of this story takes place in and around Boston; the different approaches and perceptions of the scientific research community and the medical community are also revealed. Fascinating.
