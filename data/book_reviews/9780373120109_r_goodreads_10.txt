Rating: 5/5

Sara Wood’s *Expectant Mistress* is a beautifully crafted story that effortlessly transports the reader back to the turn of the millennium—a time when the world held its breath over the looming uncertainty of Y2K, and when the rise of computers and emerging tech terminology still felt novel and transformative. The hero, firmly rooted in the tech world of that era, embodies that transition, adding a subtle but fascinating historical texture to the narrative.

At its heart, however, this is a deeply emotional and layered romance. The hero’s past is particularly poignant—having grown up within a care system that failed him, with devastating consequences. The loss of his twin brother, a tragedy rooted in that very fraught system, leaves an indelible mark on him, shaping his emotional world and life choices thereafter. In the aftermath of that grief and instability, he gravitates towards the one steady presence in his life—his kind counsellor—whom he later marries. Though their union is not born of romantic love, it is grounded in mutual respect, care, and stability, and together they build an appreciable life, even welcoming a son.

His wife, older than him by more than a decade, becomes far more than a partner—she is a stabilising force, even as she battles an incurable illness. The heroine enters this world at just sixteen, living under the same roof due to her friendship with Petra, the hero’s stepdaughter. What unfolds is a quiet, restrained emotional connection—one that neither dares to acknowledge openly, bound as they are by circumstance, loyalty, and timing.

Yet, in one of the most touching aspects of the story, the hero’s dying wife—perceptive and generous in spirit—recognises the unspoken bond between them and, in her final moments, offers her blessing.

Unable to reconcile her feelings and the situation, the heroine flees, only for fate—or perhaps inevitability—to bring them back together four years later. What follows is a rekindling that feels both earned and emotionally satisfying.

Sara Wood’s brilliance truly shines in her characterisation. From the central couple to the wonderfully eccentric yet broad-minded heroin's grandmother, every character is vividly drawn and adds depth and texture to the story. The supporting cast doesn’t merely exist—they live, breathe, and shape the emotional landscape of the novel.

This is not just a romance; it is a story about timing, emotional restraint, healing, and ultimately, courage. A truly engaging read that reminds us why Sara Wood remains such a compelling storyteller.
