Rating: 4/5

Upon hearing of the passing of David John Moore Cornwell (better known by his pen name John le Carre) in December, I realised I hadn't read a novel by him or even had one on my TBR list. I was aware for that he worked for both MI5 and MI 16 during the 1950s and 60s and went on to become a very successful author. Having just finished and enjoyed Agent Sonya: Moscow's Most Daring Wartime Spy I really was in the mood for another spy novel. While Agent Sonya is a non fiction read I was assured by a friend that John le Carre's novel reads like Non Fiction. 

A story of love and betrayal, the protagonist Alex Lemas has had enough of the spying game and wants to "to come in out of the cold" but in order to do so he must perform one final assignment which will take him deep into the heart of communist Germany and betray his country.

Without a doubt this is a very convincingly written spy thriller , and I think you find the author imputed his experiences and feelings into this novel. You get a feel for the world of the spy and cant help getting drawn into the the whole espoinage game. This is a short but fast paced novel and there are lots of twists and turns to keep the reader interested. I really enjoy novels written in this time frame and so glad I gave this one a shot. 

I listened to this book on audible and the narrator was excellent. An entertaining and worthwhile read and another novel for my favourites list.
