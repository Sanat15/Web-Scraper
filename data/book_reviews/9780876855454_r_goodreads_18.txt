Rating: 5/5

Sure, Bowles may fall on easy spikes to rescue late narratives out of their seeming insolubility, but I don’t much mind. Save a cod flaring of the corporal passions unearned (but not ‘Hemingway unearned’), it’s a pretty flawless inter-sectioning of the actors, disparate and otherwise, that combine to form the many tableaux most often lumped under the lumpen banner of ‘Islamic fundamentalism.’ Which, for anyone playing at home, differs so radically from Christian evangelical fundamentalism—and I don’t mean in religious tenets—as to be an entirely different chimera. Fundamentalism can largely go fuck itself, but Bowles contributes here, some 70-odd years ago, the multivalent force actors and their malignancies that would one day reify in Islam’s global bid for the sweepstakes showdown. Fundamental Christians, meanwhile, just get court appointments and shop at J Crew. 

Slayer.
