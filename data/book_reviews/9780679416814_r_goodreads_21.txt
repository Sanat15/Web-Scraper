Rating: 1/5

One of the more disappointing novels I've read in quite a while. Updike's literary reputation likely will fade as a time passes and this novel doesn't shine at all. It feels like dim ripoff of Nabokov as the narrator uses the excuse of talking about the Ford Administration to give us a fairly overwrought account of James Buchanan (presented here as an unpublished work of scholarship) and a fairly uninteresting account of his own (over sexualized) personal life. The whole narrative is seemingly justified with the only truly clever idea in the book, that the Ford administration was so unmemorable the void had to be filled with this other stuff. Sadly though it turns out that all of Updike's observations about sex, fidelity, moral fiber, marriage, and honor are just as unmemorable.
