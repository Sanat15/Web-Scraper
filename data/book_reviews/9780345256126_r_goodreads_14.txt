Rating: 3/5

One of the few ACL books where none of the leads is spy or working for the government, and there is no -real- sense of danger. In fact, it may be one of her "lighter" novels.

Diana is living with her mother in one of the wings of an old manor that belonged to the Chalfont for years. They finally get a tenant, a mister Richmond, who while the most decrepit wing is being refurbished, he goes to London. Diana also goes to London to be with her old friends Irene and brother.

It is a bit of ball here and strolls over the park there, and having the persistent visits of her cousin, who may be a pain for her but honestly he was fun. Diana claims she will be forever an old maid since it seems she can't fall in love like she wants to, but the book ends with confessions of love out of nowhere from her part. From his part it was a bit more obvious, but I still think the ending was super rushed. And what about Letty and Irene's brother?

The mystery wasn't really a mystery since it was obvious from almost the beginning. I think in this case ACL could have written the hero much charming since he was a bit foreboding for most of the book while Diana was charming and outspoken and very lively.
