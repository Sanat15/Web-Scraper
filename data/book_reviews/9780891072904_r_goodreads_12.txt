Rating: 5/5

Much to love and apply. I aspire to even a fraction of Charlotte Mason’s excellence, wisdom, and intuition in her interactions with children.

Especially helpful ideas to me:

-thinking about children as persons with individual personalities to channel rather then change 
-not pitting play against learning. This principle would be helpful for adults, too, since so many people treat recreation time as mindless rather than both restful/fun and fruitful. 
-when possible, trying to redirect children from situations where their worst will come out rather than waiting to see whether they fail and then punishing them for it.

My quibbles: 

-School mantras just give me cultic heebiejeebies. (“I am. I can. I ought. I will” has a little bit of a “Fear is the mind-killer” vibe to me.) 
-I appreciate the emphasis on kids making progress at their own pace, but also deeply value my own mom’s extremely high expectations for us *coupled with* teaching that helped us eventually reach them. Mason was not advocating for low expectations, but I wonder whether parents following her method might often make inaccurate assumptions about their kids’ learning pace and what is possible for them to achieve.
