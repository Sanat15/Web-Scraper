Rating: 3/5

I read THE GOLD-BUG after being told it was one of five mystery stories written by Edgar Allan Poe. Frankly, I'm surprised that this one was included. For me, it was more of an adventure tale ... and, of course, adventures often have elements of the unknown about them. But, the mystery here involves cryptography to decipher a treasure map. Yes, that means there is a puzzle-solving element to it. But, would you call Robert L. Stevenson's TREASURE ISLAND a mystery?
 
Well, I'm likely being caught up in semantics.
 
The story involves a character who develops eccentric behavior after capturing an insect known as the gold bug. (Poe's puckish sense of humor cannot avoid having him ... literally ... bitten by the gold bug.) Granted, after the main narrative is completed, there is an extensive section dedicated to how the details of the treasure map were deciphered. If a Reader wanted to participate in the problem-solving, Poe (through a character) walks through the steps. It would be possible to stop reading at any point and review the information previously provided to attempt a solution.
 
On the whole, I found THE GOLD-BUG to be engaging, although modern Readers will likely find a stereotypical racist portrayal to be quite offensive. The resolution was satisfying, yet I had no feeling of having completed a mystery when I finished reading.
