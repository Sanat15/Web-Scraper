Rating: 5/5

I have seen books by Robert Littell in bookstores for over fifty years. I assumed, based, I think, on the covers, that they were generic spy stories with lots of chases, violence and fancy gadgets. 

His books are being republished by Soho Crime. In the last month or so I read two articles raving about them. Both writers made the point that Littell's books are not generic spy stories. I decided to give him a try. I started with his first book. I was wrong again. This is not a generic spy story. 

A. J. Lewinter is a ceramics scientist working for the US government. He is helping to design a ceramic nose cone for the MIRV missiles. He goes to a science conference in Tokyo. He sneaks away, enters the Russian embassy, and announces that he wants to defect to Russia. 

The book is about how the Russians and Americans react to the defection. The Russians have to decide if this is a real defection or an American ruse. Lewinter claims to have vital information about the flight paths of MIRV missiles. It is incredibly important, if it is true. The Americans have to figure out what secrets Lewinter can reveal and decide how to respond to the defection. The Russians then have to decide if the US response is real of fake, and on and on. 

Littell follows the complicated and flawed agents, analysts and supervisors on both sides. He is very good on the black humor arising from bureaucracies under stress. The street level operatives on both sides are at war with their superiors and terrified about being blamed when things go wrong. Littell enjoys flipping back and forth between sides as they each systematically misinterpret each other.

Lewinter is a mystery in the middle of the story. We get transcripts of interviews with his wife, his brother and coworkers. None of them understand what he did. He is devious and naive at the same time. 

This is an exciting story which barrels along to an excellent ending, which surprised me. This won't be my last Littell book.
