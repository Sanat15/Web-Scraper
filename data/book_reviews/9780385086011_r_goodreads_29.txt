Rating: 4/5

** 4 stars **

The Collected Poems of Theodore Roethke will take you on a journey. It is a credit to his talent as a poet that the poems included here run the gamut from humorous and charming with simple, regular rhyme schemes to meditative and existential sonnets to blank verse poems in multiple parts that read like opium-induced fever dreams. Truly, I never knew from one page to another what was coming, which gave the book an addictive quality for me where I wanted to keep reading just to see what Roethke would do next. 

The earliest and latest poems included in this collection--Open House and The Far Field--are Roethke's most approachable (and, at least for me, more enjoyable) and charming work. I also enjoyed many of his poems included from Words for the Wind. Among those are probably his most often-anthologized poems "The Dream" and "I Knew a Woman." There's also a great poem from his volume The Lost Son that begins with the line, "I have known the inexorable sadness of pencils." That might have been my favorite line in the entire collection. Personally, I couldn't make much sense of the last sequence of poems in The Lost Son, the entirety of Praise to the End, and parts of The Waking. But I recommend trying out this collection if you like poetry and if you're willing to go along for the ride on Roethke's journey of language, form, and images.
