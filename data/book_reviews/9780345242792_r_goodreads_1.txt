Rating: 4/5

This is the second volume of Williamson &amp; Pohl's Undersea trilogy, a series for younger readers from the mid-1950s. Jim Eden, a young man who was been expelled from the Sub-Sea Academy on trumped-up charges but was vindicated in the first volume, has continuing adventures in the deeps when his friend David Craken has an encounter with sea serpents, mermen, and other terrors beneath the waves. It's not quite as engaging as the first book, but I enjoyed it very much. The underwater domed city of Marina and the aquatic environments are expertly depicted. The books are near the top of my favorites from both authors. Not all of the best science fiction happens in outer space! The covers by Gino D'Achille are breath-taking and are still among my all-time favorites, too.
