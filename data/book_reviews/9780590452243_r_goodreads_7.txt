Rating: 2/5

“Mystery in the Night Woods” by John Lawrence Peterson is, hands down, one of the weirdest children’s books I’ve ever read. Like, imagine a crime drama set in the animal kingdom, but instead of compelling villains and clever detectives, you get a flying squirrel who kidnaps Miss Owl to force her into marriage. Yes, that’s the actual plot.

The story spirals into a bizarre mix of jailbreaks, revenge plots, and random nocturnal critters who act like they’re auditioning for the woodland version of “Law &amp; Order.” There’s a bat sidekick, a badger with questionable motives, and a skunk police chief who… well, let’s just say his law enforcement skills didn’t impress me.

While the sheer absurdity of it all might have some nostalgic charm, it left me feeling like I just walked out of a fever dream. It’s the kind of book that makes you wonder, “Who thought this was a good idea for kids?” Don’t get me wrong, there are some endearing moments, and I can see why this might have been beloved back in the day, but the unintentional weirdness overshadows the charm.

Final verdict: 2.5/5 stars. Read it if you’re in the mood for something completely unhinged, but maybe don’t try to explain the plot to anyone—they’ll think you made it up.
