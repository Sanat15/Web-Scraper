Rating: 4/5

A very readable biography of Cook, naturally focussing on his three antipodean voyages. Very well-researched without being dry, and just about the right amount of (academic) humour interspersed throughout.

The only shortcoming for me was that the reader doesn't get a great sense of Cook's personality. We understand what he is like as a captain and as an explorer, but I was not left with any real idea of what Cook thought or felt. This is most likely a result of Cook's own journals, which are matter-of-fact and don't give much indication of his emotions.

All in, a very strong history of Cook's voyages and by extension, of the mapping of much of New Zealand and many Pacific islands by European navigators for the first time.
