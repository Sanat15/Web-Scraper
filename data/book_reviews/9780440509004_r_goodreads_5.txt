Rating: 5/5

This book is 20 years old and still completely relevant. Actually, it is probably more relevant now than it was at the time of writing as violence against children and teens has only increased in the last two decades. Particularly during the Covid-19 pandemic, as parents have to resort to alternative options for their child care as well as schooling, it is a well timed read. 

Rather than hand wring over the risk Covid-19 poses to your child or teenager, you would do well to read this book and more appropriately channel your concern for your offspring to focus onto the more realistic and likely threats they face of abuse, attack and/or exploitation in their day to day exposures through friends, family, schools, and child care. 

Protecting the Gift will provide ample room for reflection of what is worthy of worry (not a lot) versus preparedness and acknowledgment of risks. Mr. de Becker lays out realistic scenarios and examples to help you, and for you to help your child or teen see where risk is most likely to manifest and what to do to avoid those situations arising in the first place, as well as what to do if the situation happens. It is very common sense and straight forward, which is needed more than even in a PC world set on telling us to ignore our fear and simply act out of societal niceties, ignoring blaring alarms in the process. 

This is my favorite quote from the book, and I think it is a good summary of how so many have misplaced concerns: 

"For a species with a 100 percent mortality rate, we spend our protective resources rather strangely. One would think that children—who have the opportunity to live the longest—would be society’s central concern, but often they are not."

Highly recommend, along with The Gift of Fear.
