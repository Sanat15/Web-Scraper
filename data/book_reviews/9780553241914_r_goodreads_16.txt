Rating: 3/5

While not Stout's best work, these three novellas do keep the pot a-boiling (pun intended). I learned to oven-roast corn from the story "Murder is Corny", which I first read as a reprint in Ellery Queen Mystery Magazine back in the 1970s. We tried Wolfe's method with fresh-picked sweetcorn from the backyard garden and it was unbelievable. The recipe is the best part of the story--but it's definitely worth it. Angels sing softly in the distance when you eat corn roasted this way.

"Kill Now, Pay Later" has a slightly more noir-fiction feel, right down to the drunken widow. It's interesting to note that in both this story and Blood Will Tell, Archie has a couple of hissy-fits at Wolfe, basically because he feels left out of the loop. He doesn't yell, he gets all hurt and jealous and gives Wolfe the silent treatment, which affects the great man not a whit--but is an interesting sidelight on Archie's character.

Perhaps it's because I listened to this audio book in several sessions, but I found Blood Will Tell confusing and convoluted (aside from being wildly improbable, as most cosy murders are). My question was, why would he bother? Kick her out of the building, certainly--but the best revenge would be to find someone else, younger and prettier if not richer, to rub her nose in.
