Rating: 4/5

The worst time for any parent of young children is when the children reach that stage where their curiosity and insatiable thirst for knowledge drives them to ask never-ending questions. Whether it is about the sky, about how things work or any other issues parents struggle to answer these very same questions, which may have never been fully answered in their own tender years. And that is where this book comes into play.

 The Big Book of Questions and Answers is the answer to many parents with such inquisitive children for it not only addresses the major questions but a whole host of other ones as well. And what is best is the book breaks down the answers in brief yet descriptive and easy-to-understand replies that will satisfy some of the querying edge. And with it further broken down into sections it will make looking up answers quite easy.

 What also helps to make this book a fun read for children or one to peruse through is the brightly colorful illustrations throughout the book. Most of them are realistic but the illustrator did take some liberties in making some interesting characters whose facial expressions and portrayal makes them quite memorable to the reader such as the wild rodent on the snowmobile or a coy hippo hiding under an overhang with a shocked rhino as a tornado touches down. There is only one thing I found with with the illustrations and that is the illustrator's affinity for blue eyes, which appear in the weirdest of locations.

 Also since of this book having been around since my own days of youth and the ongoing march of science some of the answers are now a bit outdated such as the biggest dinosaur, the fact that bears don't really hibernate, red pandas aren't actual bears and the very loose definition of vegetables. Still all in all the majority of information is solid and the book is still one to be highly recommended to help young readers to start learning about the world around them even before they start to ask.
