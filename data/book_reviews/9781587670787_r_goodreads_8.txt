Rating: 4/5

In these stories, Laymon is like a Lambourghini--he accelerates to a white-knuckle thrill-ride in the blink of an eye. Every story goes from exposition to thrill almost immediately. The problem is that he doesn't always stick the landing--several of the endings are rushed and don't pack the proper punch ("The Hunt," "Bedtime Stories"), while others meander in with decent but predictable conclusions ("Desert Pickup").

But when Laymon connects, brother, he knocks you out. The title story alone is one of the best bits of short horror I've read during my year-long horror anthology binge. It's not just a great story, it's almost a little parable about the subversive role of horror in society (I don't think Laymon intended it that way, but just dig a little under the surface--it's there).
