Rating: 4/5

★★★★

The Painter of Signs was my first R.K. Narayan novel, and my biggest takeaway is just how much I loved his writing. His descriptions of everyday life in Malgudi are so warm and immersive that it genuinely feels like you’re living alongside its people. I also found Raman to be such a fascinating protagonist. I loved reading his inner monologues, and I thought Narayan did an incredible job capturing the mind of someone who constantly overthinks everything (something I’m probably a little too familiar with myself).

The relationship that surprised me the most was the one between Raman and his aunt. I really wasn’t expecting it to become the emotional heart of the story, but by the end it was easily my favourite part of the book. Daisy was an interesting character too. I liked Narayan’s portrayal of such an independent, modern woman for the time, even if I can understand why some readers might have mixed feelings about how she’s written.

The ending left me with slightly mixed feelings. I wasn’t completely sold on how Raman and Daisy’s relationship turned out, but at the same time, it also felt like the ending that made the most sense for these two characters. It wasn’t exactly the ending I wanted, but I think it was the right one. Overall, this was a wonderful introduction to Narayan’s work, and it’s definitely left me wanting to spend more time in Malgudi.
