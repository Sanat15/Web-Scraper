Rating: 5/5

the octopus by frank norris is set in late 19th-century california and is loosely based on a real conflict between wheat farmers and the southern pacific railroad. at its core, it’s about land rights and power: farmers watching the railroad slowly consume their labor, their land, their futures. the railroad becomes this mechanical, capitalist force—impersonal, inevitable, and completely indifferent to human life.
what makes the novel so devastating is that norris made this a novel for and of the people, the many lifes caught in that machinery. it’s deeply human and utterly tragic; i cried reading it. lives are ruined, not (only) because of individual cruelty, but because of “progress,” innovation, and a system that prioritizes profit over people. norris creates this tension beautifully: he writes with empathy while insisting that these inhuman forces are ultimately unstoppable. it’s brutal, overwhelming, and haunting. i really think this will stays with me for a while.
when i had to pick this up for class i did not think i would enjoy reading 700 pages about wheat farmers. boy, was i wrong. i don't think i can ever convince someone to pick this up, but i promise it is so so rewarding!
