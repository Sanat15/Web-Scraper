Rating: 5/5

Thomas B. Costain was one of the twentieth century’s finest writers of popular British history and historical fiction. He combined the skills of a journalist and novelist to make history come alive with sharp characterizations and detailed descriptions of time and place while maintaining an historian’s perspective based upon a solid foundation of research and analysis.

“The Conquering Family” is taken from Costain’s larger four volume work, “The Plantagenets.” “The Conquering Family” covers the period following the Norman Conquest, from the harsh but capable reign of Henry I to the odious tyrant John. Most of the focus is on the Angevin* monarchy, Henry II and his queen Eleanor of Aquitaine, their sons who succeeded to the throne, Richard and John, and their greatest nemesis, Phillip II of France. Add conflicts among church, state, barons, and commoners, not to mention the Third Crusade, and you have quite a story.

Henry II built an Angevin empire that, at its height, included all of England, approximately half of France, parts of Wales and Ireland. John lost most of his continental possessions, but his misrule resulted in Magna Charta, the codification of individual rights to life, liberty and property protected by due process and, most importantly, the principle that the king and his government were not above the law.

*Angevin, taken from Henry II’s title, Count of Anjou.
