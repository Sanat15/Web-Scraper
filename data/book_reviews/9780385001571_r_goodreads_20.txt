Rating: 2/5

I always think that Irving Stone's novels should be my cup of tea because I love big historical novels. Alas, I am always wrong whenever I try to read something of his.

Stone does his research and it shows in the minute details. Unfortunately, the need to share such minute details results in 600+ page slog through what everyone had for breakfast, not to mention who came to visit with them, what the sky looked like, who said what, what the carriage looked like, etc..

This book is about John Adams as seen through the eyes of his wife, Abigail. The first part of the book was okay...we saw how they courted and married and had their first child. In the meantime, John was also trying to set the foundational ideas for America's freedom, which you would think would be interesting, right? No. Even the Revolutionary War is a bit boring in this because Stone bogs down in details not necessary to the plot. 

I hit the middle of this and quit because it was just so boring. I had the same issue with his book about Freud. Too much detail that slows the momentum of the storyline.
