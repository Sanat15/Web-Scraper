Rating: 4/5

❄️ This short book put the cold of deep winter in my veins. I felt every icy page, every icy word. I remember when we were discussing this work in a Masters class that a bro asked if any of us had ever worked in a northern bush camp - he meant really north, like Alaska or the Yukon or northern BC etc. The icy dreariness and monotony of the day described in the novel, he felt, was exactly like the icy dreariness and isolation he experienced in such a work camp. I knew precisely what he was talking about because I’d had the same experience in the same sort of camp, where we all bunked in trailers while a hydroelectric dam was being constructed, and the severe cold was frost in our blood. Of course, for this seems to be the way the world turns, revisionists are now saying the Gulag never happened and any evidence of the camps is being bulldozed and removed as if the camps never were. Solzhenitsyn knew better and left us this book, and many others, as a testament to human cruelty and the darkness of despotic regimes. But also as a testament to the human ability to endure and overcome. Probably his most readable and approachable work ❄️
