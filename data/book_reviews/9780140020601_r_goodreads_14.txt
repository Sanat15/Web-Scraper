Rating: 4/5

Simply to amuse myself I keep a mental list of novels that cry out for adaptation by certain directors; and Victory is one. Though it has been filmed five times since its publication in 1915 - the first film was a 1919 silent with Lon Chaney as the bloodthirsty bandit Ricardo! - the novel’s rotating points of view and tonally contrasting Parts made me daydream of an all-auteur anthology piece. 

In Part I Conrad introduces the hospitality- 
entertainment economy in which two of the female characters are trapped, as well as the Java Sea’s weary, unsentimental culture of schooner captains and small traders (strong drinks on hotel verandahs, cards and gossip late into the hot nights). Sounds like a job for Jane Campion, right? 

Tarantino would have fun with Part II, built as it is of arch, droll, increasingly tense banter among eccentric killers and their potential victims. I’ll put the Coen brothers in too - the mysterious Mr. Jones, mostly languid but on occasion abruptly and efficiently homicidal, read like some spiritual ancestor of Anton Chigurh. 

Part III - in the jungle fastness of Simburan Island Anglo-Swedish Baron Heyst with English Alma, and Chinese Wang with his never-named native wife, make homes in the overgrown ruins of an abandoned coaling station - Part III goes to Claire Denis. 

For Part IV? A resurrected Kubrick, or Hitchcock (who storyboarded and nearly directed the original 1962 Peck-Mitchum Cape Fear). Victory’s last act is a strange blend of farce, tension and tragedy, played out under a tropical storm. 

 This reading of Victory is my first Conrad since Heart of Darkness many, many years ago. I enjoyed it. Conrad’s style is more lyrical than I remember, and Victory is cleverly structured; Didion said that before starting a new novel she first re-read Victory, as it “opens up the possibilities” of the form, “makes it seem worth it.” I think I’ll read Nostromo soon.
