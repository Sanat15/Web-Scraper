Rating: 2/5

Neither gripping nor believable, the book is an unimpressive late-Cold War story of a military officer working to uncover a mystery and prevent a new World War. The closest analogy is Tom Clancy's "Sum Of All Fears", but Clancy's universe fits just within the suspension of disbelief while this novel rockets past those limits and into imagination. The characters seem wooden and unsophisticated, like caricatures of the actors they are supposed to represent. The plot also has too many improbable parts but just enough tropes to clearly be a late 80's work of fiction.

I won't spoil the macguffin at the end, but it's what bends the plot from from political fiction to full-on high fantasy. The only reader who might be drawn to the story is the extreme cultivar of Cold-War era fiction who keeps a collection of anything written at the time, but nearly any casual reader would not be drawn in to the plot of "Iskra".
