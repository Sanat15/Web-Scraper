Rating: 2/5

It's a good book that talks about the Spanish expeditions in search of El Dorado. Hemming gives details about how the Native Americans received the Spaniards initially, but it seemed that each time the Spaniards felt the natives were getting restless with them around, so they would attack first and kill many. Then they would enslave them and force them to carry their belongings as they looked for gold, which they did not often find.

This book does not talk a lot about Pisarro and the conquest of the Incas, nor does it talk about Cortez and Mexico, because the myth of El Dorado started after these first conquests. It does talk about how there would sometimes be multiple expeditions and how they met up with each other or just missed each other. It seemed that nearly all of the Native Americans they forced to accompany them would die along the way and many of the Spaniards would, too. Most of this history chronicles what happened in the 1500s.

The last chapter talked about Sir Walter Raleigh and his attempt to get back in Queen Elizabeth's favor by finding El Dorado, but his expedition found no gold, only conflict and misadventure. There were one or two expeditions that found some gold; these were ones that went into present-day Colombia. They did not have nearly as much as the Incas, but the picture of gold art on the cover came from this culture who would flatten out the gold to make it spread farther. I don't have the book any more, but I believe these were the Muiscas.
