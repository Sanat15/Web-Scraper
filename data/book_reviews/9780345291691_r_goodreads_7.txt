Star Surgeon by James White continues the warm, humane charm of the Sector General series while expanding its scale in a big way. Once again, it’s wonderfully entertaining, but this time the narrative, while still a bit episodic, feels more focused. The story opens with a classic Sector General medical mystery, an unknown extraterrestrial patient from another galaxy, and gradually escalates until the hospital finds itself caught up in an intergalactic war. The contrast between careful diagnosis and large-scale conflict works surprisingly well.
One of the real pleasures of the book is watching Dr. Conway’s growth. It’s fun seeing him evolve from the naïve, overly idealistic newcomer who thinks he understands everything and holds some pretty strong opinions about the Monitor Corps, into someone capable of working alongside them on a dangerous alien world. The character development gives the story a bit more weight than the first installment.

I read some criticism of Merchison-essentially the only human woman in the book-as functioning solely as Conway’s love interest. But since the story is largely filtered through Conway’s perspective, that framing feels somewhat natural within the narrative, even if it leaves you wishing the character had a bit more room of her own.

Overall, this is another delightful entry in the Sector General saga: imaginative aliens, clever medical puzzles, and that rare strain of optimistic, pacifist (even in the face of war) science fiction that's making this series so much fun to read. I’m having a blast with it.
