Rating: 3/5

I bought this novel when Timescape, the science fiction imprint of the Pocket Books division of Simon &amp; Shuster, published it in 1982 with "Science Fiction" printed on both the cover and the spine, and with a very nifty Carl Lundgren cover that had an obvious science fiction reader wearing a propellor beanie standing next to piles of science fiction books and model rockets, a dragon, a UFO, etc. I was surprised to find that there isn't any genre content at all, it just has a kind of stereotyped sf fan as one of the characters. He's a socially inept nerd in 1950s California who has to move in with his sister and her husband. It's a very funny (in a very dark black-humor way) story, and a scathing satire about trendy society of the time and place. As the back cover explains, crap-artist Jack Isidore believes that the world is going to end on April 23, 1959, but his sister and her husband, Fay and Charlie Hume, are too busy with adultery, insanity, fantasy and badminton to care. It's a good novel but should come with a trigger-warning about animal abuse. I enjoyed it well enough but would never read it again.
