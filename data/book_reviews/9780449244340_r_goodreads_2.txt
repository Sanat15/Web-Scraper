Rating: 4/5

Andre Norton's third book (after THE PRINCE COMMANDS and RALESTONE LUCK -- she would go on to write or co-write over 100 more) is what we would call today "Historical Fiction." Although her first two books were enjoyable, Norton's writing is much more solid here than in the previous two books. She's clearly done her research, too. Her protagonist, Joanna, must overcome multiple obstacles to rescue her brother, who has been sold into slavery to Pre-Revolutionary War America. Norton describes both British and colonial American life in great detail. The story is interesting, with some intriguing twists in the plot. The characters never acquire much depth, but the plotting is strong and the story is interesting. Since it was written in 1942 there are some stereotypes present, but overall the tone is fairly enlightened, especially in regards to women's roles and indigenous people. (With that said, there were a few times I rolled my eyes and had to remember when it was written.)

I enjoyed it quite a bit, despite its faults.
