Rating: 3/5

Gerald Green was an American author who wrote over 20 novels. The Last Angry Man was his most well-known and was adapted into a movie. Green was born and raised in Brooklyn, his family was Jewish and his father was a medical doctor; Green worked in TV as well and was the co-creator of NBC’s Today Show. In The Last Angry Man, Green’s second novel, Sam Abelman, the main character, is born in and spends his life in Brooklyn, is Jewish and makes his career as a General Practitioner of Medicine. Also, the early days of the TV industry play a central role in the novel. 

The Last Angry Man was an enjoyable read. Set in the 50’s in Brooklyn, the characters felt authentic, and the descriptions of Brooklyn made me think I was there, experiencing it for myself. Sam Abelman, the lead character, had a strong personality but was someone you could admire and sympathize with. The portrayal of the life of a general practitioner in this time and place was descriptive, detailed and believable. I was impressed with the research necessary for Green to write those scenes. The TV and advertising industries play an important role in the story as well, and I felt I was getting a glimpse at what the early days of TV must have been like. 

I didn’t love the prose. At times, it felt stilted and even preachy. Some of the minor characters felt like caricatures. The structure of the novel was very traditional and so did not add anything to the experience. In the last third of the novel one of the major characters, Thrasher, an advertising exec, introduced a philosophy that captured how he and supposedly the author saw the world. I found some value in this depiction, but the time spent on it ultimately, for me, detracted from the story. 

Overall, a good reading experience, but did not reach the wonderful level.
