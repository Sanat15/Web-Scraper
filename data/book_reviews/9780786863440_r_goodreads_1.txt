Rating: 4/5

Pretty scattered but enjoyable reading, especially for picking up and putting down in short bits. It covers a lot of territory starting with the Irish emigration to the Americas in the 1840s due to potato blight, starvation, and incredibly bad policies by their English rulers at that time. The history of these immigrants to the new world comes first in chapters dealing with the shepherding of the Catholic Church in major cities on the east coast (not all were Catholic, but it was often safe to stick together), Irish American enthusiasm for politics—again a protection, work that these usually poorly educated farm kids found, and finally into the 20th century when many did far better than their immediate ancestors. There is good coverage of the arts, music and high office.

The illustrations and photographs are particularly interesting and the main text is interspersed with sidebars by famous people explaining some of their experiences or city life. Scattered but always interesting.
