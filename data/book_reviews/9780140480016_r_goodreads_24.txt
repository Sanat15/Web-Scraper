Rating: 3/5

This was definitely a mixed bag for me. The Doctor's Dilemma chews on some vital ethical questions, especially ones that hadn't really been codified at the time it was originally written and performed. The story is quite engrossing, and the humour actually lands really well (I'll admit I laughed out loud at several points, and I was particularly amused by the doctor who suggested everything was "blood poisoning"). Honestly though, the actual ethical conclusions that can be drawn from this were really very icky, and you absolutely tell that George Bernard Shaw was an antivaxxer eugenicist. 

I listened to the LA Theatre Works production which had solid enough performances all around. Their science-themed plays usually end with an interview with a relevant expert, which this one did. I usually love those interviews, but this one was kind of lacking considering the content of this play. The interview was only five minutes long and didn't really take the time to probe the most troubling aspects of this play.

Overall, this had its moments and I definitely did get some enjoyment out of it, but I was definitely pulled down by the very unfortunate and dated ethical themes.
