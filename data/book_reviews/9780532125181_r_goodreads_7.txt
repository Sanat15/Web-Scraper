Rating: 4/5

Another return to one of my childhood's favourite books.

This one didn't fair so well as, say, Rendezvous With Rama.

As a kid reading VOTSB I was blown away by the concept of Nexialism. 

This time around I felt its potential wasn't fully realised and also I could see some limitations as to how it would work.

I realise now it was also a 'deus ex machina' to allow the MC to do all he did and allow him to run rings around those he interacted with but felt that others would have been aware of at least SOME of it's potential, but all other branches of science seemed to exist in a vacuum in the land of the '8th Human Civilisation.'

It turns out I could only remember one of the four short stories that make up this fix-up novel.

I assumed that as I re-read the book other memory bombs would go off and I'd say "Oh wow - THAT's where I remember that from"

Nope, nothing apart from the last adventure which I still remembered vividly as it turns out.

The book itself is still a remarkable piece of work in that that some of its parts were written nearly 100 years ago.

It is a testament to the author that when I first read it (when it was ONLY forty years old) I didn't see how outdated the science and attitudes to space exploration seemed.

One remarkably good element of the book was Vogt's portrayal of aliens - they were so very...alien.

Not your typical 1950's bug eyed monsters but ones full of 'otherness' that as a kid I probably didn't fully appreciate.

I would say this was in fact the novels outstanding feature.

That, and the awesome Chris Foss artwork on the cover, which initially drew me to buy it in a second hand bookshop in the late 70's.

It still gives me a shiver of frisson when I see it - the power of book-jackets, eh?:)

Kid me gave it a 5*

Reflective, grown-up me gives it a rounded up 3*

Split the difference and call it a 4*
