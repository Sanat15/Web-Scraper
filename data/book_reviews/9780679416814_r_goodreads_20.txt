Rating: 2/5

A bizarre fictional reflection on life in the 1970s. A frustrated scholar in an academic backwater is preoccupied with his desire to make historical diamonds from the carbonic life and administration of bachelor (virgin?) President James Buchanan while simultaneously burying his snout in netherlands of his decidedly non-virgin "perfect wife" mistress. The narrative offers minimal structure -- just an occasional break between paragraphs, not even chapters. It is repleat with spaghetti-like sentences of exhausting length and complexity. It isn't clear whether the structure is intended as a comentary on the amorphous shape of the Ford era and, more generally, the 1970s, or is representative of Updike's style. One thing I do know: This is the first Updike book I have managed to finish, and it may be the last one I even try.
