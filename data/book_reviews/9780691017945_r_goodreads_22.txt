Rating: 4/5

C. G. Jung’s 'Synchronicity: An Acausal Connecting Principle' is a profound exploration of what we usually dismiss as coincidence. Jung defines synchronicity as the simultaneous occurrence of causally unrelated events that share a deep, common meaning. 

He illustrates this concept with a famous clinical example. For example, while a highly rational patient was recounting a dream about a golden scarab, a remarkably similar beetle suddenly tapped at the window and flew into the room. To provide empirical grounding for these phenomena, Jung also examines statistical data from J.B. Rhine's extra-sensory perception experiments, the intuitive method of the ancient Chinese 'I Ching' and astrological marriage horoscopes. 

Ultimately, Jung argues that the strict law of cause and effect cannot explain all natural phenomena. He proposes an 'acausal orderedness' that meaningfully connects our inner psychological states with the external physical world.
