Rating: 3/5

Looks like this will be my next read, although I probably ought to get back to some others I've put down for a while. They're languishing... I've read a few of Theroux' books and I generally like his work. This one goes back to 1971. Let's have a peek at some G'reads reviews first...

Moving along with what constitutes alacrity in my reading life. A number of G'reads reviewers have semi-panned this book with the general commentary that the story is dated and that Theroux seems more interested in writing a that should have been taken its subject matter more seriously. But here's the thing(for me, at least), this is what he does... something along the lines of black(no joke intended by me) farce and semi-depressing realism. So far this is the fourth book of his that I've read(plus one devastating short story(Monkey Hill) in the New Yorker. I haven't read any of his more straightforward travel books, which some reviewers seem to prefer. All of these, to a greater or lesser degree possess a distinctly bitter edge to the low comedy proceedings. Mr. Theroux does NOT seem to think much of the human species. especially men. But more important, to me anyways, is that he's a distinct and, dare I say it, great writer... tons of fun to read. This part of a review has everything to do with how the Theroux prose lines up with my brain. That's just the way it is, I guess.

Finished with this a few days ago along with some side reading/research to get some context. PT spent time in the Peace Corps in WE but got booted out when he went political. I spent some time in West Africa and left when my vacation was over - as planned. There are similarities with both places. The Theroux prose is ever interesting and fun to read - pungent. The story seems a bit dusty, however and for that reason I lowered my rating to 3.5*, which rounds down to 3*. Liked it... didn't love it.
