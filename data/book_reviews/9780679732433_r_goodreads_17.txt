Rating: 4/5

I want to preface the review by saying I felt there was something a tad off with the translation of this copy: I know that to read a book translated at all raises some issues, but the specific translation here felt a tad off, so I get the sense I was unable to get the same experience as I could have reading it in Spanish, or just having read a better translation.

In saying that, I thought this was a thoroughly poignant experience delving into not just the material conditions of poverty in Central America, specifically El Salvador, but also the social conditions both created by such poverty and oppression, and the social conditions that enable such systems of inequality. Its recurring flirtations with religious matters and the role religion plays in politics and poverty was intriguing as well. 

There’s something to be said here on the way One Day of Life does its best to portray the impoverished class as more than just the systems that oppress and impoverish them—something I believe a book like 1984 also engaged with to an extent. In the myriad of discussions on the topic of inequality and impoverished people, it feels as though they have been reduced to just their class, as though they are a monolith of people nigh on incapable of introspection or any type of thought beyond their immediate material concerns: One Day of Life is great in combatting that.

I struggle to summarise my thoughts on the prose itself given my concerns on the translation, though what I can say is there were some excellent treasure hidden amongst the general narrative, moments of a masterful command of language that cause a brief pause for reflection. Perhaps I also struggled with the confusing timeline and perspectives here and there. Not to a great extent, but sometimes I was left a tad disoriented and needed to retrace my steps.

Definitely a book that is something of a required reading, and has served to spark an interest in further investigating the political violence that wracked not just El Salvador but wider Central America in the past century.
